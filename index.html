    How to Read and Use This Housing Data

    This platform aggregates median home values, median gross rent, owner-occupancy rates, and price-to-rent ratios for every county in the United States that has a published estimate from the Census Bureau's American Community Survey (ACS). Rather than guessing which neighborhood is "expensive" or which rental market is "hot," you can compare thousands of counties using the same consistent, federally-published dataset. Homebuyers use the data to benchmark a target county against its state and national averages; investors use the price-to-rent ratio to screen for cash-flow-friendly markets; researchers and journalists use the Market Score to identify which counties combine affordability with long-term stability.

    There are three ways to navigate the site. Use the search bar above to jump directly to a specific county or state. Use Browse by State at the bottom of this page if you already know your region. Or use the Compare tool to overlay two to five counties on a single radar chart — handy when you are choosing between a high-momentum Sun Belt county and a stable Midwest one. Every county detail page also links to its ZIP-level sub-markets so you can drill down further.

    Understanding the Market Score

    The Market Score is a composite index we calculate for every county using four independent axes, each normalized to a 0–100 scale:

    • 💰 Affordability — how far the local median income stretches against the local median home price. Higher is better for would-be buyers.
    • 🏠 Stability — the share of owner-occupied households and the volatility of home prices over the trailing five years. High stability means fewer boom-bust swings.
    • 📈 Momentum — recent home-value appreciation and permit activity. Momentum flags counties where demand is accelerating (or cooling) faster than the national trend.
    • ⚡ Vitality — net migration, job postings, and business establishments, capturing underlying economic demand.

    The Market Score is intentionally not a buy/sell recommendation. It is a screening tool: a county scoring 90 is worth a second look, while a county scoring 40 may still be an excellent fit for a specific buyer (e.g., remote workers seeking low taxes). Always click into the county page to see the underlying numbers before making any decision.

    Data Sources and Methodology

    Every number on this site traces back to a published government or open dataset. Housing values, rent, and occupancy come from the U.S. Census Bureau's American Community Survey 5-Year Estimates. Year-over-year price change comes from the FHFA House Price Index (Federal Housing Finance Agency). Employment and establishment counts come from the Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS) QCEW. Internal migration comes from IRS Statistics of Income county-to-county migration files. Mortgage rates come from FRED (Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis).

    The ACS is a survey, not a census, so small counties carry wider margins of error — always treat single-year changes in low-population counties with caution. The FHFA HPI is quarterly and lagged by roughly one quarter. Because our data pipeline re-downloads each source and regenerates the site every month, the numbers you see here reflect the most recent public release, and the "as of" date is shown on each county detail page. If you need the raw files for your own analysis, they are freely downloadable from the original agency websites.

    The 2026 U.S. Housing Market at a Glance

    As of the latest update, 30-year mortgage rates hover in the mid-to-high 7% range, the highest sustained level since 2000. Higher financing costs have compressed affordability in most coastal metros, but they have also created pockets of opportunity in the Sun Belt and Midwest where prices have not kept pace with incomes. Counties flagged as Best Value Markets in our Rankings section currently include a mix of secondary metros in Texas, Tennessee, and the Carolinas — areas that still combine strong in-migration with a reasonable price-to-income ratio.

    On the rental side, the past eighteen months have been a story of regional divergence: rents are softening in Austin, Boise, and Phoenix, while holding firm in the Northeast and Midwest. If you are evaluating a buy-vs-rent decision, run the numbers on a specific county rather than relying on national averages — the gap between the most and least affordable county in a single state is often larger than the gap between the national average and a whole state.
