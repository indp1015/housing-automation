Understanding the 6-Axis Livability Score: A Complete Guide for Homebuyers

Guides · Published 2026-10-21 · 10 min read

Traditional real estate sites rank counties by median home value alone. But a $300,000 house in a high-crime area is a worse investment than a $400,000 house next to great schools and hospitals. Livability requires multi-dimensional thinking.

The Six Axes Explained

1. Affordability (0–100)

Combines the price-to-income ratio (median home value divided by median household income) with rent burden (annual rent divided by income). Higher scores mean housing costs less relative to local wages. Counties in Alabama and Mississippi consistently score above 80. Coastal California and New York City boroughs score below 10.

2. Stability (0–100)

Measures eight-year home price volatility. Stable markets have lower standard deviation across 2017–2024. Owner-occupancy rate is also factored in — neighborhoods with more owners versus renters tend to be more resilient during downturns.

3. Momentum (0–100)

Five-year Compound Annual Growth Rate of home prices. Higher values mean faster appreciation. But note that extreme momentum (10%+ per year) can signal a bubble rather than a virtue. Our percentile rank rewards both steady growth and sustainable appreciation.

4. Safety (0–100)

Derived from FBI UCR violent crime rate (60% weight) and property crime rate (40%). Higher scores mean safer communities. New York City boroughs and Washington, D.C. use FBI CDE state-aggregate data calibrated against official MPD and NYPD releases to ensure consistency.

5. Health (0–100)

CDC PLACES county-level chronic disease burden: obesity, diabetes, high blood pressure, physical inactivity, and mental health. Higher scores indicate a healthier population. This is a strong predictor of long-term quality of life and healthcare costs.

6. Amenities (0–100)

OpenStreetMap point-of-interest density per square mile: schools, hospitals, grocery stores, restaurants, parks, banks, and pharmacies. Higher scores mean more walkable, more convenient, more connected communities.

Why Weights Matter More Than You Think

With our default weights, the top-ranked county scores in the high 70s. But if you set Affordability to 50% and Amenities to 5%, the ranking shifts dramatically — Midwestern counties leap to the top while coastal metros fall. The same underlying data can support very different definitions of 'best'.

Try it yourself on the U.S. County Livability Score page. Every adjustment updates the full 3,128-county ranking instantly.

A Note on Data Coverage

Every axis is a percentile rank computed only over counties with valid data for that axis. Counties without five-year price data — most notably the nine Connecticut planning regions that transitioned from traditional counties to new boundaries in 2022 — are excluded from momentum-based rankings. Health data covers 2,941 of the 3,128 counties (94%) because CDC PLACES does not publish estimates for some rural counties.

Why a Fixed Baseline Matters

Each axis is a percentile rank, meaning a score of 50 represents the national median. A score of 100 is not a perfect score — it means the county ranks at the very top of the U.S. on that dimension. This is why the highest composite scores rarely exceed 80: consistency across six independent dimensions is rarer than excellence in one.
