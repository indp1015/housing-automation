The 10 Best U.S. Counties to Live in 2026: A Data-Driven Analysis

Livability · Published 2026-10-21 · 8 min read

We analyzed 3,128 U.S. counties across six independent data dimensions to identify the best places to live in 2026. The result is not a single ranking, but a transparent methodology you can adapt to your own priorities.

How We Ranked Every U.S. County

The Livability Score combines six data sources that each measure something different about quality of life. Unlike traditional rankings that focus only on home prices, this score evaluates affordability, stability, momentum, safety, health, and amenities together — and lets you choose how much each matters to you.

What These Counties Have in Common

Every county on this list scores above the 60th percentile on at least four of the six axes. Most combine moderate housing costs with above-average safety and access to grocery stores, schools, and healthcare facilities. No county reaches the top through a single metric — the diversity of the winners reflects how multidimensional livability is.

Why the Top Score Is 73.8, Not 100

Because every axis is a percentile rank, a score of 100 would require a county to lead the entire nation on all six dimensions simultaneously. In practice, no county does. The top score of 73.8 means that county ranks in the top tier across most dimensions — not that it has no trade-offs. This matters for interpretation: a high score indicates consistency, not perfection.

How to Customize This Ranking

Every axis weight can be adjusted in real time on our U.S. County Livability Score page. If you prioritize affordability above all else, raising that weight to 50% will dramatically reshuffle the list. The same data supports many different definitions of 'best'.

A Note on Data Coverage

Eleven counties are excluded from the underlying data due to missing five-year price history — most notably the nine Connecticut planning regions that transitioned from traditional counties to new boundaries in 2022. This ensures that every ranked county has comparable longitudinal data.
