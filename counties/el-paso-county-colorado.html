📝 Editor's Insight

El Paso County, Colorado, functions as a distinctively balanced market that has recently tilted toward affordability for buyers, though it remains expensive relative to national standards. The price-to-income ratio stands at 5.10, significantly above the national average of 3.20, indicating that home prices are still high relative to local earnings. However, the price-to-rent ratio of 22.7, compared to the national benchmark of 18.7, suggests that purchasing is becoming a more financially viable option than renting for long-term residents. This shift is reinforced by the fact that home values are rising faster than rents, a dynamic that historically favors equity accumulation for owners over time. The multi-year trend reveals a period of rapid appreciation that is now decelerating. Median home values surged from $238,200 in 2017 to $461,000 in 2024, representing a 93.5% increase over seven years. The peak year-over-year growth occurred in 2022 at 17.8%, but this momentum has moderated to 7.0% in 2024. Recent FHFA HPI data for 2025 shows a county year-over-year change of +0.53%, which outperforms the Colorado state average of -0.61% by 1.14 percentage points. This divergence indicates that El Paso County is retaining value or appreciating slightly while the broader state market experiences mild deflation, highlighting localized strength. Affordability challenges persist despite the slowing price growth. The median gross rent of $1,696 consumes 22.5% of the median household income of $90,363, exceeding the national average rent burden of 17.2%. While the county’s median income is 32.9% higher than the national average, home values are 102.5% higher than the national mean, creating a significant cost disparity. El Paso County also commands a 7.8% premium over the state average home value. A notable pattern is the sustained outperformance of home values against both state and national benchmarks, even as growth rates normalize. For potential buyers, the current environment offers a window where price appreciation is slowing while income levels remain robust, potentially improving affordability metrics compared to the 2022 peak. Renters should note that while rent growth is outpaced by home value growth, the absolute cost of renting remains high relative to national norms, making the transition to ownership a strategic consideration for those with stable incomes.

Market Analysis

The median home value in El Paso County is 7.8% above the Colorado state average and 102.5% above the U.S. national average. The median rent of $1,696/month is 30.3% above the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 22.7, renting is generally more cost-effective than buying in the short to medium term. The market favors buyers only for long-term holds.

El Paso County has an owner-occupancy rate of 66.2%, compared to 71.9% in Colorado and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more renter-heavy market than the U.S. average.
