📝 Local Market Analysis

El Paso County, Colorado, presents a complex housing landscape characterized by strong economic vitality but severe affordability constraints. The market is effectively a renter’s market regarding entry barriers, as the price-to-income ratio stands at 5.10, significantly exceeding the national average of 3.20. This disparity is reinforced by a rent burden of 22.5%, which outpaces the national norm of 17.2%, indicating that both purchasing and renting remain financially demanding for the median household earning $90,363. While the owner-occupancy rate of 66.2% suggests a stable residential base, the high cost of entry limits liquidity for first-time buyers, pushing demand toward the rental sector where the price-to-rent ratio of 22.7 also exceeds the national average of 18.7. Multi-year data reveals a pronounced acceleration in home values, with prices doubling from $238,200 in 2017 to $461,000 in 2024. This surge, peaking at 17.8% year-over-year growth in 2022, has since moderated to 7.0%, yet remains robust. Recent FHFA HPI data showing a 0.53% year-over-year increase contrasts with the state’s -0.61% decline, highlighting El Paso’s relative outperformance by 1.14 percentage points. This divergence suggests that local demand drivers are insulating the county from broader state-level softness. The primary engine for this resilience is strong economic fundamentals, including 253,988 total jobs and positive net migration of 15,257 filers between 2021 and 2022. However, housing supply of 5,707 new units authorized in 2025 may be insufficient to fully offset this inflow, particularly given the national mortgage rate of 7.40%, which continues to suppress buyer volume. The economic context further complicates the outlook. Although average weekly wages of $1,346 support household income, they do not keep pace with the rapid appreciation of median home values, which now sit 102.5% above the national average. The composite assessment reflects this tension: high vitality scores stem from employment and migration strength, while low affordability scores drive the overall stress classification. For potential participants, the data indicates a market where income growth lags behind asset appreciation, necessitating careful budgeting. The sustained outperformance relative to the state, combined with moderating but positive price momentum, suggests that while immediate volatility may decrease, the fundamental gap between local purchasing power and property values remains a defining structural feature of the county’s housing landscape.

Market Analysis

The median home value in El Paso County is 7.8% above the Colorado state average and 102.5% above the U.S. national average. The median rent of $1,696/month is 30.3% above the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 22.7, renting is generally more cost-effective than buying in the short to medium term. The market favors buyers only for long-term holds.

El Paso County has an owner-occupancy rate of 66.2%, compared to 71.9% in Colorado and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more renter-heavy market than the U.S. average.
