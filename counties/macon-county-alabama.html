📝 Local Market Analysis

Macon County, Alabama, functions as a distinctively affordable buyer’s market, characterized by a price-to-income ratio of 2.33, significantly below the national average of 3.20. The price-to-rent ratio of 11.6, compared to the national benchmark of 18.7, further underscores the relative value of ownership over leasing. This affordability is reinforced by a median home value of $102,000, which sits 55.2% below the national mean and 36.1% below the state average. Despite these low entry points, the market exhibits robust momentum. The FHFA House Price Index recorded a year-over-year increase of 3.61% in 2025, outperforming the Alabama state average of 2.79% by 0.82 percentage points. This recent strength follows a volatile multi-year trajectory where home values surged 11.4% in 2022 before moderating to 1.9% in 2023 and accelerating again to 6.6% in 2024. The divergence between this sharp appreciation and the relatively stable median gross rent of $734 suggests that equity gains are outpacing rental cost inflation, favoring current homeowners. The local economic foundation remains constrained, limiting broader market vitality. With only 2,344 total jobs and an average weekly wage of $874, the labor market offers limited upward mobility for residents. Housing supply is exceptionally tight, with just six new single-family units authorized in 2025 and zero multi-family permits, indicating minimal new inventory to offset demand. Although net migration added 194 filers between 2021 and 2022, the average income of incoming residents ($46,136) only marginally exceeds that of outflowing residents ($46,053), suggesting demographic shifts are driven more by lifestyle preferences than economic opportunity. The high 7.32% national mortgage rate exacerbates purchasing challenges, yet the low absolute price point keeps monthly debt service manageable for local income levels. The market’s overall stress is driven primarily by low vitality and stability, despite strong momentum. The owner-occupancy rate of 61.5% and a rent burden of 20.2%—higher than the national average of 17.2%—indicate that while buying is accessible, renting remains a significant financial strain. The disparity between the county’s strong price momentum and its weak economic indicators creates a fragile equilibrium. For potential buyers, the low price-to-rent ratio offers a compelling long-term investment case, provided they can secure financing amidst high interest rates. However, the lack of new construction and modest wage growth limit the immediate upside for rental investors, who must navigate a market where housing costs are rising faster than local earnings.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Macon County is 36.1% below the Alabama state average and 55.2% below the U.S. national average. The median rent of $734/month is 10.5% below the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 11.6, buying a home in Macon County is generally more favorable than renting for those planning to stay long-term. A ratio below 15 typically signals that purchasing is financially advantageous over a multi-year horizon.

Macon County has an owner-occupancy rate of 61.5%, compared to 73.2% in Alabama and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more renter-heavy market than the U.S. average.
