📝 Local Market Analysis

Macon County, Alabama, functions as a distinctively affordable buyer’s market, characterized by a median home value of $102,000 that sits 55.2% below the national average. The price-to-income ratio of 2.33 is significantly lower than the national benchmark of 3.20, indicating that housing costs remain accessible relative to the median household income of $43,707. Although the rent burden stands at 20.2%, slightly above the national average of 17.2%, the price-to-rent ratio of 11.6 is well below the national norm of 18.7, suggesting that purchasing offers superior value compared to renting. This structural affordability is the primary driver of the market’s high momentum, which contrasts sharply with its low vitality score, reflecting limited local economic expansion. The multi-year trend reveals a period of accelerating price appreciation, with home values rising 6.6% year-over-year in 2024 and 11.4% in 2022. This upward trajectory occurs despite a stagnant housing supply, evidenced by only six new single-family units authorized in 2025. The divergence between robust price growth and modest rent increases suggests that demand is outpacing new construction, a dynamic exacerbated by the current 7.32% mortgage rate, which may deter some buyers but does not appear to have suppressed price growth. Local economic indicators remain weak, with total employment at 2,344 jobs and an average weekly wage of $874, contributing to the low vitality metric. However, positive net migration of 194 filers provides a modest inflow of demand, helping to sustain price momentum even in the absence of significant job growth. Regional context further underscores the county’s outlier status, as home values are 36.1% below the Alabama state average, while incomes are 35.7% below the national mean. This alignment between low prices and low incomes creates a stable, albeit constrained, market environment. The high momentum score is driven by the consistent year-over-year price increases, while the low stability score likely reflects the thin housing inventory and limited economic diversification. For potential buyers, the data indicates a market where entry costs are low, but the rapid price appreciation relative to income growth may erode affordability over time. Renters face a higher cost burden compared to national norms, making homeownership a more financially viable long-term strategy despite the high mortgage rates. The combination of low supply, positive migration, and strong price momentum suggests that the market remains tight for buyers, with limited new inventory to moderate the upward pressure on existing home values.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Macon County is 36.1% below the Alabama state average and 55.2% below the U.S. national average. The median rent of $734/month is 10.5% below the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 11.6, buying a home in Macon County is generally more favorable than renting for those planning to stay long-term. A ratio below 15 typically signals that purchasing is financially advantageous over a multi-year horizon.

Macon County has an owner-occupancy rate of 61.5%, compared to 73.2% in Alabama and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more renter-heavy market than the U.S. average.
