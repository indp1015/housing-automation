📝 Local Market Analysis

Macon County, Alabama, operates as a distinctively affordable buyer’s market, characterized by a price-to-income ratio of 2.33, significantly below the national average of 3.20. This affordability is reinforced by a price-to-rent ratio of 11.6, compared to the national benchmark of 18.7, indicating that purchasing equity is financially more accessible than long-term renting. The median home value of $102,000 sits 36.1% below the state average and 55.2% below the national mean, reflecting the county’s lower cost of living relative to broader economic centers. Despite these low absolute values, the market exhibits strong momentum, with the FHFA House Price Index showing a 3.61% year-over-year increase in 2025, outperforming the state’s 2.79% growth rate by 0.82 percentage points. This recent acceleration follows a volatile period, including a sharp 11.4% jump in 2022, suggesting that while the market is stabilizing, it retains upward pressure that distinguishes it from stagnant rural areas. The economic fundamentals supporting this trend are modest yet positive. Net migration added 194 filers between 2021 and 2022, with inflows averaging $46,136 in income, slightly exceeding outflows. However, the local labor market remains constrained, with only 2,344 total jobs and an average weekly wage of $874. Housing supply is extremely limited, with just six new single-family units authorized in 2025 and zero multi-family permits, creating a tight inventory environment that likely contributes to the sustained price appreciation. The rent burden stands at 20.2% of median household income, slightly above the national average of 17.2%, suggesting that while homeownership is relatively cheap, rental costs are becoming a heavier financial strain for households earning the median $43,707. The divergence between robust price momentum and weak local vitality defines the current market dynamic. While the 6.6% price increase in 2024 signals strong asset appreciation, the underlying economic engine is small, with only 270 establishments supporting the workforce. This disparity explains the high momentum metric alongside a low vitality assessment. For potential buyers, the low entry price combined with outpacing state-level growth offers a compelling value proposition, particularly as mortgage rates remain elevated at 7.40%. Conversely, renters face a less favorable environment where housing costs consume a larger share of income than the national norm. The market is not driven by broad-based economic expansion but by supply constraints and relative affordability, making it a niche investment opportunity rather than a high-growth economic hub.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Macon County is 36.1% below the Alabama state average and 55.2% below the U.S. national average. The median rent of $734/month is 10.5% below the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 11.6, buying a home in Macon County is generally more favorable than renting for those planning to stay long-term. A ratio below 15 typically signals that purchasing is financially advantageous over a multi-year horizon.

Macon County has an owner-occupancy rate of 61.5%, compared to 73.2% in Alabama and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more renter-heavy market than the U.S. average.
