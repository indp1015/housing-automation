📝 Local Market Analysis

Bulloch County, Georgia, currently exhibits characteristics of a balanced market, though affordability metrics suggest slight pressure on buyers. The price-to-rent ratio of 18.6 aligns almost perfectly with the national average of 18.7, indicating that the relative cost of owning versus renting is standard. However, the price-to-income ratio stands at 3.78, significantly higher than the national benchmark of 3.20. This disparity is driven by a median household income of $58,810, which is 13.5% below the national average, while the median home value of $222,500 sits only 2.3% below the national mean. Consequently, local residents face a rent burden of 20.3% of income, exceeding the national average of 17.2%, suggesting that both rental and ownership costs are stretching local financial capacity relative to earnings. The market has experienced a dramatic acceleration in home values, with year-over-year price growth surging from 3.0% in 2018 to 16.1% in 2024. This recent spike contrasts sharply with the more modest 2.45% year-over-year increase reported in the latest FHFA House Price Index data, which still outperforms the Georgia state average of 1.83%. This divergence between the long-term Census trend and the recent FHFA index may reflect data lag or a stabilization of the extreme 2022-2024 growth phase. Meanwhile, the owner-occupancy rate of 53.1% remains below the typical national standard, indicating a substantial rental sector that supports the 995 monthly median rent. Economic fundamentals provide context for these price movements. The county supports 22,092 jobs with an average weekly wage of $895, a figure that lags behind the income required to sustain the rapid appreciation in home values. Housing supply appears robust, with 1,389 new units authorized in 2025, including 929 single-family homes. This construction activity, coupled with a net migration inflow of 2,039 filers, suggests sustained demand despite the high 7.40% national mortgage rate. The inflow population, with an average income of $68,632, is wealthier than the outflow population, potentially driving up local price points. For potential buyers, the high price-to-income ratio necessitates careful financial planning, while renters face above-average burden rates. The data indicates a market where price appreciation has outpaced local income growth, creating a gap that current employment wages may struggle to close.
