📝 Local Market Analysis

Beaufort County, North Carolina, functions as a moderately balanced market with distinct affordability constraints, evidenced by a price-to-income ratio of 3.30, slightly above the national average of 3.20. While the median home value of $192,700 sits 15.4% below the national mean, the median household income of $58,357 is 14.2% lower than the national benchmark, creating a tight margin for purchasing power. The rent burden stands at 17.9% of income, marginally exceeding the national average of 17.2%, indicating that rental costs are becoming a heavier financial load for residents. This dynamic suggests that while entry-level ownership remains accessible relative to national prices, the local income base lags behind housing cost inflation, pressuring household budgets. The market exhibits strong recent momentum, driven by a 13.3% year-over-year price increase in 2023 that moderated to 6.3% in 2024. Current FHFA data confirms this sustained upward trajectory, with a 3.26% year-over-year gain in 2025, significantly outperforming the state’s 0.38% growth. This outperformance correlates with positive net migration of 1,094 filers between 2021 and 2022, suggesting inbound demand is fueling price appreciation. However, the housing supply remains constrained, with only 119 new units authorized in 2025, all in the single-family sector and zero multi-family construction. This lack of rental inventory likely contributes to the rising rent burden and limits the ability of the market to absorb new demand through increased supply. Economic fundamentals provide mixed support for continued growth. Total employment stands at 13,196 jobs with an average weekly wage of $994, offering a stable but modest economic base. The national 30-year mortgage rate of 7.40% further complicates affordability, as high borrowing costs counteract the relatively low home prices. The divergence between robust price momentum and lagging income growth highlights a market in transition. While the county outperforms the state in price appreciation, the affordability metrics indicate stress. Potential buyers face a competitive environment where prices are rising faster than local wages, while renters encounter a tight market with limited new supply. The combination of positive migration, low inventory, and state-level outperformance suggests that housing values will remain resilient, but the affordability gap may limit broader demographic participation in the ownership market.
