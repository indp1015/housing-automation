📝 Local Market Analysis

Moffat County, Colorado, presents a complex housing landscape characterized by strong momentum but severe affordability constraints, positioning it as a challenging environment for both buyers and renters. The market is not a traditional buyer’s or renter’s market but rather a stressed, high-cost niche where price appreciation has significantly outpaced local income growth. With a median home value of $289,500 and a median household income of $73,849, the price-to-income ratio stands at 3.92, well above the national average of 3.20. This disparity drives a low affordability assessment, indicating that housing costs consume a disproportionate share of local earnings. Furthermore, the price-to-rent ratio of 23.2 exceeds the national benchmark of 18.7, suggesting that purchasing equity is becoming relatively less attractive compared to renting, despite the high median gross rent of $1,039. The multi-year trend reveals a dramatic acceleration in property values, particularly following a 22.7% surge in 2022. This rapid appreciation has moderated to a 10.3% increase in 2024, yet the FHFA HPI data for 2025 shows a continued 4.30% year-over-year gain, outperforming the Colorado state average of -0.61% by nearly five percentage points. This divergence highlights a notable pattern where Moffat County decouples from broader state trends, likely driven by net migration inflows of 510 filers between 2021 and 2022. The local economy supports this demand with an average weekly wage of $1,134, though the tight supply of only 24 new single-family units authorized in 2025 exacerbates price pressure. The low stability metric reflects this volatility, as the sharp price spikes create an unstable foundation for long-term affordability. Practical implications for stakeholders are clear: potential buyers face a market where entry costs are high relative to local earning power, while renters benefit from a rent burden of 16.9%, slightly below the national average of 17.2%. The vitality of the market is sustained by robust migration and employment, with 3,524 total jobs supporting the population. However, the high mortgage rate of 7.40% further strains purchasing power, making the high momentum difficult to sustain without continued external capital inflows. The composite assessment, driven by high vitality and momentum but penalized by low stability and affordability, underscores a market that is growing rapidly but becoming increasingly inaccessible to the median local household. This dynamic suggests that while the area remains economically active, the housing sector is under significant stress from demand exceeding local supply and income capacity.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Moffat County is 32.3% below the Colorado state average and 27.1% above the U.S. national average. The median rent of $1,039/month is 20.2% below the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 23.2, renting is generally more cost-effective than buying in the short to medium term. The market favors buyers only for long-term holds.

Moffat County has an owner-occupancy rate of 69.4%, compared to 71.9% in Colorado and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more renter-heavy market than the U.S. average.
