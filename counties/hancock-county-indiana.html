📝 Local Market Analysis

Hancock County, Indiana, operates as a balanced market with a slight tilt toward buyer-friendliness, evidenced by a price-to-income ratio of 2.95, which sits below the national average of 3.20. The rent burden stands at 15.5% of household income, also under the national benchmark of 17.2%, indicating that both ownership and rental costs remain manageable relative to local earnings. The price-to-rent ratio of 19.0 is nearly identical to the national average of 18.7, suggesting that the financial trade-off between buying and renting is standard for the region. This affordability is supported by a median household income of $93,186, which exceeds the national average by 37.0%, providing robust purchasing power for residents. The market has experienced significant appreciation, with median home values rising from $161,800 in 2017 to $274,800 in 2024. This trajectory includes a peak year-over-year increase of 20.6% in 2022, which has since moderated to 6.5% in 2024. Recent FHFA HPI data confirms this stabilization, showing a 5.56% year-over-year gain in 2025, outperforming the Indiana state average of 3.45% by 2.11 percentage points. A notable pattern emerges in the divergence between asset classes; while home values have surged, the median gross rent of $1,203 remains relatively stable, resulting in home values rising faster than rents. This dynamic suggests that the primary driver of housing costs is capital appreciation rather than immediate rental pressure. Regional context highlights Hancock County’s premium position, with home values 43.2% above the state average and 20.7% above the national mean. This premium is justified by strong economic fundamentals, including a net migration inflow of 2,342 filers between 2021 and 2022, driven by an average incoming income of $88,984. Local employment supports this demand, with 25,187 jobs and an average weekly wage of $1,138. Housing supply remains active, with 1,212 new units authorized in 2025, predominantly single-family homes. However, the 7.40% national mortgage rate continues to exert pressure on buyer qualification, even as local affordability metrics remain favorable. Potential buyers benefit from high income levels and moderate price-to-income ratios, while renters face standard burden levels. The combination of strong in-migration and consistent, albeit slowing, price growth indicates a resilient market structure that maintains value without the extreme volatility seen in other regions.
