📝 Local Market Analysis

Custer County, Colorado, functions as a distinctively stressed buyer’s market, characterized by severe affordability constraints despite high economic vitality. The price-to-income ratio stands at 5.40, significantly exceeding the national average of 3.20, which indicates that median home values of $392,100 are disproportionately high relative to the median household income of $72,674. Consequently, the affordability metric registers a low 21.1, dragging down the overall composite assessment. Conversely, the market exhibits strong vitality, scoring 84.0, driven by robust net migration of +475 filers and a high owner occupancy rate of 85.3%. This demographic influx, with incoming residents averaging $86,682 in income, has fueled recent price appreciation, creating a dynamic where demand outpaces local wage growth. Recent momentum reveals a sharp correction following a period of aggressive expansion. Home values surged 24.5% in 2022 and continued rising 9.3% in 2024, yet the latest FHFA HPI data indicates a 9.98% year-over-year decline, underperforming the Colorado state average by 9.37 percentage points. This divergence suggests that while long-term trends show appreciation, the immediate transactional environment is cooling rapidly. The stability score of 65.3 reflects this volatility, as the market transitions from rapid growth to contraction. Meanwhile, the price-to-rent ratio of 30.6, nearly double the national average of 18.7, highlights that purchasing remains significantly less attractive than renting on a pure cost basis, although the rent burden of 17.7% remains near the national norm. The local economic context further complicates the investment calculus. With only 767 total jobs and an average weekly wage of $914, the employment base is insufficient to support the current housing price levels, exacerbated by 7.40% mortgage rates. Housing supply is limited to 78 new single-family units authorized in 2025, with zero multi-family construction, indicating a lack of diverse housing options. Although home values are 72.2% above the national average, they remain 8.3% below the state average, suggesting that Custer County’s premium is driven by specific local demand rather than broad regional trends. Potential buyers face a market where high entry costs are met with declining short-term price momentum, while renters benefit from stable costs relative to income. The combination of high vitality, low affordability, and negative recent momentum defines this market as polarized, where long-term value appreciation is currently offset by immediate financial strain and economic fragility.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Custer County is 8.3% below the Colorado state average and 72.2% above the U.S. national average. The median rent of $1,069/month is 17.9% below the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 30.6, Custer County is a strongly rent-favored market. Home prices are high relative to rents, meaning renting is typically far cheaper than owning in the near term.

Custer County has an owner-occupancy rate of 85.3%, compared to 71.9% in Colorado and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more homeowner-heavy market than the U.S. average.
