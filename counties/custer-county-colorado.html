📝 Editor's Insight

Custer County presents a distinct investor-oriented market, characterized by a price-to-rent ratio of 29.3, significantly exceeding the national average of 18.0. This disparity suggests that ownership yields lower immediate cash flow compared to rental income potential. The five-year trend reveals divergent growth patterns, with median rents surging at a 13.3% CAGR to $1,021, while home values grew at a more moderate 6.2% CAGR to $358,800. This indicates rental demand is outpacing equity appreciation. Affordability metrics show mixed signals; the price-to-income ratio stands at 4.94, well above the national benchmark of 3.09, indicating elevated purchase costs relative to local earnings. Conversely, the rent burden is 16.9%, slightly below the national average of 17.2%. A critical insight for potential residents is that despite high owner occupancy at 85.7%, the rapid rent inflation suggests a tightening rental inventory. Buyers face significant entry barriers due to the high price-to-income multiple, whereas renters may find current burden levels manageable, though the steep historical rent growth signals limited price stability in the leasing sector.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Custer County is 8.3% below the Colorado state average and 72.2% above the U.S. national average. The median rent of $1,069/month is 17.9% below the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 30.6, Custer County is a strongly rent-favored market. Home prices are high relative to rents, meaning renting is typically far cheaper than owning in the near term.

Custer County has an owner-occupancy rate of 85.3%, compared to 71.9% in Colorado and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more homeowner-heavy market than the U.S. average.
