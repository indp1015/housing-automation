📝 Local Market Analysis

Perry County, Alabama, functions as a distinct buyer’s market, characterized by significant affordability advantages relative to national benchmarks. The median home value of $86,400 sits 62.1% below the national average and 45.9% below the state average, creating a price-to-income ratio of 2.29, which is substantially lower than the national average of 3.20. This disparity is reinforced by a price-to-rent ratio of 12.2, well under the national norm of 18.7, indicating that purchasing equity is significantly more cost-effective than long-term renting. While the median household income of $37,654 is 44.6% below the national mean, the low asset prices mitigate this income constraint, resulting in a rent burden of 18.9% for those who do lease, slightly above the national average of 17.2%. The multi-year trend reveals a volatile recovery pattern rather than linear growth. Home values dipped from $69,100 in 2017 to $65,000 in 2019 before spiking 23.2% in 2020 to reach $80,100. Subsequent years showed stabilization, with values reaching $87,700 in 2023 before a modest 1.5% correction to $86,400 in 2024. This recent moderation suggests the market has peaked after its post-pandemic surge. A notable anomaly exists in the local economic data, where the Bureau of Labor Statistics reports zero total employment despite 166 establishments, likely reflecting a data reporting limitation or an extremely small formal employment base. Despite this, net migration remained positive at +119 filers between 2021 and 2022, with inflows averaging $38,631 in income, slightly higher than outflows at $38,451, indicating a stable demographic inflow. For potential buyers, the current 7.40% national mortgage rate introduces financing costs that may offset some of the low purchase price benefits, yet the fundamental affordability remains robust. The owner-occupancy rate of 77.5% suggests a community-oriented housing stock with limited rental inventory, which supports the higher rent burden observed among the minority of renters. The divergence between the rapid 2020 price surge and the subsequent flattening of growth indicates a market that has absorbed initial demand shocks. Investors and buyers should note that while income levels lag behind national standards, the extreme discount to state and national home values provides a margin of safety that is rare in the current high-interest-rate environment. The market’s trajectory is less about speculative appreciation and more about maintaining value stability driven by low entry barriers and steady, modest population retention.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Perry County is 45.9% below the Alabama state average and 62.1% below the U.S. national average. The median rent of $592/month is 27.8% below the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 12.2, buying a home in Perry County is generally more favorable than renting for those planning to stay long-term. A ratio below 15 typically signals that purchasing is financially advantageous over a multi-year horizon.

Perry County has an owner-occupancy rate of 77.5%, compared to 73.2% in Alabama and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more homeowner-heavy market than the U.S. average.
