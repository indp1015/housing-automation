📝 Local Market Analysis

Dillingham Census Area presents a distinctly affordable housing market, characterized by a price-to-income ratio of 2.02, significantly below the national average of 3.20. This affordability is reinforced by a rent burden of 16.9%, which aligns closely with the national benchmark of 17.2%. The price-to-rent ratio of 12.0 is markedly lower than the national average of 18.7, indicating that purchasing is substantially more cost-effective than renting relative to local income levels. Consequently, the market leans heavily toward a buyer’s advantage, where entry barriers are low despite the broader economic context. The multi-year trend reveals significant volatility rather than steady growth. Home values peaked at $189,500 in 2017 before experiencing a sharp 15.0% decline in 2020 to $156,000. Although a 7.6% recovery occurred in 2022, values have since moderated, declining 2.9% in 2024 to $159,000. This recent downward momentum contrasts with the noted signal that rents are rising faster than home values, suggesting a divergence where rental demand may be outpacing owner-occupied price appreciation. The 2020 drop likely reflects the broader economic shock, while the subsequent stabilization indicates a market adjusting to local fundamentals rather than speculative pressures. Regional context further underscores the market’s uniqueness. Home values are 30.2% below the national average and 43.9% below the Alaska state average, yet median household income is 15.5% above the national mean. This disparity highlights the exceptional affordability of Dillingham relative to both its state and the nation. Economically, the area supports only 1,281 jobs with an average weekly wage of $1,583, and net migration is negligible, with just three net new filers between 2021 and 2022. This static population and limited employment base constrain housing demand, preventing the price surges seen in high-growth areas. With a 30-year mortgage rate at 7.40%, the low price point remains the primary driver of affordability. For prospective buyers, the low price-to-rent ratio and stable, low population growth suggest a market with limited upside potential but high immediate value. Renters, however, face a market where rising rents may erode their cost advantage over ownership over time. The data indicates a stable, low-cost environment driven by demographic stagnation and high relative income, rather than dynamic market forces.
