📝 Editor's Insight

Petersburg Borough exhibits a balanced market structure, evidenced by a 71.0% owner occupancy rate and a price-to-rent ratio of 20.8, which slightly exceeds the national average of 18.0. The five-year trend reveals robust appreciation, with home values growing at a 7.3% CAGR compared to a 6.1% rent CAGR. This divergence indicates that equity accumulation is currently outpacing rental cost inflation, favoring long-term ownership over leasing. Affordability metrics present a mixed picture; the price-to-income ratio of 3.91 is 26.5% higher than the national benchmark of 3.09, signaling elevated purchase costs relative to local earnings. Conversely, the rent burden stands at 18.8%, only marginally above the 17.2% national average, suggesting rental housing remains relatively accessible. A critical insight emerges from the income data: despite home values being 41.8% above the national mean, median household income is 16.7% higher than the national average. This income premium partially offsets the high price-to-income ratio, implying that local purchasing power is stronger than raw price comparisons suggest.

Market Analysis
