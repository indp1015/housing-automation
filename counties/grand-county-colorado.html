📝 Editor's Insight

Grand County, Colorado, operates as a distinctively seller-favorable market, evidenced by a price-to-rent ratio of 29.1, significantly exceeding the national average of 18.0. This disparity indicates that ownership costs are disproportionately high relative to rental alternatives. The five-year trend reveals aggressive appreciation, with home values compounding at 13.3% annually compared to an 8.5% increase in median rents. Consequently, asset values have outpaced rental income growth, widening the gap between buying and renting. Affordability metrics confirm this strain; the price-to-income ratio stands at 6.00, nearly double the national benchmark of 3.09, while the rent burden consumes 20.6% of household income against a 17.2% national average. Although median household income exceeds the national mean by 28.2%, it lags behind the 138.6% premium in home values. A critical insight for potential residents is that the 76.4% owner-occupancy rate suggests limited rental inventory, meaning the high price-to-rent ratio likely reflects scarcity rather than pure demand, making entry into the rental sector particularly competitive and expensive.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Grand County is 40.7% above the Colorado state average and 164.2% above the U.S. national average. The median rent of $1,493/month is 14.7% above the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 33.6, Grand County is a strongly rent-favored market. Home prices are high relative to rents, meaning renting is typically far cheaper than owning in the near term.

Grand County has an owner-occupancy rate of 73.4%, compared to 71.9% in Colorado and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more homeowner-heavy market than the U.S. average.
