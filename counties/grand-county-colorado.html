📝 Editor's Insight

Grand County, Colorado, functions as a distinctly seller’s market for homebuyers, characterized by extreme valuation metrics that diverge sharply from national norms. The price-to-income ratio stands at 6.79, more than double the national average of 3.20, indicating that median home values are disproportionately high relative to local earning power. Concurrently, the price-to-rent ratio of 33.6 exceeds the national benchmark of 18.7 by 80%, suggesting that purchasing equity is significantly less cost-effective than renting when comparing total housing costs. With an owner-occupancy rate of 73.4% and a median home value of $601,500, the market is firmly entrenched in a high-cost environment where entry barriers remain substantial for the average household. The multi-year trend reveals a pattern of accelerated appreciation, particularly during the 2020–2024 period. Home values surged from $285,000 in 2017 to $601,500 in 2024, representing a cumulative increase of 111%. The most dramatic growth occurred in 2022, with a 23.1% year-over-year jump, followed by an 18.6% increase in 2024. This trajectory contrasts with the more modest rent growth, where the median gross rent is $1,493. The divergence between price and rent growth signals that capital gains for homeowners have outpaced the cost of shelter for renters, creating a widening affordability gap. While the rent burden is 20.2% of income, slightly above the national average of 17.2%, the equity burden for buyers is far more severe due to the inflated price-to-income multiple. In regional context, Grand County home values are 40.7% higher than the Colorado state average and 164.2% above the national mean, despite household incomes being only 30.3% above the national average. Recent momentum, as indicated by the FHFA HPI, shows a 2.14% year-over-year increase in 2025, outperforming the state’s -0.61% decline by 2.75 percentage points. This relative resilience suggests that Grand County’s market remains robust even as broader state trends cool. For potential buyers, the data indicates that current valuations are not supported by local income levels, making financing challenging without significant external capital. Renters, conversely, face a moderate but rising cost of living, with housing costs consuming a larger share of income than the national norm. The sustained outperformance of home values relative to rents and state averages underscores a market driven by external demand rather than local wage growth.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Grand County is 40.7% above the Colorado state average and 164.2% above the U.S. national average. The median rent of $1,493/month is 14.7% above the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 33.6, Grand County is a strongly rent-favored market. Home prices are high relative to rents, meaning renting is typically far cheaper than owning in the near term.

Grand County has an owner-occupancy rate of 73.4%, compared to 71.9% in Colorado and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more homeowner-heavy market than the U.S. average.
