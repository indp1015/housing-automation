📝 Local Market Analysis

San Joaquin County operates as a distinctly renter-favorable market, characterized by a price-to-rent ratio of 25.5, significantly above the national average of 18.7. This metric, combined with an owner occupancy rate of 62.0%, suggests that purchasing is less economically rational than renting for many households. The affordability profile is severe, with a price-to-income ratio of 5.76 compared to the national benchmark of 3.20. Furthermore, the rent burden consumes 22.6% of median household income, exceeding the national average of 17.2%, indicating that even rental housing imposes a substantial financial strain on residents with a median income of $92,179. The multi-year trend reveals a period of rapid appreciation that has recently stalled. Home values surged from $281,100 in 2017 to $530,700 in 2024, peaking with 18.0% year-over-year growth in 2022. However, recent FHFA data shows a 0.88% year-over-year decline in 2025, underperforming the state average by 0.59 percentage points. This divergence between historical price growth and current stagnation is notable. While home values have risen faster than rents historically, the current negative momentum suggests a correction phase. The local economy supports this shift, with 243,606 jobs and an average weekly wage of $1,171, yet the high 7.40% mortgage rate likely suppresses buyer demand. Housing supply remains skewed toward single-family units, with 2,520 permits authorized versus only 2 multi-family units in 2025, limiting rental inventory expansion despite positive net migration of 5,144 filers. Relative to the state, San Joaquin’s median home value is 12.9% lower, yet it remains 133.1% above the national average. This regional context highlights a market that is expensive nationally but competitive within California. The low vitality and stability indicators are driven by the extreme affordability constraints and the recent price correction. For potential buyers, the current environment offers a pause in price escalation but remains inaccessible relative to income levels. Renters face high costs relative to earnings, suggesting limited leverage in negotiations unless local wage growth outpaces inflation. The combination of high interest rates, stagnant prices, and single-family-focused supply creates a market where neither buyers nor renters currently hold significant advantage, resulting in a stressed equilibrium.

Market Analysis

The median home value in San Joaquin County is 12.9% below the California state average and 133.1% above the U.S. national average. The median rent of $1,734/month is 2.6% above the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 25.5, San Joaquin County is a strongly rent-favored market. Home prices are high relative to rents, meaning renting is typically far cheaper than owning in the near term.

San Joaquin County has an owner-occupancy rate of 62.0%, compared to 63.4% in California and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more renter-heavy market than the U.S. average.
