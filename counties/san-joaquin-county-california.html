📝 Editor's Insight

San Joaquin County functions as a seller’s market, evidenced by a price-to-rent ratio of 25.2, significantly exceeding the national average of 18.1. This disparity indicates that purchasing equity is less attractive relative to rental costs than in typical markets. Over the past five years, home values appreciated at a 9.7% CAGR, outpacing the 7.8% CAGR for rents. This divergence suggests capital gains are driving value more than immediate carrying costs. Affordability remains strained, with a price-to-income ratio of 5.59, nearly double the national benchmark of 3.10. Although the rent burden of 22.1% is higher than the national 17.2%, it remains more manageable than the purchase burden. A critical insight emerges from the county’s position relative to state averages: despite being 13.4% below the California median home value, its income level is 33.5% above the national average. This implies that while absolute prices are lower than coastal California, the local wage base supports a premium market, making entry difficult for out-of-state buyers despite seemingly moderate sticker prices.

Market Analysis

The median home value in San Joaquin County is 12.9% below the California state average and 133.1% above the U.S. national average. The median rent of $1,734/month is 2.6% above the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 25.5, San Joaquin County is a strongly rent-favored market. Home prices are high relative to rents, meaning renting is typically far cheaper than owning in the near term.

San Joaquin County has an owner-occupancy rate of 62.0%, compared to 63.4% in California and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more renter-heavy market than the U.S. average.
