📝 Local Market Analysis

Cleburne County, Alabama, functions as a balanced market where affordability metrics remain favorable relative to national standards, yet recent price acceleration complicates long-term stability. The price-to-income ratio stands at 2.99, slightly below the national average of 3.20, while the rent burden is 17.3%, nearly identical to the national benchmark of 17.2%. These figures suggest that housing costs are manageable for local earners, with a median household income of $55,345 supporting a median home value of $165,600. The price-to-rent ratio of 17.3 is also below the national average of 18.7, indicating that purchasing remains a more cost-effective strategy than renting for long-term residents. However, the market’s classification is nuanced by a sharp divergence in recent growth trajectories, where home values have surged significantly while rental rates have remained comparatively stable, creating a temporary compression in the buy-versus-rent calculus. The multi-year trend reveals a period of stagnation followed by explosive appreciation. Home values remained flat between 2017 and 2021, hovering around $112,000 to $118,700, before experiencing a compound annual growth rate of approximately 12.4% from 2021 to 2024. The most recent year saw a 14.8% increase, pushing the median value to $165,600. This rapid appreciation contrasts with the FHFA House Price Index, which indicates a 0.00% year-over-year change for 2025, signaling a potential plateau or data lag in recent quarterly transactions. This divergence suggests that the initial surge may have been driven by limited inventory rather than sustained demand, as new construction remains negligible with only 11 single-family units authorized in 2025. The local economy supports this constraint, with total employment at 2,047 jobs and an average weekly wage of $1,144, which limits the pool of high-income buyers capable of sustaining further price increases. Regional context places Cleburne County 3.7% above the Alabama average but 27.3% below the national median, reflecting its status as a lower-cost rural market. Net migration of +375 filers indicates modest population growth, with inflows and outflows averaging similar incomes, suggesting a stable rather than booming demographic profile. The primary driver of the current market assessment is the tension between strong affordability and high momentum. While the low price-to-income ratio provides a solid foundation, the recent 14.8% price spike and the lack of new supply create volatility. Potential buyers face a market where entry costs have risen sharply in three years, while renters benefit from a stable rent burden. The economic backdrop, characterized by low employment density and a 7.32% mortgage rate, further constrains demand, suggesting that the recent price surge may not be easily sustained without significant new job creation or population influx.
