📝 Local Market Analysis

Sedgwick County, Colorado, functions as a distinctively affordable buyer’s market, characterized by a median home value of $149,000 that sits 34.6% below the national average. The price-to-income ratio of 2.84 is notably lower than the 3.20 national benchmark, indicating that housing costs are manageable relative to the median household income of $52,386. Furthermore, the price-to-rent ratio of 16.0 is significantly below the national average of 18.7, suggesting that purchasing is financially more advantageous than renting for long-term residents. This affordability is reinforced by a rent burden of 17.7%, which is nearly identical to the national average of 17.2%, ensuring that rental costs do not disproportionately strain household budgets. The market exhibits strong positive momentum, with home values rising 4.4% year-over-year in 2024 and maintaining a 4.41% increase in the most recent FHFA HPI data. This performance outpaces the Colorado state average, which saw a 0.61% decline, highlighting a significant regional divergence. While the county has experienced volatile growth, peaking at 16.5% in 2022, the recent moderation to single-digit increases suggests a stabilizing trend rather than a speculative bubble. The divergence between rising home values and relatively stable rents indicates that the market is driven by genuine ownership demand rather than transient rental pressure. However, the economic foundation remains fragile, with only 356 total jobs and a small population of 2,304, limiting the breadth of local income sources. Despite favorable price metrics, the market’s stability is constrained by limited economic vitality. The local economy supports only 78 establishments, creating a narrow employment base that may not sustain prolonged price appreciation without external migration. Net migration of +56 filers between 2021 and 2022 shows modest population growth, but the average income of incoming residents ($49,862) is slightly lower than those leaving ($50,274), suggesting no significant wealth influx. With national mortgage rates at 7.40%, the low absolute price of homes in Sedgwick County remains the primary competitive advantage. The composite assessment reflects a market where affordability is high, but stability and vitality are compromised by the small-scale, isolated nature of the local economy. Buyers benefit from low entry costs, while renters face standard national burden levels, making ownership the more financially efficient long-term strategy in this rural context.
