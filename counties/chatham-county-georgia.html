📝 Local Market Analysis

Chatham County, Georgia, operates as a distinctively balanced market where strong economic vitality contrasts sharply with severe affordability constraints. The price-to-rent ratio of 17.1 sits below the national average of 18.7, suggesting that renting remains a relatively competitive alternative to buying when compared to broader U.S. trends. However, the local owner occupancy rate of 56.8% indicates a significant portion of the population relies on rental housing, likely driven by the high cost of entry into homeownership. This dynamic positions the county as a hybrid environment where rental demand is robust, yet the barrier to ownership is elevated relative to local earning power. The multi-year trajectory reveals a period of aggressive appreciation that has recently moderated. Home values surged 19.3% year-over-year in 2022 before settling to a 10.8% increase in 2024, while the FHFA House Price Index shows a more recent slowdown to 2.30% growth in 2025. This deceleration aligns with the national 30-year fixed mortgage rate of 7.32%, which continues to suppress buyer demand. A notable anomaly is the divergence between asset growth and income levels; while home values have risen faster than rents, the median household income of $71,097 has not kept pace with the median home value of $302,700. Consequently, the price-to-income ratio stands at 4.26, significantly above the national benchmark of 3.20, signaling that local wages are insufficient to support current price levels without substantial external financing. Regional context further highlights the county’s premium status. Chatham’s median home value exceeds the Georgia state average by 52.9% and the national average by 32.9%, despite the median household income being only 4.6% above the national mean. This disparity is exacerbated by a rent burden of 24.8% of income, compared to the national average of 17.2%, indicating that residents allocate a disproportionate share of earnings to housing costs. The local economic foundation remains solid, supported by 154,079 jobs and an average weekly wage of $1,217, alongside a net migration gain of 8,931 filers. However, the supply of 4,426 new units authorized in 2025 may not be sufficient to offset the high demand from incoming households with average incomes near $85,000. For potential buyers, the high entry cost and elevated debt service requirements present significant hurdles, while renters face above-average financial strain. The market’s vitality is evident in its employment and migration data, but the stability of housing costs remains compromised by the widening gap between asset values and local purchasing power.
