📝 Editor's Insight

Lake County functions as a buyer’s market, evidenced by a 76.0% owner-occupancy rate and a price-to-rent ratio of 20.9, which exceeds the national average of 18.0. This metric indicates that purchasing remains more economical than renting for long-term residents. Over the past five years, home values surged at a 14.7% CAGR, outpacing the 13.0% CAGR in rents. This divergence suggests that equity accumulation is accelerating faster than rental costs, favoring those who own property. However, affordability remains strained relative to national standards. The price-to-income ratio stands at 4.28, significantly higher than the national benchmark of 3.09, while the rent burden consumes 20.5% of median income compared to the 17.2% national average. A critical insight emerges from the income data: median household income is 41.9% above the national mean, yet home values are 88.8% higher. This disparity reveals that local earning power has not fully offset the premium pricing, making the market less accessible to average-income households despite the region’s high cost of living.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Lake County is 9.0% above the Colorado state average and 104.7% above the U.S. national average. The median rent of $1,397/month is 7.3% above the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 27.8, Lake County is a strongly rent-favored market. Home prices are high relative to rents, meaning renting is typically far cheaper than owning in the near term.

Lake County has an owner-occupancy rate of 75.6%, compared to 71.9% in Colorado and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more homeowner-heavy market than the U.S. average.
