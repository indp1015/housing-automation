📝 Local Market Analysis

Lake County, Colorado, functions as a distinctively unbalanced market where extreme asset appreciation has outpaced local income growth, creating significant affordability constraints. The price-to-income ratio stands at 4.83, substantially higher than the national average of 3.20, while the price-to-rent ratio of 27.8 far exceeds the national benchmark of 18.7. Although the rent burden of 17.4% aligns closely with the national average of 17.2%, the disparity between ownership costs and rental expenses indicates a market heavily skewed toward high-value asset accumulation rather than broad-based residential accessibility. This structural imbalance is reinforced by a median home value of $466,100, which is 104.7% above the national average, despite a median household income of $96,575 that is only 42.0% above the national norm. The market’s trajectory reveals a period of explosive growth that is now moderating. Home values surged from $197,600 in 2017 to $466,100 in 2024, with peak year-over-year growth of 26.0% in 2022 slowing to 16.1% in 2024. Recent FHFA HPI data confirms this deceleration, showing a 5.86% year-over-year increase in 2025, which significantly outperforms the Colorado state average of -0.61%. This divergence highlights a county-specific momentum that contrasts with broader state trends. However, this price appreciation has not been matched by proportional rent increases, suggesting that the rental sector remains relatively stable while the ownership segment experiences rapid capital gains. The local economic base, comprising 2,206 jobs and an average weekly wage of $1,262, provides a limited employment pool that may struggle to support such high asset values without external migration. Indeed, net migration of +498 filers between 2021 and 2022, with inflow incomes averaging $70,974, suggests that wealthier newcomers are driving demand rather than local wage growth. Supply constraints further exacerbate the imbalance, with only 46 new single-family units authorized in 2025 and zero multi-family permits. This limited supply, combined with a 7.40% national mortgage rate, restricts buyer entry and sustains high price levels. The market’s vitality is high, driven by strong demand and positive net migration, but this is counterbalanced by poor affordability and moderate stability. The composite assessment reflects a market where high vitality and momentum are undermined by severe affordability challenges, resulting in a "stressed" classification. Potential buyers face a high entry barrier relative to local incomes, while renters encounter a market where ownership costs have decoupled significantly from rental expenses, indicating a bifurcated housing landscape. The data suggests that while the market remains active and outperforming the state, its fundamental affordability metrics remain a critical constraint for local residents.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Lake County is 9.0% above the Colorado state average and 104.7% above the U.S. national average. The median rent of $1,397/month is 7.3% above the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 27.8, Lake County is a strongly rent-favored market. Home prices are high relative to rents, meaning renting is typically far cheaper than owning in the near term.

Lake County has an owner-occupancy rate of 75.6%, compared to 71.9% in Colorado and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more homeowner-heavy market than the U.S. average.
