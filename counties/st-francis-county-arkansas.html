📝 Editor's Insight

St. Francis County operates as a distinct renter’s market, evidenced by a price-to-rent ratio of 9.2, significantly below the national average of 18.0. This metric indicates that rental costs are disproportionately high relative to home values, making ownership financially more attractive for qualified buyers. Over the past five years, the market has experienced rapid appreciation, with median home values growing at a 7.9% CAGR to $87,700, outpacing the 5.7% CAGR in median gross rent. This divergence suggests that asset values are inflating faster than the cost of temporary housing. Affordability remains complex; while the price-to-income ratio of 2.10 is well below the national benchmark of 3.09, indicating strong purchase affordability, the rent burden stands at 22.7%, exceeding the national average of 17.2%. Consequently, renters face significant financial strain despite low regional income levels. A critical insight is that the 55.2% owner-occupancy rate implies nearly half the population relies on rental housing. Given the substantial gap between local home values and the state average of -33.5%, potential residents should recognize that while entry-level ownership is accessible, the rental sector remains comparatively expensive and burdensome relative to local earning power.
