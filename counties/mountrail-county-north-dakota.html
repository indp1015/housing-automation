📝 Local Market Analysis

Mountrail County presents a complex housing landscape where strong affordability metrics coexist with recent price volatility. The market leans toward buyer advantage, evidenced by a price-to-income ratio of 2.88, which is below the national average of 3.20, and a rent burden of 14.1%, significantly lower than the 17.2% national benchmark. However, the price-to-rent ratio of 20.4 exceeds the national average of 18.7, suggesting that while entry costs are manageable, the long-term cost of ownership relative to renting is higher than typical. This dynamic is further complicated by a 61.5% owner-occupancy rate, indicating a stable residential base despite the small population of 9,507. The multi-year trend reveals a sharp acceleration in home values, with prices rising from $169,800 in 2017 to $232,700 in 2024. This period included a peak year-over-year increase of 17.1% in 2022, followed by a moderation to 4.7% in 2024. Notably, the most recent FHFA HPI data indicates a 6.00% year-over-year decline, a stark underperformance relative to the North Dakota state average of +4.82%. This divergence suggests that the county’s recent price surge may have been unsustainable or driven by specific local factors rather than broad market health, leading to a current correction phase. The low stability component of the market assessment reflects this volatility, contrasting with the high vitality score driven by robust migration inflows. Economic fundamentals provide a mixed backdrop for this correction. Local employment stands at 4,103 jobs with an average weekly wage of $1,641, supporting a median household income of $80,717, which is 18.7% above the national average. Migration data shows a net gain of 239 filers, with inflow incomes averaging $109,134, indicating that new residents possess higher-than-average purchasing power. However, housing supply remains constrained, with only 23 new single-family units authorized and zero multi-family units, limiting new inventory. With the 30-year fixed mortgage rate at 7.40%, high borrowing costs further suppress demand, potentially exacerbating the recent price decline. For potential buyers, the current 6% price drop offers an opportunity to acquire assets in a high-income area, while renters benefit from low burdens. The market’s trajectory is heavily influenced by the disparity between strong local income levels and the recent sharp reversal in home values, creating a volatile but potentially accessible environment for those with immediate liquidity.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Mountrail County is 32.4% above the North Dakota state average and 2.2% above the U.S. national average. The median rent of $950/month is 23.1% above the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 20.4, renting is generally more cost-effective than buying in the short to medium term. The market favors buyers only for long-term holds.

Mountrail County has an owner-occupancy rate of 61.5%, compared to 73.6% in North Dakota and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more renter-heavy market than the U.S. average.
