📝 Editor's Insight

San Francisco County operates as a distinct renter’s market, evidenced by a 38.5% owner-occupancy rate and a price-to-rent ratio of 47.6, which is 163% above the national average of 18.1. This extreme disparity indicates that purchasing is financially inaccessible relative to leasing for most demographics. The five-year trend reveals synchronized appreciation, with home values and rents compounding at 5.9% and 6.3% annually, respectively. This parallel growth suggests that rising housing costs are driving both asset inflation and rental demand, rather than one sector outpacing the other. Affordability remains severely constrained; the price-to-income ratio of 9.76 is more than triple the national benchmark of 3.10. Furthermore, while the 20.5% rent burden slightly exceeds the 17.2% national average, the absolute median rent of $2,419 consumes a disproportionate share of the median household income. A critical insight for potential residents is that despite incomes being 113.3% above the national average, the 544.7% premium on home values renders standard financial metrics invalid, requiring residents to rely on high-income brackets to achieve basic housing stability.

Market Analysis

The median home value in San Francisco County is 128.9% above the California state average and 512.4% above the U.S. national average. The median rent of $2,476/month is 46.5% above the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 46.9, San Francisco County is a strongly rent-favored market. Home prices are high relative to rents, meaning renting is typically far cheaper than owning in the near term.

San Francisco County has an owner-occupancy rate of 38.2%, compared to 63.4% in California and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more renter-heavy market than the U.S. average.
