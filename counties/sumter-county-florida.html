📝 Local Market Analysis

Sumter County, Florida, operates as a distinctively challenging environment for entry-level buyers, characterized by severe affordability constraints despite robust economic vitality. The market leans heavily toward a renter’s disadvantage, evidenced by a price-to-rent ratio of 25.3, significantly exceeding the national average of 18.7. With an owner-occupancy rate of 87.6%, the housing stock is predominantly owned, limiting rental inventory and driving the median gross rent to $1,269, which consumes 19.9% of the median household income of $76,508. This rent burden surpasses the national benchmark of 17.2%, indicating that housing costs place a disproportionate strain on local households compared to the broader U.S. market. The multi-year trajectory reveals a period of aggressive appreciation that is now stabilizing. Home values surged from $239,800 in 2017 to $385,400 in 2024, with a peak year-over-year increase of 15.8% in 2022. However, recent FHFA HPI data indicates a 3.06% year-over-year decline, marking a 3.97 percentage point underperformance relative to the Florida state average of +0.91%. This divergence suggests a localized correction or cooling phase, contrasting with the sustained growth seen in previous years. The price-to-income ratio stands at 5.04, nearly double the national average of 3.20, highlighting that home prices have outpaced local income growth, which sits only 12.5% above the national mean. Economic fundamentals remain strong, supporting long-term demand despite current price pressures. The county’s vitality is underscored by a net migration inflow of 11,130 filers between 2021 and 2022, with incoming households earning an average of $105,596, well above the local median. Housing supply is responsive, with 3,516 new units authorized in 2025, predominantly single-family homes. Nevertheless, the high mortgage rate of 7.32% further exacerbates affordability challenges for prospective buyers. The composite assessment reflects this tension: high stability and vitality scores are offset by a critically low affordability rating. For potential stakeholders, the data indicates a market where high demand and strong migration flows are colliding with elevated price points and restrictive financing conditions, creating a bifurcated landscape where wealthier migrants drive prices upward while local median-income households face increasing barriers to entry.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Sumter County is 35.5% above the Florida state average and 69.3% above the U.S. national average. The median rent of $1,269/month is 2.6% below the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 25.3, Sumter County is a strongly rent-favored market. Home prices are high relative to rents, meaning renting is typically far cheaper than owning in the near term.

Sumter County has an owner-occupancy rate of 87.6%, compared to 74.6% in Florida and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more homeowner-heavy market than the U.S. average.
