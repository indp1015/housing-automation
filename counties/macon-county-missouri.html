📝 Local Market Analysis

Macon County, Missouri, presents a distinct buyer’s market characterized by exceptional affordability relative to national benchmarks, though recent price acceleration complicates long-term stability. With a median home value of $149,100 and a median household income of $62,357, the price-to-income ratio stands at 2.39, significantly below the national average of 3.20. This affordability is reinforced by a rent burden of 12.9%, well under the 17.2% national standard, and a price-to-rent ratio of 18.5 that aligns closely with the national mean of 18.7. These metrics indicate that ownership remains a financially superior option compared to renting, supported by a high owner occupancy rate of 76.8%. However, the market is not without volatility, as evidenced by the sharp 17.4% year-over-year price increase in 2024, which outpaces the state’s 3.71% growth rate and suggests a localized surge in demand or supply constraints. The recent momentum, reflected in the FHFA HPI index rising 5.75% year-over-year, confirms that Macon County is outperforming the broader Missouri market by 2.04 percentage points. This divergence from state trends is notable, particularly given the county’s modest economic base of 3,419 jobs and an average weekly wage of $853. The limited housing supply, with only 13 new units authorized in 2025, contributes to this price pressure, as the low construction volume fails to keep pace with the net migration gain of 300 filers. While the overall affordability score remains strong at 82.6, the stability metric of 56.8 reflects the risk associated with such rapid appreciation in a small market. The vitality score of 34.7 further underscores the limited economic dynamism, where employment growth is insufficient to sustain the current pace of price increases without external factors. For potential buyers, the primary consideration is the disparity between the county’s long-term affordability and its short-term price velocity. The 34.5% discount to national home values offers significant entry-level value, yet the 7.40% mortgage rate increases monthly costs, necessitating careful budgeting. Renter’s markets remain attractive due to the low rent burden, but the rising price-to-rent dynamics suggest that the cost advantage of renting may erode over time. The data indicates a market where value is high but stability is fragile, driven by supply limitations rather than robust local economic expansion. Investors and residents should monitor the sustainability of the 17.4% price jump, as the lack of substantial new construction and modest wage growth may limit further appreciation potential. The current environment favors those with immediate purchasing power, while those waiting for stabilization may face continued pressure from the constrained inventory and positive net migration trends.
