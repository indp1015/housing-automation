📝 Local Market Analysis

Clay County, Missouri, functions as a balanced market leaning slightly toward buyers, characterized by moderate affordability relative to national standards. The price-to-income ratio stands at 3.12, marginally below the national average of 3.20, while the rent burden is 16.9% of income, also slightly under the 17.2% national benchmark. This suggests that both purchasing and renting remain accessible for median-income households, though the price-to-rent ratio of 18.5 indicates that owning is marginally more cost-effective than renting over time. The market is not extreme in either direction, with metrics hovering closely around national baselines, creating a stable environment for both renters and prospective homeowners. The multi-year trend reveals a period of significant acceleration followed by stabilization. Home values surged from $160,900 in 2017 to $275,600 in 2024, with a peak year-over-year increase of 18.8% in 2022. However, recent FHFA data shows a sharp deceleration to just 1.81% growth in 2025, which is 1.90 percentage points below the state’s 3.71% growth rate. This divergence highlights a notable anomaly: while long-term appreciation was robust, current momentum is lagging the broader Missouri market. This slowdown coincides with a 7.40% national mortgage rate, which likely suppresses demand velocity. Meanwhile, housing supply remains constrained, with only 297 new units authorized in 2025, including 207 single-family homes, which may limit future price corrections despite the current cooling. Regional context further clarifies the market’s position. Clay County home values are 56.8% above the state average and 21.0% above the national average, reflecting its premium status within Missouri. This premium is supported by a median household income of $88,468, which is 30.1% higher than the national mean. Economic fundamentals remain solid, with 95,376 total jobs and an average weekly wage of $1,251. Net migration of +4,831 filers between 2021 and 2022 indicates sustained demographic inflow, providing a baseline of demand. The overall assessment reflects a market where affordability is preserved but growth momentum has cooled significantly below state trends. Stability is compromised by the recent price deceleration, while vitality is maintained by strong income levels and positive migration. Potential buyers should note that while prices are high relative to the state, the current slowdown offers a more favorable entry point compared to the 2022 peak, whereas renters face a market where ownership remains the more financially advantageous long-term strategy.

The median home value in Clay County is 56.8% above the Missouri state average and 21.0% above the U.S. national average. The median rent of $1,243/month is 59.0% above the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 18.5, the buy-vs-rent decision in Clay County is balanced. Whether buying or renting makes more sense depends on your time horizon, down payment capacity, and local market conditions.
