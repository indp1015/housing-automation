📝 Local Market Analysis

Washington County, Colorado, functions as a balanced market leaning toward affordability for homeowners, characterized by a 73.6% owner-occupancy rate and a median home value of $226,200. The price-to-income ratio stands at 3.37, slightly above the national average of 3.20, while the rent burden is 16.9%, marginally below the national 17.2%. These metrics indicate that while housing costs are accessible relative to income, the premium paid for ownership over renting is evident in the 19.9 price-to-rent ratio, which exceeds the national benchmark of 18.7. This structure suggests that long-term equity accumulation remains a viable strategy for buyers, despite the modestly higher entry cost compared to the national norm. The multi-year trend reveals a period of significant acceleration followed by stabilization. Home values surged from $120,300 in 2017 to $226,200 in 2024, with a peak year-over-year increase of 20.4% in 2022. Recent data shows this growth moderating to 3.6% in 2024, yet the FHFA HPI indicates a current 7.05% year-over-year gain, outperforming the Colorado state average of -0.61% by 7.66 percentage points. This divergence highlights a localized strength where Washington County continues to appreciate while the broader state market stagnates. However, this price momentum contrasts with a minimal housing supply, evidenced by only three new single-family units authorized in 2025 and zero multi-family permits. Such constrained supply likely sustains price resilience, particularly as net migration remains positive with 103 additional filers in 2021-2022. Economic fundamentals support this stability, with a median household income of $67,167 and an average weekly wage of $995. Despite the 7.32% national mortgage rate, the county’s affordability remains competitive because home values are 47.1% below the state average. The local economy, comprising 678 jobs across 134 establishments, provides a stable but limited employment base. The primary driver of the market’s current standing is momentum, which scores higher than stability or vitality due to the recent price outperformance against the state. Conversely, affordability and vitality scores remain moderate, reflecting the small population of 4,831 and the tight supply constraints. Potential buyers should note that while the market is currently outperforming state trends, the lack of new construction means future price appreciation will likely depend heavily on existing inventory turnover rather than new supply expansion.
