📝 Local Market Analysis

Clinton County, Illinois, operates as a distinctively affordable market relative to national standards, characterized by a price-to-income ratio of 2.22, significantly lower than the national average of 3.20. This affordability is reinforced by a rent burden of 12.6%, well below the national 17.2% benchmark, and a price-to-rent ratio of 17.6, which sits under the national 18.7 average. These metrics suggest a balanced-to-buyer’s environment where purchasing costs remain manageable against local earnings, with the median household income of $86,588 providing strong support for the median home value of $192,400. The market exhibits a clear upward trajectory in asset values, with home prices rising from $142,900 in 2017 to $192,400 in 2024, representing a cumulative gain of approximately 34.6%. While the peak year-over-year growth occurred in 2022 at 12.2%, recent momentum has moderated to 6.6% in 2024. The FHFA House Price Index confirms this sustained appreciation, showing a 5.48% year-over-year increase in 2025, which tracks nearly identically to the Illinois state average of 5.54%. This consistent price appreciation contrasts with the more stable rental market, indicating a divergence where equity accumulation is outpacing rental cost increases. Regional context highlights Clinton County’s premium positioning within Illinois, where home values are 25.7% higher than the state average, while incomes are 27.3% above the national mean. This wealth concentration is supported by positive net migration of 465 filers and a robust local economy with an average weekly wage of $948. However, housing supply remains constrained, with only 51 new units authorized in 2025, predominantly single-family homes. This limited new construction, combined with a 7.40% national mortgage rate, creates friction for entry-level buyers despite the favorable price-to-income ratio. The high owner occupancy rate of 80.3% further indicates a stable, established housing stock. For stakeholders, the data suggests that while affordability remains attractive compared to national peers, the rapid recent appreciation and low supply growth may limit further short-term price concessions, making timing critical for prospective purchasers seeking to capitalize on the current income-to-price disparity.
