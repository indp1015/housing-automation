📝 Local Market Analysis

St. Landry Parish presents a complex housing landscape characterized by strong price momentum but severe affordability constraints. The market is currently skewed toward renters, evidenced by a price-to-rent ratio of 15.9, which sits below the national average of 18.7, suggesting that renting is relatively more cost-effective than buying. This dynamic is reinforced by a rent burden of 20.2% of household income, exceeding the national benchmark of 17.2%. While the price-to-income ratio of 3.20 aligns precisely with the national average, this metric masks the underlying stress caused by low median household income of $44,462, which is 34.6% below the national mean. Consequently, the market exhibits high momentum but low stability, driven by recent price surges rather than broad-based economic vitality. The multi-year trend reveals a volatile trajectory, with home values spiking 18.8% in 2022 before moderating to a slight 0.3% decline in 2024. This recent stabilization contrasts sharply with the FHFA HPI data, which indicates a 6.14% year-over-year increase in 2025, outperforming the Louisiana state average of 1.59% by 4.55 percentage points. This divergence suggests that while census-based estimates show flatlining, actual mortgage transactions reflect continued appreciation. A notable anomaly is the divergence between price and rent growth; home values are rising faster than rents, potentially indicating a shift in buyer demand or a lag in rental market adjustments. This trend is supported by positive net migration of 710 filers, though the average income of incoming residents ($55,980) only modestly exceeds local averages, limiting the depth of purchasing power. Economic fundamentals remain constrained, with total employment at 19,016 jobs and an average weekly wage of $928. Housing supply is limited to 153 new single-family units in 2025, with zero multi-family permits, which may sustain price pressure despite high mortgage rates of 7.40%. The market’s classification as stressed is driven primarily by low affordability and vitality scores, as the low owner-occupancy rate of 70.1% and modest employment base fail to support the recent price gains. For potential buyers, the current environment offers entry points 37.4% below national home values, yet the high cost of borrowing and stagnant income growth pose significant risks. Renters may find relative stability given the lower price-to-rent ratio, but the outperformance of local prices relative to state trends suggests that long-term equity gains may be inconsistent with the broader regional economic outlook.
