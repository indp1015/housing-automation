📝 Local Market Analysis

New Hanover County operates as a distinctively renter-favorable market, characterized by a price-to-rent ratio of 22.8, significantly exceeding the national average of 18.7. This metric indicates that purchasing is substantially more expensive relative to rental costs than in typical U.S. markets. The affordability crisis is starkly evident in the price-to-income ratio of 5.16, which is 61% higher than the national benchmark of 3.20. Consequently, the median household income of $75,166 supports a median home value of $387,800, creating a severe financial barrier for local buyers. While the rent burden sits at 22.6% of income, slightly above the national 17.2% average, the primary pressure point remains ownership costs rather than rental expenses. The market exhibits strong momentum driven by external demand rather than local income growth. Home values rose 9.6% year-over-year in 2024, continuing a trend of double-digit increases seen in 2022 and 2023. Recent FHFA data confirms this resilience, showing a 4.67% year-over-year increase in 2025, which outpaces the state’s 0.38% growth by 4.29 percentage points. This divergence suggests that New Hanover is capturing inflows from outside the region. Indeed, net migration added 7,544 filers between 2021 and 2022, with incoming households reporting an average income of $107,518, significantly higher than the local median. This demographic shift fuels demand despite a 7.40% national mortgage rate, which suppresses local purchasing power. Supply dynamics further complicate the landscape. With 2,332 new units authorized in 2025, including 1,208 single-family homes, construction is attempting to meet demand, yet the owner-occupancy rate remains at 61.4%. The local economy supports this growth through 117,274 jobs and an average weekly wage of $1,256, though this wage level does not align with the high cost of entry. The market’s vitality is high, driven by robust population growth and employment, but stability is compromised by the extreme affordability gap. Buyers face a market where price appreciation consistently outpaces income growth, while renters benefit from a relatively stable, albeit rising, rental market. The combination of high migration, premium pricing relative to state averages, and sustained price momentum defines New Hanover as a high-demand, low-affordability coastal market.

Market Analysis

The median home value in New Hanover County is 64.8% above the North Carolina state average and 70.3% above the U.S. national average. The median rent of $1,417/month is 45.2% above the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 22.8, renting is generally more cost-effective than buying in the short to medium term. The market favors buyers only for long-term holds.

New Hanover County has an owner-occupancy rate of 61.4%, compared to 71.8% in North Carolina and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more renter-heavy market than the U.S. average.
