📝 Local Market Analysis

Will County, Illinois, functions as a balanced housing market characterized by strong economic fundamentals and moderate affordability relative to national standards. The area’s median home value of $319,600 sits 40.4% above the national average, yet this premium is partially offset by a median household income of $109,984, which exceeds the national mean by 61.7%. Consequently, the price-to-income ratio of 2.91 remains below the national average of 3.20, indicating that local purchasing power supports current price levels without excessive strain. Similarly, the rent burden of 16.0% is slightly lower than the national average of 17.2%, suggesting that rental costs are manageable for residents. However, the market is not without tension; the price-to-rent ratio of 18.2 is marginally below the national average of 18.7, implying that buying offers a slight cost advantage over renting, though the gap is narrow. The multi-year trajectory reveals a significant acceleration in home values, particularly during the 2022 surge of 13.7% year-over-year, which has since moderated to 7.2% in 2024. Recent FHFA data shows a 4.60% year-over-year increase, underperforming the Illinois state average of 5.54% by 0.94 percentage points. This deceleration suggests a cooling phase after the pandemic-era spike. A notable pattern emerges in the divergence between asset classes: home values are rising faster than rents, which may pressure renters to transition to ownership if their incomes remain stable. The local economy supports this demand, with 234,668 jobs and an average weekly wage of $1,191. Net migration of +5,152 filers between 2021 and 2022 further bolsters demand, while 1,993 new housing units authorized in 2025 provide limited supply relief, with single-family homes accounting for 90% of permits. Stability is the primary driver of the market’s resilience, evidenced by the 89.0 stability metric, while vitality lags at 43.3 due to constrained supply growth relative to population inflows. The 7.32% national mortgage rate adds friction to entry-level transactions, potentially extending the period of renting for some demographics. For prospective buyers, the current environment offers a window where income levels comfortably cover debt obligations, given the favorable price-to-income ratio. Renters should note that while monthly costs are low relative to income, the rapid appreciation of home values may erode the long-term financial advantage of renting. The market’s strength is underpinned by robust employment and positive net migration, but the slowing price growth relative to the state average indicates a maturing market rather than a speculative bubble.
