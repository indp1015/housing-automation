📝 Local Market Analysis

Schenectady County presents a mixed-market profile characterized by strong price momentum but constrained affordability. The market leans slightly toward buyers due to a price-to-rent ratio of 16.5, which is below the national average of 18.7, suggesting that purchasing is relatively more economical than renting compared to broader U.S. trends. However, the owner-occupancy rate stands at 64.0%, indicating a substantial rental sector. The composite assessment reflects this tension, with high momentum scores offset by lower stability and vitality metrics, driven by the rapid recent escalation in home values relative to local income levels. From 2017 to 2024, median home values surged from $164,100 to $235,700, representing a cumulative increase of approximately 43.6%. This growth accelerated sharply in 2022 with a 14.8% year-over-year jump before moderating to 5.6% in 2024. Recent FHFA HPI data confirms continued strength, showing a 4.15% year-over-year increase in 2025, outpacing the New York state average of 3.55%. This divergence signals that Schenectady is outperforming its regional peers, likely fueled by net positive migration of 2,442 filers between 2021 and 2022. While median household income of $79,623 exceeds the national average by 17.1%, the price-to-income ratio of 2.96 remains slightly below the national benchmark of 3.20, preserving a degree of relative affordability. Nevertheless, the rent burden of 18.0% slightly exceeds the national average of 17.2%, indicating that rental costs are becoming a heavier financial load for households. The local economic context supports sustained demand, with total employment at 49,874 jobs and an average weekly wage of $1,434. Housing supply remains tight, with only 110 new units authorized in 2025, split between 66 single-family and 44 multi-family homes. This limited construction pipeline, combined with a 7.40% national mortgage rate, creates friction for new entrants while supporting existing equity. The primary data anomaly is the decoupling of price growth from income growth; home values have risen significantly faster than the 17.1% income advantage over national averages suggests. Consequently, while the market exhibits robust momentum and outperforms state benchmarks, the stability score remains low due to the volatility of recent price spikes. Potential buyers face a competitive environment where rapid appreciation outpaces local wage growth, while renters encounter rising costs that erode disposable income, making the market a high-growth but increasingly expensive proposition for middle-income households.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Schenectady County is 16.2% below the New York state average and 3.5% above the U.S. national average. The median rent of $1,194/month is 2.2% above the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 16.5, the buy-vs-rent decision in Schenectady County is balanced. Whether buying or renting makes more sense depends on your time horizon, down payment capacity, and local market conditions.

Schenectady County has an owner-occupancy rate of 64.0%, compared to 69.3% in New York and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more renter-heavy market than the U.S. average.
