📝 Editor's Insight

Sevier County operates as a distinct buyer’s market, evidenced by a price-to-rent ratio of 12.8, significantly below the national average of 18.0. This metric indicates that purchasing is substantially more cost-effective than renting relative to local income levels. Over the past five years, home values have appreciated at a 7.3% CAGR, outpacing the 5.7% CAGR in gross rents. This divergence suggests that equity accumulation is accelerating faster than rental costs, favoring long-term ownership over leasing. Affordability remains robust, with a price-to-income ratio of 2.16 compared to the national benchmark of 3.09. Furthermore, the rent burden sits at 16.8%, marginally below the national average of 17.2%. A critical insight for potential residents is that while the county is 47.4% cheaper than the national average, its median income is 21.7% below the national mean. Consequently, the low price-to-income ratio is driven more by depressed local wages than by exceptionally low housing costs, requiring buyers to carefully assess local employment stability against the rapid 7.3% appreciation rate.
