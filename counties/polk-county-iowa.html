📝 Local Market Analysis

Polk County, Iowa, functions as a balanced market with a slight lean toward buyers, evidenced by a price-to-income ratio of 3.14, which sits marginally below the national average of 3.20. The rent burden stands at 16.5% of median household income, also slightly below the national benchmark of 17.2%, indicating that both ownership and rental costs remain relatively manageable for the local demographic. The price-to-rent ratio of 19.0 is nearly identical to the national average of 18.7, suggesting that the cost of buying versus renting is in equilibrium, with no significant arbitrage opportunity favoring one side over the other. This stability is supported by a median household income of $83,576, which exceeds the national average by 22.9%, providing a solid economic foundation for housing demand. The multi-year trend reveals a period of accelerated appreciation that is now moderating. Home values surged 15.3% in 2022 before decelerating to 5.5% in 2024, while the FHFA House Price Index shows a recent year-over-year change of +1.70% in 2025. This recent deceleration places the county 0.55 percentage points below the state’s 2.25% growth rate, indicating a temporary underperformance relative to Iowa’s broader market. A notable divergence exists between asset classes, as home values are rising faster than rents, which have remained comparatively stable. This pattern suggests that supply constraints or higher construction costs are disproportionately affecting new home inventory rather than the rental sector. The county’s housing supply, with 4,730 new units authorized in 2025, includes a near-equal split between single-family and multi-family homes, helping to address demand across both segments. Regional context highlights Polk County’s premium position within the state, with median home values 53.2% above the Iowa average. This premium is justified by robust local economic indicators, including 275,721 total jobs and an average weekly wage of $1,456. Net migration of +6,467 filers between 2021 and 2022 further underscores sustained demographic growth, with incoming residents earning slightly less than those leaving, potentially stabilizing the lower end of the income distribution. The current 30-year fixed mortgage rate of 7.40% imposes higher carrying costs on buyers, yet the county’s strong income levels and moderate affordability metrics mitigate this pressure. For potential buyers, the market offers entry points below national cost benchmarks, while renters benefit from a rent burden that remains below the national average. The combination of strong employment, balanced supply, and moderating price growth creates a stable environment where neither buyers nor renters face acute financial distress, though the divergence in asset appreciation rates warrants monitoring for future shifts in the rental-to-ownership cost dynamic.
