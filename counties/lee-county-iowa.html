📝 Local Market Analysis

Lee County, Iowa, functions as a distinct buyer’s market, characterized by significant affordability advantages relative to national benchmarks. The price-to-income ratio stands at 2.27, substantially below the national average of 3.20, while the rent burden is 16.1% of median income, slightly lower than the 17.2% national norm. This affordability is reinforced by a price-to-rent ratio of 14.1, which is markedly lower than the national average of 18.7, indicating that purchasing is financially more advantageous than renting for most households. The median home value of $141,400 is 37.9% below the national average and 17.3% below the Iowa state average, positioning the county as a low-cost entry point for homeownership. Recent trends reveal a period of accelerated appreciation, with median home values rising from $90,200 in 2017 to $141,400 in 2024. This trajectory includes a sharp 17.7% increase in 2022 and a 14.4% jump in 2023, far outpacing the modest rent growth reflected in the stable $835 median gross rent. The most recent FHFA HPI data confirms this momentum, showing a 7.19% year-over-year increase, which outperforms the Iowa state average of 2.25% by 4.94 percentage points. This divergence suggests that home values are rising faster than rental costs, a pattern often associated with limited supply or increasing demand for ownership. The local economy supports this growth through a median household income of $62,382 and an average weekly wage of $1,164, though total employment remains modest at 11,531 jobs. Supply constraints appear to be a primary driver of recent price increases. Only 80 new housing units were authorized in 2025, with just 20 designated for single-family homes, a figure insufficient to meet the net migration inflow of 540 filers recorded between 2021 and 2022. This tight supply, combined with a 7.40% national mortgage rate, creates a competitive environment for buyers despite the low absolute price points. The high owner-occupancy rate of 76.3% further indicates a stable, established housing stock. For prospective buyers, the current environment offers substantial value relative to income, but the rapid appreciation over the past two years suggests that entry costs are rising quickly. Renters face a stable cost structure, with rent burdens remaining below national averages, providing a temporary alternative to the escalating purchase prices. The combination of positive net migration and limited new construction signals that the market is tightening, potentially limiting future affordability gains for first-time buyers.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Lee County is 17.3% below the Iowa state average and 37.9% below the U.S. national average. The median rent of $835/month is about equal to the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 14.1, buying a home in Lee County is generally more favorable than renting for those planning to stay long-term. A ratio below 15 typically signals that purchasing is financially advantageous over a multi-year horizon.

Lee County has an owner-occupancy rate of 76.3%, compared to 75.8% in Iowa and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more homeowner-heavy market than the U.S. average.
