📝 Local Market Analysis

Carter County, Missouri, functions as a distinctively affordable buyer’s market, characterized by low entry barriers but significant volatility. The median home value of $159,400 sits 30.0% below the national average and 9.3% below the state mean, while the price-to-income ratio of 3.19 aligns precisely with the national benchmark of 3.20. This parity indicates that despite lower absolute prices, housing costs remain proportional to local earnings, which average $50,000 annually. Consequently, the market offers structural affordability, though the 60.7% owner-occupancy rate suggests a substantial rental sector that remains accessible, with a rent burden of 15.7% falling below the national average of 17.2%. The most striking feature of this market is its extreme instability, evidenced by a stability metric of only 5.0 out of 100. Home values have fluctuated wildly, swinging from a 29.0% surge in 2018 to a 13.0% decline in 2019, followed by a 33.8% spike in 2020 and an 18.5% drop in 2023. This erratic pattern contrasts sharply with the smoother national trajectory, creating a high-risk environment for investors and buyers alike. The price-to-rent ratio of 20.2 exceeds the national average of 18.7, implying that purchasing is slightly more expensive relative to renting than in typical markets, yet the recent 8.0% year-over-year price increase outpaces rent growth, signaling a shift toward owner-occupied demand. Economic fundamentals remain fragile, with only 1,028 total jobs and a minimal housing supply of five new single-family units authorized in 2025. While net migration added 58 filers between 2021 and 2022, the small population base of 5,297 means even modest demographic shifts can distort local metrics. The current 7.40% mortgage rate further constrains purchasing power, yet the low absolute price point mitigates this impact. Vitality scores remain low at 18.2, reflecting the limited economic engine behind the housing sector. For potential buyers, the primary risk is not affordability but volatility; the market’s history of double-digit annual swings suggests that short-term holding periods carry significant downside risk. Renter’s perspectives are more favorable, as the low rent burden provides stability against the chaotic price movements affecting the ownership side. Ultimately, the market is defined by cheap entry prices undermined by unpredictable value retention and a thin economic foundation.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Carter County is 9.3% below the Missouri state average and 30.0% below the U.S. national average. The median rent of $656/month is 16.1% below the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 20.2, renting is generally more cost-effective than buying in the short to medium term. The market favors buyers only for long-term holds.

Carter County has an owner-occupancy rate of 60.7%, compared to 73.7% in Missouri and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more renter-heavy market than the U.S. average.
