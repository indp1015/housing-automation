📝 Local Market Analysis

Marshall County, Indiana, presents a balanced market leaning slightly toward buyers, characterized by strong price momentum but constrained economic vitality. The market classification is supported by a price-to-income ratio of 2.85, which is below the national average of 3.20, and a rent burden of 15.7%, underperforming the national benchmark of 17.2%. These metrics indicate that housing costs remain manageable relative to local incomes, with the median household income of $71,808 exceeding the national average by 5.6%. The owner-occupancy rate of 76.4% further stabilizes the housing stock, suggesting a community with significant equity accumulation and residential permanence. Trend analysis reveals a period of accelerated appreciation, with median home values rising from $130,100 in 2017 to $204,900 in 2024. This trajectory includes a sharp 13.7% increase in 2022 and an 11.1% gain in 2024. Recent FHFA data confirms this momentum, showing an 8.21% year-over-year increase in 2025, significantly outpacing the state average of 3.45%. This divergence highlights a specific local dynamic where home values are rising faster than rents, which have remained relatively stable. The primary driver of this appreciation appears to be limited new supply, with only 118 units authorized in 2025, predominantly single-family homes, against a backdrop of net positive migration of 710 filers between 2021 and 2022. The economic context reveals a mixed picture. While employment stands at 17,540 jobs with an average weekly wage of $1,058, the low number of new multi-family units (only two) suggests limited rental expansion. This scarcity likely contributes to the sustained price growth despite a high national mortgage rate of 7.40%. The composite assessment reflects this duality: high momentum scores are offset by lower vitality metrics, as the local economy lacks the diversification of larger metropolitan areas. For potential buyers, the current environment offers affordability relative to national standards, yet the rapid price appreciation and tight supply indicate that entry costs are rising quickly. Renters face a more stable cost structure, with gross rents at $938 per month, but may find long-term homeownership increasingly competitive due to the strong upward pressure on asset values. The market’s strength is rooted in demographic inflows and supply constraints rather than broad-based wage growth, creating a distinct regional outlier within Indiana.
