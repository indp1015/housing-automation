📝 Local Market Analysis

Pasquotank County exhibits a distinct buyer’s market dynamic, primarily driven by a price-to-rent ratio of 16.7, which sits below the national average of 18.7. This metric suggests that purchasing offers a more favorable long-term financial position than renting, despite the county’s median home value of $234,600 exceeding the state average by a negligible 0.3%. The market is characterized by strong momentum, evidenced by a recent FHFA HPI year-over-year increase of 11.48%, significantly outpacing the North Carolina state average of 0.38%. This robust price appreciation follows a multi-year trend where values rose from $158,900 in 2017 to $234,600 in 2024, with peak growth of 12.3% recorded in 2022 before moderating to 5.1% in 2024. While home values have surged, median gross rent remains at $1,170, indicating that price growth has outpaced rental adjustments, creating a divergence that favors equity accumulation for owners. Affordability remains the primary constraint for residents, with a price-to-income ratio of 3.52, slightly above the national benchmark of 3.20. The median household income of $66,589 lags behind the national average by 2.1%, contributing to a rent burden of 21.1% of income, compared to the national average of 17.2%. This affordability challenge is compounded by a limited local economy, where total employment stands at 12,099 jobs with an average weekly wage of $928. However, the market shows signs of vitality through positive net migration of 1,338 filers and a construction pipeline of 274 new single-family units authorized in 2025. The absence of multi-family permits suggests that new supply will primarily cater to owner-occupants, potentially intensifying competition for existing rental stock. The overall market assessment reflects a tension between high momentum and low stability. The 86.8 momentum rating is driven by the significant outperformance of state price indices, while the 21.4 affordability rating highlights the strain on household budgets. With mortgage rates at 7.40%, the cost of entry is elevated, yet the relative strength of home values against rents supports the long-term value proposition. The 67.5% owner-occupancy rate indicates a stable housing base, but the disparity between income growth and price appreciation suggests that entry-level buyers face significant barriers. Consequently, the market is best described as one of strong price momentum constrained by affordability, where the primary risk for buyers is the pace of wage growth relative to sustained price increases.
