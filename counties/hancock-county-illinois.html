📝 Local Market Analysis

Hancock County, Illinois, operates as a distinctively affordable market characterized by extreme value relative to national benchmarks. With a median home value of $113,800, the county sits 50.0% below the national average and 25.6% below the state average. This affordability is reinforced by a price-to-income ratio of 1.73, significantly lower than the national mean of 3.20, and a rent burden of 14.2%, which is 3.0 percentage points below the national average of 17.2%. The price-to-rent ratio of 12.1 further indicates that purchasing is substantially more cost-effective than renting compared to broader U.S. trends, where the average is 18.7. These metrics collectively suggest a market where entry barriers are low, though the high owner-occupancy rate of 81.3% limits inventory turnover. Recent price appreciation has been robust, with the FHFA HPI showing a 5.85% year-over-year increase in 2025, slightly outpacing the state’s 5.54% growth by 0.31 percentage points. This momentum follows a period of accelerated growth, peaking at 11.7% in 2022 before moderating to 5.0% in 2024. The consistency between home value and rent growth rates suggests a balanced adjustment rather than a speculative bubble. However, the local economic foundation is thin, with only 3,274 total jobs and an average weekly wage of $997. Despite this limited employment base, the median household income of $65,865 remains within 3.1% of the national average, likely supported by remote work or commuter patterns. Net migration of +367 filers between 2021 and 2022, with inflow incomes averaging $69,366, indicates a stable demographic inflow that sustains demand. The most notable structural constraint is the severe lack of new housing supply, with only one new unit authorized in 2025. This near-total absence of new construction creates a supply-side rigidity that could sustain price appreciation even if demand plateaus. With 30-year fixed mortgage rates at 7.40%, financing costs remain a significant hurdle for buyers, yet the low absolute price point mitigates the impact of high interest rates compared to higher-cost markets. For potential buyers, the primary risk is limited inventory and liquidity due to the high owner-occupancy rate. Renter’s equity is stable, but the low rent burden suggests that tenants face minimal financial strain relative to their incomes. The market’s trajectory is driven less by local job creation and more by external migration and the scarcity of existing housing stock, making it a low-risk, low-reward environment for investors seeking rapid capital appreciation, but a highly accessible option for end-users prioritizing cost efficiency.
