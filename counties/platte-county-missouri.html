📝 Local Market Analysis

Platte County, Missouri, currently functions as a balanced market with distinct affordability challenges, characterized by a price-to-income ratio of 3.59 compared to the national average of 3.20. The market exhibits strong momentum, evidenced by an 8.7% year-over-year appreciation in median home values to $345,100 in 2024, which has moderated from a peak of 14.7% in 2022. Recent FHFA HPI data confirms this cooling trend, showing a 5.09% annual increase that outpaces the state’s 3.71% growth by 1.38 percentage points. This sustained appreciation is driven by robust regional vitality, reflected in a median household income of $96,227, which exceeds the national average by 41.5%. However, the high income levels have not fully offset the rapid rise in housing costs, resulting in a price-to-rent ratio of 21.6, significantly above the national benchmark of 18.7. This divergence suggests that ownership costs are escalating faster than rental alternatives, creating pressure on potential buyers despite the relatively low rent burden of 16.6%, which remains below the national average of 17.2%. The county’s economic fundamentals support continued demand, with 42,837 total jobs and an average weekly wage of $1,152. Migration patterns further underscore this demand, as net inflows of 3,417 filers between 2021 and 2022 indicate sustained population growth. Housing supply remains constrained relative to this demand, with only 425 new units authorized in 2025, split nearly evenly between single-family and multi-family structures. This limited supply, combined with a 67.4% owner-occupancy rate, suggests that the market is absorbing new entrants through existing inventory rather than new construction. The significant premium over the state average, where Platte County home values sit 96.4% higher, highlights its status as a high-cost outlier within Missouri. Consequently, the market’s stability is tested by the gap between rising asset values and the current 7.40% national mortgage rate, which increases monthly payment obligations for prospective owners. For stakeholders, the data indicates a favorable environment for current homeowners due to strong equity accumulation, but a challenging entry point for first-time buyers. The affordability metric of 39.4 reflects the strain of high entry prices relative to local incomes, while the vitality score of 76.8 captures the strength of the local labor market and income levels. Renter’s markets appear slightly more accessible on a monthly basis, yet the long-term cost of renting is elevated by the high price-to-rent ratio. The overall dynamic is shaped by the tension between strong economic fundamentals and high asset valuations, requiring careful consideration of total cost of living rather than relying solely on income metrics. The moderating price growth signals a potential stabilization phase, though the underlying demand drivers remain intact.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Platte County is 96.4% above the Missouri state average and 51.6% above the U.S. national average. The median rent of $1,334/month is 70.6% above the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 21.6, renting is generally more cost-effective than buying in the short to medium term. The market favors buyers only for long-term holds.

Platte County has an owner-occupancy rate of 67.4%, compared to 73.7% in Missouri and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more renter-heavy market than the U.S. average.
