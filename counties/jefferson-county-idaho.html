📝 Local Market Analysis

Jefferson County, Idaho, operates as a distinctively seller’s market characterized by extreme price appreciation relative to local income levels. The median home value of $408,400 stands 79.4% above the national average, while the price-to-income ratio of 4.71 significantly exceeds the national benchmark of 3.20. This affordability gap is further highlighted by a price-to-rent ratio of 29.9, nearly double the national average of 18.7, indicating that purchasing equity is currently far less accessible than renting for the median household. Despite the high entry costs, the rent burden remains slightly below the national average at 15.8%, suggesting that rental housing provides a more financially sustainable option for residents compared to homeownership under current valuation conditions. The market exhibits a pronounced trend of accelerating price growth, with year-over-year appreciation surging from 9.5% in 2021 to 21.9% in 2024. This rapid escalation outpaces local economic fundamentals, as median household income sits at $86,679, only 27.5% above the national mean. Recent FHFA HPI data confirms this momentum, showing a 6.38% year-over-year increase in 2025, which outperforms the Idaho state average of 3.48% by 2.90 percentage points. This divergence between housing values and broader economic indicators suggests a supply-constrained environment where demand significantly exceeds available inventory. The local economy supports only 6,806 jobs with an average weekly wage of $888, which is insufficient to sustain such high valuations without external capital inflows. Supply constraints are evident in the 2025 building permit data, which authorized just 312 new units, predominantly single-family homes, against a population of 33,154. Concurrently, net migration added 835 filers between 2021 and 2022, with incoming residents earning an average of $79,440, further intensifying demand in a market already strained by limited new construction. The national 30-year fixed mortgage rate of 7.40% exacerbates affordability challenges, effectively reducing purchasing power for potential buyers. For prospective residents, the data indicates that the rental market offers better immediate financial stability, while homeowners face significant barriers due to the widening gap between asset values and local earning potential. The sustained outperformance of Jefferson County relative to both state and national trends underscores a localized housing bubble dynamic driven by low supply and high demand.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Jefferson County is 15.0% above the Idaho state average and 79.4% above the U.S. national average. The median rent of $1,138/month is 17.6% above the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 29.9, Jefferson County is a strongly rent-favored market. Home prices are high relative to rents, meaning renting is typically far cheaper than owning in the near term.

Jefferson County has an owner-occupancy rate of 83.0%, compared to 75.2% in Idaho and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more homeowner-heavy market than the U.S. average.
