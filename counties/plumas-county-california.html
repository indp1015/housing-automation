📝 Editor's Insight

Plumas County exhibits a distinct seller’s market, evidenced by a price-to-rent ratio of 26.4, significantly exceeding the national average of 18.1. This disparity indicates that purchasing is comparatively more expensive relative to renting than in most other U.S. regions. From 2019 to 2023, median home values surged at a 7.8% CAGR, reaching $327,400, while rents grew more modestly at 3.1%. This divergence suggests capital appreciation is outpacing rental income growth, potentially compressing investor returns. Affordability remains strained; the price-to-income ratio stands at 5.04, nearly double the national benchmark of 3.10, despite median household income of $64,946 tracking closely with national levels. Although the rent burden of 19.1% is only marginally above the national average, the high cost barrier for homeownership is pronounced. A critical insight for potential residents is that despite home values being 42.6% below the California state average, the local market’s rapid appreciation rate and low population density of 19,607 create a tight supply environment. Buyers face elevated entry costs relative to local earnings, while renters benefit from slower rent inflation, making leasing a financially prudent alternative to purchasing in this specific economic context.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Plumas County is 40.9% below the California state average and 58.2% above the U.S. national average. The median rent of $1,194/month is 29.3% below the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 25.1, Plumas County is a strongly rent-favored market. Home prices are high relative to rents, meaning renting is typically far cheaper than owning in the near term.

Plumas County has an owner-occupancy rate of 74.6%, compared to 63.4% in California and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more homeowner-heavy market than the U.S. average.
