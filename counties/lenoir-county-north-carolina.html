📝 Local Market Analysis

Lenoir County, North Carolina, functions as a distinctively affordable market where price-to-income dynamics favor buyers over renters. With a median home value of $120,500 and a median household income of $45,143, the price-to-income ratio stands at 2.67, significantly below the national average of 3.20. Conversely, the price-to-rent ratio of 11.7 is markedly lower than the national benchmark of 18.7, indicating that purchasing is substantially more cost-effective than renting. This structural advantage is reinforced by a rent burden of 22.9% of income, which, while higher than the national average of 17.2%, remains manageable relative to the low absolute price points. The market is currently outperforming the broader state, with the FHFA House Price Index rising 2.06% year-over-year compared to North Carolina’s 0.38% increase, signaling robust local momentum despite a high 7.40% national mortgage rate. The recent trajectory reveals a sharp acceleration in home values following a period of stagnation. After declining from $93,500 in 2017 to $91,600 in 2021, prices surged 10.9% in 2022 and another 9.6% in 2023, reaching $120,500 by 2024. This rapid appreciation has outpaced income growth, creating a temporary affordability squeeze despite the low baseline prices. The stability of the market is currently compromised by this volatility, as evidenced by the low stability metric in the composite assessment. However, the momentum axis remains strong, driven by this consistent year-over-year price growth. Meanwhile, vitality is moderate, supported by a net migration gain of 959 filers and an average inflow income of $55,124, which exceeds the local median. Economic fundamentals provide a mixed backdrop for this appreciation. Local employment totals 24,073 jobs with an average weekly wage of $1,017, which supports demand but remains below national income levels. Housing supply is constrained, with only 131 new units authorized in 2025, predominantly single-family homes, limiting the ability to meet growing demand. This scarcity, combined with positive net migration, likely contributes to the sustained price increases. For potential buyers, the current environment offers entry into a market that is 47.1% below the national average in value, though the recent rapid appreciation suggests that the historical low-cost barrier is eroding. Renters face a less favorable position, as the low price-to-rent ratio makes ownership the financially superior long-term strategy, provided they can navigate the current high interest rates. The market’s classification as a buyer’s market is thus driven by structural affordability and strong momentum, even as stability metrics reflect the recent turbulence in price discovery.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Lenoir County is 48.8% below the North Carolina state average and 47.1% below the U.S. national average. The median rent of $861/month is 11.8% below the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 11.7, buying a home in Lenoir County is generally more favorable than renting for those planning to stay long-term. A ratio below 15 typically signals that purchasing is financially advantageous over a multi-year horizon.

Lenoir County has an owner-occupancy rate of 59.2%, compared to 71.8% in North Carolina and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more renter-heavy market than the U.S. average.
