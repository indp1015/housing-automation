📝 Editor's Insight

Ouray County functions as a distinctively seller’s market, characterized by a 78.0% owner-occupancy rate and a price-to-rent ratio of 39.9, nearly double the national average of 18.0. This extreme valuation disparity indicates that purchasing is significantly less attractive than renting relative to national standards. Over the past five years, home values surged at a 10.5% CAGR, rising from $449,000 to $670,100, while rents grew merely 3.4% annually. This divergence suggests that equity appreciation has outpaced rental cost inflation, rewarding long-term holders while penalizing new entrants. Affordability remains strained; the 7.92 price-to-income ratio is 2.5 times the national benchmark of 3.09, despite household incomes exceeding national averages by 28.1%. Although the 19.8% rent burden is only slightly above the 17.2% national norm, the primary barrier to entry is the capital required for purchase. A critical insight for prospective residents is that the county’s small population of 5,024 creates a thin inventory pool, meaning the 10.5% value growth reflects scarcity-driven pricing rather than broad-based demand, making liquidity and exit strategy crucial considerations for any buyer.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Ouray County is 73.0% above the Colorado state average and 224.9% above the U.S. national average. The median rent of $1,786/month is 37.2% above the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 34.5, Ouray County is a strongly rent-favored market. Home prices are high relative to rents, meaning renting is typically far cheaper than owning in the near term.

Ouray County has an owner-occupancy rate of 82.5%, compared to 71.9% in Colorado and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more homeowner-heavy market than the U.S. average.
