📝 Local Market Analysis

Hancock County, Iowa, functions as a distinctively affordable buyer’s market, characterized by a price-to-income ratio of 2.00, significantly lower than the national average of 3.20. The median home value of $141,400 sits 37.9% below the national mean, while the median household income of $70,601 exceeds the national average by 3.8%. This disparity creates exceptional purchasing power for local residents, further evidenced by a rent burden of only 12.5% of income, well under the national 17.2% benchmark. The market classification is reinforced by an owner-occupancy rate of 82.5%, indicating a stable, long-term housing base rather than a transient rental environment. Recent price appreciation has been robust, with home values rising from $97,600 in 2017 to $141,400 in 2024, representing a cumulative gain of approximately 45%. The most significant acceleration occurred between 2021 and 2023, with year-over-year increases of 16.5% and 11.5%, respectively. However, this momentum appears to be cooling; the FHFA HPI index indicates a recent 1.95% year-over-year decline in 2025, underperforming the state’s 2.25% growth by 4.20 percentage points. This divergence between the long-term upward trend and short-term correction suggests a market adjusting to higher financing costs, with the 30-year fixed mortgage rate currently at 7.40%. Economic fundamentals provide limited support for sustained rapid growth, as the local economy comprises only 5,064 jobs with an average weekly wage of $1,201. Housing supply remains constrained, with just seven new single-family units authorized in 2025 and zero multi-family permits, which may limit future inventory expansion. Migration data shows a modest net inflow of 189 filers, with incoming households earning slightly less than those departing, suggesting a demographic shift toward lower-income residents. Consequently, the market’s stability is driven primarily by affordability and low entry barriers rather than economic vitality or high job growth. The low vitality score reflects the small employment base and limited new construction, while the high affordability score dominates the overall assessment. For potential buyers, the current environment offers favorable entry points relative to income levels, though the recent price correction and high interest rates introduce near-term volatility. Renter dynamics remain favorable, with a price-to-rent ratio of 16.0, indicating that purchasing remains a financially efficient alternative to long-term renting given the low debt service burden.
