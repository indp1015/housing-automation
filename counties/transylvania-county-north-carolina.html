📝 Local Market Analysis

Transylvania County operates as a highly stressed buyer’s market, characterized by extreme affordability constraints despite strong economic vitality. The price-to-income ratio stands at 5.64, nearly double the national average of 3.20, while the price-to-rent ratio of 32.2 significantly exceeds the national benchmark of 18.7. These metrics indicate that housing costs have decoupled from local earning power, creating a structural barrier for prospective homeowners. The composite assessment reflects this tension, with vitality scoring high at 77.1 due to robust inbound migration, but affordability dragging the overall stability to a moderate 47.9. The market has experienced a dramatic upward trajectory, with median home values rising from $202,100 in 2017 to $373,600 in 2024. This period saw peak annual growth of 19.3% in 2022, moderating to 11.2% in 2024. Recent FHFA data confirms continued momentum, showing a 6.42% year-over-year increase, which outpaces the North Carolina state average of 0.38% by over six percentage points. This rapid appreciation is driven by significant net migration, with 1,370 new filers moving into the county in 2021-2022, bringing an average income of $85,048. However, local supply remains severely constrained, with only 176 new single-family units authorized in 2025 and zero multi-family developments, failing to meet the demand surge. Local economic fundamentals present a mixed picture. While total employment stands at 8,312 jobs with an average weekly wage of $976, the median household income of $66,184 is slightly below the national average. The current 30-year mortgage rate of 7.40% further exacerbates affordability challenges, making entry-level purchasing difficult for local residents. The divergence between high vitality and low affordability suggests that while the community is attracting external wealth, internal purchasing power is lagging. For potential buyers, the market offers strong asset appreciation potential but requires substantial capital reserves. Renters face a relatively stable burden at 17.5% of income, near the national average, but face limited long-term equity opportunities due to the high price-to-rent ratio. The data indicates a market where external demand outstrips local income growth, resulting in sustained price pressure despite slowing annual growth rates.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Transylvania County is 58.8% above the North Carolina state average and 64.1% above the U.S. national average. The median rent of $967/month is 0.9% below the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 32.2, Transylvania County is a strongly rent-favored market. Home prices are high relative to rents, meaning renting is typically far cheaper than owning in the near term.

Transylvania County has an owner-occupancy rate of 74.7%, compared to 71.8% in North Carolina and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more homeowner-heavy market than the U.S. average.
