📝 Local Market Analysis

Worcester County operates as a distinctly renter-favorable market, characterized by a price-to-rent ratio of 24.8, significantly exceeding the national average of 18.7. This metric indicates that purchasing equity is comparatively expensive relative to leasing costs, pushing the affordability axis to a low 24.4 out of 100. The county’s median home value of $423,700 stands 86.1% above the national mean, while the median household income of $95,939 is only 41.1% higher than the national average. Consequently, the price-to-income ratio reaches 4.42, well above the national benchmark of 3.20, creating a substantial barrier for first-time buyers despite the region’s robust economic output. Trend analysis reveals a period of aggressive appreciation that has recently moderated. Home values surged 15.3% year-over-year in 2022 before settling to 8.4% in 2024, a trajectory that has outpaced rent growth, as evidenced by the widening gap between home values and rental costs. This divergence suggests that capital gains for current owners are strong, contributing to a momentum score of 60.1, the highest component of the composite assessment. However, this rapid escalation strains stability, which sits at 38.6, as the market adjusts to post-pandemic volatility. The recent FHFA HPI indicates a 3.36% year-over-year increase, slightly lagging the state’s 3.63% growth, suggesting that while the county remains dynamic, its explosive growth phase may be transitioning into a more normalized state-level trajectory. The local economic foundation supports continued demand, with 301,530 total jobs and an average weekly wage of $1,410. Net migration of 4,610 filers and a high owner-occupancy rate of 65.8% underscore persistent demand pressure. Housing supply, with 1,690 new units authorized in 2025, including 914 multi-family units, aims to alleviate pressure but remains insufficient to offset the 7.40% national mortgage rate environment. The vitality score of 40.1 reflects a market that is economically active yet constrained by affordability. For prospective buyers, the high entry cost and elevated interest rates present significant hurdles, whereas renters face a 17.8% rent burden, marginally above the national average. The data indicates a market where high incomes are necessary to navigate the premium pricing structure, with the gap between state and national averages highlighting Worcester’s unique position as a high-cost area within a more moderate state context.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Worcester County is 33.2% below the Massachusetts state average and 86.1% above the U.S. national average. The median rent of $1,426/month is 11.7% below the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 24.8, renting is generally more cost-effective than buying in the short to medium term. The market favors buyers only for long-term holds.

Worcester County has an owner-occupancy rate of 65.8%, compared to 67.2% in Massachusetts and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more renter-heavy market than the U.S. average.
