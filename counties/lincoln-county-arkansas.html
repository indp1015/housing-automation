📝 Local Market Notes

Lincoln County, Arkansas, operates as a distinct buyer’s market, characterized by a median home value of $108,000 and a price-to-income ratio of 2.09. This ratio is significantly lower than the national average of 3.20, indicating that local housing costs are substantially more accessible relative to the median household income of $51,778. The market structure further supports ownership, with an owner-occupancy rate of 76.1% and a price-to-rent ratio of 12.1, which is well below the national benchmark of 18.7. These metrics suggest that purchasing remains a more financially efficient strategy than renting for long-term residents, as the cost of entry is low relative to both income levels and rental expenses. Recent market dynamics reveal a pattern of high volatility followed by stabilization. Home values surged from $66,100 in 2017 to a peak of $112,500 in 2022, driven by a 22.5% year-over-year increase. However, the market corrected in 2023 with a 5.7% decline before stabilizing at $108,000 in 2024, reflecting a modest 1.8% recovery. This trajectory contrasts with the steady rise in median gross rent, which stands at $744 per month. The divergence between the sharp peak-and-correction cycle in home values and the consistent upward pressure on rents indicates that while asset prices have normalized, the underlying cost of housing services continues to climb. This suggests that the initial rapid appreciation was likely driven by temporary demand shocks rather than sustained structural economic growth. Regional context highlights Lincoln County’s affordability advantage, with home values sitting 52.6% below the national average and 23.7% below the Arkansas state average. The local economy, supported by 1,381 total jobs and an average weekly wage of $887, provides a stable but modest income base. Migration data from 2021-2022 shows a net inflow of 116 filers, with incoming residents earning an average of $54,657, slightly below the outflow average of $55,334. This modest net migration, combined with a low rent burden of 17.2%, suggests a balanced demand environment. For potential buyers, the current 7.40% mortgage rate remains a significant hurdle, but the low price-to-income ratio mitigates this cost. The market’s recent momentum, marked by the transition from double-digit growth to single-digit stability, signals a maturing market where affordability remains the primary driver of activity rather than speculative investment.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Lincoln County is 23.7% below the Arkansas state average and 52.6% below the U.S. national average. The median rent of $744/month is 3.9% below the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 12.1, buying a home in Lincoln County is generally more favorable than renting for those planning to stay long-term. A ratio below 15 typically signals that purchasing is financially advantageous over a multi-year horizon.

Lincoln County has an owner-occupancy rate of 76.1%, compared to 71.1% in Arkansas and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more homeowner-heavy market than the U.S. average.
