📝 Editor's Insight

Lincoln County, Arkansas, functions as a distinct buyer’s market, evidenced by a price-to-rent ratio of 13.2 compared to the national average of 18.0. This metric indicates that purchasing equity is significantly more cost-effective than long-term renting. The five-year trend reveals robust appreciation, with home values growing at an 8.6% CAGR, substantially outpacing the 5.9% CAGR for rents. This divergence suggests that asset values are rising faster than the cost of occupancy, favoring capital accumulation for owners. Affordability remains strong, with a price-to-income ratio of 2.05, well below the national benchmark of 3.09. Similarly, the rent burden sits at 15.6%, under the national average of 17.2%. A critical insight is that despite home values being 50.1% below the national mean, the low income base of $51,667 maintains relative affordability. However, the rapid 8.6% appreciation rate implies that entry-level pricing is eroding quickly, making the current sub-$106,000 median a narrowing window for first-time buyers seeking leverage against rising regional costs.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Lincoln County is 23.7% below the Arkansas state average and 52.6% below the U.S. national average. The median rent of $744/month is 3.9% below the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 12.1, buying a home in Lincoln County is generally more favorable than renting for those planning to stay long-term. A ratio below 15 typically signals that purchasing is financially advantageous over a multi-year horizon.

Lincoln County has an owner-occupancy rate of 76.1%, compared to 71.1% in Arkansas and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more homeowner-heavy market than the U.S. average.
