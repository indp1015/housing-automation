📝 Local Market Analysis

Floyd County, Kentucky, operates as a distinctively affordable buyer’s market, characterized by a median home value of $98,700 and a price-to-income ratio of 2.39, significantly below the national average of 3.20. The affordability profile is further reinforced by a price-to-rent ratio of 12.2, well under the national benchmark of 18.7, suggesting that homeownership remains a more financially accessible option than long-term renting for local residents. Despite this affordability, the market exhibits a complex trend history where median home values surged from $72,900 in 2017 to $98,700 in 2024, representing a 35.4% cumulative increase. This appreciation was driven by a peak year-over-year gain of 13.7% in 2022, which has since moderated to 5.8% in 2024. However, recent momentum signals a shift, as the FHFA HPI indicates a 1.15% year-over-year decline in 2025, a performance that underperforms the Kentucky state average of +4.26% by 5.41 percentage points. This divergence between the long-term appreciation trend and the immediate quarterly cooling suggests a market in transition, potentially correcting after the rapid post-pandemic spike. The economic fundamentals supporting this market are constrained by limited vitality and supply. With only two new housing units authorized in 2025, inventory remains tight, yet the local economy supports just 8,762 jobs with an average weekly wage of $957. This employment base is modest, and the median household income of $41,279 trails the national average by 39.3%. Migration data from 2021-2022 shows a net inflow of 352 filers, indicating slight population growth, but the average income of incoming residents ($50,137) is only marginally higher than those leaving ($50,355), suggesting limited income-driven demand shocks. The high owner-occupancy rate of 72.2% further stabilizes the market, reducing rental pressure and supporting the low rent burden of 19.5% compared to the national 17.2%. The composite assessment of this market is driven primarily by strong affordability metrics, which are offset by low vitality and emerging stability concerns. The significant gap between the county’s home values, which are 38.6% below the state average, and the national mortgage rate of 7.40% creates a challenging financing environment despite low asset prices. The recent HPI decline highlights a disconnect from broader state trends, implying that local demand may be reaching a ceiling relative to income levels. For potential buyers, the current environment offers entry points at historically low price-to-income ratios, but the recent price correction and minimal new construction suggest that immediate equity growth is uncertain. Renters benefit from relatively low costs, though the high owner-occupancy rate limits rental supply, potentially constraining future rent increases. The market’s trajectory is thus defined by a balance between exceptional affordability and a cautious, cooling momentum that diverges from both state and national appreciation patterns.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Floyd County is 38.6% below the Kentucky state average and 56.7% below the U.S. national average. The median rent of $672/month is 15.3% below the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 12.2, buying a home in Floyd County is generally more favorable than renting for those planning to stay long-term. A ratio below 15 typically signals that purchasing is financially advantageous over a multi-year horizon.

Floyd County has an owner-occupancy rate of 72.2%, compared to 73.4% in Kentucky and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more renter-heavy market than the U.S. average.
