📝 Editor's Insight

Union County, Georgia, exhibits characteristics of a renter’s market, primarily driven by extreme valuation metrics relative to local income levels. The price-to-rent ratio stands at 31.7, significantly exceeding the national average of 18.7, indicating that purchasing a home is substantially more expensive on a monthly basis than renting. This structural imbalance is compounded by a price-to-income ratio of 4.65, which is 45% higher than the national benchmark of 3.20. Consequently, while the owner occupancy rate remains high at 84.4%, the financial barrier to entry for new buyers is considerable, pushing potential homeowners toward the rental sector where the rent burden is only 14.7% of income, well below the national average of 17.2%. The multi-year trend reveals a sharp acceleration in home values that has outpaced both income growth and rent increases. After modest fluctuations between 2017 and 2020, median home values surged by 16.8% in 2022 and another 12.8% in 2024, reaching $307,400. This trajectory contrasts with the relatively stable median gross rent of $808, creating a significant divergence where asset values are rising faster than rental costs. Recent FHFA HPI data confirms this momentum, showing a 5.94% year-over-year increase in 2025, which outperforms the Georgia state average of 1.83% by 4.11 percentage points. This suggests that Union County is experiencing localized demand pressures that are distinct from broader state trends, likely driven by specific regional economic factors or migration patterns. In terms of regional context, Union County’s median home value is 55.3% higher than the Georgia state average and 35.0% above the national mean, despite median household income being slightly below the national average at $66,176. This disparity highlights a premium placed on housing in this specific county compared to its peers. For prospective buyers, the data indicates that affordability is constrained by high entry costs relative to local earnings, whereas renters benefit from a low cost-of-living burden. The sustained outperformance of the county’s housing index relative to the state suggests that the current valuation premium is robust, though the widening gap between home prices and rents may continue to limit buyer liquidity in the near term.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 31.7, Union County is a strongly rent-favored market. Home prices are high relative to rents, meaning renting is typically far cheaper than owning in the near term.

Union County has an owner-occupancy rate of 84.4%, compared to 70.6% in Georgia and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more homeowner-heavy market than the U.S. average.
