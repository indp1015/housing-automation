📝 Local Market Analysis

Decatur County, Iowa, functions as a distinctively affordable buyer’s market, characterized by a median home value of $113,900, which sits 50.0% below the national average and 33.4% under the state mean. The local price-to-income ratio of 1.93 is substantially lower than the national benchmark of 3.20, indicating that housing costs remain manageable relative to the median household income of $59,044. Furthermore, the rent burden stands at 12.4% of income, well below the national average of 17.2%, reinforcing the county’s status as a low-cost-of-living area. While the 30-year fixed mortgage rate of 7.40% increases monthly payment obligations, the low entry price point mitigates this impact compared to higher-priced national markets. The market exhibits a pronounced upward trajectory in home values, with year-over-year growth accelerating from 0.7% in 2018 to a peak of 12.9% in 2023, before moderating to 4.0% in 2024. This appreciation pattern contrasts with the relatively stable median gross rent of $611, resulting in a price-to-rent ratio of 15.5 that remains below the national average of 18.7. This divergence suggests that ownership equity is accumulating faster than rental costs, a trend likely driven by limited new construction, as only one single-family unit was authorized in 2025. The scarcity of new supply, combined with a net migration inflow of 214 filers between 2021 and 2022, has likely tightened the existing inventory, pushing values higher despite the small population base of 7,759. Economic fundamentals provide a mixed backdrop for this appreciation. The local economy supports 1,770 jobs with an average weekly wage of $805, which translates to an annualized income that aligns with the median household data. However, the owner-occupancy rate of 69.7% leaves a significant rental sector, though the low rent burden suggests ample affordability for tenants. The recent moderation in price growth from 12.9% to 4.0% indicates a potential stabilization phase following the rapid 2022-2023 surge. For stakeholders, the primary implication is that while entry costs are low, the rapid value increase over the past five years—rising from $76,300 to $113,900—has eroded some of the historical discount to national averages. The convergence of low new supply and positive net migration continues to support price stability, making ownership a financially viable strategy for those with local employment ties, while renters benefit from minimal cost pressure relative to their incomes.
