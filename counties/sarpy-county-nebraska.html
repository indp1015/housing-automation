📝 Local Market Analysis

Sarpy County, Nebraska, operates as a predominantly buyer’s market, characterized by strong economic vitality that outpaces immediate housing affordability constraints. The price-to-income ratio of 3.04 sits below the national average of 3.20, while the rent burden stands at 15.5%, significantly lower than the 17.2% national benchmark. Although the price-to-rent ratio of 19.6 exceeds the national average of 18.7, suggesting ownership is relatively more expensive than renting, the robust median household income of $103,321 provides substantial purchasing power. This financial resilience supports a market where demand is driven by high-income migration rather than speculative investment, evidenced by a net inflow of 5,825 filers in 2021-2022 with average incomes exceeding $95,000. The multi-year trend reveals a period of rapid appreciation that is now stabilizing. Home values surged 15.9% in 2022 before moderating to 9.2% in 2024, yet the recent FHFA HPI indicates a further deceleration to 3.08% year-over-year in 2025. This slowdown in price growth contrasts with the county’s continued outperformance of the state, where home values are 90.8% above the state average. A notable divergence exists between the high vitality score of 83.5 and the lower stability and momentum scores, reflecting a market that is economically strong but experiencing cooling price momentum. The 7.40% national mortgage rate further suppresses transaction volume, contributing to the challenging stability metric despite high employment figures of 72,470 jobs and an average weekly wage of $1,146. Housing supply remains a critical factor, with 1,594 new units authorized in 2025, including 1,221 single-family homes, which helps mitigate potential shortages. However, the significant gap between the county’s home values, which are 37.9% above the national average, and its income levels, 51.9% above the national average, suggests that while affordability is better than the national norm, it is not inexpensive. Potential buyers benefit from a market where income growth has historically kept pace with price increases, whereas renters face a market where rent growth is lagging behind price appreciation. The combination of high owner occupancy at 70.4% and strong local employment creates a stable foundation, but the decelerating price momentum indicates a shift from rapid gains to a more normalized growth trajectory. This environment favors long-term holders and high-income buyers who can leverage local wage premiums to navigate the current rate environment.
