📝 Local Market Analysis

Itawamba County, Mississippi, functions as a distinctly buyer’s market, characterized by a price-to-income ratio of 2.56, which is significantly lower than the national average of 3.20. This affordability advantage is reinforced by a price-to-rent ratio of 14.6, well below the national benchmark of 18.7, indicating that purchasing offers superior long-term value relative to renting. The rent burden stands at 17.5% of median household income, nearly identical to the national average of 17.2%, suggesting that rental costs are not disproportionately high compared to local earnings. Consequently, the market dynamics favor homeownership, with a high owner-occupancy rate of 79.0% reflecting the structural preference for equity ownership over leasing. The recent trajectory of home values reveals a period of accelerated appreciation, with median home values rising from $87,000 in 2017 to $142,400 in 2024. This represents a cumulative increase of approximately 63.7% over seven years, with particularly sharp gains of 15.0% in 2022 and 16.3% in 2024. However, the FHFA House Price Index indicates a recent deceleration, with a year-over-year change of only +0.89% in 2025, slightly outpacing the state’s +0.60% growth. This divergence between the long-term Census trend and the short-term FHFA momentum suggests that the initial rapid appreciation phase is stabilizing. The local economic context supports this stabilization, as total employment stands at 4,809 jobs with an average weekly wage of $858, which is modest relative to the rising housing costs. Furthermore, housing supply remains extremely constrained, with only nine new single-family units authorized in 2025 and zero multi-family units, creating a low-inventory environment that likely contributed to the earlier price spikes. Migration data shows a net inflow of 373 filers between 2021 and 2022, with incoming households averaging an income of $58,604, slightly higher than the median household income of $55,546. This demographic shift introduces a modestly higher-income cohort, potentially supporting price stability despite the national 30-year fixed mortgage rate of 7.40%, which limits borrowing capacity. The market’s vitality is constrained by the small economic base and limited new construction, resulting in a composite assessment that highlights strong affordability and stability but lower vitality. The primary drivers of the market’s current standing are its exceptional price-to-income metrics and the lag in new supply, which keeps the market accessible for local buyers while limiting rapid expansion. Potential buyers should note that while entry prices remain below national averages, the recent surge in values has narrowed the gap with state averages, where Itawamba County now sits 4.8% above the state median.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Itawamba County is 4.8% above the Mississippi state average and 37.5% below the U.S. national average. The median rent of $811/month is 0.9% above the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 14.6, buying a home in Itawamba County is generally more favorable than renting for those planning to stay long-term. A ratio below 15 typically signals that purchasing is financially advantageous over a multi-year horizon.

Itawamba County has an owner-occupancy rate of 79.0%, compared to 72.4% in Mississippi and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more homeowner-heavy market than the U.S. average.
