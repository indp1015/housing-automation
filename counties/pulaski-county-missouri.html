📝 Local Market Analysis

Pulaski County, Missouri, presents a complex housing landscape characterized by strong momentum but constrained affordability. The market leans toward a buyer’s environment for those with sufficient income, driven by a price-to-income ratio of 2.94, which remains below the national average of 3.20. However, the rent burden stands at 20.7% of income, exceeding the national benchmark of 17.2%, suggesting that renting is relatively expensive for local households. This dynamic is reinforced by a price-to-rent ratio of 14.2, significantly lower than the national average of 18.7, indicating that purchasing is comparatively more cost-effective than renting. The recent FHFA HPI data reveals a robust 12.18% year-over-year increase, outpacing the state’s 3.71% growth by 8.47 percentage points, signaling intense local demand relative to the broader Missouri market. The multi-year trend illustrates a period of rapid appreciation following a stagnation phase from 2020 to 2021. Home values surged from $160,000 in 2021 to $189,800 in 2024, with a peak year-over-year gain of 10.9% in 2022. This growth has moderated to 0.1% in the latest Census data, yet the FHFA index suggests continued underlying strength. A notable divergence exists between the stable Census estimates and the higher FHFA momentum, potentially reflecting recent transaction-level pricing pressures. Economically, the county supports 7,971 jobs with an average weekly wage of $775, while new construction remains limited to 99 single-family units authorized in 2025, creating a supply-constrained environment. Net migration of +2,779 filers further intensifies demand, with incoming households averaging $55,651 in income. Affordability remains the primary challenge, reflected in the low stability and affordability components of the overall market assessment. While the momentum axis scores highly due to the 12.18% HPI growth, the vitality and stability metrics are tempered by the high rent burden and modest employment base. Home values are 8.0% above the state average but 16.6% below the national mean, positioning the county as a regional outlier in growth but a national laggard in absolute price levels. For potential participants, the data suggests that while entry prices are accessible relative to national norms, the rapid appreciation rate and high rental costs require careful financial planning. The 7.40% national mortgage rate further impacts borrowing costs, making the lower price-to-rent ratio a critical factor for those weighing purchase against rental options in this high-growth, supply-tight market.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Pulaski County is 8.0% above the Missouri state average and 16.6% below the U.S. national average. The median rent of $1,112/month is 42.2% above the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 14.2, buying a home in Pulaski County is generally more favorable than renting for those planning to stay long-term. A ratio below 15 typically signals that purchasing is financially advantageous over a multi-year horizon.

Pulaski County has an owner-occupancy rate of 61.2%, compared to 73.7% in Missouri and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more renter-heavy market than the U.S. average.
