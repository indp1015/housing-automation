📝 Local Market Analysis

Russell County, Kansas, functions as a distinct buyer’s market, characterized by extreme affordability relative to national benchmarks. With a median home value of $119,000, the price-to-income ratio stands at 1.91, significantly below the national average of 3.20. This affordability is reinforced by a rent burden of 15.5%, which is lower than the national average of 17.2%. The price-to-rent ratio of 12.3, compared to the national average of 18.7, further indicates that purchasing is financially advantageous over renting. Although the local economy supports only 1,951 jobs with an average weekly wage of $929, the low cost of living ensures that housing remains accessible. The 75.1% owner-occupancy rate suggests a stable residential base, while the 2025 authorization of 30 new single-family units indicates a modest but consistent supply of new housing stock. Recent market dynamics reveal a sharp acceleration in home values, with a 11.8% year-over-year increase in 2024, following a period of moderate growth between 2019 and 2021. This surge contrasts with the broader trend signal indicating that rents are rising faster than home values, a pattern that may reflect shifting demand dynamics. The county’s median home value remains 14.3% below the Kansas state average and 47.7% below the national average, positioning it as a significantly more affordable option within the region. Migration data shows a net gain of 177 filers between 2021 and 2022, with inflows averaging $58,322 in income, suggesting a steady influx of residents who may be drawn by the low entry costs. However, the current 30-year fixed mortgage rate of 7.40% introduces a higher carrying cost for new buyers, potentially tempering the immediate appeal of the low median price. The economic context is shaped by limited employment opportunities, with 247 establishments supporting the local workforce. The divergence between the rapid 2024 price appreciation and the slower historical growth rates suggests a potential correction or catch-up phase in the market. For potential buyers, the low price-to-income ratio offers substantial equity potential, but the rising mortgage rates necessitate careful budgeting. Renters face a more complex situation, as the rising rent trend, despite a low overall burden, indicates increasing costs for those opting not to purchase. The data underscores a market where affordability remains the primary driver, but recent price momentum requires attention to long-term financial planning. The combination of low supply, stable migration, and competitive pricing creates a niche market that appeals to cost-conscious buyers seeking high value relative to income.
