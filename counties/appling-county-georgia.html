📝 Local Market Analysis

Appling County, Georgia, functions as a distinctively affordable buyer’s market, characterized by a price-to-income ratio of 1.64, less than half the national average of 3.20. With a median home value of $76,700 and a median household income of $46,651, the cost of entry is significantly lower than both the state and national benchmarks, where home values sit 61.2% and 66.3% above local levels, respectively. The price-to-rent ratio of 8.6, compared to the national norm of 18.7, further underscores the financial advantage of ownership over renting, despite a rent burden of 19.0% that slightly exceeds the national average of 17.2%. This structural affordability is the primary driver of the market’s high viability for potential homeowners, even as other metrics suggest underlying fragility. The market exhibits significant volatility and recent downward pressure, moving from a peak of $87,100 in 2020 to $76,700 in 2024. This decline is corroborated by the FHFA HPI, which indicates a 9.21% year-over-year drop, sharply underperforming the Georgia state average of +1.83% by 11.04 percentage points. This divergence highlights a stressed stability profile, where local price corrections are outpacing broader state trends. A notable anomaly is the divergence between falling home values and rising rents, suggesting that while asset prices retreat, immediate housing costs for tenants remain elevated. This dynamic is exacerbated by a 7.32% national mortgage rate, which limits purchasing power despite low absolute prices. Economic fundamentals present a mixed picture that constrains long-term vitality. The local economy supports only 6,433 jobs with an average weekly wage of $1,302, limiting the depth of the buyer pool. However, migration data from 2021-2022 shows a net inflow of 294 filers, with incoming residents earning an average of $58,414, potentially providing a modest income boost to the local tax base. Housing supply remains constrained, with only 21 new units authorized in 2025, including 17 single-family homes. This limited new construction, combined with a 72.5% owner-occupancy rate, suggests a tight inventory that may eventually stabilize prices. The overall market assessment reflects high affordability but low stability and momentum, driven by the recent price correction and modest economic base. For prospective buyers, the current environment offers exceptional value relative to income, though the declining price trend and high interest rates introduce significant financial risk for those seeking immediate equity growth.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Appling County is 61.2% below the Georgia state average and 66.3% below the U.S. national average. The median rent of $739/month is 23.4% below the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 8.6, buying a home in Appling County is generally more favorable than renting for those planning to stay long-term. A ratio below 15 typically signals that purchasing is financially advantageous over a multi-year horizon.

Appling County has an owner-occupancy rate of 72.5%, compared to 70.6% in Georgia and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more renter-heavy market than the U.S. average.
