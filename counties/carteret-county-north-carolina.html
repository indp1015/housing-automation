📝 Local Market Analysis

Carteret County, North Carolina, functions as a distinctively unbalanced market where strong momentum and vitality are severely constrained by poor affordability. The composite assessment reveals a tension between high economic vitality, driven by robust price appreciation, and low affordability, indicated by a price-to-income ratio of 4.61 compared to the national average of 3.20. This disparity classifies the area as challenging for entry-level buyers, as home values sit 46.4% above the national mean while household incomes lag at only 6.4% above the national average. The market is not a traditional buyer’s or renter’s market but rather a high-cost environment where asset appreciation outpaces local income growth, creating a significant barrier to ownership despite a healthy 74.7% owner-occupancy rate. Recent trends underscore a rapid acceleration in asset values. Median home values surged from $197,700 in 2017 to $333,300 in 2024, with a peak year-over-year increase of 16.7% in 2022. Although this growth has moderated to 9.6% in 2024, the FHFA House Price Index confirms continued strength, showing a 9.52% year-over-year gain in 2025. This performance vastly outpaces the state average of 0.38%, highlighting a regional divergence where Carteret County is outperforming North Carolina by 9.14 percentage points. A notable anomaly exists in the price-to-rent ratio of 25.1, significantly higher than the national average of 18.7, suggesting that rental costs are not keeping pace with soaring home values. This divergence implies that while owning is increasingly expensive, renting remains relatively less burdensome, with a rent burden of 18.4% of income, only slightly above the national norm of 17.2%. The local economic context provides limited support for these price levels. With an average weekly wage of $876 and total employment of 20,663 jobs, the labor market does not fully justify the premium pricing. Housing supply remains tight, with only 471 new units authorized in 2025, predominantly single-family homes, which fails to meet the demand signaled by a net migration inflow of 2,654 filers in 2021-2022. These incoming residents, with an average income of $92,007, exceed the local median, injecting higher purchasing power into the market. However, the current 30-year fixed mortgage rate of 7.40% further exacerbates affordability constraints. Consequently, the market’s stability is undermined by the gap between rapid value appreciation and stagnant local wages, making the high momentum and vitality scores unsustainable without significant income growth or increased housing supply.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Carteret County is 41.6% above the North Carolina state average and 46.4% above the U.S. national average. The median rent of $1,108/month is 13.5% above the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 25.1, Carteret County is a strongly rent-favored market. Home prices are high relative to rents, meaning renting is typically far cheaper than owning in the near term.

Carteret County has an owner-occupancy rate of 74.7%, compared to 71.8% in North Carolina and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more homeowner-heavy market than the U.S. average.
