📝 Local Market Analysis

Elliott County, Kentucky, presents a distinct buyer’s market characterized by extreme affordability but weak local economic vitality. With a median home value of $123,500 and a median household income of $45,776, the price-to-income ratio stands at 2.70, significantly below the national average of 3.20. This affordability is further underscored by a rent burden of 11.3%, well under the national benchmark of 17.2%. However, the market’s structural weakness is evident in the price-to-rent ratio of 23.8, which exceeds the national average of 18.7, suggesting that purchasing is financially more advantageous than renting despite the low absolute costs. The high owner-occupancy rate of 81.7% indicates a stable, albeit stagnant, housing stock where long-term residents dominate the landscape. Recent trends reveal a sharp acceleration in property values, with a 26.8% year-over-year increase in 2024 following a 19.3% surge in 2022. This rapid appreciation contrasts with the modest median gross rent of $432, creating a notable divergence where home values are rising much faster than rental costs. This pattern suggests a potential correction in valuation relative to local income levels, as the 2020 dip of 7.4% and subsequent recovery indicate volatility rather than steady growth. The local economy offers limited support for this price surge, with only 318 total jobs and an average weekly wage of $855, which translates to an annualized wage of approximately $44,460, barely exceeding the median household income. Consequently, the market’s momentum is fragile, driven more by external factors or data anomalies than by robust internal economic expansion. Regional context further isolates Elliott County from broader market trends, as home values sit 45.8% below the national average and 23.2% below the Kentucky state average. Migration data shows a net gain of 67 filers, with inflow incomes averaging $43,462, slightly lower than outflow incomes of $44,849, indicating that new residents are not bringing significant financial capital to sustain high price growth. The current 7.40% mortgage rate adds pressure to monthly payments, yet the low entry price keeps the barrier to ownership low. For prospective buyers, the primary risk lies in the disconnect between soaring asset values and stagnant local wages, while renters benefit from minimal cost burdens. The overall market assessment reflects high affordability scores offset by low vitality and stability metrics, highlighting a community where housing is accessible but economic resilience remains limited.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Elliott County is 23.2% below the Kentucky state average and 45.8% below the U.S. national average. The median rent of $432/month is 45.5% below the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 23.8, renting is generally more cost-effective than buying in the short to medium term. The market favors buyers only for long-term holds.

Elliott County has an owner-occupancy rate of 81.7%, compared to 73.4% in Kentucky and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more homeowner-heavy market than the U.S. average.
