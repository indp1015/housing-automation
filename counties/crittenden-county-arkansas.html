📝 Editor's Insight

Crittenden County exhibits a strong renter’s market, evidenced by a price-to-rent ratio of 14.6, well below the national average of 18.0. This metric indicates that renting is significantly more cost-effective than purchasing relative to local income levels. The five-year trend reveals robust appreciation, with home values growing at a 7.9% CAGR compared to a 4.8% CAGR for rents. This divergence suggests that existing homeowners have accumulated substantial equity, while new entrants face a widening gap between asset values and rental costs. Affordability remains favorable for buyers, as the price-to-income ratio of 2.86 sits below the national benchmark of 3.09. However, renters face a 19.5% income burden, exceeding the national average of 17.2%. A critical insight is that despite the county’s home values being 27% below the national mean, they are 17.5% above the Arkansas state average. This premium indicates localized demand drivers, likely tied to regional economic stability, making Crittenden an outlier within its state context.
