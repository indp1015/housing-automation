📝 Local Market Analysis

Menominee County, Michigan, presents a balanced market structure, characterized by a price-to-rent ratio of 18.3, which aligns closely with the national average of 18.7. This parity suggests that neither buying nor renting holds a significant financial advantage based purely on asset valuation metrics. The market’s stability is further underscored by a high owner-occupancy rate of 80.7% and a median home value of $151,800, which sits 33.3% below the national average. While the area outperforms the state in recent price appreciation, with a FHFA Home Price Index increase of 4.31% compared to the state’s 3.68%, the fundamental affordability remains robust. The price-to-income ratio of 2.69 is notably lower than the national benchmark of 3.20, and the rent burden of 14.7% is below the national average of 17.2%, indicating that housing costs remain manageable relative to local earning power. The multi-year trend reveals a period of accelerated appreciation, with home values rising from $95,900 in 2017 to $151,800 in 2024, representing a cumulative gain of approximately 58%. The peak year-over-year growth of 14.1% in 2022 has since moderated to 6.4% in 2024, suggesting a normalization of price increases. A notable divergence exists between the strong momentum in home values and the relatively stable rental market, where the median gross rent stands at $692. This price-to-rent dynamic, combined with the fact that home values are rising faster than rents, indicates that the ownership segment is currently absorbing more of the market’s growth. However, the local economic base remains constrained, with only 4,996 total jobs and an average weekly wage of $986, which limits the depth of the buyer pool. Supply constraints are evident in the housing sector, with only 28 new single-family units authorized in 2025 and zero multi-family permits, creating a tight inventory environment. Despite this, net migration of 484 filers between 2021 and 2022 suggests a modest population influx, with inflow and outflow incomes nearly identical at approximately $59,800. The current 7.40% mortgage rate poses a challenge for affordability, yet the low entry price point mitigates this impact. Consequently, the market’s vitality is modest, reflected in the low employment base and limited new construction, while stability and affordability remain the primary strengths. Potential buyers benefit from the low price-to-income ratio, whereas renters face a stable but non-depreciating asset class. The overall market profile is driven by strong affordability and stability, offsetting lower vitality and moderate momentum, resulting in a balanced environment for housing participants.
