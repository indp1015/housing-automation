📝 Local Market Analysis

Blount County exhibits characteristics of a balanced market with a slight tilt toward affordability, driven by a price-to-income ratio of 2.73, which sits below the national average of 3.20. The rent burden of 14.5% is also lower than the 17.2% national benchmark, indicating that both ownership and rental costs remain manageable relative to local incomes. However, the price-to-rent ratio of 18.8 aligns almost perfectly with the national average of 18.7, suggesting that the relative value of buying versus renting is standard, with no significant arbitrage opportunity for either side. This equilibrium is supported by a high owner-occupancy rate of 81.0%, reflecting deep local attachment to homeownership. The multi-year trend reveals a period of rapid appreciation followed by stabilization. Home values surged 15.7% in 2022, nearly doubling the typical annual growth rate, before moderating to 3.2% in 2024. Recent FHFA data shows a slight 0.68% year-over-year decline in 2025, underperforming the state’s 2.79% growth by 3.47 percentage points. This divergence suggests that while the state market continues to expand, Blount County has entered a cooling phase. The primary driver of this stagnation appears to be limited economic vitality, evidenced by only 6,946 total jobs and an average weekly wage of $913. Furthermore, new housing supply is critically low, with only 20 units authorized in 2025, including just 16 single-family homes. Despite this supply constraint, the lack of price acceleration implies that demand is not strong enough to outpace the limited inventory, likely due to the 7.32% national mortgage rate suppressing buyer purchasing power. Affordability remains the county’s strongest attribute, with home values sitting 23.1% below the national average and 5.6% below the national income average. However, the local economy’s limited scale constrains long-term growth potential. Net migration of +791 filers indicates modest population growth, but the average income of incoming residents ($63,510) is nearly identical to those leaving ($63,821), suggesting that migration is not significantly upgrading the local income base. The composite assessment reflects high stability and affordability but low vitality, as the thin labor market and minimal construction activity limit upward pressure on prices. For potential buyers, the current environment offers entry points below national benchmarks, but the recent price dip and flat wage growth suggest that significant capital appreciation is unlikely in the immediate term without broader economic expansion.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Blount County is 9.7% above the Alabama state average and 23.1% below the U.S. national average. The median rent of $776/month is 5.4% below the state average.

Blount County has an owner-occupancy rate of 81.0%, compared to 73.2% in Alabama and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more homeowner-heavy market than the U.S. average.
