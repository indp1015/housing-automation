📝 Local Market Analysis

Miami County, Indiana, operates as a distinctly buyer’s market, characterized by significant affordability advantages relative to national benchmarks. The median home value of $126,200 sits 44.6% below the national average, resulting in a price-to-income ratio of 2.06 compared to the national standard of 3.20. Similarly, the price-to-rent ratio of 12.5 is substantially lower than the national 18.7, indicating that purchasing offers a more favorable financial entry point than renting. With a rent burden of only 16.4% of income, slightly below the national 17.2%, the market provides robust cost efficiency for both owners and tenants. This affordability profile is the primary driver of the region’s strong stability metrics, offsetting concerns related to local economic vitality. The market has experienced a sharp acceleration in home values, with year-over-year growth spiking to 15.2% in 2022 before moderating to 6.9% in 2024. This surge contrasts with the recent FHFA HPI data for 2025, which shows a mere 0.73% county growth, lagging significantly behind the state’s 3.45% increase. This divergence suggests that the earlier price surge may have been driven by specific transactional factors or data lags, as current momentum appears to be stalling relative to broader Indiana trends. The local economy supports this cautious outlook, with only 6,781 total jobs and an average weekly wage of $952. While net migration added 490 filers between 2021 and 2022, the inflow income of $54,877 is modestly lower than the outflow income of $55,436, suggesting that incoming residents are not significantly boosting purchasing power. Housing supply remains limited, with 84 new units authorized in 2025, split between 48 single-family and 36 multi-family homes. This constrained inventory, combined with a 76.2% owner-occupancy rate, indicates a tight market for available properties despite high affordability. The 7.40% national mortgage rate further complicates entry costs, though the low price point mitigates monthly payment burdens. The composite assessment reflects a market where strong affordability and stability are undermined by weak local economic vitality and slowing price momentum. The low vitality score is driven by the limited employment base and modest wage levels, which constrain long-term demand growth. Consequently, while the county offers exceptional value for entry-level buyers, the stagnating price index and economic indicators suggest that significant appreciation is not currently supported by fundamental local drivers. Potential buyers benefit from low entry costs, but investors should note the underperformance relative to state trends.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Miami County is 34.2% below the Indiana state average and 44.6% below the U.S. national average. The median rent of $838/month is 7.9% below the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 12.5, buying a home in Miami County is generally more favorable than renting for those planning to stay long-term. A ratio below 15 typically signals that purchasing is financially advantageous over a multi-year horizon.

Miami County has an owner-occupancy rate of 76.2%, compared to 76.3% in Indiana and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more homeowner-heavy market than the U.S. average.
