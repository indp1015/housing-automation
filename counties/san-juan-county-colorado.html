📝 Local Market Analysis

San Juan County presents a distinctively stressed rental environment, characterized by a price-to-rent ratio of 36.3, nearly double the national average of 18.7. This extreme disparity, coupled with a price-to-income ratio of 5.56 compared to the national 3.20, indicates that homeownership is significantly out of reach for the median household earning $77,824. While the rent burden sits at 15.3%, slightly below the national 17.2%, the high entry cost for equity creates a bifurcated market where renting is the primary accessible option, yet still expensive relative to local wages. The market is neither a buyer’s nor a traditional renter’s market, but rather a constrained, high-cost environment driven by limited inventory and specific demographic inflows. The multi-year trend reveals volatile appreciation, with home values surging 18.1% in 2023 before moderating to 6.3% in 2024. However, recent FHFA data signals a sharp reversal, showing a 3.16% year-over-year decline in 2025, underperforming the state by 2.55 percentage points. This divergence between long-term appreciation and recent depreciation suggests a correction phase. The local economy, with only 355 total jobs and an average weekly wage of $815, lacks the broad-based employment depth to sustain such high valuations independently. Consequently, the market relies heavily on external factors, evidenced by net migration of +39 filers in 2021-2022, where inflows averaged $72,870 in income, slightly below the outflow average of $74,418. Supply constraints further exacerbate affordability challenges, with only 21 new units authorized in 2025, comprising 13 single-family and 8 multi-family homes. This minimal construction pipeline fails to keep pace with the 724-person population’s needs, particularly given the 52.9% owner-occupancy rate. The low stability metric reflects this volatility, driven by the recent price drop and high sensitivity to mortgage rates, currently at 7.40%. Conversely, the vitality score remains high due to the persistent, albeit slowing, long-term appreciation trend and positive net migration. Potential buyers face a market where recent price corrections may offer entry points, but the fundamental affordability gap remains wide. Renters encounter a market where high price-to-rent ratios indicate that rental costs are disproportionately high relative to the value of the underlying asset, reflecting a scarcity premium rather than pure demand-driven inflation. The combination of low employment base, high interest rates, and limited supply creates a fragile equilibrium, where minor shifts in migration or national rates could trigger significant price adjustments.

Market Analysis

The median home value in San Juan County is 1.2% above the Colorado state average and 89.9% above the U.S. national average. The median rent of $992/month is 23.8% below the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 36.3, San Juan County is a strongly rent-favored market. Home prices are high relative to rents, meaning renting is typically far cheaper than owning in the near term.

San Juan County has an owner-occupancy rate of 52.9%, compared to 71.9% in Colorado and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more renter-heavy market than the U.S. average.
