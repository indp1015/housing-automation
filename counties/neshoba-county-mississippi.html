📝 Local Market Analysis

Neshoba County, Mississippi, exhibits characteristics of a balanced market with a distinct lean toward affordability, driven by a median home value of $100,000 and a median household income of $57,013. The price-to-income ratio stands at 1.75, significantly below the national average of 3.20, indicating strong purchasing power relative to asset prices. Similarly, the price-to-rent ratio of 9.9 is less than half the national benchmark of 18.7, suggesting that homeownership remains a more financially efficient option than renting. The rent burden is 17.7% of income, marginally above the national average of 17.2%, yet remains within a sustainable range for local residents. The multi-year trend reveals a period of accelerating appreciation, with home values rising from $80,400 in 2017 to $100,000 in 2024. This represents a cumulative increase of approximately 24.4% over seven years. The pace of growth peaked at 9.1% in 2022 but has since moderated to 3.5% in 2024, aligning with the current FHFA HPI year-over-year change of 3.34%. Notably, this county is outperforming the broader Mississippi market, which saw only a 0.60% increase, by a differential of 2.74 percentage points. This divergence highlights localized strength despite the state’s slower pace. However, the housing supply is extremely constrained, with only three new single-family units authorized in 2025 and zero multi-family units, a factor that likely sustains price momentum despite the high 7.40% national mortgage rate. Economic fundamentals support this stability, as indicated by a high owner-occupancy rate of 78.1% and positive net migration of 355 filers between 2021 and 2022. The local economy supports 6,683 jobs with an average weekly wage of $1,126, providing a stable base for demand. The market’s primary weakness lies in vitality, reflected by the minimal new construction pipeline and limited employment growth relative to larger metros. Consequently, the overall assessment is driven by high stability and affordability scores, which offset the low vitality metric. For potential buyers, the data suggests a market where entry costs are low relative to income, but supply constraints may limit inventory options. Renter’s are facing a market where the cost of renting is high relative to the cost of buying, reinforcing the long-term financial advantage of ownership in this rural Mississippi county.
