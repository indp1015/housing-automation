📝 Local Market Analysis

Jay County, Indiana, functions as a distinct buyer’s market, characterized by significant affordability advantages relative to national standards. The price-to-income ratio stands at 2.14, substantially below the national average of 3.20, while the price-to-rent ratio of 12.7 is markedly lower than the national benchmark of 18.7. These metrics indicate that purchasing equity is financially more accessible than renting, supported by a median home value of $117,500 against a median household income of $54,969. The rent burden is also favorable, consuming only 16.8% of income compared to the national average of 17.2%. This affordability profile is reinforced by the fact that home values are 48.4% below the national average and 38.8% below the state average, creating a low-barrier entry point for potential homeowners. The market exhibits strong upward momentum, with the FHFA HPI showing a 10.40% year-over-year increase, significantly outpacing the state’s 3.45% growth. This 6.95 percentage point outperformance highlights a robust local demand signal. However, a notable divergence exists between price appreciation and rental costs; while home values have risen consistently since 2017, reaching $117,500 in 2024, the median gross rent remains modest at $770. This suggests that the housing stock is primarily owner-occupied, with a 79.9% owner occupancy rate limiting rental supply dynamics. The rapid price growth, including an 8.5% jump in 2023, contrasts with the relatively stable rental market, indicating that capital gains are currently driven by purchase transactions rather than rental inflation. Economic fundamentals provide a mixed backdrop to this momentum. Local employment is limited, with only 6,115 jobs and an average weekly wage of $961, which may constrain long-term purchasing power despite current affordability. Housing supply is extremely tight, with only 25 new single-family units authorized in 2025 and zero multi-family permits, suggesting that price increases are supply-constrained rather than demand-excessive. Positive net migration of 234 filers indicates modest population growth, but the small inflow does not appear sufficient to sustain the high velocity of price appreciation without broader economic expansion. The high stability and momentum indicators are driven by these low supply levels and strong price appreciation, whereas the low vitality score reflects the limited job base and minimal new construction activity. For prospective buyers, the current environment offers low entry costs, but the rapid price escalation relative to local wage growth warrants caution regarding long-term financial sustainability.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Jay County is 38.8% below the Indiana state average and 48.4% below the U.S. national average. The median rent of $770/month is 15.4% below the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 12.7, buying a home in Jay County is generally more favorable than renting for those planning to stay long-term. A ratio below 15 typically signals that purchasing is financially advantageous over a multi-year horizon.

Jay County has an owner-occupancy rate of 79.9%, compared to 76.3% in Indiana and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more homeowner-heavy market than the U.S. average.
