📝 Editor's Insight

Lincoln County’s housing market currently functions as a renter’s market, evidenced by a price-to-rent ratio of 22.6, significantly exceeding the national average of 18.0. This disparity indicates that rental costs are disproportionately high relative to purchase prices, making ownership more financially viable than renting. Over the past five years, home values surged at a 12.1% CAGR, rising from $147,100 to $232,100, while rents grew merely 2.3% annually. This divergence suggests that the rapid appreciation of equity has outpaced the cost of occupancy, fundamentally altering the investment calculus for local residents. Affordability remains a critical constraint, as the price-to-income ratio stands at 4.28, well above the national benchmark of 3.09. Although the rent burden of 18.9% is only slightly higher than the national 17.2%, the high entry barrier for buyers is more severe. A non-obvious insight is that the median home value is 41.7% below the Colorado state average, implying that while local income levels are 17.8% under the national mean, the area offers substantially lower absolute entry costs than the broader state, potentially attracting remote workers seeking value.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Lincoln County is 42.3% below the Colorado state average and 8.3% above the U.S. national average. The median rent of $868/month is 33.3% below the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 23.7, renting is generally more cost-effective than buying in the short to medium term. The market favors buyers only for long-term holds.

Lincoln County has an owner-occupancy rate of 71.7%, compared to 71.9% in Colorado and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more renter-heavy market than the U.S. average.
