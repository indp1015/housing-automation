📝 Editor's Insight

Arkansas County presents a distinct buyer’s advantage, evidenced by a price-to-rent ratio of 13.5, significantly below the national average of 18.0. This metric indicates that purchasing is substantially more economical than renting relative to local income levels. The five-year trend reveals robust appreciation, with home values compounding at 9.9% annually compared to a 5.4% increase in rents. This divergence suggests that equity accumulation for owners is outpacing the cost of living for tenants. Affordability remains strong locally, as the price-to-income ratio sits at 2.13, well under the national benchmark of 3.09. Furthermore, the rent burden is only 15.8% of median income, slightly better than the national 17.2% average. A critical, non-obvious insight lies in the county’s 67.5% owner-occupancy rate. This high ownership density implies a limited inventory of rental units, which likely suppresses rent growth despite rising home values. Consequently, renters face a tighter supply environment than the moderate rent increases suggest, while buyers benefit from a market where entry costs remain low relative to regional income levels.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Arkansas County is 2.4% below the Arkansas state average and 39.4% below the U.S. national average. The median rent of $843/month is 8.9% above the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 13.7, buying a home in Arkansas County is generally more favorable than renting for those planning to stay long-term. A ratio below 15 typically signals that purchasing is financially advantageous over a multi-year horizon.

Arkansas County has an owner-occupancy rate of 64.6%, compared to 71.1% in Arkansas and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more renter-heavy market than the U.S. average.
