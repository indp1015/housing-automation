📝 Local Market Analysis

Madison County, Arkansas, exhibits characteristics of a balanced market with a slight tilt toward buyers, driven by affordability metrics that align closely with national averages. The price-to-income ratio stands at 3.14, marginally below the national benchmark of 3.20, while the rent burden is 15.4%, significantly lower than the national average of 17.2%. These figures suggest that both purchasing and renting remain financially accessible relative to local earning power. However, the price-to-rent ratio of 20.4 exceeds the national average of 18.7, indicating that ownership costs are becoming relatively more expensive compared to rental alternatives, a divergence that warrants attention for long-term financial planning. The market has experienced substantial appreciation, with median home values rising from $108,500 in 2017 to $178,100 in 2024. This trajectory includes a peak year-over-year increase of 22.1% in 2022, moderating to 12.9% in 2024. While home values have surged, median gross rent has remained comparatively stable, creating a widening gap between asset growth and rental costs. This divergence suggests that the primary driver of market activity is owner-occupancy demand rather than rental pressure. The county’s home values are 21.8% below the national average but 25.9% above the Arkansas state average, positioning Madison County as a premium market within its state despite its smaller economic scale. Economic fundamentals support this sustained growth. Local employment totals 2,957 jobs with an average weekly wage of $906, translating to an annualized wage that supports the median household income of $56,798. Migration data from 2021-2022 shows a net inflow of 517 filers, with incoming residents earning an average of $57,257, slightly above outgoing residents at $56,730, indicating a modest but positive demographic shift. Housing supply remains constrained, with only 22 new single-family units authorized in 2025 and zero multi-family permits, limiting new inventory. With the 30-year fixed mortgage rate at 7.40%, financing costs remain elevated, yet the low price-to-income ratio mitigates some of this pressure. Potential buyers should note that while current affordability is strong, the rapid appreciation trend and limited new construction suggest that entry-level pricing may continue to shift upward, while renters benefit from a low burden relative to income.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Madison County is 25.9% above the Arkansas state average and 21.8% below the U.S. national average. The median rent of $729/month is 5.8% below the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 20.4, renting is generally more cost-effective than buying in the short to medium term. The market favors buyers only for long-term holds.

Madison County has an owner-occupancy rate of 79.5%, compared to 71.1% in Arkansas and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more homeowner-heavy market than the U.S. average.
