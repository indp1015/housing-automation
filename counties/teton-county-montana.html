📝 Local Market Analysis

Teton County, Montana, exhibits characteristics of a challenging market for prospective homebuyers, driven by rapid appreciation that outpaces local income growth. The median home value of $264,000 stands 15.9% above the national average, while the price-to-income ratio of 3.75 exceeds the national benchmark of 3.20. This disparity indicates that housing costs are becoming less accessible relative to the median household income of $70,430. Conversely, the renter segment faces a different dynamic; the rent burden is only 12.4% of income, significantly below the national average of 17.2%, suggesting that rental housing remains relatively affordable compared to ownership. However, the price-to-rent ratio of 30.1 is substantially higher than the national average of 18.7, highlighting a structural divergence where owning is becoming increasingly expensive relative to renting. The market’s momentum is robust, with home values rising 12.1% year-over-year in 2024 and maintaining a 7.21% increase in the latest FHFA HPI data, outperforming the state by 4.62 percentage points. This growth trajectory contrasts with the county’s limited housing supply, which saw only two new units authorized in 2025. Such constrained supply, combined with net migration of 170 filers, likely contributes to sustained price pressure. The local economy supports this demand with an average weekly wage of $905, yet total employment remains modest at 1,338 jobs. The high momentum score reflects this consistent upward trend, while the lower vitality score underscores the small population base of 6,353 residents and limited economic diversification. Affordability remains the primary constraint, as evidenced by the 54.6 affordability metric. Although the county’s home values are 4.7% below the state average, the faster rate of appreciation compared to state trends suggests a narrowing gap. The 75.5% owner-occupancy rate indicates a stable housing stock, but the rapid price increases may deter new entrants. For potential buyers, the combination of a 7.40% mortgage rate and rising home values creates significant financial hurdles. Renters, however, benefit from lower cost burdens, though the high price-to-rent ratio suggests that the long-term cost of ownership is escalating faster than rental alternatives. The data reveals a market where momentum is strong, but affordability is tightening, particularly for those seeking to enter the owner-occupant segment.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Teton County is 4.7% below the Montana state average and 15.9% above the U.S. national average. The median rent of $730/month is 13.3% below the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 30.1, Teton County is a strongly rent-favored market. Home prices are high relative to rents, meaning renting is typically far cheaper than owning in the near term.

Teton County has an owner-occupancy rate of 75.5%, compared to 73.0% in Montana and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more homeowner-heavy market than the U.S. average.
