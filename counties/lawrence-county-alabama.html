📝 Local Market Analysis

Lawrence County, Alabama, presents a distinctively affordable housing environment characterized by strong owner-occupancy and limited new supply. With a median home value of $172,300 and a median household income of $66,071, the price-to-income ratio stands at 2.61, significantly below the national average of 3.20. This affordability is reinforced by a rent burden of 13.7%, well under the national benchmark of 17.2%. Consequently, the market leans heavily toward buyers who prioritize low entry costs over rapid appreciation, supported by an owner-occupancy rate of 81.7%. The price-to-rent ratio of 19.1, slightly above the national average of 18.7, suggests that while renting is cheap, buying remains the more financially efficient long-term strategy for qualified purchasers. The multi-year trend reveals a period of robust price acceleration, with home values increasing from $98,700 in 2017 to $172,300 in 2024, representing a compound annual growth rate of approximately 7.3%. However, this momentum has recently decelerated. The FHFA House Price Index indicates a year-over-year decline of 2.29% in 2025, marking a 5.08 percentage point underperformance relative to the state’s 2.79% growth. This divergence highlights a cooling phase following the 14.3% surge in 2024. Meanwhile, median gross rent remains modest at $753, indicating that rental demand has not kept pace with the earlier housing price spike. The stability of the local economy is reflected in a high stability metric, driven by consistent income levels and low volatility in occupancy rates, despite the recent price correction. Economic fundamentals provide a mixed backdrop for future activity. Local employment totals 4,038 jobs with an average weekly wage of $998, which aligns with the county’s income profile but offers limited upward pressure on housing demand. Housing supply is extremely constrained, with only six new single-family units authorized in 2025 and zero multi-family permits, which historically supports price floors even during corrections. Net migration of +463 filers between 2021 and 2022 demonstrates modest population growth, though the average income of incoming filers ($58,849) is lower than the county median, suggesting an influx of cost-conscious residents rather than high-income earners. The current 30-year mortgage rate of 7.32% further dampens demand, making the low price-to-income ratio the primary driver of market vitality. Ultimately, the market’s strength is rooted in affordability and stability, while its weakness lies in stalling momentum and limited economic expansion.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Lawrence County is 7.9% above the Alabama state average and 24.3% below the U.S. national average. The median rent of $753/month is 8.2% below the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 19.1, the buy-vs-rent decision in Lawrence County is balanced. Whether buying or renting makes more sense depends on your time horizon, down payment capacity, and local market conditions.
