📝 Local Market Analysis

Oregon County, Missouri, presents a balanced market leaning toward affordability, characterized by a price-to-income ratio of 2.63, significantly below the national average of 3.20. The median home value of $113,300 is 50.2% below the national mean and 35.5% below the state average, reflecting a deep discount relative to broader markets. This affordability is reinforced by a price-to-rent ratio of 14.8, well under the national benchmark of 18.7, suggesting that purchasing remains a more financially efficient long-term strategy than renting. However, the market exhibits a notable divergence in momentum. While the ACS data indicates a 3.7% year-over-year decline in median home values in 2024, the FHFA House Price Index reports a 9.35% year-over-year increase for 2025, outperforming the state’s 3.71% growth rate by 5.64 percentage points. This discrepancy suggests that recent transaction data reflects stronger underlying demand or a correction in earlier valuation estimates, creating a complex trend where historical declines are being offset by recent price appreciation. The economic foundation supporting this market is modest but stable. With only 1,994 total jobs and an average weekly wage of $732, local income levels lag 36.7% behind the national average, which constrains the ceiling for price appreciation. Despite this, net migration of 390 filers between 2021 and 2022 indicates sustained demographic inflow, with incoming households earning slightly more than those leaving. This migration pattern, combined with a 74.9% owner-occupancy rate, provides a baseline of stability, yet the low vitality score of 41.1 reflects the limited local economic engine. The rent burden of 17.8% is nearly identical to the national average of 17.2%, indicating that rental costs are not disproportionately straining households relative to their incomes. The recent acceleration in the FHFA index, despite the 7.40% national mortgage rate, signals that local supply constraints or specific transaction dynamics are driving prices upward faster than the state norm. For potential buyers, the current environment offers an entry point at a significant discount to national standards, but the recent 9.35% price surge suggests that the bottom may have passed. Renters face a market where home values are becoming less attractive relative to rising rents, as indicated by the trend signal that rents are rising faster than home values. The overall assessment is driven by high momentum and strong affordability, partially offset by low vitality and moderate stability, resulting in a balanced profile that favors those with local income constraints but offers limited upside potential compared to higher-growth regions.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Oregon County is 35.5% below the Missouri state average and 50.2% below the U.S. national average. The median rent of $639/month is 18.3% below the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 14.8, buying a home in Oregon County is generally more favorable than renting for those planning to stay long-term. A ratio below 15 typically signals that purchasing is financially advantageous over a multi-year horizon.

Oregon County has an owner-occupancy rate of 74.9%, compared to 73.7% in Missouri and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more homeowner-heavy market than the U.S. average.
