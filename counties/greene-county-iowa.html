📝 Local Market Analysis

Greene County, Iowa, presents a distinctly affordable buyer’s market, characterized by a price-to-income ratio of 2.45, significantly lower than the national average of 3.20. With a median home value of $155,400 and a median household income of $63,409, the county offers substantial value relative to national benchmarks. The rent burden stands at 14.8% of income, well below the national average of 17.2%, further reinforcing the affordability profile. However, the market is not without volatility. Home values have surged dramatically, rising from $88,900 in 2017 to $155,400 in 2024, representing a 74.8% cumulative increase over seven years. This rapid appreciation contrasts sharply with the relatively stable median gross rent of $781, creating a notable divergence where asset values are climbing faster than rental costs. The recent momentum is robust yet moderating. The FHFA House Price Index indicates a year-over-year increase of 6.38% in 2025, outperforming the Iowa state average of 2.25% by 4.13 percentage points. While the peak annual growth of 19.9% occurred in 2022, the deceleration to 10.6% in 2024 and the current 6.38% pace suggests a normalization of price appreciation. This trend is supported by positive net migration of 147 filers between 2021 and 2022, with incoming households averaging an income of $70,599, slightly higher than those leaving. Despite this demand, housing supply remains extremely constrained, with only six new single-family units authorized in 2025 and zero multi-family permits, limiting the ability to absorb new demand. The local economic context remains modest, with total employment at 2,438 jobs and an average weekly wage of $1,101. The low vitality score reflects this small economic base and limited new construction. Consequently, while affordability remains a strong driver of market attractiveness, stability is compromised by the recent rapid price escalation and thin supply pipeline. Potential buyers benefit from low entry costs relative to income, but the rapid historical appreciation suggests that price gains may not be linear. Rents remain cheap, but the widening gap between rising home values and flat rents indicates a shifting dynamic where ownership becomes increasingly accessible relative to renting, provided buyers can navigate the modest local employment landscape. The market is fundamentally driven by affordability and momentum, offset by limited economic vitality and supply constraints.
