📝 Local Market Analysis

Suffolk County, Massachusetts, operates as a distinctly renter-favorable market, characterized by extreme affordability constraints that discourage homeownership. The price-to-income ratio stands at 7.38, more than double the national average of 3.20, while the price-to-rent ratio of 27.6 significantly exceeds the national benchmark of 18.7. These metrics indicate that purchasing equity is disproportionately expensive relative to income, whereas renting, though burdening 26.7% of median household income against a national average of 17.2%, remains the more accessible entry point. This structural imbalance is reinforced by a low owner-occupancy rate of 36.5%, suggesting a housing stock heavily weighted toward rental units rather than owner-occupied homes. Price appreciation has shown a clear deceleration trend, moderating from a peak year-over-year increase of 12.9% in 2022 to 3.7% in 2024. The FHFA Home Price Index confirms this stabilization, with recent quarterly data showing a 3.40% annual increase, slightly lagging the state average of 3.63%. This cooling momentum contrasts with the county’s historical volatility, where values surged from $430,900 in 2017 to $705,800 in 2024. Notably, home values are rising faster than rents, a divergence that typically signals a shifting balance of power toward sellers, yet the absolute price levels remain prohibitive for the median household earning $95,631. The county’s home values are 11.3% above the state average and 210% above the national mean, highlighting its premium status within the broader New England real estate landscape. The local economic context further complicates the buying landscape. With 631,022 total jobs and an average weekly wage of $2,721, the labor market is robust, yet housing supply remains insufficient to meet demand. In 2025, only 2,338 new units were authorized, with multi-family construction dominating at 2,107 units compared to just 231 single-family homes. This supply structure supports the rental-heavy market dynamic. Additionally, net migration data from 2021-2022 reveals a loss of 3,474 filers, with outflowing residents earning slightly more ($150,877) than inflowing residents ($147,352), suggesting a potential drain of higher-income households. The current 7.40% mortgage rate exacerbates these affordability issues, driving the overall market assessment toward stress. Consequently, the market is defined by high stability in price growth but critically low vitality for prospective buyers, who face a landscape where rental costs consume a significant portion of income, and purchase costs remain detached from local earning power.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Suffolk County is 11.3% above the Massachusetts state average and 210.0% above the U.S. national average. The median rent of $2,129/month is 31.8% above the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 27.6, Suffolk County is a strongly rent-favored market. Home prices are high relative to rents, meaning renting is typically far cheaper than owning in the near term.

Suffolk County has an owner-occupancy rate of 36.5%, compared to 67.2% in Massachusetts and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more renter-heavy market than the U.S. average.
