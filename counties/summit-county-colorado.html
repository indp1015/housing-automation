📝 Local Market Analysis

Summit County, Colorado, operates as a severely constrained buyer’s market, characterized by extreme valuation premiums and limited inventory. The median home value of $939,900 stands 312.8% above the national average and 119.9% above the state average, reflecting a price-to-income ratio of 8.56 compared to the national benchmark of 3.20. This disconnect between asset values and local purchasing power is further highlighted by a price-to-rent ratio of 40.6, nearly double the national norm of 18.7. Consequently, the affordability dimension of the market is critically low, scoring just 6.1 out of 100, which remains the primary driver of the overall stressed classification despite high vitality metrics. The multi-year trend reveals a period of accelerated appreciation that has recently moderated. Home values surged from $547,700 in 2017 to $939,900 in 2024, with peak year-over-year growth of 18.4% in 2022 slowing to 10.5% in 2024. Recent FHFA data indicates a further deceleration, with the 2025 Home Price Index rising only 1.75%, outperforming the state’s -0.61% decline by 2.36 percentage points. This divergence between the sustained rise in home prices and the more stable rent burden of 21.1% suggests that equity accumulation is outpacing rental cost inflation. The market’s vitality score of 91.1 is underpinned by robust economic fundamentals, including a median household income of $109,773 and net migration inflows of 1,815 filers with high average incomes, which continue to support demand despite high mortgage rates of 7.32%. Supply constraints remain a defining feature, with only 318 new units authorized in 2025 against a population of 31,017. The local economy supports 19,709 jobs with an average weekly wage of $1,106, yet the high cost of entry limits accessibility. For potential buyers, the data indicates that current valuations are significantly decoupled from local income levels, driven largely by external wealth and migration rather than local wage growth. Renters face a burden that exceeds the national average, though it remains below the threshold of severe stress. The combination of high vitality and low affordability creates a market where stability is fragile, dependent on sustained external capital inflows rather than internal economic expansion. The 72.1% owner-occupancy rate reflects the depth of equity held by current residents, but the high entry barrier suggests limited turnover and continued pressure on existing inventory.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Summit County is 119.9% above the Colorado state average and 312.8% above the U.S. national average. The median rent of $1,928/month is 48.1% above the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 40.6, Summit County is a strongly rent-favored market. Home prices are high relative to rents, meaning renting is typically far cheaper than owning in the near term.

Summit County has an owner-occupancy rate of 72.1%, compared to 71.9% in Colorado and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more renter-heavy market than the U.S. average.
