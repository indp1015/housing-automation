📝 Editor's Insight

Summit County operates as a distinctively seller’s market, characterized by extreme price-to-income disparities. The 8.01 price-to-income ratio is 160% higher than the national average of 3.09, indicating severe structural unaffordability for local earners. Over five years, home values surged at a 10.1% CAGR, significantly outpacing the 7.4% rent growth. This divergence signals that equity accumulation in ownership far exceeds rental cost adjustments, favoring existing homeowners over new entrants. The price-to-rent ratio of 38.3, double the national benchmark of 18.0, suggests that purchasing offers superior long-term financial leverage despite high entry barriers. A critical insight emerges from the 69.2% owner occupancy rate; with nearly seven in ten residents owning homes, the rental inventory is inherently constrained. This limited supply, coupled with incomes only 61% above the national mean, creates a persistent barrier for renters. Consequently, the market remains rigidly segmented, where high appreciation rates benefit capital holders while excluding those reliant on the shrinking rental segment.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Summit County is 119.9% above the Colorado state average and 312.8% above the U.S. national average. The median rent of $1,928/month is 48.1% above the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 40.6, Summit County is a strongly rent-favored market. Home prices are high relative to rents, meaning renting is typically far cheaper than owning in the near term.

Summit County has an owner-occupancy rate of 72.1%, compared to 71.9% in Colorado and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more renter-heavy market than the U.S. average.
