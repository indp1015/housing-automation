📝 Local Market Analysis

Washington, D.C. operates as a distinctively renter-heavy market, characterized by a low owner-occupancy rate of 41.5% and a price-to-rent ratio of 31.4, significantly exceeding the national average of 18.7. This structural imbalance indicates that purchasing equity is disproportionately expensive relative to leasing costs, favoring renters in terms of immediate cash flow while presenting high barriers for potential homeowners. The multi-year trajectory reveals a period of rapid appreciation, with home values climbing from $537,400 in 2017 to $737,100 in 2024, punctuated by a sharp 10.9% surge in 2022. However, recent momentum has decelerated, with year-over-year growth moderating to 1.7% in 2024 and a current FHFA HPI change of merely +0.40%, underperforming the state average by 1.13 percentage points. This stabilization suggests the market is cooling from its post-pandemic peak, though prices remain elevated. Affordability metrics highlight severe stress, with a price-to-income ratio of 6.71 compared to the national benchmark of 3.20. Median household income of $109,870 lags significantly behind the high cost of housing, resulting in a rent burden of 21.3% of income, well above the national norm of 17.2%. Despite incomes being 61.6% higher than the national average, they do not offset home values that are 223.7% above the national mean. Economically, the region supports 514,196 jobs with an average weekly wage of $2,381, yet housing supply remains constrained. Only 1,591 new units were authorized in 2025, predominantly multi-family (1,410 units), which does little to alleviate single-family housing scarcity. Net migration of +5,950 filers, with incoming households averaging $142,063 in income, continues to inject demand into a market facing a 7.32% mortgage rate, further suppressing buyer purchasing power. The overall market assessment reflects a "Stressed" condition, driven primarily by extreme affordability deficits and limited stability, despite moderate vitality. While the strong local economy and positive migration trends provide underlying demand support, the divergence between high income levels and exorbitant housing costs creates a fragile equilibrium. The recent slowdown in price appreciation, coupled with high interest rates, indicates that the market is adjusting to more realistic valuation levels. For stakeholders, the data suggests that while rental markets remain robust due to high demand and limited owner-occupancy, prospective buyers face a challenging environment where income growth has not kept pace with historical price inflation, necessitating a careful evaluation of long-term carrying costs against current equity access.

Market Analysis

The median home value in District of Columbia is about equal to the District of Columbia state average and 223.7% above the U.S. national average. The median rent of $1,954/month is about equal to the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 31.4, District of Columbia is a strongly rent-favored market. Home prices are high relative to rents, meaning renting is typically far cheaper than owning in the near term.

District of Columbia has an owner-occupancy rate of 41.5%, compared to 41.5% in District of Columbia and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more renter-heavy market than the U.S. average.
