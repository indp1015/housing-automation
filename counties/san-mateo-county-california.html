📝 Editor's Insight

San Mateo County operates as a distinct renter’s market, evidenced by a price-to-rent ratio of 43.0, nearly double the national average of 18.1. This metric indicates that purchasing equity is significantly more expensive relative to leasing costs than in typical U.S. regions. Over the past five years, home values expanded at an 8.2% CAGR, substantially outpacing the 5.7% CAGR for gross rents. This divergence suggests that capital appreciation in owned housing has accelerated faster than the cost of tenancy, potentially widening the wealth gap between owners and renters. Affordability remains severely constrained, with a price-to-income ratio of 9.58 compared to the national benchmark of 3.10. Although the rent burden sits at 22.3%, it still exceeds the national average of 17.2%. A critical insight for residents is that despite incomes running 135.2% above the national mean, the 597.9% premium in home values renders ownership inaccessible for most households. The data confirms that high earnings do not offset extreme asset pricing, making rental stability a primary financial strategy for the majority of the population.

Market Analysis

The median home value in San Mateo County is 161.8% above the California state average and 606.5% above the U.S. national average. The median rent of $2,893/month is 78.8% above the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 43.0, San Mateo County is a strongly rent-favored market. Home prices are high relative to rents, meaning renting is typically far cheaper than owning in the near term.

San Mateo County has an owner-occupancy rate of 59.1%, compared to 63.6% in California and 73.0% nationally. This indicates a more renter-heavy market than the U.S. average.
