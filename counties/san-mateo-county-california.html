📝 Local Market Analysis

San Mateo County operates as a severely constrained buyer’s market, characterized by extreme affordability barriers and limited supply relative to high-income demand. The median home value of $1,559,600 sits 584.9% above the national average, creating a price-to-income ratio of 9.82, which is three times the national benchmark of 3.20. Similarly, the price-to-rent ratio of 44.5 far exceeds the national norm of 18.7, indicating that purchasing remains significantly more expensive relative to rental costs than in most other regions. This structural imbalance is reinforced by an owner-occupancy rate of 58.5% and a median household income of $158,855, which, while 133.6% above the national average, fails to offset the county’s premium pricing. The composite assessment reflects this stress, with affordability metrics driving the lowest scores, while vitality remains moderate due to robust local economic indicators. Trend data reveals a decelerating but persistent upward trajectory in home values, which rose 4.4% year-over-year in 2024 after a peak of 17.6% in 2022. However, recent FHFA HPI data indicates a slight contraction, with a -0.61% year-over-year change in 2025, slightly underperforming the state’s -0.29% decline. This divergence suggests that while long-term appreciation has been strong, short-term momentum is cooling. A notable pattern is the faster growth of home values relative to rents, where the median gross rent of $2,922 imposes a 22.1% income burden, exceeding the national average of 17.2%. Despite this, the local economy remains robust, supported by 380,332 jobs and an average weekly wage of $4,284. Housing supply remains tight, with only 2,216 new units authorized in 2025, predominantly multi-family, while net migration remains negligible at -317 filers, indicating a stable but not expanding resident base. For prospective buyers, the 7.40% national mortgage rate further strains purchasing power, making the high entry price even more prohibitive. Renter’s burden is elevated but manageable for high-income earners, yet the low affordability score signals that the market is not accessible to the median household. The stability axis of the market assessment is dampened by the volatility seen in the 2022 surge, while momentum is tempered by the recent negative HPI shift. Ultimately, San Mateo’s market is defined by its disconnect from national affordability norms, driven by high wages and scarce supply, resulting in a landscape where asset values remain elevated despite recent price stabilization.

Market Analysis

The median home value in San Mateo County is 156.0% above the California state average and 584.9% above the U.S. national average. The median rent of $2,922/month is 72.9% above the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 44.5, San Mateo County is a strongly rent-favored market. Home prices are high relative to rents, meaning renting is typically far cheaper than owning in the near term.

San Mateo County has an owner-occupancy rate of 58.5%, compared to 63.4% in California and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more renter-heavy market than the U.S. average.
