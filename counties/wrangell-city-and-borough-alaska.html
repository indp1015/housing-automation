📝 Local Market Analysis

Wrangell City and Borough presents a distinctively stressed housing environment, characterized by a severe affordability deficit rather than a traditional buyer’s or renter’s dynamic. The market is fundamentally unbalanced against household purchasing power, with a price-to-income ratio of 4.65, significantly exceeding the national average of 3.20. While the price-to-rent ratio of 24.9 suggests that buying is relatively more expensive than renting compared to national norms (18.7), the overall affordability index of 19.3/100 indicates that neither ownership nor rental options are easily accessible for the median earner. This structural imbalance is exacerbated by a median household income of $63,750, which trails the national average by 6.2%, while the median home value of $296,400 sits 30.2% above the national mean. The resulting affordability constraint is the primary driver of the market’s low composite vitality, limiting broad-based participation in the housing sector. Trend analysis reveals a period of rapid appreciation followed by stabilization. Home values surged 18.6% in 2022, reaching $273,800, before moderating to a 2.3% increase in 2024. This deceleration aligns with the current 7.32% national mortgage rate, which dampens buyer demand. A notable anomaly exists in the divergence between asset values and rental costs; while home prices have risen substantially, the median gross rent remains modest at $993, resulting in a rent burden of 18.7%. This suggests that the rental market has not fully adjusted to the higher asset values, potentially offering a temporary reprieve for renters, though the long-term stability is questioned by the low stability score of 15.9/100. The momentum axis, scoring 51.2/100, reflects this recent price growth, yet it is insufficient to offset the profound affordability and stability deficits. Regional context further isolates this market, as local employment consists of only 595 jobs across 80 establishments, with an average weekly wage of $1,123. Housing supply is critically constrained, with only one new unit authorized in 2025, ensuring that inventory remains scarce. Migration data shows a slight net inflow of 56 filers, but the economic base is too small to sustain the current price levels without external capital. Consequently, the market functions less as a standard residential hub and more as a niche, high-barrier environment where limited supply and rising asset values outpace local income growth. For stakeholders, the data indicates that entry barriers are high, with ownership costs disproportionately elevated relative to local earnings, while the rental sector remains comparatively anchored but vulnerable to future supply shocks.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Wrangell City and Borough is 4.6% above the Alaska state average and 30.2% above the U.S. national average. The median rent of $993/month is 20.4% below the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 24.9, renting is generally more cost-effective than buying in the short to medium term. The market favors buyers only for long-term holds.

Wrangell City and Borough has an owner-occupancy rate of 59.1%, compared to 64.7% in Alaska and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more renter-heavy market than the U.S. average.
