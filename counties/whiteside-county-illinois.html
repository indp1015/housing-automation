📝 Local Market Analysis

Whiteside County, Illinois, functions as a distinctively affordable buyer’s market, characterized by a price-to-income ratio of 1.88, significantly below the national average of 3.20. The median home value of $127,100 sits 44.2% below the national mean and 16.9% below the Illinois state average, while the median household income of $67,500 aligns almost perfectly with the national figure, deviating by only 0.7%. This disparity creates a favorable environment for homeownership, further supported by a rent burden of 15.2%, which is lower than the national benchmark of 17.2%. The price-to-rent ratio of 12.4, compared to the national average of 18.7, indicates that purchasing remains substantially more cost-effective than renting, reinforcing the market’s classification as buyer-friendly. Historical data reveals a period of rapid appreciation followed by stabilization. Home values surged 11.5% year-over-year in 2022, rising from $109,600 to $122,200, before moderating to a 1.0% increase in 2024 to reach $127,100. This recent deceleration contrasts with the FHFA House Price Index, which recorded a 3.98% year-over-year increase in 2025, though this still underperforms the Illinois state average of 5.54% by 1.56 percentage points. The divergence between the slower Census-based annual growth and the higher FHFA quarterly momentum suggests that while long-term appreciation has cooled, recent transactional pricing remains resilient. Rents have risen in tandem with home values, indicating a balanced adjustment rather than a speculative bubble in either sector. The local economic landscape supports this stability through limited new supply and modest population growth. With only 23 single-family units authorized in 2025 and zero multi-family permits, housing inventory remains constrained, which likely sustains price floors despite high mortgage rates of 7.40%. Total employment stands at 15,492 jobs with an average weekly wage of $989, providing a stable economic base. Net migration of 788 filers between 2021 and 2022, with inflow and outflow incomes nearly identical at approximately $68,000, suggests demographic stability rather than a surge in high-income demand. For potential buyers, the combination of low entry costs, strong income parity with national averages, and constrained supply suggests a market where affordability is preserved without significant risk of rapid depreciation. Renters face a less favorable environment, as the low price-to-rent ratio makes long-term rental commitments less financially advantageous compared to purchasing.
