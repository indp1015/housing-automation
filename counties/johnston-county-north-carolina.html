📝 Local Market Analysis

Johnston County, North Carolina, functions as a distinctively balanced market, characterized by strong demographic vitality but constrained affordability. The median home value of $305,600 sits 29.9% above the state average and 34.2% above the national mean, yet the median household income of $83,384 exceeds the national benchmark by 22.6%. This income resilience supports a price-to-income ratio of 3.66, which is moderately higher than the national average of 3.20, indicating that while purchasing power is robust, the cost of entry has outpaced wage growth. Conversely, the rent burden stands at 16.5% of income, slightly below the national average of 17.2%, suggesting that renting remains a relatively accessible alternative despite rising property values. The owner-occupancy rate of 76.1% reflects a stable, established residential base rather than a transient rental market. The most notable data pattern is the significant divergence between asset appreciation and current momentum. From 2017 to 2024, median home values nearly doubled, rising from $150,700 to $305,600, with peak annual growth of 18.5% in 2022. However, recent FHFA HPI data for 2025 shows a county year-over-year change of only +1.45%, outperforming the state’s +0.38% but signaling a sharp deceleration from the prior seven-year trend. This stabilization aligns with the national 30-year fixed mortgage rate of 7.40%, which likely suppresses transaction velocity and limits further rapid appreciation. The market’s vitality is driven by net migration of +6,759 filers and robust local employment, with 56,591 jobs and an average weekly wage of $1,079. Housing supply is actively expanding, with 3,002 new units authorized in 2025, predominantly single-family homes, which may help moderate future price pressures. Affordability remains the primary constraint, with a price-to-rent ratio of 22.2 significantly exceeding the national average of 18.7, implying that buying is currently less cost-effective than renting on a monthly basis. The composite assessment reflects this tension: high vitality scores stem from strong migration and economic activity, while low momentum and affordability scores result from the recent price plateau and elevated entry costs. For prospective buyers, the market offers stability and strong economic fundamentals, but the high price-to-rent ratio suggests that waiting for further price correction or focusing on rental options may be more financially prudent in the near term. The data indicates a market transitioning from a rapid appreciation phase to a mature, stabilized environment where income growth must keep pace with high baseline values to maintain accessibility.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Johnston County is 29.9% above the North Carolina state average and 34.2% above the U.S. national average. The median rent of $1,146/month is 17.4% above the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 22.2, renting is generally more cost-effective than buying in the short to medium term. The market favors buyers only for long-term holds.

Johnston County has an owner-occupancy rate of 76.1%, compared to 71.8% in North Carolina and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more homeowner-heavy market than the U.S. average.
