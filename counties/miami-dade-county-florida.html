📝 Local Market Analysis

Miami-Dade County functions as a severely stressed buyer’s market, characterized by extreme affordability constraints that dominate the local housing landscape. The price-to-income ratio stands at 6.45, more than double the national average of 3.20, while the rent burden consumes 30.6% of median household income compared to the national benchmark of 17.2%. These metrics indicate that both purchasing and renting are disproportionately expensive relative to local earnings, which are only 5.5% above the national mean. Consequently, the market exhibits significant stress, with the affordability dimension driving the overall assessment of market health to a low level of 1.5 out of 100. Long-term trends reveal a sustained appreciation cycle, with median home values rising from $242,800 in 2017 to $463,000 in 2024. Although the peak year-over-year growth of 16.3% in 2022 has moderated to 8.8% in 2024, recent FHFA data indicates a further deceleration to 2.79% in 2025. This slowdown still outpaces the Florida state average of 0.91%, suggesting that Miami-Dade retains relative strength despite the broader cooling. A notable divergence exists between asset classes: home values are rising faster than rents, pushing the price-to-rent ratio to 21.1, well above the national average of 18.7. This suggests that ownership costs are escalating more rapidly than rental alternatives, potentially pressuring the renter segment as they face high costs without the equity accumulation benefits of ownership. Regional context highlights Miami-Dade’s premium status, with home values 103.3% above the national average and 62.8% above the state average. The local economy supports this demand through 1.14 million jobs and an average weekly wage of $1,565, yet the high cost of capital, with 30-year mortgage rates at 7.32%, further suppresses buyer demand. Housing supply remains robust, with 16,535 new units authorized in 2025, predominantly multi-family (13,869 units), which may alleviate some rental pressure but does little to address the single-family shortage. Migration data shows a slight net outflow of 4,472 filers, yet the inflow of high-income earners (average $100,723) continues to support price resilience. Ultimately, the market’s vitality is driven by strong economic fundamentals and high-income migration, but this is severely offset by affordability metrics that remain far detached from national norms.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Miami-Dade County is 62.8% above the Florida state average and 103.3% above the U.S. national average. The median rent of $1,829/month is 40.4% above the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 21.1, renting is generally more cost-effective than buying in the short to medium term. The market favors buyers only for long-term holds.

Miami-Dade County has an owner-occupancy rate of 52.2%, compared to 74.6% in Florida and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more renter-heavy market than the U.S. average.
