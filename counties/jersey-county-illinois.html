📝 Local Market Analysis

Jersey County, Illinois, operates as a distinctly buyer’s market, characterized by significant affordability advantages and stable ownership patterns. With a median home value of $171,900, the county sits 24.5% below the national average, yet it commands a 12.3% premium over the Illinois state average. This disparity is reinforced by a price-to-income ratio of 2.14, which is substantially lower than the national benchmark of 3.20, indicating that local incomes, averaging $80,361, support housing costs far more effectively than in typical U.S. markets. The 80.5% owner-occupancy rate further cements the area’s status as a stable residential hub rather than a transient rental corridor. Recent market dynamics reveal a period of accelerating momentum that has outpaced broader state trends. The FHFA House Price Index recorded a 7.35% year-over-year increase in 2025, outperforming the Illinois average of 5.54% by 1.81 percentage points. This sharp appreciation follows a volatile decade, including a 10.1% surge in 2022, though growth has moderated to 1.8% in the latest Census data. A notable divergence exists between asset classes, as rents are rising faster than home values, suggesting shifting demand pressures within the rental segment. Despite this, the rent burden remains low at 12.6% of income, well below the national 17.2% average, ensuring that both buyers and renters face manageable financial obligations. The market’s vitality is constrained by limited economic scale, a factor reflected in the low vitality component of the composite assessment. With only 3,928 total jobs and 358 establishments, the local labor base is small, though average weekly wages of $887 suggest higher-quality employment. Housing supply remains tight, with just 39 new single-family units authorized in 2025 and zero multi-family construction, which likely contributes to sustained price appreciation. Positive net migration of 291 filers indicates demographic growth, but the high national mortgage rate of 7.32% acts as a structural headwind for new entrants. Consequently, the market’s strength is driven primarily by exceptional affordability and strong recent price momentum, while economic vitality remains a secondary, weaker pillar due to the limited employment base.
