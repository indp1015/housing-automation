📝 Local Market Analysis

Williams County, North Dakota, functions as a balanced market with distinct affordability constraints, characterized by a 53.0% owner-occupancy rate and a price-to-rent ratio of 21.1, which exceeds the national average of 18.7. This metric indicates that purchasing is relatively more expensive compared to renting, yet the 15.7% rent burden remains below the national average of 17.2%, suggesting rental housing remains accessible relative to local incomes. The median home value of $284,800 stands 62.0% above the state average, driven by a median household income of $85,595 that is 25.9% higher than the national mean. Consequently, the price-to-income ratio of 3.33 sits slightly above the national benchmark of 3.20, indicating moderate affordability pressure for buyers despite robust local earnings. Recent momentum is strong, with the FHFA House Price Index showing a 6.82% year-over-year increase in 2025, outperforming the state average of 4.82% by 2.00 percentage points. This acceleration follows a volatile multi-year trend, where home values dipped 5.5% in 2021 before recovering with consecutive gains of 5.9% and 5.0% in 2022 and 2023, respectively. The 2024 value of $284,800 reflects a 6.3% increase, signaling sustained appreciation. This upward trajectory is supported by positive net migration of 2,069 filers between 2021 and 2022, with inflowing residents earning an average of $99,522, contributing to demand. However, housing supply remains constrained, with only 145 new single-family units authorized in 2025 and zero multi-family permits, limiting the capacity to absorb this demographic growth. The local economic context further shapes this dynamic, as 23,774 total jobs and an average weekly wage of $1,840 provide a stable employment base. Despite these fundamentals, the market exhibits lower stability scores due to historical price volatility, specifically the sharp declines observed in 2019 and 2021. In contrast, vitality metrics are high, reflecting strong employment and migration patterns. For potential buyers, the 7.40% national mortgage rate amplifies the cost of entry, making the premium over state averages a significant consideration. Renter’s perspective is less pressured by immediate cost-of-living burdens, as the rent burden remains favorable, but the low owner-occupancy rate suggests a substantial rental population that may face competition if supply does not increase. The divergence between strong momentum and moderate affordability creates a challenging environment for new entrants, where high income levels are offset by elevated asset prices and limited new construction.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Williams County is 62.0% above the North Dakota state average and 25.1% above the U.S. national average. The median rent of $1,123/month is 45.5% above the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 21.1, renting is generally more cost-effective than buying in the short to medium term. The market favors buyers only for long-term holds.

Williams County has an owner-occupancy rate of 53.0%, compared to 73.6% in North Dakota and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more renter-heavy market than the U.S. average.
