📝 Local Market Analysis

Richland County, Illinois, functions as a distinctively affordable buyer’s market, characterized by a median home value of $112,000 that sits 50.8% below the national average. The price-to-income ratio of 1.79 is significantly lower than the national benchmark of 3.20, while the price-to-rent ratio of 11.6 is well below the national average of 18.7, indicating that purchasing remains substantially more cost-effective than renting. This affordability is reinforced by a rent burden of 15.5% of median household income, which is below the national average of 17.2%. Despite these favorable metrics for homeownership, the market exhibits a specific divergence where rents are rising faster than home values, suggesting a shifting dynamic in residential demand. The multi-year trend reveals a period of rapid appreciation followed by stabilization. After a modest 2.2% increase in 2019, prices surged 18.2% in 2022 to reach $106,400, before moderating to a 5.1% gain in 2024. Recent FHFA data confirms this cooling momentum, showing a 4.06% year-over-year increase in 2025, which underperforms the state’s 5.54% growth by 1.48 percentage points. This relative underperformance highlights that while the county continues to appreciate, it is gaining value more slowly than the broader Illinois market. The local economic context supports this moderate trajectory, with total employment at 4,935 jobs and an average weekly wage of $1,000. Housing supply remains extremely constrained, with only eight new single-family units authorized in 2025 and zero multi-family permits, yet net migration of +359 filers indicates sustained population growth that may continue to pressure limited inventory. For potential buyers, the current environment offers significant entry-level affordability, particularly given the 7.40% national mortgage rate, which makes the low median home value a critical buffer against high borrowing costs. The combination of low price-to-income ratios and positive net migration suggests a stable foundation for long-term equity accumulation. However, renters face a different challenge, as the trend of rents outpacing home value growth implies that the cost of renting is becoming less advantageous relative to purchasing. The stark contrast between the county’s 73.8% owner-occupancy rate and the minimal new construction pipeline suggests that existing inventory turnover will drive future price movements. Ultimately, the data points to a market where affordability remains the primary driver of demand, insulated from the sharper price corrections seen in higher-cost urban centers, but subject to the structural constraints of limited new supply and moderate wage growth.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Richland County is 26.8% below the Illinois state average and 50.8% below the U.S. national average. The median rent of $805/month is 7.2% below the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 11.6, buying a home in Richland County is generally more favorable than renting for those planning to stay long-term. A ratio below 15 typically signals that purchasing is financially advantageous over a multi-year horizon.

Richland County has an owner-occupancy rate of 73.8%, compared to 75.8% in Illinois and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more homeowner-heavy market than the U.S. average.
