📝 Editor's Insight

Logan County presents a mixed market dynamic, leaning slightly toward buyers due to a price-to-rent ratio of 18.5, which aligns closely with the national average of 18.0. However, the 5.7 percentage point gap between owner occupancy and the national norm suggests a persistent rental demand. The five-year trend reveals significant appreciation, with home values growing at a 9.6% CAGR, substantially outpacing the 6.9% rent increase. This divergence indicates that equity accumulation is currently more robust than rental yield growth. Affordability remains strained; the 4.14 price-to-income ratio exceeds the national benchmark of 3.09, while the 22.4% rent burden sits well above the 17.2% average. Consequently, both buyers and renters face elevated costs relative to the $55,074 median income. A critical, non-obvious insight is the county’s 42.7% price discount relative to the Colorado state average. This significant regional disparity implies that Logan County offers a substantially lower entry barrier than the broader state market, potentially attracting residents seeking affordability despite local income constraints.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Logan County is 42.6% below the Colorado state average and 7.7% above the U.S. national average. The median rent of $1,013/month is 22.2% below the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 20.2, renting is generally more cost-effective than buying in the short to medium term. The market favors buyers only for long-term holds.

Logan County has an owner-occupancy rate of 66.3%, compared to 71.9% in Colorado and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more renter-heavy market than the U.S. average.
