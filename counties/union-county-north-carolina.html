📝 Local Market Analysis

Union County, North Carolina, operates as a distinctively owner-occupied market, evidenced by an 81.6% ownership rate and a price-to-rent ratio of 23.2, which significantly exceeds the national average of 18.7. This metric suggests that purchasing is structurally more expensive relative to rental costs than in most U.S. counties, yet the high ownership rate indicates strong local demand for equity. The market is not a traditional renter’s market, as rent burden sits at 17.4% of income, nearly identical to the national average of 17.2%, indicating that rental affordability is not the primary driver of housing decisions. Instead, the data points to a balanced but increasingly expensive environment where home values have decoupled from income growth. The multi-year trend reveals a dramatic acceleration in home values, with median prices rising from $215,200 in 2017 to $417,300 in 2024. This 93.8% cumulative increase far outpaces the 51.3% income premium relative to national averages, resulting in a price-to-income ratio of 4.06 compared to the national benchmark of 3.20. While 2022 saw a peak year-over-year surge of 20.4%, recent momentum has moderated to 11.5% in 2024. However, FHFA data from 2025 shows a further deceleration to 3.49% year-over-year, a rate that still outperforms the state average of 0.38% by 3.11 percentage points. This divergence highlights that while the county continues to outperform the broader state, the rapid appreciation phase is cooling. Economic fundamentals remain robust, supported by 66,804 jobs and an average weekly wage of $1,256. Net migration of 6,409 filers between 2021 and 2022, with inflowing households averaging $126,429 in income, suggests that demand is driven by higher-earning residents rather than price-sensitive buyers. With 2,707 new units authorized in 2025, primarily single-family homes, supply is attempting to catch up with demand. Nevertheless, the 7.40% national mortgage rate exacerbates affordability constraints, contributing to a low affordability assessment. The market’s vitality is high due to strong employment and migration, but stability is challenged by the significant gap between home values and household incomes. Potential buyers should note that while price growth is slowing, the entry cost remains 77.3% above the state average, making timing and financing conditions critical factors in transaction viability.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Union County is 77.3% above the North Carolina state average and 83.3% above the U.S. national average. The median rent of $1,496/month is 53.3% above the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 23.2, renting is generally more cost-effective than buying in the short to medium term. The market favors buyers only for long-term holds.

Union County has an owner-occupancy rate of 81.6%, compared to 71.8% in North Carolina and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more homeowner-heavy market than the U.S. average.
