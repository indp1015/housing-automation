📝 Editor's Insight

Pueblo County exhibits a balanced market structure, evidenced by a 68.3% owner-occupancy rate and a 21.4 price-to-rent ratio, which slightly exceeds the national average of 18.0. The five-year trend reveals significant appreciation, with home values growing at a 13.4% CAGR compared to a 6.3% CAGR for rents. This divergence indicates that equity accumulation outpaces rental cost inflation, favoring long-term ownership over leasing. However, affordability remains strained relative to national benchmarks. The price-to-income ratio stands at 4.37, substantially higher than the 3.09 national average, while the rent burden consumes 20.4% of income versus the 17.2% national norm. A critical insight for potential residents is the disparity between local and state valuations; despite being 27.9% above the national home value average, properties remain 31.7% below the Colorado state median. This suggests that while absolute prices have surged, the market retains relative affordability within the state context, provided incomes align with the $62,250 median household figure.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Pueblo County is 31.7% below the Colorado state average and 27.9% above the U.S. national average. The median rent of $1,059/month is 15.5% below the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 21.4, renting is generally more cost-effective than buying in the short to medium term. The market favors buyers only for long-term holds.

Pueblo County has an owner-occupancy rate of 68.3%, compared to 72.1% in Colorado and 73.0% nationally. This indicates a more renter-heavy market than the U.S. average.
