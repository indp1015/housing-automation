📝 Local Market Analysis

Rapides Parish operates as a balanced market leaning toward affordability, characterized by a price-to-income ratio of 3.29, slightly above the national average of 3.20. The price-to-rent ratio of 15.9 remains below the national benchmark of 18.7, indicating that purchasing offers a more cost-effective long-term strategy than renting, despite a rent burden of 20.7% exceeding the national 17.2%. This structure suggests that while entry costs are manageable relative to local incomes, rental costs consume a disproportionate share of household budgets. The market’s momentum is evident in the multi-year appreciation trajectory, where median home values rose from $133,800 in 2017 to $181,100 in 2024. This growth was punctuated by a significant spike of 11.6% in 2022, which has since moderated to a 1.1% increase in 2024. Recent FHFA data confirms this stabilization, showing a 1.43% year-over-year change that slightly lags the state average of 1.59%, suggesting the local market is tracking just below state-wide trends. The affordability profile is constrained by local economic fundamentals. With a median household income of $54,967, which is 19.2% below the national average, residents face tighter financial margins. However, the county’s home values are 8.2% higher than the Louisiana state average, creating a relative premium that may pressure future growth. Employment data shows 45,101 jobs with an average weekly wage of $1,006, providing a stable but modest income base. Housing supply remains limited, with only 241 new units authorized in 2025, predominantly single-family homes, which likely contributes to sustained price levels despite slowing growth. Migration data indicates a net inflow of 1,236 filers, with incoming residents earning an average of $76,130, slightly lower than the outflow average, suggesting that while population is growing, the incoming demographic may not significantly alter the income distribution. The market’s vitality is further shaped by the current 7.40% mortgage rate, which increases monthly payment burdens for buyers. While the price-to-rent ratio favors ownership, the high rent burden suggests that tenants face significant financial strain. The divergence between the strong historical price appreciation and the current slowing momentum indicates a market transitioning from a boom phase to a stabilization period. The overall assessment reflects a market with strong momentum but moderate stability, driven by the gap between rising asset values and stagnant income growth. The limited new construction and modest job growth suggest that supply constraints will continue to support values, even as affordability metrics remain challenging for the average household.
