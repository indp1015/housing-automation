📝 Local Market Analysis

Hyde County operates as a distinctly renter-friendly market, characterized by a price-to-rent ratio of 8.2, significantly below the national average of 18.7. This structural imbalance suggests that renting is far more cost-effective than purchasing, a dynamic reinforced by a median home value of $124,000 that sits 47.3% below the North Carolina state average. The affordability profile is mixed; while the price-to-income ratio of 2.31 is favorable compared to the national benchmark of 3.20, the rent burden stands at 28.2% of median household income, exceeding the national norm of 17.2%. Consequently, the market exhibits high momentum but low stability, reflected in a composite assessment that prioritizes current price appreciation over long-term holding power. Recent trends reveal a volatile yet recovering trajectory. After a sharp decline from the 2019 peak of $141,500 to $105,300 in 2022, values have stabilized with modest growth of 3.7% in 2024. However, the FHFA Home Price Index indicates a recent acceleration, with a 6.48% year-over-year increase that outpaces the state’s 0.38% growth by 6.10 percentage points. This divergence suggests localized demand is driving prices higher despite a broader state-level stagnation. The economic foundation remains constrained, with only 1,199 total jobs and an average weekly wage of $803, limiting the purchasing power of the local workforce. Furthermore, the housing supply is tightly constrained, with just 35 new single-family units authorized in 2025 and zero multi-family construction, which likely contributes to the upward pressure on both sales prices and rents. Migration data provides context for this demand, showing a net inflow of 132 filers between 2021 and 2022, though incoming residents earn slightly less ($58,255) than those leaving ($60,265). With 30-year mortgage rates hovering at 7.40%, the high cost of financing further discourages entry for price-sensitive buyers. For potential stakeholders, the data indicates that while entry-level home prices are accessible relative to national standards, the rapid recent appreciation and high rent burdens create a challenging environment for long-term affordability. The market’s vitality is driven by limited supply and localized price momentum, but the lack of economic diversification and high rent costs temper the overall stability of the housing sector.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Hyde County is 47.3% below the North Carolina state average and 45.5% below the U.S. national average. The median rent of $1,263/month is 29.4% above the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 8.2, buying a home in Hyde County is generally more favorable than renting for those planning to stay long-term. A ratio below 15 typically signals that purchasing is financially advantageous over a multi-year horizon.

Hyde County has an owner-occupancy rate of 71.9%, compared to 71.8% in North Carolina and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more renter-heavy market than the U.S. average.
