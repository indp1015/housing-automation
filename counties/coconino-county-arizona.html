📝 Local Market Analysis

Coconino County operates as a severely stressed buyer’s market, characterized by extreme affordability constraints that outweigh strong economic vitality. The market classification is driven primarily by a price-to-income ratio of 6.14, nearly double the national average of 3.20, and a rent burden of 24.2%, significantly exceeding the 17.2% national benchmark. These metrics indicate that housing costs consume a disproportionate share of household resources, limiting purchasing power for local residents despite a median household income of $72,966. The composite assessment reflects this imbalance, where vitality scores are high due to robust migration and employment, but affordability and stability metrics remain critically low, creating a structural tension in the housing ecosystem. Trend analysis reveals a period of accelerated appreciation that has only recently begun to moderate. Home values surged 18.4% year-over-year in 2022 before decelerating to 8.4% in 2024, yet prices remain 96.7% above the national average and 68.6% above the state average. A notable divergence exists between price and rent growth, with home values rising faster than rents, pushing the price-to-rent ratio to 25.3 compared to the national 18.7. This suggests that ownership costs are escalating more rapidly than rental alternatives, further straining affordability. The 2021-2022 migration data shows a net inflow of 3,161 filers, with incoming households earning an average of $82,212, slightly higher than those leaving, indicating that wealthier migrants are fueling demand in a market where local wages average $1,020 weekly. Economic context highlights a supply-demand mismatch exacerbated by limited new construction. With only 634 new units authorized in 2025, including 421 single-family homes, the supply pipeline is insufficient to offset the persistent demand driven by migration. The current 30-year fixed mortgage rate of 7.32% compounds the affordability crisis, making monthly payments on the $448,000 median home value prohibitively high for the median household. Consequently, the market exhibits high momentum and vitality but lacks stability, as the gap between local income levels and housing costs continues to widen. Potential buyers face a landscape where entry barriers are significantly higher than national norms, while renters experience above-average cost burdens, reflecting a market where external demand outpaces local economic capacity to support housing costs.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Coconino County is 68.6% above the Arizona state average and 96.7% above the U.S. national average. The median rent of $1,473/month is 37.3% above the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 25.3, Coconino County is a strongly rent-favored market. Home prices are high relative to rents, meaning renting is typically far cheaper than owning in the near term.

Coconino County has an owner-occupancy rate of 59.7%, compared to 70.7% in Arizona and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more renter-heavy market than the U.S. average.
