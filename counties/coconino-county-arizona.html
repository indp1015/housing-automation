📝 Editor's Insight

Coconino County exhibits a distinct seller’s market, evidenced by a price-to-rent ratio of 24.5, significantly exceeding the national average of 18.0. This disparity suggests that purchasing offers superior long-term value relative to renting, despite current entry barriers. The five-year trend reveals a 10.1% annual compounded growth rate in home values, substantially outpacing the 4.5% increase in rents. This divergence indicates that equity accumulation through ownership has accelerated faster than rental cost escalation, reinforcing the structural advantage of homeownership in this region. However, affordability remains constrained, with a price-to-income ratio of 5.92, nearly double the national benchmark of 3.09. Additionally, the rent burden stands at 24.2% of median income, well above the 17.2% national average. A critical insight for potential residents is that the 58.8% owner-occupancy rate, combined with high price-to-income metrics, signals limited inventory liquidity. Consequently, both buyers and renters face compressed negotiating power, as the market’s high valuation relative to local income levels creates persistent demand pressure that suppresses concessions for either party.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Coconino County is 69.8% above the Arizona state average and 95.3% above the U.S. national average. The median rent of $1,406/month is 38.4% above the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 24.5, renting is generally more cost-effective than buying in the short to medium term. The market favors buyers only for long-term holds.

Coconino County has an owner-occupancy rate of 58.8%, compared to 70.3% in Arizona and 73.0% nationally. This indicates a more renter-heavy market than the U.S. average.
