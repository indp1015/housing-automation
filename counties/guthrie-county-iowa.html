📝 Local Market Analysis

Guthrie County, Iowa, operates as a distinctively affordable buyer’s market, characterized by a price-to-income ratio of 2.32, significantly below the national average of 3.20. With a median home value of $180,100 and a median household income of $77,711, the county offers substantial value relative to its cost of living. The rent burden stands at 13.0% of income, well under the national benchmark of 17.2%, further reinforcing the region’s affordability profile. Despite these favorable metrics, the market has experienced a pronounced upward trajectory in home values, with prices rising from $112,600 in 2017 to $180,100 in 2024. This represents a cumulative increase of approximately 60% over seven years, driven by annual gains that frequently exceeded 7%, including a 10.9% jump in 2022. The recent momentum, however, signals a cooling phase. The FHFA House Price Index indicates a modest 1.36% year-over-year change in 2025, underperforming the Iowa state average of 2.25% by 0.89 percentage points. This deceleration contrasts with the aggressive growth seen in previous years, suggesting a normalization of pricing after a period of rapid appreciation. Economically, the county supports 2,337 jobs with an average weekly wage of $1,077, providing a stable but modest employment base. Housing supply remains constrained, with only 33 new units authorized in 2025, split between 17 single-family and 16 multi-family homes. Meanwhile, net migration of +236 filers between 2021 and 2022 indicates sustained demographic interest, with inflowing residents earning an average income of $89,870. The divergence between the strong long-term price appreciation and the current slower momentum highlights a market transitioning from high growth to stability. The high owner-occupancy rate of 84.0% suggests a settled community, while the price-to-rent ratio of 17.8, slightly below the national average of 18.7, indicates that buying remains more cost-effective than renting over time. For potential buyers, the current environment offers entry into a market that has historically appreciated steadily but is now exhibiting more tempered growth. Renters face a competitive landscape where low rent burdens are offset by limited new supply. The overall market stability is driven by low volatility relative to national trends, while vitality is supported by consistent, albeit modest, population growth and wage levels. The combination of high affordability and stable ownership rates positions Guthrie County as a resilient, low-risk residential market, distinct from the higher-cost, higher-volatility dynamics observed in metropolitan areas.

Market Analysis

With a price-to-rent ratio of 17.8, the buy-vs-rent decision in Guthrie County is balanced. Whether buying or renting makes more sense depends on your time horizon, down payment capacity, and local market conditions.
