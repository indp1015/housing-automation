📝 Local Market Analysis

Gulf County, Florida, functions as a balanced market with distinct affordability challenges, characterized by a price-to-rent ratio of 17.6, which sits slightly below the national average of 18.7. This metric suggests that purchasing is marginally more cost-effective than long-term renting, a structural advantage reinforced by an 80.8% owner-occupancy rate. However, the market faces significant affordability headwinds, evidenced by a price-to-income ratio of 4.09, substantially higher than the national benchmark of 3.20. Consequently, the rent burden consumes 23.3% of median household income, exceeding the national average of 17.2%, indicating that both buyers and renters face elevated financial pressure relative to their earnings. The multi-year trend reveals a period of accelerated appreciation that has recently moderated. Home values surged from $151,400 in 2017 to $250,000 in 2024, with a peak year-over-year increase of 19.2% in 2023 before settling to 6.1% in 2024. Recent FHFA HPI data for 2025 indicates a 3.46% annual gain, outperforming the Florida state average of 0.91% by 2.55 percentage points. This divergence highlights a notable pattern where home values are rising faster than rents, a dynamic often associated with strong equity growth for owners but potential displacement risks for renters. The county’s vitality is further underscored by robust net migration of +877 filers between 2021 and 2022, with incoming residents averaging $90,619 in income, significantly higher than the $77,323 average of those departing. Regional context places Gulf County’s median home value 9.8% above the national average but 12.1% below the Florida state average, reflecting its position as a lower-cost entry point within a high-performing state. Local economic fundamentals remain constrained, with only 3,569 total jobs and an average weekly wage of $1,086, which limits local purchasing power despite external migration inflows. Housing supply is exclusively single-family, with 302 units authorized in 2025 and zero multi-family construction, a structural gap that may limit rental inventory expansion. With a 30-year fixed mortgage rate at 7.32%, high financing costs compound the affordability strain. The overall analytical assessment is driven by high vitality and moderate momentum, which are offset by critically low affordability scores, resulting in a challenging environment where strong demand metrics clash with limited local income growth and high entry costs.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Gulf County is 12.1% below the Florida state average and 9.8% above the U.S. national average. The median rent of $1,187/month is 8.9% below the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 17.6, the buy-vs-rent decision in Gulf County is balanced. Whether buying or renting makes more sense depends on your time horizon, down payment capacity, and local market conditions.

Gulf County has an owner-occupancy rate of 80.8%, compared to 74.6% in Florida and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more homeowner-heavy market than the U.S. average.
