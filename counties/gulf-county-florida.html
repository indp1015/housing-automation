📝 Local Market Analysis

Gulf County, Florida, presents a mixed market profile that leans slightly toward renters despite a high owner-occupancy rate of 80.8%. The price-to-rent ratio of 17.6 sits below the national average of 18.7, suggesting that purchasing is relatively more expensive compared to renting on a cost basis. However, the median home value of $250,000 is only 9.8% above the national average, while the median household income of $61,179 is 10.0% below the national norm. This disparity drives a price-to-income ratio of 4.09, significantly higher than the national benchmark of 3.20, indicating that local buyers face greater financial strain relative to their earnings than the average American household. The market has experienced a pronounced acceleration in home values, with year-over-year growth spiking to 19.2% in 2023 before moderating to 6.1% in 2024. Recent FHFA data confirms this momentum, showing a 3.46% year-over-year increase in 2025, which outpaces the Florida state average of 0.91% by 2.55 percentage points. This divergence highlights a notable pattern where home prices are rising faster than rents, as evidenced by the trend signal. While the 2023 surge may reflect post-pandemic migration effects, the continued outperformance of the state average suggests persistent local demand. The influx of 877 net new tax filers between 2021 and 2022, with incoming residents averaging $90,619 in income, further supports the thesis of wealthier migrants driving up property values. Affordability remains a critical constraint for local residents, with a rent burden of 23.3% of income compared to the national average of 17.2%. The local economy, supported by 3,569 jobs and an average weekly wage of $1,086, provides a stable but modest income base that may not fully support the rising price floor. Housing supply is currently skewed entirely toward single-family units, with 302 permits authorized in 2025 and zero multi-family construction, which limits rental inventory growth. With the 30-year fixed mortgage rate at 7.40%, the high price-to-income ratio exacerbates the barrier to entry for first-time buyers. Consequently, while the market is outperforming state trends, the combination of high debt service costs and limited rental supply creates a challenging environment for both prospective buyers and tenants seeking affordable housing options.
