📝 Local Market Analysis

La Paz County operates as a distinctively affordable market, characterized by a price-to-income ratio of 2.74, which is significantly below the national average of 3.20. With a median home value of $135,800, the county sits 40.4% below the national median and 48.9% below the state average. This affordability is reinforced by a price-to-rent ratio of 14.1, well under the national norm of 18.7, suggesting that ownership remains a more financially accessible option than long-term renting for many residents. The rent burden of 19.5% slightly exceeds the national average of 17.2%, indicating that while homes are cheap, rental costs are becoming a modest strain on household budgets. The market has experienced a dramatic valuation shift, with median home values rising from $77,400 in 2017 to $135,800 in 2024. This trajectory includes a peak year-over-year increase of 23.3% in 2021, moderating to 15.5% in 2024. Recent FHFA data confirms continued momentum, showing a 10.49% year-over-year price increase in 2025, which outpaces the Arizona state average of 0.52% by nearly ten percentage points. This divergence highlights a localized surge in demand that is decoupling the county’s housing values from broader state trends. The primary driver appears to be net migration, with 797 new filers arriving in 2021-2022, bringing an average income of $54,276, which is higher than the local median household income of $49,478. Supply constraints and economic limitations define the current landscape. Only 62 new single-family units were authorized in 2025, with zero multi-family permits, creating a tight inventory that likely supports price appreciation. The local economy supports 4,621 jobs with an average weekly wage of $917, which may not fully sustain the rising home prices without external income sources. For potential buyers, the low entry price offers value, but the rapid appreciation rate and limited new supply suggest that affordability gaps may widen. Renters face a market where home values are rising faster than rents, potentially reducing the long-term financial advantage of renting. The 7.40% national mortgage rate further complicates purchasing power, making the low median price a critical buffer for local buyers.

The median home value in La Paz County is 48.9% below the Arizona state average and 40.4% below the U.S. national average. The median rent of $802/month is 25.3% below the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 14.1, buying a home in La Paz County is generally more favorable than renting for those planning to stay long-term. A ratio below 15 typically signals that purchasing is financially advantageous over a multi-year horizon.
