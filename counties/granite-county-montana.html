📝 Local Market Analysis

Granite County, Montana, functions as a distinctively unbalanced market characterized by severe owner-occupancy constraints and high relative costs. The price-to-income ratio stands at 5.95, nearly double the national average of 3.20, indicating that median home values of $352,700 are significantly detached from the median household income of $59,265. Conversely, the rent burden is only 13.7% of income, well below the national benchmark of 17.2%, suggesting that renting remains a more financially accessible option for the local population. This divergence creates a structural imbalance where ownership is priced out of reach for the median earner, while rental costs remain comparatively moderate relative to local wages. The multi-year trend reveals a period of rapid appreciation followed by recent stagnation. Home values surged from $218,900 in 2017 to $352,700 in 2024, with a peak year-over-year increase of 19.8% in 2023. However, recent FHFA data indicates a 1.74% year-over-year decline in 2025, underperforming the state average by 4.33 percentage points. This recent correction contrasts sharply with the earlier boom, highlighting a market that has cooled after a period of intense growth. The price-to-rent ratio of 43.5, nearly triple the national average of 18.7, underscores that equity accumulation through ownership is currently less efficient than renting, a pattern exacerbated by the 7.40% national mortgage rate. Economic fundamentals provide limited support for sustained high valuations. With only 687 total jobs and an average weekly wage of $843, the local labor market offers modest income growth potential. Although net migration of 167 filers between 2021 and 2022 suggests inbound demand, the inflowing population’s average income of $97,162 significantly exceeds the local median, indicating that new residents are likely high-earning retirees or remote workers rather than local wage earners. This demographic shift contributes to the high home values but does not necessarily support broad-based economic vitality. The overall market assessment reflects stress primarily driven by affordability metrics, where the disconnect between home prices and local incomes dominates the analytical landscape. Stability is further compromised by the recent negative momentum in home prices, which lags behind the state’s positive trend, signaling a localized correction within a broader regional context.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Granite County is 27.4% above the Montana state average and 54.9% above the U.S. national average. The median rent of $675/month is 19.8% below the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 43.5, Granite County is a strongly rent-favored market. Home prices are high relative to rents, meaning renting is typically far cheaper than owning in the near term.

Granite County has an owner-occupancy rate of 77.9%, compared to 73.0% in Montana and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more homeowner-heavy market than the U.S. average.
