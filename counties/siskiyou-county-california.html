📝 Editor's Insight

Siskiyou County exhibits a buyer’s market dynamic, evidenced by a 69.7% owner-occupancy rate and a price-to-rent ratio of 22.7, which exceeds the national average of 18.1. This disparity suggests that purchasing equity is relatively more attractive than long-term renting, despite high entry costs. The five-year trend reveals robust appreciation, with home values compounding at 9.4% annually compared to a 5.1% rent increase. This divergence indicates that asset values are outpacing carrying costs, potentially narrowing the gap between buying and renting for long-term holders. However, affordability remains strained. The price-to-income ratio stands at 5.13, significantly above the 3.10 national benchmark, while the rent burden consumes 22.6% of median household income versus the 17.2% national norm. These metrics confirm that local housing costs disproportionately impact residents relative to their earnings. A critical insight for potential movers is that while home values are 50.2% below the California average, they are 32.9% above the national mean. This positioning implies that affordability challenges stem from local income levels, which trail the national average by 16.3%, rather than purely high property prices.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Siskiyou County is 50.2% below the California state average and 34.5% above the U.S. national average. The median rent of $1,043/month is 35.5% below the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 22.7, renting is generally more cost-effective than buying in the short to medium term. The market favors buyers only for long-term holds.

Siskiyou County has an owner-occupancy rate of 69.7%, compared to 63.6% in California and 73.0% nationally. This indicates a more renter-heavy market than the U.S. average.
