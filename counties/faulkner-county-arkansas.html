📝 Local Market Analysis

Faulkner County, Arkansas, exhibits a balanced market structure with distinct affordability pressures, characterized by a price-to-income ratio of 3.46, slightly above the national average of 3.20. The rent burden stands at 17.9% of household income, marginally exceeding the national benchmark of 17.2%, indicating that while homeownership costs are elevated, rental obligations remain relatively contained. This market is neither definitively a buyer’s nor a renter’s market; rather, it reflects a transitional phase where high momentum coexists with tightening affordability metrics. The owner-occupancy rate of 62.3% suggests a stable residential base, yet the rising cost of entry for new buyers is evident in the widening gap between home values and median incomes. The multi-year trend reveals a significant acceleration in home values, with prices surging from $153,500 in 2017 to $232,700 in 2024, representing a cumulative increase of approximately 51.6%. This growth was particularly sharp in 2022, when prices jumped 14.0% year-over-year, and continued at 10.1% in 2024. In contrast, the median gross rent of $1,005 per month suggests that rental costs have not kept pace with the rapid appreciation of home values. This divergence is highlighted by the price-to-rent ratio of 19.3, slightly above the national average of 18.7, implying that purchasing is becoming less attractive relative to renting compared to broader national trends. The recent FHFA HPI data confirms this momentum, showing a 3.55% year-over-year increase, which outperforms the state average of 1.09% by 2.46 percentage points. Regional context further underscores Faulkner County’s unique position, as home values are 64.5% higher than the state average, despite median household income being only 1.0% below the national mean. This disparity is driven by strong migration patterns, with a net inflow of 2,878 filers in 2021-2022, and robust local employment, evidenced by 39,319 jobs and an average weekly wage of $983. Housing supply, with 703 new units authorized in 2025, may be insufficient to cool the rapid price appreciation, particularly given the 7.40% national mortgage rate which limits borrowing capacity. The market’s vitality is strong, driven by population growth and economic expansion, but stability is compromised by the high cost of entry. For potential buyers, the primary challenge is the elevated price-to-income ratio, while renters face a relatively stable cost environment compared to the steep climb in home values. The data suggests a market where demand outstrips supply, leading to sustained price growth that outpaces income growth.
