📝 Local Market Analysis

Toole County, Montana, presents a complex housing landscape characterized by a buyer’s advantage in relative value but significant volatility in price appreciation. The market leans toward a buyer’s environment when considering absolute price levels, as the median home value of $218,400 sits 21.1% below the state average and 4.1% below the national mean. However, the classification is complicated by a price-to-rent ratio of 22.0, which exceeds the national average of 18.7, suggesting that while purchasing is relatively cheaper than in larger metros, the cost of ownership versus renting has become less favorable. The affordability profile remains tight, with a price-to-income ratio of 3.78, notably higher than the national benchmark of 3.20, indicating that local incomes of $57,731 are not keeping pace with rising asset values. The multi-year trend reveals extreme instability rather than steady growth. After a sharp 9.6% decline in 2020, home values surged 22.2% in 2021 and continued climbing at 16.5% in 2024. This rapid appreciation contrasts with the recent FHFA HPI data, which shows a negligible 0.34% year-over-year change in 2025, a significant underperformance relative to Montana’s 2.59% state-wide growth. This divergence suggests that the explosive growth seen in census estimates may be lagging or that recent transaction volumes are cooling sharply. The economic context supports this cooling, as total employment stands at only 1,277 jobs with an average weekly wage of $1,128, providing limited local income support for sustained high price increases. Furthermore, the national 30-year mortgage rate of 7.40% further constrains purchasing power, making the high price-to-income ratio a critical barrier for local buyers. Migration patterns offer a mixed signal, with a net inflow of 252 filers in 2021-2022, though incoming residents earned slightly less ($54,799) than those leaving ($56,503), potentially limiting long-term demand strength. The low owner occupancy rate of 63.1% and a rent burden of 17.2%, perfectly aligned with the national average, suggest a balanced rental sector despite the housing market's volatility. The primary driver of the market’s stressed condition is the combination of high price-to-income ratios and recent price volatility, which undermines stability. While vitality is moderate due to positive net migration, the lack of robust local employment growth and the sharp deceleration in recent price momentum indicate that the market is currently adjusting to previous rapid gains. Potential buyers face high entry costs relative to local wages, while renters encounter a market where the value proposition of ownership is increasingly challenged by elevated price-to-rent dynamics.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Toole County is 21.1% below the Montana state average and 4.1% below the U.S. national average. The median rent of $826/month is 1.9% below the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 22.0, renting is generally more cost-effective than buying in the short to medium term. The market favors buyers only for long-term holds.

Toole County has an owner-occupancy rate of 63.1%, compared to 73.0% in Montana and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more renter-heavy market than the U.S. average.
