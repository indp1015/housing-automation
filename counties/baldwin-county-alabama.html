📝 Local Market Analysis

Baldwin County presents a complex housing landscape characterized by strong economic vitality but constrained affordability. The market leans toward a renter’s advantage in terms of relative cost efficiency, evidenced by a price-to-rent ratio of 20.7, which exceeds the national average of 18.7, suggesting that purchasing is less immediately favorable than renting for those prioritizing cash flow. However, the high owner-occupancy rate of 77.6% indicates a robust base of local equity. The county’s median home value of $316,900 is 98.5% above the state average, reflecting its status as Alabama’s premium coastal market, while the median household income of $78,775 sits 15.8% above the national mean. This income level supports a price-to-income ratio of 4.02, significantly higher than the national benchmark of 3.20, highlighting a substantial affordability gap for average earners. Recent momentum has shifted from rapid appreciation to stabilization. While home values surged 17.4% in 2022, the FHFA House Price Index now shows a modest 2.26% year-over-year increase, underperforming the state’s 2.79% growth rate. This deceleration contrasts with the persistent upward trajectory of home values, which have risen from $182,000 in 2017 to $316,900 in 2024. The divergence between slowing price growth and steady rent burdens, currently at 19.5% of income compared to the national 17.2%, suggests that while new construction is adding supply—2,665 units authorized in 2025, predominantly single-family—prices remain elevated relative to local purchasing power. The strong economic foundation, supported by 76,195 jobs and an average weekly wage of $1,013, alongside a net migration inflow of 9,022 filers, sustains demand despite the cooling price appreciation. The overall market assessment is driven by high vitality, reflected in strong migration and employment metrics, but tempered by low affordability scores. The stability component remains solid at 66.9, anchored by the consistent income levels and high homeownership rates. However, the affordability metric, scoring only 19.9, is the primary drag on the composite evaluation. For potential buyers, the 7.40% national mortgage rate exacerbates the monthly cost burden, making the entry price point challenging relative to the local income profile. Renters may find more flexibility given the lower rent burden compared to other high-cost coastal areas, though the price-to-rent ratio indicates that long-term equity accumulation via purchase remains a strategic consideration for those with incomes above the median. The market is neither a buyer’s nor a renter’s market in the traditional sense, but rather a high-vitality, low-affordability environment where income thresholds strictly determine access to homeownership.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Baldwin County is 98.5% above the Alabama state average and 39.2% above the U.S. national average. The median rent of $1,277/month is 55.7% above the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 20.7, renting is generally more cost-effective than buying in the short to medium term. The market favors buyers only for long-term holds.

Baldwin County has an owner-occupancy rate of 77.6%, compared to 73.2% in Alabama and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more homeowner-heavy market than the U.S. average.
