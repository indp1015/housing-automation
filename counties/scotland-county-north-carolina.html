📝 Local Market Analysis

Scotland County, North Carolina, presents a complex market dynamic characterized by extreme price acceleration against a backdrop of constrained local economic capacity. The area functions as a renter’s market in terms of valuation, evidenced by a price-to-rent ratio of 12.3, significantly below the national average of 18.7, suggesting that purchasing is comparatively more attractive than long-term renting. However, the market is currently experiencing a sharp upward correction in home values. The FHFA House Price Index indicates a recent year-over-year increase of 18.81%, dramatically outpacing the state average of 0.38%. This surge follows a period of moderate growth, with median home values rising from $85,200 in 2017 to $114,900 in 2024, marking a cumulative increase of approximately 35% over seven years. Affordability remains a critical constraint, with a price-to-income ratio of 2.51 that, while below the national average of 3.20, is strained by the rapid price appreciation. The median household income of $45,730 lags significantly behind both national and state averages, creating a disparity where housing costs are rising faster than local earnings. This divergence is highlighted by the stability metric, which reflects the volatility of the recent price spikes compared to the slower growth in rents, which averaged $778 per month. The rent burden stands at 20.4% of income, exceeding the national average of 17.2%, indicating that tenants face moderate financial pressure despite the low absolute rent levels. The local economic context reveals a tight supply-demand imbalance. With only 59 new single-family units authorized in 2025 and zero multi-family construction, the housing supply is insufficient to absorb the net inflow of 711 migrant filers observed between 2021 and 2022. Local employment supports this limited growth, with 10,464 total jobs and an average weekly wage of $973. The high momentum score is driven entirely by the exceptional 18.81% price growth, which contrasts sharply with the low vitality and stability scores. This suggests that while the market is gaining traction due to limited inventory and migration, the underlying economic fundamentals, including the 7.40% mortgage rate and modest wage growth, do not yet support the current valuation trajectory. Buyers should note that the rapid appreciation has compressed the affordability gap, making entry more difficult despite the county’s historically low price points relative to the national average.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Scotland County is 51.2% below the North Carolina state average and 49.5% below the U.S. national average. The median rent of $778/month is 20.3% below the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 12.3, buying a home in Scotland County is generally more favorable than renting for those planning to stay long-term. A ratio below 15 typically signals that purchasing is financially advantageous over a multi-year horizon.

Scotland County has an owner-occupancy rate of 61.8%, compared to 71.8% in North Carolina and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more renter-heavy market than the U.S. average.
