📝 Local Market Analysis

Rowan County, Kentucky, functions as a balanced market with a slight lean toward renters, evidenced by a price-to-rent ratio of 16.3, which is below the national average of 18.7. This metric suggests that purchasing is not significantly more advantageous than renting compared to broader U.S. trends. The county’s median home value of $161,800 sits just 0.6% above the state average but remains 28.9% below the national mean, positioning it as an affordable entry point relative to the wider economy. With an owner-occupancy rate of 61.5%, the market exhibits a healthy mix of ownership and rental demand, supported by a median gross rent of $825 per month. Recent momentum indicates a robust recovery phase, with the FHFA House Price Index showing a 12.10% year-over-year increase in 2025, significantly outperforming the state’s 4.26% growth by 7.84 percentage points. This sharp acceleration follows a period of steady appreciation, where values rose from $121,100 in 2017 to $161,800 in 2024, peaking at 8.9% annual growth in 2022 before moderating to 3.8% in 2024. The divergence between the recent 12.1% HPI spike and the prior 3.8% Census-based growth suggests a recent surge in transaction prices, likely driven by limited inventory. Indeed, only eight new units were authorized in 2025, comprising three single-family and five multi-family homes, indicating a severe supply constraint that likely fuels current price appreciation. Affordability remains a critical constraint despite the low price-to-income ratio of 2.98, which is favorable compared to the national average of 3.20. However, the rent burden stands at 18.2% of income, exceeding the national average of 17.2%, suggesting that rental costs are becoming a heavier financial load for residents. The local economic foundation is modest, with total employment at 8,163 jobs and an average weekly wage of $963, while net migration of +547 filers between 2021 and 2022 shows stable population growth. The national 30-year fixed mortgage rate of 7.40% further complicates purchasing power, as high interest rates offset the county’s lower absolute home prices. Consequently, while the market demonstrates strong momentum and vitality due to supply scarcity and price gains, affordability and stability metrics remain challenged by rising costs relative to local incomes.
