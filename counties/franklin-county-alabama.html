📝 Local Market Analysis

Franklin County, Alabama, operates as a distinct buyer’s market, characterized by significant affordability advantages relative to national benchmarks. The median home value of $136,000 stands 40.3% below the national average, while the price-to-income ratio of 2.55 is markedly lower than the national mean of 3.20. This affordability is reinforced by a rent burden of 15.2%, which is below the national average of 17.2%, suggesting that housing costs do not disproportionately strain household budgets. The price-to-rent ratio of 16.8 further indicates that purchasing remains a more financially efficient option than renting compared to the national standard of 18.7. These metrics collectively justify the classification of the county as favorable for homeownership, particularly for local earners with a median household income of $53,338. The multi-year trend reveals a period of robust appreciation followed by recent stabilization. Home values surged from $87,600 in 2017 to $136,000 in 2024, with a peak year-over-year increase of 13.4% in 2022. However, the most recent FHFA HPI data for 2025 shows a modest county year-over-year change of +0.96%, significantly underperforming the state’s +2.79% growth. This divergence highlights a notable pattern where Franklin County’s price momentum has decelerated relative to the broader Alabama market. While home values are rising faster than rents, the recent slowdown in price appreciation suggests a maturing market phase rather than speculative overheating. The local economic context supports this stability, with total employment at 9,012 jobs and an average weekly wage of $952. Housing supply remains constrained, with only 27 new units authorized in 2025, including 15 single-family homes, which may limit future inventory increases. Migration data indicates a net inflow of 344 filers between 2021 and 2022, with incoming residents earning an average of $54,170, slightly below the outflow average. This modest demographic growth aligns with the steady, albeit slowing, price trajectory. The current 30-year fixed mortgage rate of 7.40% imposes higher monthly payment burdens, yet the low entry price point mitigates this impact for qualified buyers. For potential purchasers, the combination of low price-to-income ratios and slowing price growth offers a favorable entry point compared to national averages. Renters may find limited leverage, as the low rent burden suggests stable rental demand. The market’s relative underperformance against the state average suggests that Franklin County is not currently experiencing the rapid appreciation seen in other parts of Alabama, positioning it as a stable, affordable option within a broader regional context of moderate economic activity.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Franklin County is 14.8% below the Alabama state average and 40.3% below the U.S. national average. The median rent of $676/month is 17.6% below the state average.
