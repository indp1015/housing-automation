📝 Local Market Analysis

Franklin County, Alabama, operates as a distinctively affordable market, characterized by a price-to-income ratio of 2.55, significantly below the national average of 3.20. The median home value of $136,000 sits 14.8% below the state average and 40.3% below the national mean, reflecting a buyer’s advantage driven by low entry costs rather than high demand. This affordability is mirrored in the rental sector, where the rent burden stands at 15.2% of income, under the national benchmark of 17.2%. Consequently, the market is classified as a buyer’s environment, supported by a price-to-rent ratio of 16.8 that remains below the national 18.7, suggesting that purchasing offers better long-term value than renting for qualified households. The multi-year trend reveals a period of rapid appreciation followed by stabilization. Home values surged from $87,600 in 2017 to $136,000 in 2024, with a peak year-over-year increase of 13.4% in 2022. However, recent momentum has cooled sharply, with the FHFA HPI showing only a 0.96% year-over-year change in 2025, underperforming the state’s 2.79% growth by 1.83 percentage points. This divergence between historical price acceleration and current stagnation highlights a market in transition. Notably, home values are rising faster than rents, indicating a shift in economic leverage toward ownership, although the absolute price levels remain modest. The low vitality score of 23.3 underscores structural constraints, including limited new supply with only 27 units authorized in 2025 and a small employment base of 9,012 jobs. Economic fundamentals provide a mixed backdrop for this market. While net migration of 344 filers suggests modest population growth, the average weekly wage of $952 and median household income of $53,338 limit purchasing power. The national 30-year mortgage rate of 7.40% further pressures affordability, yet the low price-to-income ratio mitigates this impact compared to higher-cost regions. Potential buyers benefit from significant equity potential relative to national averages, while renters face lower financial strain. The stability score of 52.1 reflects the tension between strong historical price growth and current economic headwinds. Ultimately, Franklin County presents a low-cost entry point for housing, where affordability metrics strongly favor ownership, but economic vitality and recent price momentum suggest limited short-term appreciation drivers compared to broader state trends.
