📝 Editor's Insight

New Castle County, Delaware, functions as a balanced market with a slight lean toward buyer advantage, characterized by a 69.0% owner-occupancy rate and a price-to-rent ratio of 20.3. This ratio sits above the national average of 18.7, indicating that long-term ownership is generally more cost-effective than renting, although the margin is narrowing. The market’s stability is further evidenced by a median household income of $91,166, which supports the current median home value of $352,200. While the county’s home values are 54.7% higher than the national average, they align almost perfectly with the state average, differing by only 0.5%, suggesting the county is a primary driver of Delaware’s broader housing economics. Affordability metrics reveal a moderate strain relative to national benchmarks. The price-to-income ratio stands at 3.86, significantly exceeding the national average of 3.20, indicating that home prices have outpaced local income growth. Similarly, the rent burden is 19.1% of income, compared to the 17.2% national norm. However, the most notable pattern is the divergence between asset appreciation and rental costs. Home values surged 13.2% in 2022 before moderating to 6.8% in 2024, whereas the current FHFA HPI indicates a recent year-over-year change of only 2.82%. This deceleration in price growth, which currently trails the state’s 3.23% increase by 0.41 percentage points, suggests the market is cooling from its 2022 peak. The trend signal confirms that home values are rising faster than rents, but the recent HPI data implies this gap is stabilizing as price volatility decreases. For prospective stakeholders, the data indicates a market transitioning from rapid appreciation to steady growth. The 2.82% recent HPI change signals a normalization of pricing after the 2022 spike. Buyers should note that while the price-to-income ratio is elevated, the slowing momentum in the FHFA index suggests less aggressive competition compared to the 2022 peak. Renters face a 19.1% income burden, which is manageable but above the national average, reinforcing the financial logic of ownership given the 20.3 price-to-rent ratio. The county’s performance, tracking slightly below the state average in recent quarters, highlights a localized cooling effect that may offer a more stable entry point than the broader regional trend. Ultimately, the market is defined by high absolute values but moderating growth rates, requiring careful alignment of household income with the 3.86 price-to-income multiple to ensure long-term financial viability.

Market Analysis

The median home value in New Castle County is about equal to the Delaware state average and 54.7% above the U.S. national average. The median rent of $1,448/month is 7.6% above the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 20.3, renting is generally more cost-effective than buying in the short to medium term. The market favors buyers only for long-term holds.

New Castle County has an owner-occupancy rate of 69.0%, compared to 74.4% in Delaware and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more renter-heavy market than the U.S. average.
