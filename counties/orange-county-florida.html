📝 Local Market Notes

Orange County, Florida, operates as a moderately balanced market, though affordability metrics skew toward buyer disadvantage. The price-to-income ratio of 4.89 significantly exceeds the national average of 3.20, indicating that median home values are disproportionately high relative to local earning power. Conversely, the rent burden stands at 26.7%, well above the 17.2% national norm, suggesting that rental costs also impose a substantial financial strain on households. The price-to-rent ratio of 18.3 aligns closely with the national average of 18.7, implying that the relative cost of owning versus renting is not the primary driver of market stress; rather, absolute price levels are the dominant factor. The market has experienced rapid appreciation, with median home values rising from $192,400 in 2017 to $390,100 in 2024. This represents a compound annual growth rate of approximately 12.3% over the seven-year period, peaking with an 18.8% surge in 2022 before moderating to 8.9% in both 2023 and 2024. Recent momentum has further decelerated, with the FHFA House Price Index showing only a 0.81% year-over-year increase in 2025, slightly lagging the state average by 0.10 percentage points. This slowdown suggests a stabilization phase following the post-pandemic boom. Notably, home values are rising faster than rents, a divergence that may limit the incentive for renters to transition to ownership despite the high entry costs. Regional context highlights Orange County’s premium status, with home values 37.2% above the Florida state average and 71.3% above the national mean. This premium is supported by strong economic fundamentals, including 870,276 total jobs and an average weekly wage of $1,356. However, the 7.40% national mortgage rate exacerbates affordability challenges, increasing monthly debt service for potential buyers. Housing supply remains robust, with 13,140 new units authorized in 2025, including 8,919 multi-family units, which may help moderate future inflationary pressure on rents. Net migration of 10,529 filers between 2021 and 2022 indicates sustained demographic inflow, with incoming households earning an average of $86,219, slightly higher than outgoing residents. For prospective buyers, the combination of high price-to-income ratios and elevated mortgage rates creates a challenging entry environment, while renters face significant cost burdens relative to their income levels. The market’s trajectory suggests a shift from rapid appreciation to a more stable, supply-influenced equilibrium.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Orange County is 37.2% above the Florida state average and 71.3% above the U.S. national average. The median rent of $1,775/month is 36.2% above the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 18.3, the buy-vs-rent decision in Orange County is balanced. Whether buying or renting makes more sense depends on your time horizon, down payment capacity, and local market conditions.

Orange County has an owner-occupancy rate of 56.8%, compared to 74.6% in Florida and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more renter-heavy market than the U.S. average.
