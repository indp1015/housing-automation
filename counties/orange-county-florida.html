📝 Local Market Analysis

Orange County, Florida, functions as a distinctly buyer’s market, characterized by significant affordability constraints relative to national benchmarks. The price-to-income ratio stands at 4.89, substantially exceeding the national average of 3.20, while the rent burden consumes 26.7% of median household income compared to the 17.2% national norm. These metrics indicate that both purchasing and leasing remain financially demanding for the average resident, despite a median household income of $79,719. The multi-year trend reveals a period of rapid appreciation that is now moderating. Home values increased from $192,400 in 2017 to $390,100 in 2024, with a peak year-over-year surge of 18.8% in 2022. However, recent momentum has slowed, with the FHFA House Price Index showing only a 0.81% year-over-year increase in 2025, slightly lagging the state’s 0.91% growth. This deceleration contrasts with the historical trend where home values rose faster than rents, a pattern that has begun to stabilize as the price-to-rent ratio of 18.3 aligns closely with the national average of 18.7. This convergence suggests that the previous divergence between asset appreciation and rental costs is narrowing, offering a more balanced valuation landscape. Regionally, Orange County home values are 37.2% higher than the Florida state average and 71.3% above the national mean, reflecting its status as a high-cost area within a high-growth state. The local economy supports this premium through 870,276 total jobs and an average weekly wage of $1,356. Housing supply remains robust, with 13,140 new units authorized in 2025, including 8,919 multi-family units, which helps mitigate supply shocks. Net migration of +10,529 filers indicates sustained demographic inflow, though the high 7.40% national mortgage rate continues to suppress demand-side purchasing power. The overall market assessment is driven primarily by low affordability scores and moderate stability, while vitality remains relatively strong due to consistent employment and population growth. For prospective buyers, the current environment offers entry points at historically high price points but with slower appreciation rates, whereas renters face elevated cost burdens that may not decrease significantly in the near term.

The median home value in Orange County is 37.2% above the Florida state average and 71.3% above the U.S. national average. The median rent of $1,775/month is 36.2% above the state average.
