📝 Editor's Insight

Routt County exhibits a severe renter’s market imbalance, evidenced by a price-to-rent ratio of 34.2, nearly double the national average of 18.0. This indicates that purchasing is significantly more expensive relative to rental costs than in typical U.S. markets. The five-year trend reveals synchronized appreciation, with home values and rents both compounding at approximately 9.0% to 9.5% annually. This parallel growth suggests that the local housing supply is constrained, driving both asset prices and monthly occupancy costs upward simultaneously. Affordability is critically strained, as the price-to-income ratio sits at 7.22, more than double the national benchmark of 3.09. Additionally, the rent burden consumes 21.1% of median household income, exceeding the 17.2% national norm. A critical, non-obvious insight is that despite incomes running 58.8% above the national average, they have failed to keep pace with the 41.3% surge in median home values since 2019. Consequently, even high-income earners face diminishing purchasing power, indicating that local wage growth has structurally lagged behind the rapid expansion of the real estate sector.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Routt County is 99.9% above the Colorado state average and 275.4% above the U.S. national average. The median rent of $1,917/month is 47.2% above the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 37.2, Routt County is a strongly rent-favored market. Home prices are high relative to rents, meaning renting is typically far cheaper than owning in the near term.

Routt County has an owner-occupancy rate of 76.0%, compared to 71.9% in Colorado and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more homeowner-heavy market than the U.S. average.
