📝 Local Market Analysis

Andrew County, Missouri, operates as a balanced market leaning toward affordability, characterized by a price-to-income ratio of 2.85, which is below the national average of 3.20. The rent burden stands at 15.2% of income, also under the national benchmark of 17.2%, indicating that housing costs remain manageable relative to local earnings. This affordability is reinforced by a median household income of $75,625, which exceeds the national average by 11.2%, providing residents with stronger purchasing power compared to the broader U.S. population. The market classification is further supported by a price-to-rent ratio of 18.7, aligning precisely with the national average, suggesting that neither renting nor buying holds a distinct structural advantage based on cost metrics alone. The trend data reveals a significant acceleration in home values, with annual growth rates climbing from 1.6% in 2020 to 9.8% in 2024. This rapid appreciation has pushed the median home value to $215,400, which is 22.6% above the state average, despite being 5.4% below the national mean. However, recent momentum has decelerated; the FHFA HPI shows a year-over-year increase of only 1.71%, underperforming the state’s 3.71% growth by 2.00 percentage points. This divergence highlights a cooling effect, likely influenced by the high 30-year fixed mortgage rate of 7.40%, which dampens buyer demand. The local economy supports this stability with 2,065 total jobs and an average weekly wage of $791, while net migration of +373 filers indicates a modest population influx. Housing supply remains extremely constrained, with only three new units authorized in 2025, limiting the ability of new construction to alleviate price pressures. The overall market assessment is driven by strong affordability and stability, which offset lower vitality scores. The low vitality metric reflects the limited new construction pipeline and modest economic expansion, as evidenced by the small number of establishments and jobs. While the long-term trend shows robust price appreciation, the recent flattening of growth signals a maturing market phase. For potential buyers, the current environment offers entry points at prices below national averages, yet the high mortgage rates and limited new supply mean that inventory remains tight. Renters benefit from a low rent burden, but the rising home values suggest that the gap between renting and buying costs is narrowing. The data indicates a market where affordability remains a key competitive factor, but momentum is shifting from rapid growth to stabilization.
