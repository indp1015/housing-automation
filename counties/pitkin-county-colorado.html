📝 Local Market Analysis

Pitkin County operates as an extreme seller’s market, characterized by a price-to-income ratio of 11.10, which is nearly four times the national average of 3.20. The median home value of $1,139,100 exceeds the state average by 166.4% and the national average by 400.3%, while the median household income of $102,645 is only 51.0% above the national norm. This disparity creates severe housing stress, evidenced by a rent burden of 23.4% compared to the national 17.2% and a price-to-rent ratio of 47.4, nearly triple the national benchmark of 18.7. Consequently, the market exhibits low affordability and stability, with vitality remaining high due to strong demand indicators. Recent data reveals a significant divergence between long-term appreciation and current momentum. While home values surged from $593,600 in 2017 to $1,139,100 in 2024, the most recent FHFA HPI indicates a 4.75% year-over-year decline, underperforming the state by 4.14 percentage points. This deceleration follows a period of explosive growth, with peak year-over-year increases of 24.9% in 2022 and 24.0% in 2023, before moderating to 0.7% in 2024. The sharp contrast between the multi-year upward trajectory and the current negative quarterly momentum suggests a market correction phase. Meanwhile, the local economy supports this high-cost environment through high average weekly wages of $1,569 and robust net migration of 848 filers, with in-migrants earning an average income of $560,492. Supply constraints further exacerbate affordability issues. Only 36 new single-family units were authorized in 2025, with zero multi-family construction, against a population of 16,985. This limited supply, combined with a 7.40% national mortgage rate, restricts buyer eligibility and sustains high entry barriers. The owner-occupancy rate of 62.8% indicates a balanced occupancy structure, but the financial burden on residents remains disproportionate to local income levels. The market’s primary drivers are low affordability and reduced stability due to recent price volatility, offset by high vitality from sustained wealth inflows and employment strength. Potential participants must recognize that while economic fundamentals remain strong, the current price environment offers minimal leverage for buyers, and the recent price decline reflects a broader revaluation rather than a structural collapse, given the persistent high-income migration and wage premiums.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Pitkin County is 166.4% above the Colorado state average and 400.3% above the U.S. national average. The median rent of $2,004/month is 53.9% above the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 47.4, Pitkin County is a strongly rent-favored market. Home prices are high relative to rents, meaning renting is typically far cheaper than owning in the near term.

Pitkin County has an owner-occupancy rate of 62.8%, compared to 71.9% in Colorado and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more renter-heavy market than the U.S. average.
