📝 Local Market Analysis

Lafayette County presents a buyer-favorable market characterized by significant undervaluation relative to national and state benchmarks. The price-to-income ratio of 2.50 sits well below the 3.20 national average, while the price-to-rent ratio of 14.6 remains substantially under the 18.7 national figure, indicating ownership economics favor buying over renting. Rent burden at 17.1% of median income aligns with the 17.2% national average, suggesting rental affordability is neutral. The 77.2% owner-occupancy rate reflects a stable, owner-dominated community with limited investor competition. Price appreciation has accelerated dramatically since 2020, rising from $100,600 to $157,200—a 56.3% increase over four years—with the last three years showing compounding momentum at 15.8%, 11.0%, and 19.5% annually. This trajectory diverges sharply from rent growth, as evidenced by the declining price-to-rent ratio, signaling that home values are decoupling from fundamental cash-flow metrics. The county's median home value sits 31.0% below the national average and 44.7% below the Florida state average, while median income trails the nation by only 7.7%, creating a pronounced value gap. Market Analysis

The median home value in Lafayette County is 44.7% below the Florida state average and 31.0% below the U.S. national average. The median rent of $896/month is 31.2% below the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 14.6, buying a home in Lafayette County is generally more favorable than renting for those planning to stay long-term. A ratio below 15 typically signals that purchasing is financially advantageous over a multi-year horizon.

Lafayette County has an owner-occupancy rate of 77.2%, compared to 74.6% in Florida and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more homeowner-heavy market than the U.S. average.
