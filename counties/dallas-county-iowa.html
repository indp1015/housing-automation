📝 Local Market Analysis

Dallas County, Iowa, functions as a distinctively strong renter’s market, characterized by significant price-to-rent disparities that diverge sharply from national norms. The price-to-rent ratio stands at 22.6, substantially higher than the national average of 18.7, indicating that purchasing is currently less cost-effective relative to renting when compared to broader U.S. benchmarks. This classification is reinforced by a rent burden of 15.4%, which is lower than the national average of 17.2%, suggesting that rental housing remains relatively accessible despite rising values. The market exhibits a clear divergence between asset appreciation and rental yield, where home values have outpaced rent growth, creating a structural imbalance that favors those already holding equity or seeking affordable rental options. The trend interpretation reveals a period of accelerated appreciation followed by recent stabilization. Home values surged by 15.0% in 2022, moderating to 6.7% in 2024, while the FHFA HPI indicates a recent YoY increase of only 1.36% in 2025. This recent slowdown places the county underperforming relative to the state average of 2.25%, signaling a cooling of the previous momentum. Despite this moderation, the median home value of $355,600 remains 107.9% above the state average and 56.2% above the national average. This premium is supported by robust local economic fundamentals, including a median household income of $102,379, which exceeds the national average by 50.6%. The price-to-income ratio of 3.47 is slightly elevated compared to the national benchmark of 3.20, reflecting the high cost of entry relative to local earnings. Notable patterns include a strong positive net migration of 3,578 filers between 2021 and 2022, with inflow households earning an average of $138,682. This demographic influx, combined with 875 new units authorized in 2025, suggests ongoing demand pressure that may sustain value retention. However, the current 7.40% national mortgage rate significantly impacts purchasing power, making the high price-to-income ratio a critical barrier for new entrants. The local economy, supported by 50,982 jobs and an average weekly wage of $1,572, provides a stable foundation, yet the disparity between the high home values and the lower rent burden creates a bifurcated market. For potential buyers, the high entry cost relative to income and the favorable rent burden suggest that renting may offer superior short-term financial flexibility. Conversely, long-term holders benefit from the substantial appreciation observed between 2017 and 2024, where values increased from $218,000 to $355,600. The data indicates a market where rental affordability is preserved even as ownership costs remain elevated relative to both state and national peers.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Dallas County is 107.9% above the Iowa state average and 56.2% above the U.S. national average. The median rent of $1,311/month is 57.6% above the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 22.6, renting is generally more cost-effective than buying in the short to medium term. The market favors buyers only for long-term holds.

Dallas County has an owner-occupancy rate of 69.0%, compared to 75.8% in Iowa and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more renter-heavy market than the U.S. average.
