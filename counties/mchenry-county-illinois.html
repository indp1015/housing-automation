📝 Local Market Analysis

McHenry County presents a balanced housing market characterized by strong stability and momentum, though affordability remains a secondary concern. The county’s 82.7% owner occupancy rate and a price-to-rent ratio of 18.3, slightly below the national average of 18.7, indicate a stable environment where ownership is prevalent but renting remains viable. With a median home value of $308,100 and a median household income of $104,802, the price-to-income ratio stands at 2.94, outperforming the national benchmark of 3.20. This suggests that while entry costs are high, local earning power supports ownership better than the national average. The rent burden of 16.0% also trails the national 17.2%, further reinforcing the balanced nature of the market for both tenants and buyers. Trend analysis reveals significant acceleration in home values, with year-over-year growth jumping from 3.2% in 2021 to 12.0% in 2022, followed by sustained increases of 6.0% and 7.4% in subsequent years. This trajectory diverges from rent growth, where the median gross rent of $1,401 has not kept pace with the rapid appreciation of home values. The FHFA HPI confirms this momentum, showing a 5.85% year-over-year increase in 2025, which slightly outpaces the state’s 5.54% growth. This relative outperformance highlights McHenry County’s strength within Illinois, where home values are 101.3% above the state average. The county’s economic foundation supports this growth, with 83,397 total jobs and an average weekly wage of $1,091. Additionally, net migration of +4,075 filers between 2021 and 2022, driven by inflows averaging $100,139 in income, injects consistent demand into the market. Supply dynamics and interest rates currently temper extreme volatility. With 1,393 new units authorized in 2025, including 919 single-family homes, the construction pipeline attempts to match demand, yet the 7.40% mortgage rate limits buyer purchasing power. The high stability metric, reflected in the consistent multi-year appreciation without correction, contrasts with the moderate vitality score, which is constrained by the high absolute price point relative to national averages. Ultimately, the market is driven by robust local incomes and positive migration, creating a resilient environment where price growth is sustained by economic fundamentals rather than speculative bubbles. Buyers face higher entry barriers due to the 35.3% premium over national home values, but the favorable income-to-price ratio and strong job market provide a solid foundation for long-term equity accumulation.
