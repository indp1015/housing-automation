📝 Local Market Analysis

Benson County, North Dakota, functions as a distinctly buyer’s market, characterized by extreme affordability metrics that significantly undercut national benchmarks. The price-to-income ratio stands at 1.41, less than half the national average of 3.20, while the rent burden is only 11.0% of median household income compared to the national 17.2%. With a median home value of $90,000 and median gross rent of $588, the price-to-rent ratio of 12.8 indicates that purchasing is financially superior to renting when compared to the national ratio of 18.7. These figures suggest that housing costs impose minimal financial strain on residents, creating a highly accessible entry point for potential homeowners relative to their $64,033 median income. The market exhibits strong recent momentum, reversing a brief 2.0% decline in 2022 with an 8.3% year-over-year increase in 2024 to reach the current $90,000 median. This acceleration follows a period of moderate growth between 2017 and 2021, where values rose from $68,900 to $80,900. The parallel rise in home values and rents suggests a synchronized market adjustment rather than a divergence, indicating that both rental and ownership sectors are responding to similar underlying economic pressures. However, the county’s overall stability remains moderate, reflecting the volatility inherent in small, rural economies where single-year fluctuations can disproportionately impact long-term averages. Regional context highlights Benson County’s position well below state and national averages, with home values 48.8% lower than the North Dakota average and 60.5% below the national mean. This discount is partially offset by income levels that are only 5.8% below the national average, enhancing relative affordability. The local economic base is constrained, with only 503 total jobs and 102 establishments, yet the average weekly wage of $1,291 supports the observed income levels. Migration data shows a net gain of 47 filers, indicating slight population stabilization rather than rapid growth. The high vitality score is notably low at 22.8, reflecting the limited economic dynamism and small population of 5,808. Consequently, the balanced overall assessment of 64.3 is driven primarily by exceptional affordability, which compensates for the lack of vibrant economic expansion and limited local employment opportunities. For prospective buyers, the low entry cost and favorable financing environment, despite the 7.40% mortgage rate, present a compelling value proposition, though the small market size limits liquidity and resale options.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Benson County is 48.8% below the North Dakota state average and 60.5% below the U.S. national average. The median rent of $588/month is 23.8% below the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 12.8, buying a home in Benson County is generally more favorable than renting for those planning to stay long-term. A ratio below 15 typically signals that purchasing is financially advantageous over a multi-year horizon.

Benson County has an owner-occupancy rate of 73.1%, compared to 73.6% in North Dakota and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more renter-heavy market than the U.S. average.
