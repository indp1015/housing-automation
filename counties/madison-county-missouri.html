📝 Local Market Analysis

Madison County, Missouri, exhibits characteristics of a balanced market with distinct affordability advantages, characterized by a Price-to-Income Ratio of 2.92, which is below the national average of 3.20. The median home value of $159,000 sits 30.2% below the national mean, while the median household income of $54,434 is 19.9% lower than the national benchmark. This relative affordability is reinforced by a Rent Burden of 17.8%, nearly identical to the national average of 17.2%, and a Price-to-Rent Ratio of 16.4, indicating that ownership costs are comparatively low relative to rental expenses. The market is not a distressed buyer’s market but rather an accessible entry point for local earners, supported by a 73.7% owner-occupancy rate that suggests stable residential tenure. The multi-year trend reveals significant volatility followed by recent stabilization. After a sharp 22.5% increase in 2023, home values moderated to a 2.0% gain in 2024. However, recent FHFA data indicates a 0.64% year-over-year decline in 2025, underperforming the state’s 3.71% growth by 4.35 percentage points. This divergence highlights a cooling momentum phase. While home values have risen faster than rents historically, the current negative price trajectory contrasts with the state’s positive growth, suggesting localized softness. The economic base supports this mixed outlook, with only 2,718 total jobs and an average weekly wage of $794, which aligns closely with the median household income. Housing supply remains constrained, with 40 new units authorized in 2025, all multi-family, and zero single-family permits, potentially limiting entry-level options. Migration data shows a net inflow of 275 filers between 2021 and 2022, with average incomes for inflows ($53,123) slightly lower than outflows ($53,661), indicating modest demographic stability rather than a boom. The national 30-year mortgage rate of 7.40% significantly impacts carrying costs, making the low price-to-income ratio a critical factor for potential buyers. The overall market assessment is driven by strong affordability metrics but tempered by declining price momentum and limited economic vitality. The stability axis is pressured by the recent price dip, while vitality is constrained by the small employment base. For prospective residents, the low entry price offers value, but the lack of new single-family construction and the recent price correction suggest a cautious approach to investment timing. The market remains attractive for end-users seeking affordability, but investors may face headwinds from the current price stagnation relative to the broader Missouri market.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Madison County is 9.5% below the Missouri state average and 30.2% below the U.S. national average. The median rent of $808/month is 3.3% above the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 16.4, the buy-vs-rent decision in Madison County is balanced. Whether buying or renting makes more sense depends on your time horizon, down payment capacity, and local market conditions.

Madison County has an owner-occupancy rate of 73.7%, compared to 73.7% in Missouri and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more homeowner-heavy market than the U.S. average.
