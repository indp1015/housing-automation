📝 Editor's Insight

Yolo County operates as a distinct buyer’s market, evidenced by a 53.6% owner-occupancy rate and a price-to-rent ratio of 28.2, significantly exceeding the national average of 18.1. This metric indicates that purchasing is statistically more cost-effective than renting relative to local income levels. Over the past five years, home values appreciated at an 8.7% CAGR, outpacing the 7.3% CAGR in median gross rent. This divergence suggests capital gains in the housing stock are accelerating faster than rental yields, potentially narrowing the long-term financial gap between owning and renting. Affordability remains constrained; the price-to-income ratio stands at 6.69, more than double the national benchmark of 3.10. While the rent burden of 23.7% exceeds the national average of 17.2%, it remains below the 30% threshold for severe cost strain. A critical insight for residents is that despite home values sitting only 4.0% above the state average, the local income level is 33.9% above the national mean. This income disparity partially offsets the high price-to-income ratio, making Yolo County relatively more accessible than its raw price metrics suggest when compared against broader national standards rather than just state peers.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Yolo County is 1.9% above the California state average and 172.6% above the U.S. national average. The median rent of $1,857/month is 9.9% above the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 27.9, Yolo County is a strongly rent-favored market. Home prices are high relative to rents, meaning renting is typically far cheaper than owning in the near term.

Yolo County has an owner-occupancy rate of 54.3%, compared to 63.4% in California and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more renter-heavy market than the U.S. average.
