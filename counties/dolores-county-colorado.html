📝 Local Market Analysis

Dolores County presents a complex micro-market characterized by rapid appreciation against a backdrop of limited economic infrastructure. With a median home value of $247,600 and a median household income of $64,907, the price-to-income ratio stands at 3.81, exceeding the national average of 3.20. This disparity, combined with a rent burden of 18.2% compared to the national 17.2%, indicates a market that is increasingly strained for both buyers and renters. The price-to-rent ratio of 20.9 further suggests that purchasing is becoming relatively more expensive than renting when compared to broader national norms, shifting the balance away from a traditional buyer’s advantage. The trend data reveals a dramatic acceleration in housing values, with median prices surging 43.8% in 2020 and continuing to climb at a 6.8% annual pace in 2024. This trajectory contrasts sharply with the county’s historical baseline; in 2017, the median value was merely $122,000. The FHFA HPI index, which recorded a 12.03% year-over-year increase in 2021, highlights a period where the county significantly outperformed the state average of -0.61%. This divergence suggests that local demand drivers are decoupled from broader state-level economic trends, likely fueled by specific inflows rather than general regional growth. Regional context underscores the county’s isolation from state averages, as Dolores home values sit 42.1% below the Colorado mean, despite being 8.7% above the national average. This anomaly is driven by a small population of 2,432 residents and a constrained employment base of only 314 jobs across 84 establishments. The net migration of +126 filers between 2021 and 2022, with incoming households earning slightly more than those leaving, indicates a modest but steady influx of higher-income residents. However, the current 7.40% mortgage rate exacerbates affordability pressures, contributing to a low vitality assessment. The stability of the market is further complicated by the high owner-occupancy rate of 81.3%, which limits rental supply and keeps gross rents at $986. Ultimately, the combination of elevated price-to-income ratios, rapid historical appreciation, and limited local economic capacity creates a stressed environment where affordability metrics lag significantly behind price growth.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Dolores County is 42.1% below the Colorado state average and 8.7% above the U.S. national average. The median rent of $986/month is 24.3% below the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 20.9, renting is generally more cost-effective than buying in the short to medium term. The market favors buyers only for long-term holds.

Dolores County has an owner-occupancy rate of 81.3%, compared to 71.9% in Colorado and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more homeowner-heavy market than the U.S. average.
