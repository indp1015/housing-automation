📝 Editor's Insight

Dolores County exhibits a distinct renter’s market structure, evidenced by a price-to-rent ratio of 11.4, significantly below the national average of 18.0. This metric indicates that purchasing property is comparatively more economical than long-term leasing relative to income levels. Over the past five years, the market experienced explosive appreciation, with home values growing at a compound annual rate of 18.8% and rents rising at 19.9%. The slightly higher rent growth suggests that rental demand is currently outpacing ownership demand, likely driven by limited housing inventory. Despite this rapid inflation, affordability remains strained. The rent burden stands at 28.7% of median household income, nearly double the national average of 17.2%, while the price-to-income ratio of 3.29 slightly exceeds the national benchmark of 3.09. A critical insight for potential residents is the disparity between local and state valuations. Home values are 41.7% below the Colorado average, yet rents have surged to levels that consume a disproportionate share of local earnings. This divergence implies that while entry-level ownership may appear accessible compared to state peers, the immediate cash flow required for renting is substantially more burdensome than regional norms suggest.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Dolores County is 42.1% below the Colorado state average and 8.7% above the U.S. national average. The median rent of $986/month is 24.3% below the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 20.9, renting is generally more cost-effective than buying in the short to medium term. The market favors buyers only for long-term holds.

Dolores County has an owner-occupancy rate of 81.3%, compared to 71.9% in Colorado and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more homeowner-heavy market than the U.S. average.
