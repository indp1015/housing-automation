📝 Local Market Analysis

Worcester County, Maryland, functions as a distinctly renter’s market, characterized by extreme valuation disparities that disadvantage homeowners relative to tenants. The price-to-rent ratio stands at 26.9, significantly exceeding the national average of 18.7, indicating that purchasing equity is substantially more expensive than leasing. This structural imbalance is reinforced by a price-to-income ratio of 4.58, which far outpaces the national benchmark of 3.20. Consequently, the affordability metric is critically low, driving the overall assessment toward a challenging status. While the rent burden sits at 17.0%, nearly identical to the national average of 17.2%, the cost of ownership remains prohibitive for the median household earning $81,745. The market exhibits strong momentum, with the FHFA HPI showing a 2.58% year-over-year increase in 2025, outperforming the state’s 0.95% growth by 1.63 percentage points. This recent acceleration follows a period of aggressive appreciation, where home values surged 12.2% in 2023 and 7.6% in 2024. However, this price growth has decoupled from rental trends, creating a divergence where home values are rising faster than rents. The 2017-2024 trend reveals a cumulative increase of nearly 49% in median home values, suggesting a sustained upward trajectory that has not been matched by corresponding income growth or rental adjustments. Regional context highlights Worcester County’s premium positioning, with median home values 64.6% above the national average and 1.3% above the state mean. This premium is supported by robust local fundamentals, including a net migration gain of 1,766 filers and an average weekly wage of $850, which exceeds typical regional norms. Despite this economic vitality, the housing supply remains constrained, with only 242 new single-family units authorized in 2025 and zero multi-family permits, limiting absorption capacity. The high owner-occupancy rate of 78.0% further reduces available inventory. With the 30-year fixed mortgage rate at 7.40%, the high price-to-income ratio exacerbates entry barriers. The composite assessment reflects high stability and vitality driven by strong migration and wage data, but these positive factors are heavily offset by the severe affordability constraints inherent in the current valuation structure.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Worcester County is 1.3% above the Maryland state average and 64.6% above the U.S. national average. The median rent of $1,159/month is 20.6% below the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 26.9, Worcester County is a strongly rent-favored market. Home prices are high relative to rents, meaning renting is typically far cheaper than owning in the near term.

Worcester County has an owner-occupancy rate of 78.0%, compared to 72.7% in Maryland and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more homeowner-heavy market than the U.S. average.
