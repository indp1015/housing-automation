📝 Local Market Analysis

Jefferson County, Iowa, exhibits characteristics of a balanced market leaning slightly toward buyers, driven by significant affordability advantages relative to national standards. The price-to-income ratio stands at 2.65, well below the national average of 3.20, while the rent burden is precisely aligned with the national mean at 17.2%. The price-to-rent ratio of 15.4 further supports ownership viability compared to the national benchmark of 18.7. These metrics indicate that housing costs remain manageable for local households, with a median home value of $163,400 and a median household income of $61,620. The owner-occupancy rate of 67.7% reflects a stable tenure structure, typical of small rural communities where equity accumulation is a primary housing strategy. The market has undergone a dramatic structural shift, transitioning from stagnation to rapid appreciation before stabilizing. Home values remained flat between 2017 and 2021, hovering near $115,000, before surging 18.4% in 2022 and 13.9% in 2023. This aggressive growth has now moderated to a 1.4% increase in 2024 and a 0.33% year-over-year change in the latest FHFA HPI data. This recent deceleration signals a cooling market, as the county is currently underperforming the Iowa state average of +2.25% by 1.92 percentage points. The divergence between the sharp price appreciation and relatively stable rents suggests that asset values have decoupled from immediate rental demand, creating a temporary gap in valuation dynamics. Regional context highlights Jefferson County’s affordability advantage, with home values sitting 28.2% below the national average and 4.5% below the state mean. Local economic fundamentals support this stability, with total employment at 5,804 jobs and an average weekly wage of $1,125. Net migration of +432 filers indicates sustained demographic inflow, though housing supply remains extremely constrained, with only two new units authorized in 2025. This scarcity, combined with a 7.40% national mortgage rate, limits rapid price expansion despite strong demand. For potential buyers, the current environment offers entry points below national cost structures, while renters face standard burden levels. The market’s trajectory suggests a transition from speculative growth to fundamental stability, anchored by local income levels and limited new construction.
