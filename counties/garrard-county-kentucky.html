📝 Local Market Analysis

Garrard County, Kentucky, presents a complex housing landscape characterized by a significant affordability gap despite strong price appreciation. The market leans toward a buyer’s disadvantage in terms of entry costs, evidenced by a price-to-income ratio of 3.25, which slightly exceeds the national average of 3.20. However, the rent burden sits at 15.1% of income, well below the national norm of 17.2%, suggesting that renting remains a more financially sustainable option for many residents. The price-to-rent ratio of 21.5, compared to the national average of 18.7, further indicates that purchasing is less economically attractive than renting when calculated on a pure cost basis. This divergence highlights a market where ownership costs have outpaced rental obligations, creating a bifurcated dynamic for potential residents. The multi-year trend reveals a period of aggressive price inflation, with home values rising from $131,000 in 2017 to $204,900 in 2024, representing a cumulative increase of approximately 56.4%. Annual growth rates accelerated sharply between 2021 and 2024, peaking at 9.7% in the latest ACS data. In contrast, the FHFA HPI indicates a recent slowdown, with a 3.00% year-over-year change in 2025, underperforming the state average of 4.26%. This deceleration suggests that the rapid appreciation phase may be moderating. The local economic context supports this shift, as total employment stands at only 1,978 jobs with an average weekly wage of $1,031, limiting the local purchasing power necessary to sustain such high price growth. Furthermore, the county’s home values are 27.4% above the state average, yet median household income is 7.2% below the national mean, creating a structural mismatch between asset values and local earnings. Stability metrics remain robust, driven by a high owner-occupancy rate of 79.9% and consistent net migration inflows of 387 filers in the 2021-2022 period. However, vitality indicators are weak, reflected in the low total establishment count of 422 and the small population base of 17,568. The current 7.40% national mortgage rate exacerbates affordability challenges, making the high price-to-income ratio even more pronounced. For potential buyers, the data suggests that while the market has cooled from its peak growth rates, entry barriers remain high relative to local wages. Renters may find better value, given the low rent burden and the disparity between purchase and rental costs. The overall market stability is maintained by entrenched ownership, but the lack of economic vitality and rising costs pose long-term challenges for new entrants seeking to purchase in this rural Kentucky county.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Garrard County is 27.4% above the Kentucky state average and 10.0% below the U.S. national average. The median rent of $794/month is about equal to the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 21.5, renting is generally more cost-effective than buying in the short to medium term. The market favors buyers only for long-term holds.

Garrard County has an owner-occupancy rate of 79.9%, compared to 73.4% in Kentucky and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more homeowner-heavy market than the U.S. average.
