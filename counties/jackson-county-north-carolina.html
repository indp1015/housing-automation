📝 Local Market Analysis

Jackson County, North Carolina, exhibits characteristics of a stressed buyer’s market, driven primarily by severe affordability constraints rather than lack of demand. The price-to-income ratio stands at 5.10, significantly exceeding the national average of 3.20, while the price-to-rent ratio of 26.2 indicates that purchasing is substantially less attractive than the national benchmark of 18.7. These metrics suggest that local income levels, averaging $55,815, are insufficient to support the current median home value of $284,700, creating a structural disconnect between earnings and asset prices. The multi-year trend reveals aggressive appreciation, with home values rising 60.7% from 2017 to 2024. This growth outpaces the state average by 21.0% and the national average by 25.0%, yet it is not matched by proportional wage growth. Recent momentum is confirmed by the FHFA HPI, which shows a 6.18% year-over-year increase, outperforming the state’s 0.38% gain by 5.80 percentage points. This divergence between rapid price appreciation and stagnant local wages highlights a market where external demand, rather than local economic expansion, is driving values. Notably, the median gross rent of $904 places the rent burden at 19.4% of income, slightly above the national average of 17.2%, suggesting that rental costs are also becoming increasingly burdensome for residents. Economic context further complicates the landscape. With only 11,326 total jobs and an average weekly wage of $902, the local labor base is small. However, net migration of 1,605 filers, with an average incoming income of $71,893, suggests an influx of higher-earning residents who may be purchasing entry-level or mid-range homes, thereby pushing prices upward. The housing supply remains tight, with 232 new units authorized in 2025, all single-family, and zero multi-family construction, limiting options for lower-income households. The high 7.40% mortgage rate exacerbates affordability issues, making financing difficult for local buyers. Consequently, the market’s vitality, driven by strong momentum and migration, contrasts sharply with its low affordability and stability scores. Buyers face high entry costs relative to local incomes, while renters encounter rising costs that erode household disposable income, resulting in a market that is active but financially precarious for the average resident.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Jackson County is 21.0% above the North Carolina state average and 25.0% above the U.S. national average. The median rent of $904/month is 7.4% below the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 26.2, Jackson County is a strongly rent-favored market. Home prices are high relative to rents, meaning renting is typically far cheaper than owning in the near term.

Jackson County has an owner-occupancy rate of 60.8%, compared to 71.8% in North Carolina and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more renter-heavy market than the U.S. average.
