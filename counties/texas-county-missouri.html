📝 Local Market Analysis

Texas County, Missouri, presents a balanced market profile characterized by strong momentum but constrained economic vitality. The price-to-income ratio of 2.93 falls below the national average of 3.20, and the rent burden of 15.9% is lower than the national 17.2%, indicating favorable affordability metrics for both owners and renters. However, the market is not purely a buyer’s advantage due to significant recent price acceleration. The median home value of $146,700 sits 16.5% below the state average and 35.6% below the national mean, reflecting a distinct regional discount. This discount persists despite a 19.0% year-over-year increase in home values in 2024, a sharp rise from the modest 2.1% growth observed in 2023. Such rapid appreciation suggests a supply-demand imbalance rather than broad-based economic expansion, particularly given that the median household income of $50,036 remains 26.4% below the national average. The divergence between housing price growth and local economic indicators is the most notable pattern in this market. While the FHFA House Price Index shows an 8.48% year-over-year increase, outperforming the state’s 3.71% growth by 4.77 percentage points, the local economy offers limited support for sustained price gains. Total employment stands at only 3,804 jobs with an average weekly wage of $787, and new housing supply is negligible, with just four units authorized in 2025. This scarcity of new construction likely contributes to the momentum score of 89.4, which contrasts sharply with the vitality score of 42.3. The low vitality reflects the small labor base and limited establishment count of 566, suggesting that price gains are driven more by inventory constraints and migration—evidenced by a net inflow of 880 filers—than by robust local job creation. For potential buyers, the current environment presents a complex trade-off. While affordability ratios remain attractive relative to national benchmarks, the recent 19.0% price spike indicates that entry costs are rising rapidly. Rents, at a median of $665, remain accessible, with a price-to-rent ratio of 18.4 nearly identical to the national average of 18.7. The stability score of 51.1 underscores the volatility inherent in a small market experiencing double-digit price jumps without corresponding income growth. With 30-year fixed mortgage rates at 7.40%, the carrying cost for new buyers is elevated, further complicating the decision to purchase versus rent. The data suggests a market where short-term price momentum is strong, but long-term affordability is tethered to a modest economic base and limited housing supply.
