📝 Local Market Analysis

Rooks County, Kansas, functions as a distinctively affordable buyer’s market, characterized by extreme price-to-income disparities relative to national standards. With a median home value of $89,000 and a median household income of $62,500, the price-to-income ratio stands at 1.42, significantly below the national average of 3.20. This affordability is further underscored by a rent burden of 13.0%, which is lower than the national average of 17.2%. The market’s low price-to-rent ratio of 11.0, compared to the national benchmark of 18.7, suggests that purchasing remains a more financially efficient strategy than renting for long-term residents, despite the current high interest rate environment. The multi-year trend reveals a volatile but ultimately stabilizing market. After a modest decline from 2020 to 2021, home values surged 14.7% in 2022, reaching $88,200, before moderating to a 2.0% increase in 2023 and a slight 1.1% decrease in 2024. This recent deceleration contrasts with the broader signal that rents are rising faster than home values, indicating a potential shift in relative affordability between ownership and tenancy. The 2022 spike likely reflects national inflationary pressures rather than local demand, given the county’s small population of 4,813 and limited economic base. The current 7.40% mortgage rate further complicates entry for buyers, yet the low absolute price point mitigates monthly payment burdens compared to higher-cost regions. Regional context highlights Rooks County’s position as a low-cost outlier within Kansas. Home values are 35.9% below the state average and 60.9% below the national mean, while income levels are only 8.1% below the national average. This disparity creates a unique value proposition for retirees or remote workers seeking low-cost living. However, the local economy is constrained by limited employment, with only 1,067 jobs and an average weekly wage of $943. Housing supply remains negligible, with just three new single-family units authorized in 2025 and no multi-family construction. Net migration of +50 filers suggests stable population retention rather than explosive growth. Consequently, the market lacks the dynamic supply-demand pressures seen in urban centers, resulting in price stability. For potential buyers, the low entry cost and high owner-occupancy rate of 74.3% indicate a stable, low-churn community, though limited local job growth may restrict long-term income potential for residents relying solely on regional employment.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 11.0, buying a home in Rooks County is generally more favorable than renting for those planning to stay long-term. A ratio below 15 typically signals that purchasing is financially advantageous over a multi-year horizon.

Rooks County has an owner-occupancy rate of 74.3%, compared to 74.2% in Kansas and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more homeowner-heavy market than the U.S. average.
