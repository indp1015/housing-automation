📝 Local Market Analysis

Martin County, Minnesota, functions as a distinctively affordable buyer’s market, characterized by low entry barriers relative to national standards. The median home value of $163,500 sits 28.2% below the national average, while the price-to-income ratio of 2.75 is significantly lower than the national benchmark of 3.20. This affordability is further supported by a rent burden of 15.3%, which is less than the national average of 17.2%. However, the market’s stability is compromised by a recent surge in price volatility. Home values rose 14.3% year-over-year in 2022, a sharp acceleration from the 2.2% growth seen in 2018, before moderating to 3.0% in 2024. This erratic price trajectory contrasts with the county’s current underperformance relative to the state, where the FHFA HPI indicates a 0.60% annual increase compared to the state’s 2.42% gain. The economic foundation of the county reveals constraints on vitality and long-term growth. With only 7,109 total jobs and an average weekly wage of $1,053, local purchasing power is limited. Housing supply is extremely tight, with just 13 new units authorized in 2025, all single-family, and zero multi-family construction. This scarcity, combined with a net migration of +413 filers, suggests that demand is outpacing new inventory, yet price growth has slowed. The divergence between the strong 2022 price spike and the current 0.60% HPI momentum indicates a market in correction or stagnation. Furthermore, the 7.40% national mortgage rate imposes a significant financing cost, potentially dampening further demand in a market already sensitive to income levels. Affordability remains the primary driver of the county’s favorable composite standing, offsetting weaknesses in stability and vitality. The price-to-rent ratio of 17.9 is slightly below the national average of 18.7, suggesting that renting remains a viable alternative to buying, though the owner-occupancy rate of 68.9% indicates a preference for homeownership. The data highlights a market where low absolute prices provide entry-level opportunities, but limited employment growth and minimal new construction create structural risks. Potential buyers benefit from below-average price-to-income metrics, yet must navigate a volatile price history and a local economy that lags behind state-wide trends. The lack of multi-family development and low total employment suggest that future value appreciation may remain constrained unless regional economic expansion accelerates.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Martin County is 31.4% below the Minnesota state average and 28.2% below the U.S. national average. The median rent of $761/month is 18.9% below the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 17.9, the buy-vs-rent decision in Martin County is balanced. Whether buying or renting makes more sense depends on your time horizon, down payment capacity, and local market conditions.

Martin County has an owner-occupancy rate of 68.9%, compared to 77.9% in Minnesota and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more renter-heavy market than the U.S. average.
