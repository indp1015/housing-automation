📝 Local Market Analysis

Crowley County, Colorado, functions primarily as a renter’s market, characterized by a price-to-rent ratio of 9.8, significantly below the national average of 18.7. This metric indicates that renting is comparatively more expensive relative to home ownership costs than in most other U.S. regions. The market exhibits extreme volatility, with median home values surging from $71,200 in 2021 to $116,200 in 2024, representing a cumulative increase of approximately 63%. However, this rapid appreciation contrasts sharply with recent momentum; the FHFA House Price Index recorded a year-over-year decline of 4.85% in 2024, underperforming the Colorado state average by 4.24 percentage points. This divergence suggests that the earlier surge may have been driven by specific, non-recurring factors rather than sustained broad-based demand, leading to current price corrections. Affordability metrics present a mixed picture. The price-to-income ratio stands at 2.38, well below the national average of 3.20, indicating that home values are low relative to local earning power. Conversely, the rent burden is 24.2% of median household income, exceeding the national average of 17.2%, which places significant financial pressure on tenants. Regionally, the county’s median home value is 72.8% below the Colorado state average and 49.0% below the national mean. This deep discount reflects the county’s limited economic vitality, evidenced by only 614 total jobs and an average weekly wage of $1,033. The low employment base constrains local purchasing power, keeping home values suppressed despite the recent upward trend. The primary drivers of the county’s stressed market status are low stability and vitality, coupled with moderate momentum. The high volatility in home values, including a 30.2% jump in 2022 followed by a recent decline, undermines market stability. Meanwhile, the small population of 5,647 and minimal economic output limit long-term vitality. Migration data shows a net gain of 83 filers, but with average incomes hovering around $42,000, these new residents do not significantly boost the local income ceiling. For potential buyers, the current 7.40% mortgage rate combined with the recent price correction may offer entry points, but the thin local job market poses risks for long-term value retention. Renters face high relative costs, making the lease term a critical consideration given the disparity between rent burdens and local income levels.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Crowley County is 72.8% below the Colorado state average and 49.0% below the U.S. national average. The median rent of $984/month is 24.4% below the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 9.8, buying a home in Crowley County is generally more favorable than renting for those planning to stay long-term. A ratio below 15 typically signals that purchasing is financially advantageous over a multi-year horizon.

Crowley County has an owner-occupancy rate of 69.5%, compared to 71.9% in Colorado and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more renter-heavy market than the U.S. average.
