📝 Local Market Analysis

Russell County, Kentucky, exhibits a balanced market structure with a slight tilt toward buyers, driven by a price-to-rent ratio of 20.0, which exceeds the national average of 18.7. This metric suggests that purchasing offers a more favorable long-term value proposition than renting, despite the high cost of entry relative to local wages. The market is characterized by a median home value of $163,200, which is 28.3% below the national average but slightly above the state mean by 1.5%. This positioning indicates that while the county remains affordable on a national scale, it has recently outpaced broader Kentucky trends in price appreciation. The owner-occupancy rate of 70.8% further supports the stability of the housing stock, suggesting a community with significant equity accumulation among residents. The most striking feature of the local market is the divergence between historical price growth and current momentum. Home values surged from $87,300 in 2017 to $163,200 in 2024, representing an 85.8% cumulative increase. This rapid appreciation, peaking at 22.1% year-over-year in 2023, has outpaced rent growth, where the median gross rent stands at only $681 per month. Consequently, the price-to-income ratio has risen to 3.65, exceeding the national benchmark of 3.20. This gap highlights a growing affordability strain, as median household income of $44,738 lags behind the pace of housing costs. The rent burden of 18.3% also slightly exceeds the national average of 17.2%, indicating that even rental options are becoming less accessible relative to local earnings. Recent data reveals a significant deceleration in price momentum. The FHFA House Price Index shows a mere 1.42% year-over-year increase for 2025, substantially underperforming the state’s 4.26% growth rate by 2.84 percentage points. This slowdown aligns with a constrained supply environment, where only five new single-family units were authorized in 2025, and a high national mortgage rate of 7.32%. Local economic fundamentals are modest, with total employment at 5,004 jobs and an average weekly wage of $888. Although net migration of 416 filers indicates population growth, the inflow of higher-income residents (average $54,301) may be exerting upward pressure on prices. The overall market assessment reflects stress primarily driven by affordability and stability metrics, as the rapid price appreciation has outstripped income growth and local economic capacity, resulting in a composite evaluation that flags the county as stressed despite its relative affordability compared to national averages.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Russell County is 1.5% above the Kentucky state average and 28.3% below the U.S. national average. The median rent of $681/month is 14.1% below the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 20.0, renting is generally more cost-effective than buying in the short to medium term. The market favors buyers only for long-term holds.

Russell County has an owner-occupancy rate of 70.8%, compared to 73.4% in Kentucky and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more renter-heavy market than the U.S. average.
