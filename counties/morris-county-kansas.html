📝 Local Market Analysis

Morris County, Kansas, operates as a distinctly buyer’s market, characterized by significant affordability advantages relative to national benchmarks. The price-to-income ratio stands at 2.21, substantially below the national average of 3.20, while the rent burden is only 14.8% of income compared to the national 17.2%. This affordability is reinforced by a price-to-rent ratio of 14.9, well under the national 18.7, suggesting that purchasing offers stronger financial leverage than renting. With an owner-occupancy rate of 80.1% and median home values at $129,600, the market remains accessible for local households earning a median income of $58,686. The relative position of home values, sitting 43.1% below the national average and 6.6% below the state average, underscores the county’s status as a low-cost housing region within Kansas. Recent market dynamics reveal a sharp recovery and sustained momentum following a brief post-pandemic dip. Home values declined from $103,000 in 2019 to $91,900 in 2021, but subsequently surged by 21.5% in 2022 and maintained double-digit or high-single-digit growth, reaching $129,600 in 2024. This upward trajectory contrasts with the relatively stable median gross rent of $724, indicating that price appreciation is outpacing rental inflation. A notable anomaly is the divergence between the robust price growth and the modest economic base; total employment stands at only 1,054 jobs with an average weekly wage of $828. Despite this limited local labor pool, net migration added 103 filers between 2021 and 2022, with inflows averaging $69,641 in income, slightly higher than outflows. This suggests that external demand, rather than local job creation, is driving the housing market. Housing supply remains extremely constrained, with only six new single-family units authorized in 2025 and zero multi-family permits, which likely contributes to the sustained price appreciation. The current 30-year fixed mortgage rate of 7.40% poses a challenge for affordability, yet the low baseline price keeps entry costs manageable compared to national peers. For prospective buyers, the market offers significant equity potential given the historical volatility and recent strong gains, while renters face minimal cost pressure relative to their income. The combination of low supply, positive net migration, and high owner-occupancy rates creates a resilient market structure where demand is supported by both local stability and external inflows, despite the small scale of the local economy.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Morris County is 6.6% below the Kansas state average and 43.1% below the U.S. national average. The median rent of $724/month is 9.7% below the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 14.9, buying a home in Morris County is generally more favorable than renting for those planning to stay long-term. A ratio below 15 typically signals that purchasing is financially advantageous over a multi-year horizon.

Morris County has an owner-occupancy rate of 80.1%, compared to 74.2% in Kansas and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more homeowner-heavy market than the U.S. average.
