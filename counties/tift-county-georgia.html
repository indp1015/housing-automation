📝 Local Market Analysis

Tift County, Georgia, functions as a balanced market leaning toward buyer-favorable conditions, defined by a price-to-rent ratio of 17.7, which sits below the national average of 18.7. This metric indicates that purchasing is comparatively more economical than renting, supported by a median home value of $165,200 and a median gross rent of $777. The affordability profile is robust relative to national standards, with a price-to-income ratio of 3.10, slightly lower than the national benchmark of 3.20. Furthermore, the rent burden stands at 17.5% of household income, nearly identical to the national average of 17.2%, suggesting that rental costs remain manageable for the typical resident earning a median $53,255. The market exhibits a distinct divergence in growth trajectories between sales and rentals. While home values surged 15.4% year-over-year to reach $165,200 in 2024, recent FHFA HPI data for 2025 indicates a slowdown to a 1.92% annual increase, slightly outpacing the state’s 1.83% growth. This sharp deceleration from the 2024 spike suggests the market is stabilizing after a period of rapid appreciation. In contrast, the long-term trend reveals consistent, moderate growth in home values from $112,200 in 2017, with no comparable data provided for rent growth rates, though the rising home values relative to rents signal a shifting dynamic. The county’s economic vitality is supported by positive net migration of 913 filers and 177 new housing units authorized in 2025, including 111 single-family homes, which helps offset the high 7.40% national mortgage rate. Regional context places Tift County at a significant discount to broader averages, with home values 27.5% below the national mean and 16.5% below the Georgia state average. Despite these lower price points, the local economy, characterized by 16,152 jobs and an average weekly wage of $933, provides a stable foundation. The composite assessment highlights strong momentum and vitality, driven by recent price appreciation and population inflows, yet these strengths are counterbalanced by low stability scores. This instability likely reflects the volatility seen in the 2024 price jump and the subsequent correction in 2025. For prospective buyers, the current environment offers entry points below national norms, while renters face a market where the cost of ownership is increasingly competitive with long-term rental commitments. The interplay of affordable pricing, moderate income levels, and stabilizing price growth creates a unique niche for residential investment in this rural Georgia county.

The median home value in Tift County is 16.5% below the Georgia state average and 27.5% below the U.S. national average. The median rent of $777/month is 19.5% below the state average.
