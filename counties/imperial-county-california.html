📝 Local Market Analysis

Imperial County presents a complex housing landscape characterized by significant affordability challenges despite its status as a low-cost region relative to the national average. The market exhibits strong buyer-side dynamics, evidenced by a Price-to-Rent Ratio of 24.4, which substantially exceeds the national benchmark of 18.7. This metric suggests that purchasing is mathematically more advantageous than renting for long-term occupants, although high entry costs temper this advantage. The Price-to-Income Ratio stands at 5.37, nearly double the national average of 3.20, indicating that median household incomes of $57,681 struggle to support the median home value of $309,600. Consequently, while the market favors buyers in a theoretical sense, the actual purchasing power of local residents remains constrained, creating a bifurcated market where affordability is tightly linked to income levels. Recent price appreciation has been robust, with median home values rising from $167,700 in 2017 to $309,600 in 2024, representing an 84.6% cumulative increase over seven years. The most recent FHFA HPI data indicates a year-over-year gain of 7.26%, outperforming the California state average of -0.29% by 7.55 percentage points. This divergence highlights Imperial County’s relative strength within the state, even as broader California markets have stagnated or declined. However, this price growth has not been mirrored by proportional income growth, as the average weekly wage of $895 translates to an annualized figure that lags behind the pace of housing cost inflation. The rent burden sits at 22.0% of income, also above the national norm of 17.2%, suggesting that rental markets are also tightening as more residents face cost pressures. Supply constraints and economic fundamentals further shape this trajectory. With only 242 new units authorized in 2025, predominantly single-family homes, the housing supply remains insufficient to meet demand, particularly given a population of 180,202 and a positive net migration of 766 filers. The low total employment base of 47,209 jobs limits the depth of the local buyer pool, yet the consistent inflow of residents suggests underlying demand resilience. The 7.40% national mortgage rate adds significant carrying costs, exacerbating the affordability gap for prospective buyers. For stakeholders, the data indicates a market where price appreciation is driven by structural supply shortages and relative value compared to the rest of California, rather than local income growth. Renter’s burdens are rising, and buyers face high entry barriers, resulting in a market that is technically favorable for equity accumulation but practically inaccessible for the median local household without substantial external financial aid or higher-than-average earnings.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Imperial County is 49.2% below the California state average and 36.0% above the U.S. national average. The median rent of $1,056/month is 37.5% below the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 24.4, renting is generally more cost-effective than buying in the short to medium term. The market favors buyers only for long-term holds.

Imperial County has an owner-occupancy rate of 56.8%, compared to 63.4% in California and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more renter-heavy market than the U.S. average.
