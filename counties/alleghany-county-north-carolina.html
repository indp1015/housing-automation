📝 Local Market Analysis

Alleghany County, North Carolina, operates as a distinctively balanced market driven by robust momentum but constrained by affordability metrics. The price-to-income ratio stands at 4.55, significantly exceeding the national average of 3.20, indicating that home values are rising faster than local incomes. However, the rent burden of 17.2% aligns perfectly with the national benchmark, suggesting that rental costs remain proportionate to earnings despite the rising asset values. This divergence highlights a market where ownership is becoming increasingly expensive relative to wages, yet renting remains a financially stable alternative for households. From 2017 to 2024, median home values surged from $136,000 to $214,800, representing a compound annual growth rate that outpaced the broader state. The most recent FHFA HPI data shows a year-over-year increase of 5.88%, outperforming the North Carolina state average of 0.38% by 5.50 percentage points. This sustained appreciation is fueled by a net migration of 360 filers between 2021 and 2022, with incoming residents averaging $67,155 in income, which is notably higher than the county’s median household income of $47,172. This demographic shift introduces higher-income buyers into a market with limited new supply, as only 82 single-family units were authorized in 2025, with zero multi-family permits issued. The local economic context further complicates the affordability landscape. With total employment at 2,786 jobs and an average weekly wage of $908, the labor market supports a niche, high-value housing sector rather than a broad-based residential boom. The current 30-year fixed mortgage rate of 7.40% exacerbates the cost of entry for buyers, making the 4.55 price-to-income ratio less attainable for the median household. Consequently, the market exhibits high vitality and momentum, evidenced by the strong price appreciation and positive net migration, but suffers from low stability and affordability scores. The significant gap between the county’s home values and the state average, combined with a price-to-rent ratio of 26.5 compared to the national 18.7, suggests that while asset values are appreciating rapidly, the fundamental cost of living for residents remains a primary constraint. Potential buyers face a competitive environment where demand from higher-income migrants drives prices upward, while local wage growth lags behind, creating a bifurcated market where investment value is high but resident affordability is strained.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Alleghany County is 8.7% below the North Carolina state average and 5.7% below the U.S. national average. The median rent of $675/month is 30.8% below the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 26.5, Alleghany County is a strongly rent-favored market. Home prices are high relative to rents, meaning renting is typically far cheaper than owning in the near term.

Alleghany County has an owner-occupancy rate of 74.4%, compared to 71.8% in North Carolina and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more homeowner-heavy market than the U.S. average.
