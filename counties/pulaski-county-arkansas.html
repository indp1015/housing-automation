📝 Local Market Analysis

Pulaski County, Arkansas, exhibits a balanced market structure, characterized by a price-to-rent ratio of 16.6, which sits below the national average of 18.7, suggesting that ownership costs are not disproportionately higher than renting. However, the market leans slightly toward buyers due to a 57.0% owner-occupancy rate and a positive net migration of 5,750 filers between 2021 and 2022. This demographic influx, with an average income of $93,352, exceeds the local median household income of $62,873, injecting higher purchasing power into the housing stock. The recent FHFA HPI index of 643.04 reflects a modest 1.10% year-over-year increase, tracking almost perfectly with the state’s 1.09% growth, indicating that the county is no longer outpacing the broader Arkansas market but remains stable within it. The affordability profile presents a mixed picture. The price-to-income ratio stands at 3.41, slightly above the national benchmark of 3.20, while the rent burden consumes 20.6% of income compared to the national 17.2%. Despite these elevated costs relative to income, the median home value of $214,700 remains 5.7% below the national average, though it is 51.8% higher than the state average. This divergence highlights that Pulaski County functions as a premium market within Arkansas, driven by the presence of state government and major corporate employers. The local economy supports this premium with 211,280 jobs and an average weekly wage of $1,211, which is robust relative to the area’s income levels. Housing supply remains constrained, with only 1,705 new units authorized in 2025, predominantly single-family homes, limiting the ability to rapidly cool the market. Momentum has moderated significantly from the peak 12.8% appreciation seen in 2022 to 7.6% in 2024, signaling a normalization of growth rates. This deceleration is critical for stability, as the market shifts from a boom phase to a steady state. The editorial assessment of the market reflects this transition, where strong momentum and vitality are offset by weaker affordability and stability metrics. For potential buyers, the 7.40% national mortgage rate increases the monthly payment burden, making the slightly elevated price-to-income ratio a more acute concern. Renter’s advantage is limited by the low price-to-rent ratio, which typically favors buyers, yet the high rent burden suggests that long-term ownership may still offer better value retention than renting in this specific geographic context. The data indicates a maturing market where price growth is aligning with broader regional trends rather than exhibiting exceptional local outperformance.
