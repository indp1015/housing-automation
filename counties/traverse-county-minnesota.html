📝 Local Market Analysis

Traverse County operates as a distinct buyer’s market, characterized by extreme affordability and robust price acceleration. The median home value of $114,100 stands 49.9% below the national average and 52.1% below the Minnesota state average, creating a price-to-income ratio of 1.81 compared to the national benchmark of 3.20. This significant valuation gap drives an exceptional affordability profile, further evidenced by a rent burden of only 11.7% of income versus the national 17.2%. The market’s momentum is underscored by a recent FHFA Home Price Index increase of 30.32% year-over-year, dramatically outpacing the state’s 2.42% growth. This sharp divergence suggests a localized surge in demand relative to the broader regional housing landscape, where home values are rising faster than rents, indicating strong equity accumulation for owners. The multi-year trend reveals a volatile but upward trajectory, with median values jumping from $79,800 in 2017 to $114,100 in 2024. The peak annual growth of 17.9% in 2022 has since moderated to 3.6% in 2024, suggesting a stabilization phase after the initial spike. However, the economic foundation remains thin, with only 748 total jobs and an average weekly wage of $957. This limited employment base constrains the market’s vitality, as the low number of establishments (135) limits local income growth potential. Despite this, migration data shows a net inflow of 94 filers, with incoming residents earning an average of $79,597, slightly higher than the outflow average of $81,999, indicating a modest but positive demographic shift that supports demand. Supply constraints further amplify price pressure, as only six new single-family units were authorized in 2025, with zero multi-family permits. This negligible new construction against a population of 3,243 ensures that inventory remains tight, sustaining upward price pressure even as mortgage rates sit at 7.40%. The high owner-occupancy rate of 81.8% reflects a stable, community-oriented housing stock, yet the low stability score in the composite assessment likely stems from the small sample size and economic fragility. For potential buyers, the primary advantage is the substantial equity cushion relative to income, while renters face minimal cost burdens. The market’s strength is driven primarily by affordability and momentum, offsetting the weak vitality scores caused by limited economic diversification and minimal new housing supply.
