📝 Local Market Analysis

Wilkinson County presents a distinctively affordable housing landscape, characterized by a median home value of $86,300, which sits 62.1% below the national average. This extreme affordability is reflected in a price-to-income ratio of 1.90, significantly lower than the national benchmark of 3.20, and a price-to-rent ratio of 8.7, indicating that purchasing is substantially more cost-effective than renting. However, the market structure reveals a divergence in affordability metrics; while the price-to-income ratio suggests high buyer accessibility, the rent burden stands at 21.8% of income, exceeding the national average of 17.2%. This discrepancy highlights a market where rental costs are relatively high compared to local income levels, despite low absolute home prices. The combination of a 80.5% owner-occupancy rate and a price-to-rent ratio well below the national norm of 18.7 classifies this as a buyer-favorable environment, yet the elevated rent burden complicates the rental sector's appeal for lower-income households. The recent price trajectory exhibits significant volatility, with a sharp 13.8% increase in 2022 followed by a 5.0% decline in 2024. This correction contrasts with the FHFA HPI’s recent 2.94% year-over-year growth, which outpaces the Georgia state average of 1.83%, suggesting underlying strength in mortgage-backed transactions despite the broader valuation dip. This divergence between the ACS median value decline and the HPI growth may reflect differences in transaction data sources or the specific mix of homes sold. Economically, the county’s limited employment base of 2,425 jobs and an average weekly wage of $1,231 constrain local purchasing power, while new housing supply remains minimal with only 18 single-family units authorized in 2025. Net migration of +135 filers indicates modest population growth, but the inflow and outflow average incomes are nearly identical at approximately $52,000, suggesting no significant demographic shift toward higher earners. Market vitality is currently constrained by economic fundamentals, as evidenced by the low vitality component of the composite assessment. While momentum is supported by the HPI outperformance, stability is tempered by the recent price correction and the tight labor market. For potential buyers, the low entry price offers exceptional value relative to national standards, particularly given the 7.32% mortgage rate which, while high nationally, remains manageable against the low home price. Renters, however, face a less favorable environment due to the high rent burden relative to the median household income of $45,465. The market’s primary driver is affordability, which remains the strongest attribute, offsetting weaker vitality metrics related to local economic expansion and supply constraints.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Wilkinson County is 56.4% below the Georgia state average and 62.1% below the U.S. national average. The median rent of $825/month is 14.5% below the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 8.7, buying a home in Wilkinson County is generally more favorable than renting for those planning to stay long-term. A ratio below 15 typically signals that purchasing is financially advantageous over a multi-year horizon.

Wilkinson County has an owner-occupancy rate of 80.5%, compared to 70.6% in Georgia and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more homeowner-heavy market than the U.S. average.
