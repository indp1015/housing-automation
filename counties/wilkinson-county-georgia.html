📝 Local Market Analysis

Wilkinson County, Georgia, exhibits characteristics of a balanced market with a slight tilt toward renters, driven by significant affordability disparities between ownership and rental sectors. The price-to-income ratio stands at 1.90, substantially below the national average of 3.20, indicating that home purchase prices are relatively low compared to local earnings. However, the rent burden is 21.8% of household income, exceeding the national benchmark of 17.2%, suggesting that rental costs are disproportionately high relative to the median household income of $45,465. This divergence is further highlighted by a price-to-rent ratio of 8.7, roughly half the national average of 18.7, implying that renting is less economically advantageous than buying from a pure cost-benefit perspective, despite the higher immediate rent burden. The multi-year trend reveals a volatile recovery following the pandemic. Home values surged 13.8% in 2022 to reach a peak of $90,800 in 2023, before correcting 5.0% in 2024 to $86,300. This recent decline contrasts with the FHFA HPI data, which shows a 2.94% year-over-year increase in 2025, outperforming the Georgia state average of 1.83% by 1.11 percentage points. This discrepancy suggests that while census-based estimates show a recent dip, mortgage transaction data indicates underlying price resilience. The market’s relative position remains deeply discounted, with median home values 56.4% below the state average and 62.1% below the national mean, reflecting the county’s lower median income, which is 33.1% below the national figure. Economic fundamentals provide limited support for sustained price growth. The local economy supports only 2,425 jobs across 150 establishments, with an average weekly wage of $1,231, which may constrain buyer purchasing power. Housing supply is minimal, with only 18 new single-family units authorized in 2025 and zero multi-family permits, creating a tight inventory environment. Although net migration was positive at +135 filers in 2021-2022, the average income of incoming filers ($52,097) only modestly exceeds the local median. With national 30-year fixed mortgage rates at 7.40%, high borrowing costs remain a headwind for demand. For potential buyers, the low price-to-income ratio offers an entry point, but the high rent burden signals that local income levels may not fully support current rental market pressures, creating a complex affordability landscape where ownership is cheaper relative to income, yet rents remain a significant financial strain.
