📝 Editor's Insight

Costilla County exhibits a distinct renter’s market dynamic, evidenced by a price-to-rent ratio of 16.7, which falls below the national average of 18.0. This metric suggests that renting is relatively more economical than purchasing, despite the high owner-occupancy rate of 78.5%. The five-year trend reveals rapid inflation in housing costs, with rents surging at a 12.7% CAGR compared to an 11.0% CAGR for home values. This divergence indicates that rental demand is outpacing supply growth, pushing monthly costs higher than the appreciation of fixed mortgage payments. Affordability remains a critical constraint; the price-to-income ratio stands at 4.69, significantly exceeding the national benchmark of 3.09. Furthermore, the rent burden reaches 28.2%, nearly double the national average of 17.2%. A concrete insight emerges from the income disparity: with median household income at $36,519, residents face a dual squeeze where both purchase and rental costs are escalating faster than local wage growth. Consequently, the market is characterized by acute affordability stress rather than simple price levels, requiring careful budgeting for any new entrant.
