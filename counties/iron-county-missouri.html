📝 Local Market Analysis

Iron County, Missouri, exhibits a balanced market profile, characterized by strong affordability metrics that offset moderate economic vitality. The price-to-income ratio stands at 2.44, significantly below the national average of 3.20, while the rent burden is 16.3%, slightly under the national 17.2% benchmark. These figures indicate that housing costs remain accessible relative to local earning power. The county’s median home value of $119,300 is 47.6% below the national average and 32.1% below the Missouri state average, reflecting its position as a low-cost region within a broader affordable housing landscape. Recent momentum is the primary driver of the market’s current trajectory, with the FHFA House Price Index surging 15.61% year-over-year in 2025, outperforming the state by 11.90 percentage points. This sharp acceleration follows a period of consistent growth, including a 17.4% peak in 2022, suggesting that price appreciation is currently decoupling from slower rent growth. While home values are rising faster than rents, the price-to-rent ratio of 15.0 remains below the national 18.7, indicating that long-term ownership costs are still competitive. However, this rapid price escalation contrasts with the county’s limited housing supply, where only 12 new units were authorized in 2025, and a small labor base of 2,754 jobs paying an average weekly wage of $990. The economic context reveals a market supported by net positive migration of 236 filers, yet constrained by low employment volume and high national mortgage rates of 7.40%. The composite assessment of the market reflects high momentum scores driven by the recent price spike, but lower stability and vitality scores due to the small population of 9,446 and modest income levels averaging $48,850. For potential buyers, the data suggests that while entry prices are low, the recent velocity of price increases requires careful consideration of long-term carrying costs. Renters face a favorable burden profile, but the divergence between accelerating home prices and stable rents may signal shifting dynamics in the local inventory. Ultimately, the market is defined by a tension between exceptional affordability and rapid, supply-constrained price appreciation, creating a complex environment for residential investment and consumption.
