📝 Local Market Analysis

Kent County, Maryland, functions as a distinctively seller’s market, characterized by robust price appreciation that significantly outpaces rental growth. The price-to-rent ratio stands at 22.8, well above the national average of 18.7, indicating that purchasing is currently less economically efficient than renting for many households. This dynamic is reinforced by a 72.5% owner-occupancy rate, suggesting a stable, established housing stock rather than a transient rental hub. The market’s momentum is evident in the FHFA House Price Index, which shows a 4.92% year-over-year increase, substantially outperforming the state’s 0.95% growth rate by nearly four percentage points. This divergence highlights a localized demand surge that is decoupling the county from broader state trends. Affordability remains the primary constraint for prospective buyers, with a price-to-income ratio of 4.13 compared to the national benchmark of 3.20. Although the median household income of $80,147 exceeds the national average by 17.9%, it lags behind the rising home values, which are 45.3% above the national median. Renter burden is slightly elevated at 18.1% of income versus the national 17.2%, yet remains manageable. The economic foundation supports this demand through positive net migration of 556 filers, with incoming residents earning an average of $99,084, suggesting a high-income demographic is fueling the price premium. However, local employment is modest, with only 6,468 total jobs and an average weekly wage of $1,039, implying that many residents may commute to larger employment centers. Housing supply remains tight, with only 53 new units authorized in 2025, including 37 single-family homes, which is insufficient to cool the rapid price escalation observed from 2022 to 2024. The recent spike in home values, reaching $330,900, contrasts with the slower rent growth, creating a widening gap that favors existing homeowners over new entrants. The current 7.40% mortgage rate further exacerbates the affordability challenge, increasing monthly carrying costs for buyers. Consequently, the market exhibits high vitality and strong momentum, driven by affluent in-migrants and limited supply, but suffers from low affordability scores. Buyers face a competitive environment where prices are rising faster than incomes, while renters benefit from relatively stable rent burdens. The data suggests a market where equity accumulation for owners is accelerating, but entry costs for new buyers are becoming increasingly prohibitive relative to local wage levels.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Kent County is 10.6% below the Maryland state average and 45.3% above the U.S. national average. The median rent of $1,209/month is 17.1% below the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 22.8, renting is generally more cost-effective than buying in the short to medium term. The market favors buyers only for long-term holds.

Kent County has an owner-occupancy rate of 72.5%, compared to 72.7% in Maryland and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more renter-heavy market than the U.S. average.
