📝 Local Market Analysis

Del Norte County presents a distinctively stressed housing environment, characterized by severe affordability constraints despite moderate price momentum. The market leans toward a renter’s disadvantage, evidenced by a price-to-rent ratio of 23.1, significantly higher than the national average of 18.7. This indicates that purchasing equity is relatively cheaper than long-term renting, yet the high entry barrier limits buyer access. The price-to-income ratio stands at 5.10, nearly double the national benchmark of 3.20, while the rent burden consumes 22.1% of median household income compared to the national 17.2%. These metrics confirm that housing costs are disproportionately high relative to local earning power, creating a structural affordability crisis that suppresses market vitality. Trend analysis reveals a period of aggressive appreciation followed by stabilization. Home values surged 18.8% in 2022 and 13.1% in 2023, moderating to 7.0% in 2024. This recent deceleration aligns with the FHFA HPI’s recent 0.85% year-over-year increase, which outpaces the California state average of -0.29%. This divergence suggests local resilience, likely driven by net migration inflows of 527 filers and a housing supply of 113 new units, with 96 being multi-family. However, this supply is insufficient to offset demand pressures in a county with only 4,768 total jobs and an average weekly wage of $1,021. The economic base is thin, with median household income at $67,058, barely matching the national average, yet home values are 50.2% above the national mean. This disconnect highlights a market where external demand or investment interest may be inflating prices beyond local income capacity. The composite assessment reflects low stability and affordability, dragging down the overall market health rating. While momentum remains positive relative to the state, the underlying economic fundamentals are fragile. The high owner-occupancy rate of 70.1% suggests a stable existing stock, but the rapid price appreciation outpacing rent growth creates volatility risks. For prospective residents, the data indicates that while price growth is slowing, the absolute cost of entry remains prohibitive for the average local earner. The 7.32% national mortgage rate further exacerbates monthly payment burdens, making the 5.10 price-to-income ratio even more challenging. Consequently, the market is defined by a tension between resilient price performance and deep affordability stress, limiting broad participation and concentrating value in a smaller segment of the population.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Del Norte County is 43.9% below the California state average and 50.2% above the U.S. national average. The median rent of $1,233/month is 27.0% below the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 23.1, renting is generally more cost-effective than buying in the short to medium term. The market favors buyers only for long-term holds.

Del Norte County has an owner-occupancy rate of 70.1%, compared to 63.4% in California and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more renter-heavy market than the U.S. average.
