📝 Editor's Insight

Harris County, Georgia, presents a distinct market profile characterized by strong home equity accumulation and relative affordability for homeowners. The market leans toward a buyer’s advantage regarding entry costs, evidenced by a price-to-income ratio of 3.11, which sits below the national average of 3.20. Furthermore, the rent burden stands at just 12.9% of median household income, significantly lower than the national benchmark of 17.2%. These metrics suggest that while housing costs are elevated, they remain manageable relative to local earning power, particularly for those seeking ownership. The high owner-occupancy rate of 88.8% reinforces the stability of the local housing stock, indicating a deep-rooted preference for homeownership over renting in this region. The multi-year trend reveals a period of aggressive appreciation, particularly during the recent surge. Home values climbed from $194,800 in 2017 to $302,800 in 2024, representing a cumulative increase of approximately 55.4%. The most significant jump occurred in 2022, with a 14.7% year-over-year gain, followed by a continued 8.6% increase in 2024. Recent FHFA data confirms this momentum, showing a 5.15% year-over-year rise in 2025, which outpaces the state average of 1.83% by 3.32 percentage points. This divergence highlights that Harris County is outperforming broader Georgia trends, driven by local demand factors that are decoupling its growth from the state’s slower pace. A notable pattern emerges in the disparity between price and rent metrics. The price-to-rent ratio of 24.1 is substantially higher than the national average of 18.7, suggesting that buying is less economically attractive than renting on a pure cost-of-capital basis compared to other regions. This divergence indicates that while rents have risen, they have not kept pace with the rapid escalation of home values. In terms of regional context, median home values are 53.0% above the state average, while incomes are 43.1% above the national average. This income premium helps cushion the impact of high home prices, but the rapid appreciation rate implies that new entrants face a steeper cost barrier than previous cohorts. For potential buyers, the current environment reflects a premium market where equity gains have been substantial, but entry prices have risen sharply. Renters benefit from a low burden ratio, but the high price-to-rent spread signals that converting rental savings into equity requires navigating a market where home values are expanding faster than the underlying rental yield would typically justify. The data supports a narrative of a resilient, high-income community experiencing robust asset appreciation that exceeds both state and national norms.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Harris County is 53.0% above the Georgia state average and 33.0% above the U.S. national average. The median rent of $1,048/month is 8.6% above the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 24.1, renting is generally more cost-effective than buying in the short to medium term. The market favors buyers only for long-term holds.

Harris County has an owner-occupancy rate of 88.8%, compared to 70.6% in Georgia and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more homeowner-heavy market than the U.S. average.
