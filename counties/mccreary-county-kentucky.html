📝 Local Market Analysis

McCreary County operates as a distinctively affordable buyer’s market, characterized by a median home value of $85,300 and a price-to-income ratio of 2.53, significantly below the national average of 3.20. The price-to-rent ratio of 12.3 further underscores the relative value of ownership compared to the national benchmark of 18.7. However, this affordability is contextualized by a median household income of $33,750, which is 50.4% lower than the national mean. The county’s home values sit 47.0% below the state average, indicating a deep discount relative to broader Kentucky trends. While the market is accessible, the low income base constrains purchasing power, creating a bifurcated dynamic where entry-level affordability is high but overall economic vitality remains limited. Recent trends reveal significant volatility, with home values experiencing a 9.7% decline in 2023 before rebounding 10.8% in 2024. This sharp recovery contrasts with the FHFA HPI’s more modest 2.58% year-over-year increase, a figure that underperforms the state’s 4.26% growth rate. The divergence between the Census-based median value surge and the mortgage-based HPI suggests potential lags in data reporting or a shift in transaction mix. Meanwhile, the rent burden stands at 20.5% of income, exceeding the national average of 17.2%, indicating that while homeownership is cheaper, renting remains a significant financial strain for local households. The owner-occupancy rate of 74.2% reflects a stable housing stock, yet the limited local economy, with only 1,631 total jobs and an average weekly wage of $729, restricts broader economic resilience. The composite assessment of this market is driven primarily by strong momentum metrics, which score highly due to the recent price rebound, but is heavily penalized by low vitality and stability scores. The sparse employment base and modest migration net gain of +275 filers contribute to the low vitality rating, as the local labor market lacks the depth to sustain robust housing demand. The stability score is dampened by the erratic price fluctuations observed between 2023 and 2024. For potential buyers, the 7.40% national mortgage rate significantly impacts monthly payments, but the low entry price mitigates this cost compared to higher-priced markets. Renters face a tighter squeeze, with median rents of $577 consuming a larger share of income than the national norm. Ultimately, the market presents a low-cost entry point for buyers, but the underlying economic fragility and income limitations require careful consideration of long-term financial sustainability.
