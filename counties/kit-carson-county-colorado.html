📝 Local Market Analysis

Kit Carson County, Colorado, functions as a balanced market with distinct affordability constraints, characterized by a price-to-income ratio of 3.41, slightly above the national average of 3.20. While the rent burden stands at 16.6%, marginally below the national 17.2%, the price-to-rent ratio of 20.5 exceeds the national benchmark of 18.7, suggesting that ownership costs are becoming relatively more expensive compared to rental alternatives. This dynamic indicates a shift where housing equity accumulation is outpacing rental cost inflation, a trend evident in the divergence between rising home values and more stable rental rates. The market exhibits significant volatility, with home values surging from $130,200 in 2017 to $239,500 in 2024, representing an 83.9% cumulative increase. Although the 2019 dip of 13.1% reflects temporary instability, the subsequent recovery, including a 26.1% jump in 2021, has moderated to an 8.2% annual gain in 2024. Recent FHFA data confirms this deceleration, showing a 3.36% year-over-year increase in 2025, which outperforms the Colorado state average of -0.61% by 3.97 percentage points. This relative strength suggests local demand resilience despite broader regional softening. Economic fundamentals support this price appreciation, driven by a median household income of $70,259, which is 3.3% above the national average. However, the local economy remains small, with only 2,270 total jobs and 287 establishments. Housing supply is severely constrained, with just four new units authorized in 2025, all single-family. This scarcity, combined with positive net migration of 224 filers, intensifies competition for existing inventory. The low stability component of the overall market assessment is driven by the high volatility observed in the 2017-2024 price series, whereas momentum and vitality are supported by consistent income growth and population inflows. For potential buyers, the 7.40% national mortgage rate further pressures affordability, making the current price-to-income ratio the primary barrier to entry. Renters may find relative relief given the lower rent burden, but the rising price-to-rent ratio indicates that long-term rental costs are likely to track closer to ownership costs, reducing the financial advantage of renting in this specific rural Colorado market.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Kit Carson County is 44.0% below the Colorado state average and 5.2% above the U.S. national average. The median rent of $973/month is 25.3% below the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 20.5, renting is generally more cost-effective than buying in the short to medium term. The market favors buyers only for long-term holds.

Kit Carson County has an owner-occupancy rate of 72.6%, compared to 71.9% in Colorado and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more renter-heavy market than the U.S. average.
