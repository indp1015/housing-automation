📝 Editor's Insight

Hinsdale County functions as a distinct renter’s market, evidenced by a price-to-rent ratio of 33.3, nearly double the national average of 18.0. This metric indicates that purchasing equity provides significantly less immediate financial leverage than renting, despite high ownership rates. The five-year trend reveals a divergence where rents escalated at a 9.8% CAGR, outpacing the 5.8% CAGR for home values. This suggests that rental demand is surging relative to property appreciation, potentially driven by limited housing inventory or seasonal tourism pressures. Affordability remains strained for buyers, with a price-to-income ratio of 6.82, more than twice the national benchmark of 3.09. Although the rent burden of 20.5% slightly exceeds the 17.2% national average, it remains manageable compared to the severe cost of entry for homeowners. A critical, non-obvious insight is the county’s extreme demographic scarcity, with only 939 residents. This micro-market volatility means that single-unit transactions can disproportionately skew median values, making historical trends less reliable indicators of individual property performance than broader regional data would suggest.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Hinsdale County is 2.4% above the Colorado state average and 92.3% above the U.S. national average. The median rent of $1,171/month is 10.1% below the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 31.2, Hinsdale County is a strongly rent-favored market. Home prices are high relative to rents, meaning renting is typically far cheaper than owning in the near term.

Hinsdale County has an owner-occupancy rate of 76.1%, compared to 71.9% in Colorado and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more homeowner-heavy market than the U.S. average.
