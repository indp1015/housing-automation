📝 Local Market Analysis

Rosebud County, Montana, functions as a distinctively momentum-driven market characterized by rapid recent appreciation despite underlying affordability constraints. The price-to-income ratio stands at 3.65, exceeding the national average of 3.20, while the price-to-rent ratio of 22.6 significantly outpaces the national benchmark of 18.7. These metrics indicate that ownership costs are becoming increasingly prohibitive relative to local earning power, with the median household income of $49,914 lagging 26.6% behind the national mean. Consequently, the market leans toward a renter’s advantage in terms of immediate cash flow, as the rent burden of 16.2% remains slightly below the national average of 17.2%, offering a relatively lower entry barrier for tenants compared to prospective buyers. The most striking feature of this market is the divergence between historical stability and recent explosive growth. After remaining nearly flat between 2017 and 2020, home values surged 21.3% in 2021 and continued climbing to a peak of $188,900 in 2023. Although the 2024 ACS data shows a 3.4% correction to $182,400, the FHFA HPI reports a current year-over-year increase of 8.80%, outperforming the Montana state average of 2.59% by 6.21 percentage points. This sustained upward trajectory contrasts sharply with the minimal housing supply, evidenced by only two new units authorized in 2025. Such scarcity, combined with net positive migration of 201 filers, suggests that demand is outstripping new construction, fueling continued price pressure despite the high 7.40% national mortgage rate. Economically, the county’s limited base of 2,189 jobs and an average weekly wage of $1,446 provides modest support for the housing boom. The low owner occupancy rate of 63.6% further highlights the structural gap between rental and ownership segments. While the high momentum score reflects this aggressive price appreciation, the stability metric is severely compromised by the volatility seen in the 2021-2023 spike and the subsequent 2024 dip. For potential stakeholders, the data presents a complex landscape: renters benefit from manageable costs relative to income, while buyers face a market where prices have decoupled from local wage growth. The significant underperformance relative to the state average in home values (-34.1%) suggests that while momentum is strong, the absolute price point remains accessible compared to broader Montana trends, yet the rapid recent gains warrant caution regarding entry timing.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Rosebud County is 34.1% below the Montana state average and 19.9% below the U.S. national average. The median rent of $672/month is 20.2% below the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 22.6, renting is generally more cost-effective than buying in the short to medium term. The market favors buyers only for long-term holds.

Rosebud County has an owner-occupancy rate of 63.6%, compared to 73.0% in Montana and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more renter-heavy market than the U.S. average.
