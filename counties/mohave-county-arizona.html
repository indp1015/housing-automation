📝 Editor's Insight

Mojave County exhibits characteristics of a buyer’s market, evidenced by a price-to-rent ratio of 20.2, which exceeds the national average of 18.0. This metric suggests that purchasing is relatively more attractive than renting compared to broader national standards. Over the past five years, home values surged at a 12.1% CAGR, significantly outpacing the 6.1% rent increase. This divergence indicates that asset appreciation has driven equity growth faster than rental cost inflation, potentially narrowing the gap between buying and renting costs. However, affordability remains strained. The county’s price-to-income ratio of 4.54 is 47% higher than the national benchmark of 3.09, reflecting that home values have risen disproportionately to local incomes. Additionally, the rent burden stands at 22.5%, well above the 17.2% national average. A critical insight is that while home values are 19.1% above the national average, household incomes are 15.4% below it. This income-value disconnect implies that despite lower absolute prices than coastal markets, local purchasing power is insufficient to support current valuations without substantial external income sources.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Mohave County is 4.0% above the Arizona state average and 19.7% above the U.S. national average. The median rent of $1,047/month is 3.1% above the state average.

Mohave County has an owner-occupancy rate of 73.6%, compared to 70.3% in Arizona and 73.0% nationally. This indicates a more homeowner-heavy market than the U.S. average.
