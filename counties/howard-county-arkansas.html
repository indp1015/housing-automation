📝 Local Market Analysis

Howard County, Arkansas, exhibits a distinct buyer’s market profile, characterized by a price-to-rent ratio of 18.6, which aligns closely with the national average of 18.7, and a median home value of $153,900 that sits 32.4% below the national mean. The market’s affordability remains constrained relative to local earnings, with a price-to-income ratio of 3.37 exceeding the national benchmark of 3.20. Despite this, the rent burden stands at 18.2% of median household income, slightly above the national average of 17.2%, indicating that while ownership costs are elevated relative to local wages, rental costs are proportionally high. This dual pressure suggests that neither segment enjoys significant leverage, though the lower absolute price point favors buyers with fixed incomes over long-term holders of rental agreements. The multi-year trend reveals a period of aggressive appreciation, with home values surging 13.7% in 2024 following a 16.4% jump in 2022. This momentum is further corroborated by the FHFA HPI, which shows a 12.19% year-over-year increase, significantly outperforming the Arkansas state average of 1.09% by 11.10 percentage points. This divergence highlights a localized surge in demand or supply constraints that is decoupling Howard County from broader state-level trends. The economic context supports this volatility, as the county’s total employment of 4,934 jobs and average weekly wage of $913 provide a modest but stable base. Net migration of +216 filers between 2021 and 2022 indicates a slight population gain, with inflow incomes averaging $53,224, suggesting an influx of higher-earning residents that may be driving price appreciation faster than local wage growth. The composite assessment reflects strong momentum, driven by the rapid price increases, but is tempered by low vitality and stability scores. The 22.4 vitality score reflects the limited economic scale, with only 302 establishments supporting the workforce. Meanwhile, the 33.7 affordability score underscores the strain on households, particularly given that median household income of $45,603 lags the national average by 32.9%. For potential participants, the 7.32% national mortgage rate further complicates entry, as high interest costs amplify the already elevated price-to-income ratio. The data indicates a market where rapid price growth is outpacing fundamental economic indicators, creating a scenario where short-term momentum is strong but long-term affordability risks remain significant.
