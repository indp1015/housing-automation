📝 Local Market Analysis

Jackson County, Florida, operates as a distinctively affordable buyer’s market, characterized by a median home value of $120,800, which sits 46.9% below the national average. The price-to-income ratio of 2.46 significantly outperforms the national benchmark of 3.20, indicating strong purchasing power relative to asset prices. This affordability is reinforced by a price-to-rent ratio of 11.6, well below the national average of 18.7, suggesting that homeownership remains a more cost-effective long-term strategy than renting for qualified buyers. However, the market is not without financial strain; the rent burden stands at 21.1% of median household income, exceeding the national average of 17.2%, which places pressure on the 24.1% of households that rent. Trend analysis reveals a period of accelerated appreciation between 2022 and 2024, with home values rising from $113,300 to $120,800. This recent 6.1% year-over-year increase contrasts with the stagnation seen in 2023 (+0.5%) and the modest growth of 2019 (+0.2%). Despite this recent uptick, the FHFA House Price Index indicates a current year-over-year change of only +0.22%, lagging behind the Florida state average of +0.91%. This divergence suggests that while long-term valuation has improved, immediate transactional momentum is cooling. The local economic foundation supports this stability, with 9,485 total jobs and an average weekly wage of $916. Housing supply remains constrained, with only 98 new units authorized in 2025, predominantly single-family homes, which may limit future inventory growth. Migration data from 2021-2022 shows a net inflow of 1,362 filers, though average incomes for incoming residents ($53,405) are slightly lower than those leaving ($54,546), suggesting a demographic shift toward lower-income households rather than high-wealth migration. This dynamic, combined with the national 30-year fixed mortgage rate of 7.32%, creates a challenging environment for new entrants despite low asset prices. The overall market assessment reflects a balance between high stability and moderate vitality. While affordability metrics are favorable, the low score in vitality stems from the limited economic scale and the high rent burden. The market’s primary strength lies in its relative value compared to state and national averages, offering a lower entry point for buyers, though the recent slowdown in price growth and high interest rates temper immediate demand. Potential buyers should note that while entry costs are low, the limited supply and rising rents indicate that the window for favorable terms may be narrowing as the local economy adjusts to its new demographic composition.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Jackson County is 57.5% below the Florida state average and 46.9% below the U.S. national average. The median rent of $865/month is 33.6% below the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 11.6, buying a home in Jackson County is generally more favorable than renting for those planning to stay long-term. A ratio below 15 typically signals that purchasing is financially advantageous over a multi-year horizon.

Jackson County has an owner-occupancy rate of 75.9%, compared to 74.6% in Florida and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more homeowner-heavy market than the U.S. average.
