📝 Local Market Analysis

Honolulu County operates as a severely constrained renter’s market, characterized by extreme entry barriers for prospective homeowners. The price-to-income ratio stands at 8.45, more than double the national average of 3.20, while the price-to-rent ratio of 35.9 significantly exceeds the national benchmark of 18.7. These metrics indicate that purchasing is financially prohibitive for the median household, which earns $106,195 annually against a median home value of $897,500. Consequently, the market dynamics favor long-term tenants, as the high cost of ownership relative to income forces a substantial portion of the population into the rental sector, where the median gross rent of $2,083 consumes 23.5% of household income, well above the national norm of 17.2%. Trend analysis reveals a distinct deceleration in price appreciation following a sharp spike. Home values increased by 14.5% in 2022 before moderating to 2.8% in 2024. Current FHFA data reflects this cooling, with a year-over-year index change of +1.40%, which underperforms the broader state trend of +5.93% by 4.53 percentage points. This divergence suggests that while statewide housing demand remains robust, Honolulu County is experiencing relative price stagnation compared to its peers. The local economy supports this stability through 360,796 total jobs and an average weekly wage of $1,351, yet the housing supply of only 1,446 new units authorized in 2025 remains insufficient to alleviate structural scarcity. Net migration of +7,204 filers further pressures the limited inventory, maintaining high demand despite the high national mortgage rate of 7.32%. The overall market assessment reflects a critical imbalance between vitality and affordability. While momentum indicators show positive but slowing price growth, the stability and affordability axes are heavily penalized by the county’s premium valuation, which sits 294.1% above the national average. The high owner-occupancy rate of 59.8% masks the underlying tension between stagnant wage growth relative to asset prices and persistent migration inflows. For stakeholders, the data indicates a market where rental costs remain a significant financial burden, and home equity accumulation is hindered by the disconnect between median incomes and asset values. The recent underperformance relative to the state average signals a potential correction phase, yet the fundamental supply constraints and high migration levels continue to support elevated price levels, keeping the market in a stressed condition for both buyers and renters.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Honolulu County is 12.4% above the Hawaii state average and 294.1% above the U.S. national average. The median rent of $2,083/month is 13.0% above the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 35.9, Honolulu County is a strongly rent-favored market. Home prices are high relative to rents, meaning renting is typically far cheaper than owning in the near term.

Honolulu County has an owner-occupancy rate of 59.8%, compared to 67.0% in Hawaii and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more renter-heavy market than the U.S. average.
