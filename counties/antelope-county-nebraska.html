📝 Local Market Analysis

Antelope County, Nebraska, operates as a distinctively affordable buyer’s market, characterized by extreme value relative to national benchmarks. The median home value of $130,300 sits 42.8% below the national average and 20.9% below the state mean, resulting in a price-to-income ratio of just 1.97 compared to the national standard of 3.20. This affordability is reinforced by a rent burden of only 12.6%, well below the 17.2% national average, and a price-to-rent ratio of 15.7, suggesting that purchasing remains significantly more cost-effective than renting. The market classification is further supported by a high owner-occupancy rate of 79.0%, indicating entrenched local equity rather than transient rental demand. Recent trends reveal a sharp acceleration in asset values that diverges significantly from broader economic indicators. While home prices remained relatively flat between 2017 and 2021, they surged 21.7% in 2022 and continued rising at 11.5% in 2024. Current FHFA data confirms this momentum, with a year-over-year increase of 13.51%, outperforming the Nebraska average of 1.96% by 11.55 percentage points. This rapid appreciation contrasts with the modest 30-year fixed mortgage rate of 7.40%, which typically suppresses demand in higher-priced markets but has not halted growth here. The primary driver appears to be constrained supply; with only 20 new single-family units authorized in 2025 and zero multi-family construction, the limited inventory is likely amplifying price increases despite a small population of 6,305. Economic fundamentals present a mixed picture that drives the market’s stability and vitality metrics. Local employment stands at 1,791 jobs with an average weekly wage of $984, supporting a median household income of $66,105, which is nearly on par with the national average (2.8% lower). However, the low employment base limits long-term economic resilience, contributing to a lower vitality assessment. Migration data shows a net gain of 58 filers, with incoming households earning slightly less than those leaving, suggesting a stable but not rapidly expanding demographic base. For prospective buyers, the current environment offers exceptional entry-point affordability, though the recent double-digit price appreciation suggests that the historical low-cost entry window is narrowing. Renters face minimal financial strain, but the strong owner-occupancy preference and minimal new construction indicate that rental stock remains scarce, keeping rent-to-price dynamics favorable for homeownership.

The median home value in Antelope County is 20.9% below the Nebraska state average and 42.8% below the U.S. national average. The median rent of $692/month is 13.1% below the state average.
