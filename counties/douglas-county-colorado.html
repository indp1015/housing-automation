📝 Local Market Analysis

Douglas County, Colorado, operates as a distinctively balanced market with strong vitality but significant affordability constraints. The composite assessment highlights a tension between robust economic fundamentals and high entry barriers. With a median household income of $149,594 and an average weekly wage of $1,766, the local economy supports substantial purchasing power, yet the median home value of $713,600 creates a price-to-income ratio of 4.77, significantly exceeding the national average of 3.20. This disparity drives the low affordability metric, indicating that while the market is stable and active, it remains expensive relative to local earnings. The high owner-occupancy rate of 77.4% further underscores the market’s establishment as a primary residence hub rather than a speculative investment zone. Trend analysis reveals a period of rapid appreciation followed by stabilization. Home values surged from $407,100 in 2017 to $713,600 in 2024, with a peak year-over-year increase of 17.5% in 2022. Recent momentum has moderated to a 5.9% annual increase, aligning with the FHFA HPI’s current 1.74% year-over-year growth, which outpaces the state average of -0.61%. This divergence suggests Douglas County is outperforming the broader Colorado market, likely driven by sustained net migration of 9,757 filers and strong local employment of 132,547 jobs. The price-to-rent ratio of 27.1, compared to the national 18.7, indicates that purchasing remains the dominant long-term strategy despite high costs, as rent burden sits at a manageable 17.6% of income. The market’s vitality is further supported by consistent housing supply, with 2,398 new units authorized in 2025, including 1,465 single-family homes. However, the 7.40% national mortgage rate adds friction to transaction costs, potentially dampening buyer velocity. The significant premium of 66.9% over the state average home value reflects the county’s desirability and high-income demographic, where inflowing residents earn an average of $169,801. Consequently, the market exhibits high stability and vitality scores due to strong economic indicators and population growth, while the affordability score remains suppressed by the high price-to-income and price-to-rent multiples. Potential participants should recognize that while the market is resilient and growing, the entry price point is substantially higher than national benchmarks, requiring higher income levels to achieve standard affordability metrics.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Douglas County is 66.9% above the Colorado state average and 213.4% above the U.S. national average. The median rent of $2,193/month is 68.4% above the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 27.1, Douglas County is a strongly rent-favored market. Home prices are high relative to rents, meaning renting is typically far cheaper than owning in the near term.

Douglas County has an owner-occupancy rate of 77.4%, compared to 71.9% in Colorado and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more homeowner-heavy market than the U.S. average.
