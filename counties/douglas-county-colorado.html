📝 Editor's Insight

Douglas County exhibits a pronounced owner-occupancy bias, with a 77.8% rate and a price-to-rent ratio of 26.8, significantly exceeding the national average of 18.0. This disparity indicates that purchasing remains more economically advantageous than renting for qualified buyers, despite high entry costs. Over five years, median home values surged at a 9.5% CAGR, outpacing the 5.0% rent inflation, suggesting sustained capital appreciation driven by specific local demand factors rather than general market trends. Affordability presents a mixed profile; while the rent burden of 17.3% aligns closely with the national 17.2% benchmark, the price-to-income ratio of 4.62 is substantially higher than the national 3.09. This divergence highlights that housing costs are manageable for renters but challenging for buyers. A critical insight is that the county’s median household income of $145,737, which is 120.9% above the national average, partially offsets the 4.62 price-to-income ratio. Consequently, the market is not uniformly unaffordable but is segmented, where high-income professionals can absorb the premium, while lower-income households face significant barriers to homeownership despite reasonable rental costs.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Douglas County is 69.3% above the Colorado state average and 217.1% above the U.S. national average. The median rent of $2,095/month is 67.2% above the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 26.8, Douglas County is a strongly rent-favored market. Home prices are high relative to rents, meaning renting is typically far cheaper than owning in the near term.

Douglas County has an owner-occupancy rate of 77.8%, compared to 72.1% in Colorado and 73.0% nationally. This indicates a more homeowner-heavy market than the U.S. average.
