📝 Editor's Insight

Alamosa County presents a balanced market leaning toward renters, evidenced by a price-to-rent ratio of 20.2, which exceeds the national average of 18.0. This metric indicates that purchasing costs are high relative to rental expenses, yet the 56.4% owner-occupancy rate suggests sufficient inventory for buyers. The five-year trend reveals significant appreciation, with home values growing at an 8.6% CAGR compared to a 6.6% CAGR for rents. This divergence signals that equity accumulation is outpacing rental cost inflation, favoring long-term owners over short-term tenants. Affordability remains a critical constraint; the price-to-income ratio stands at 4.25, substantially above the national benchmark of 3.09. Additionally, the rent burden consumes 21.1% of median income, surpassing the national average of 17.2%. A non-obvious insight is the county’s extreme income disparity relative to national averages. With median household income 22.0% below the national mean, the modest 2.9% premium in home values masks severe affordability challenges. Residents must prioritize income stability, as the market’s low absolute prices do not offset the high cost of living relative to local earnings, making entry-level financing particularly difficult despite seemingly low sticker prices.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Alamosa County is 44.1% below the Colorado state average and 5.0% above the U.S. national average. The median rent of $907/month is 30.3% below the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 22.0, renting is generally more cost-effective than buying in the short to medium term. The market favors buyers only for long-term holds.

Alamosa County has an owner-occupancy rate of 55.9%, compared to 71.9% in Colorado and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more renter-heavy market than the U.S. average.
