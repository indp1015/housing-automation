📝 Local Market Analysis

Chatham County, North Carolina, functions as a distinctively balanced market with strong underlying vitality, though affordability constraints remain significant. The composite assessment reflects high stability and vitality scores driven by robust economic fundamentals, contrasted by a low affordability rating. The county exhibits a price-to-income ratio of 4.73, substantially exceeding the national average of 3.20, which indicates that home values have outpaced local earnings growth. However, the rent burden stands at 15.0% of income, below the national average of 17.2%, suggesting that rental housing remains relatively accessible compared to ownership costs. This divergence highlights a market where entry-level ownership is challenging, yet the rental sector provides a more manageable cost structure for residents. Trend analysis reveals a period of aggressive appreciation, with median home values rising from $251,600 in 2017 to $446,200 in 2024, a cumulative increase of approximately 77%. Recent momentum remains strong, with the FHFA HPI showing a 5.99% year-over-year increase in 2025, significantly outperforming the North Carolina state average of 0.38%. This outperformance is supported by positive net migration of 2,445 filers between 2021 and 2022, where incoming residents reported an average income of $137,765, higher than the outflow average. The local economy supports this demand through 17,591 total jobs and an average weekly wage of $1,078. Housing supply remains constrained by a lack of multi-family development, with 517 new units authorized in 2025 consisting entirely of single-family homes. This limited supply, combined with a 7.40% national mortgage rate, creates a competitive environment for buyers. The regional context underscores Chatham County’s premium positioning, with home values 96.0% above the national average and 89.6% above the state average. While income levels are 38.7% above the national mean, they have not kept pace with the 96.0% premium on housing costs, driving the affordability score down to 38.8. The price-to-rent ratio of 31.5, nearly double the national average of 18.7, further illustrates the disparity between ownership and rental costs. For potential buyers, the high price-to-income ratio and rising mortgage rates necessitate careful financial planning, while renters may find the current rent burden favorable relative to national standards. The market’s vitality is sustained by high-income migration and stable employment, but the sharp divergence between price growth and income growth suggests that affordability will remain the primary barrier for entry-level buyers in the near term.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Chatham County is 89.6% above the North Carolina state average and 96.0% above the U.S. national average. The median rent of $1,180/month is 20.9% above the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 31.5, Chatham County is a strongly rent-favored market. Home prices are high relative to rents, meaning renting is typically far cheaper than owning in the near term.

Chatham County has an owner-occupancy rate of 80.6%, compared to 71.8% in North Carolina and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more homeowner-heavy market than the U.S. average.
