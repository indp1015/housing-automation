📝 Editor's Insight

Citrus County, Florida, presents a balanced market profile with distinct affordability challenges for buyers, though it remains relatively neutral for renters. The price-to-rent ratio stands at 18.5, nearly identical to the national average of 18.7, suggesting that the financial burden of renting is comparable to owning when considering long-term costs. However, the owner-occupancy rate of 85.2% indicates a strong preference for homeownership, which may suppress rental supply and keep median gross rents at $1,107. This high occupancy rate, combined with a median household income of $56,546, creates a structural dynamic where housing demand is heavily weighted toward purchase rather than lease, influencing local inventory levels and price stability. The multi-year trend reveals a period of aggressive appreciation followed by recent stabilization. Home values surged from $117,400 in 2017 to $245,500 in 2024, representing a cumulative increase of over 109%. The peak year-over-year growth of 20.2% in 2022 has since moderated to 10.0% in 2024, indicating a cooling but still positive trajectory. This pattern is corroborated by the FHFA House Price Index, which shows a recent year-over-year change of +0.96%, slightly outpacing the state average of +0.91%. This slight outperformance suggests that while the explosive growth phase has ended, Citrus County maintains a marginal strength relative to the broader Florida market, likely driven by sustained migration and limited new construction relative to demand. Affordability metrics highlight a significant divergence from national benchmarks, particularly for purchasers. The price-to-income ratio of 4.34 is substantially higher than the national average of 3.20, reflecting that homes cost significantly more relative to local earning power. Similarly, the rent burden of 23.5% exceeds the national average of 17.2%, indicating that renters spend a larger share of their income on housing than the typical American household. Regionally, Citrus County home values are 13.7% below the Florida state average, yet local incomes are 16.8% below the national average. This gap suggests that while the county is cheaper than the state median, the lower income base exacerbates the relative cost of entry. Potential buyers and renters must recognize that while the market has cooled from its 2022 peak, the fundamental affordability gap remains wide, with housing costs rising faster than local wages, necessitating careful financial planning to sustain long-term residency.
