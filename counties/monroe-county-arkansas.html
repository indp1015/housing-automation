📝 Editor's Insight

Monroe County operates as a distinct buyer’s market, evidenced by a price-to-rent ratio of 11.4, significantly below the national average of 18.0. This metric indicates that purchasing is substantially more cost-effective than renting for long-term residents. The five-year trend reveals a divergence in asset appreciation, with home values rising at a 7.6% CAGR compared to a 3.9% CAGR for rents. This suggests that equity accumulation through ownership is currently outpacing the cost of tenancy. Affordability remains a strong advantage, as the price-to-income ratio stands at 1.87, well below the national benchmark of 3.09. Additionally, the rent burden sits at 16.5%, slightly under the national average of 17.2%. A critical insight lies in the county’s relative positioning; despite being 37.7% below the state average in home values, the income gap is only 33.4% below the national mean. This disparity implies that local purchasing power is relatively stronger than the raw price differential suggests, making the market particularly accessible for income-qualified buyers seeking high value retention relative to their earnings.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Monroe County is 37.3% below the Arkansas state average and 61.0% below the U.S. national average. The median rent of $614/month is 20.7% below the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 12.0, buying a home in Monroe County is generally more favorable than renting for those planning to stay long-term. A ratio below 15 typically signals that purchasing is financially advantageous over a multi-year horizon.

Monroe County has an owner-occupancy rate of 62.6%, compared to 71.1% in Arkansas and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more renter-heavy market than the U.S. average.
