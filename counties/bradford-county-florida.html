📝 Editor's Insight

Bradford County’s housing market exhibits characteristics of a balanced market, leaning slightly toward buyer favorability. The owner‑occupancy rate of 73.3% indicates a substantial share of households own their homes, while the median gross rent of $863 per month represents 16.4% of the median household income ($63,050), below the national rent‑burden average of 17.2%. This relatively low rent burden, combined with a price‑to‑income ratio of 3.37 (slightly above the national average of 3.20), suggests that purchasing a home requires a modestly higher income multiple than typical U.S. markets, yet rental costs remain comparatively affordable. Over the 2017‑2024 period, home values rose from $90,500 to $212,500, reflecting a cumulative increase of 135% and an average annual growth rate of approximately 12%. The most pronounced jumps occurred in 2022 (+20.8%) and 2023‑2024 (+17.2% and +17.9% respectively), indicating accelerating appreciation after a more moderate rise earlier in the decade. Rent data are not provided in the trend detail, but the derived price‑to‑rent ratio of 20.5 exceeds the national average of 18.7, signaling that home prices have risen faster than rents, widening the gap between owning and leasing costs. Relative to broader benchmarks, Bradford County’s median home value is 6.7% below the national median and 25.3% below the Florida state median, while its median household income is 7.3% beneath the national average. These disparities underscore that the county remains a lower‑cost housing market within both state and national contexts, even as local price growth outpaces state trends. A notable pattern is the divergence between price and rent growth: while home values have surged, the rent burden has stayed below the national average, suggesting that rental supply may be keeping pace with demand or that rent increases have been moderated. This divergence reinforces the price‑to‑rent ratio’s elevation above national levels. The FHFA House Price Index for 2025 shows a county‑level year‑over‑year increase of +1.58%, outperforming the state’s +0.91% change by 0.67 percentage points. This recent momentum confirms that Bradford County’s home price appreciation continues to exceed statewide momentum, albeit at a more modest pace than the double‑digit gains seen in 2022‑2024. For prospective buyers, the elevated price‑to‑income ratio implies that financing a home may require a larger share of income compared with national norms, though absolute prices remain below state and national medians. Renters benefit from a relatively low rent burden, but the rising price‑to‑rent ratio suggests that purchasing may become comparatively less attractive
