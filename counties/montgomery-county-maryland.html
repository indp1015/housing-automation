📝 Local Market Analysis

Montgomery County, Maryland, functions as a distinctly renter-friendly market, driven by extreme valuation premiums that suppress owner affordability while sustaining robust rental demand. The price-to-income ratio stands at 4.83, significantly exceeding the national average of 3.20, while the price-to-rent ratio of 25.8 far outpaces the national benchmark of 18.7. These metrics indicate that purchasing equity is substantially more expensive relative to income and rental costs than in typical U.S. jurisdictions. Consequently, the market classification leans heavily toward renters, as the financial threshold for homeownership remains prohibitive for the median household earning $132,450. The composite assessment reflects this stress, with affordability scoring only 18.4 out of 100, dragging down the overall stability and vitality indices despite strong momentum. The multi-year trend reveals a sharp acceleration in home values, peaking at 15.8% year-over-year growth in 2022 before moderating to 4.1% in 2024. This surge is contextualized by the county’s median home value of $640,300, which is 73.0% higher than the state average and 181.2% above the national mean. Recent FHFA HPI data confirms a slowdown in price appreciation, with a 1.32% year-over-year increase in 2025, slightly outpacing the state’s 0.95% growth. This divergence between historical price spikes and current cooling suggests a market adjusting to high interest rates, currently at 7.40%. Meanwhile, the rent burden of 18.7% remains only marginally above the national average of 17.2%, indicating that rental costs are more aligned with local income levels than purchase prices are. Economic fundamentals support sustained demand, with 364,761 total jobs and an average weekly wage of $1,761. Migration data shows a net gain of 1,045 filers, with inflowing residents averaging $157,389 in income, suggesting affluent demographic shifts that underpin high valuations. Housing supply remains constrained, with only 1,411 new units authorized in 2025, limiting the ability to meet demand. For potential buyers, the primary barrier is the 4.83 price-to-income ratio, which requires significant income leverage or down payments. Renters, however, face a more balanced cost structure, though the 25.8 price-to-ratio implies that long-term rental costs are high relative to the asset value. The market’s vitality is driven by high-income employment and migration, yet the affordability axis remains the critical constraint, keeping the overall assessment in the stressed category despite strong momentum.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Montgomery County is 73.0% above the Maryland state average and 181.2% above the U.S. national average. The median rent of $2,068/month is 41.7% above the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 25.8, Montgomery County is a strongly rent-favored market. Home prices are high relative to rents, meaning renting is typically far cheaper than owning in the near term.

Montgomery County has an owner-occupancy rate of 65.3%, compared to 72.7% in Maryland and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more renter-heavy market than the U.S. average.
