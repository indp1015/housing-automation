📝 Editor's Insight

Jackson County functions as a distinct renter’s market, evidenced by a price-to-rent ratio of 14.0, significantly below the national average of 18.0. This metric indicates that renting is comparatively cheaper than purchasing, a structural advantage for tenants in this small community of 1,422 residents. The five-year trend reveals a severe divergence in asset appreciation, with rents surging at a 24.1% CAGR to reach $1,484 monthly, while home values grew at a more moderate 6.2% CAGR to $250,000. This rapid rental inflation outpaces property value growth, suggesting acute pressure on the limited rental inventory. Affordability remains strained despite the lower home prices; the price-to-income ratio stands at 5.98, nearly double the national benchmark of 3.09, while the rent burden consumes 42.6% of median income compared to the 17.2% national average. A critical insight for potential residents is that the high owner-occupancy rate of 73.2% limits rental supply. Consequently, the 24.1% rent CAGR likely reflects scarcity rather than broad economic expansion, meaning new renters face a thin, rapidly appreciating market where housing costs escalate faster than local wage growth or property values.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Jackson County is 37.2% below the Colorado state average and 17.6% above the U.S. national average. The median rent of $1,484/month is 18.4% above the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 14.0, buying a home in Jackson County is generally more favorable than renting for those planning to stay long-term. A ratio below 15 typically signals that purchasing is financially advantageous over a multi-year horizon.

Jackson County has an owner-occupancy rate of 73.2%, compared to 72.1% in Colorado and 73.0% nationally. This indicates a more homeowner-heavy market than the U.S. average.
