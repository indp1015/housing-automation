📝 Local Market Analysis

Jackson County, Colorado, functions as a distinctively stressed market characterized by severe affordability constraints rather than a traditional buyer’s or renter’s advantage. The price-to-income ratio stands at 5.03, significantly exceeding the national average of 3.20, while the rent burden consumes 42.8% of median household income compared to the national norm of 17.2%. These metrics indicate that both ownership and rental options impose substantial financial strain on residents, with the median household income of $47,667 lagging 29.9% below the national average. Consequently, the market is defined by high entry barriers relative to local earning potential, rendering it inaccessible for many without external income sources or significant debt leverage. The multi-year trend reveals volatility and a recent deceleration in home values, which declined 4.2% year-over-year to $239,600 in 2024. This contrasts with the FHFA HPI showing a 5.78% year-over-year increase, a divergence likely driven by the small sample size of mortgage transactions in this low-volume market. Notably, the county outperforms the state average by 6.39 percentage points in recent price momentum, yet it remains 44.0% below the state median home value. This disconnect highlights that while recent transactional data suggests upward pressure, the broader valuation context remains depressed relative to Colorado’s overall housing market. The limited housing supply, with only two new units authorized in 2025, and a net migration gain of just 33 filers, suggest that inventory constraints are not driving rapid appreciation but rather stabilizing a small, static population base. Economic fundamentals further complicate the outlook, as total employment consists of only 381 jobs across 76 establishments, with an average weekly wage of $1,034. This sparse economic base limits the depth of the buyer pool, contributing to a stability score of 27.3 out of 100. The composite assessment reflects a market where momentum (60.0) and vitality (54.0) are undermined by critical affordability deficits (4.6). For potential stakeholders, the primary implication is that local income levels do not support the current price points, creating a structural mismatch. The 7.40% national mortgage rate exacerbates this burden, making debt service costs prohibitive for the median earner. Ultimately, the market’s trajectory is less about price appreciation and more about the tension between rising rents and stagnant local wages, necessitating a focus on income adequacy rather than asset growth.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Jackson County is 44.0% below the Colorado state average and 5.2% above the U.S. national average. The median rent of $1,701/month is 30.6% above the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 11.7, buying a home in Jackson County is generally more favorable than renting for those planning to stay long-term. A ratio below 15 typically signals that purchasing is financially advantageous over a multi-year horizon.

Jackson County has an owner-occupancy rate of 71.1%, compared to 71.9% in Colorado and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more renter-heavy market than the U.S. average.
