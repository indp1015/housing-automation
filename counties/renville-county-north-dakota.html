📝 Local Market Analysis

Renville County, North Dakota, functions as a highly affordable buyer’s market, characterized by a price-to-income ratio of 2.23, significantly below the national average of 3.20. The median home value of $166,000 sits 27.1% below the national mean, while the median household income of $74,301 exceeds the national benchmark by 9.3%. This disparity creates a favorable environment for homeownership, further supported by a rent burden of 13.4%, which is well under the national average of 17.2%. The price-to-rent ratio of 16.7 also remains below the national standard of 18.7, indicating that purchasing is generally more cost-effective than long-term renting. Consequently, the market exhibits strong affordability metrics, though this is tempered by limited economic vitality, evidenced by total employment of only 454 jobs and an average weekly wage of $1,072. The multi-year trend reveals significant volatility followed by stabilization. Home values surged 16.4% in 2022, reaching a peak before moderating to a 1.6% increase in 2024. This deceleration aligns with the current 30-year fixed mortgage rate of 7.40%, which suppresses rapid appreciation. A notable pattern is the convergence of home value and rent growth rates, suggesting a balanced adjustment rather than a divergence that would typically signal an impending correction. Net migration is negligible, with only three net filers entering the county in 2021-2022, and the average income of inflowing residents ($87,572) is nearly identical to outflowing residents ($88,243). This demographic stagnation, combined with just one new single-family unit authorized in 2025, indicates a static supply-demand dynamic. The overall market assessment is driven by high affordability and moderate momentum, offset by low vitality and stability scores. The low vitality score reflects the small population of 2,304 and limited business establishments, which constrain long-term economic resilience. Stability is further challenged by the historical price swings, including the 5.0% decline in 2020. For potential buyers, the primary consideration is the low entry cost relative to income, but the lack of new construction and minimal population growth suggest limited liquidity and appreciation potential. Renter demand is stable, with median gross rent at $828 per month, but the high owner-occupancy rate of 78.7% limits the rental inventory. The market is effectively a niche, low-growth environment where affordability is the primary driver of value, rather than speculative momentum or robust economic expansion.
