📝 Local Market Analysis

Chariton County, Missouri, operates as a distinctly buyer’s market, characterized by extreme affordability metrics that significantly diverge from national norms. The price-to-income ratio stands at 2.25, well below the national average of 3.20, while the rent burden is only 9.5% of household income compared to the 17.2% national benchmark. These figures indicate that housing costs consume a minimal portion of local earnings, making homeownership highly accessible relative to the median household income of $71,520. However, the price-to-rent ratio of 23.6 exceeds the national average of 18.7, suggesting that while absolute costs are low, the relative value of buying versus renting is less favorable than in typical markets. This dynamic creates a balanced entry point for buyers, though the economic incentive to purchase is driven more by low absolute prices than by rental cost comparisons. The multi-year trend reveals significant volatility, with home values surging 35.3% year-over-year in 2024 to reach $160,900. This sharp increase contrasts with the FHFA HPI’s more moderate 7.40% county-wide growth, which still outperforms the state’s 3.71% rate. The discrepancy between the Census data and mortgage-based indices may reflect a shift in transaction types or local market adjustments. Despite this recent spike, the county remains 8.4% below the state average and 29.3% below the national median, anchoring it in the lower price tier. The local economy supports this stability through a median household income that exceeds the national average by 5.2%, yet employment remains constrained with only 1,338 total jobs and an average weekly wage of $906. Housing supply is critically limited, with only two new units authorized in 2025, both single-family, which likely contributes to the recent price acceleration. Migration data shows a net inflow of 96 filers, with incoming residents averaging an income of $62,199, slightly lower than those leaving. This modest population growth, combined with minimal new construction, suggests that current price momentum is driven by scarcity rather than robust economic expansion. The composite assessment highlights high affordability and moderate stability, but low vitality due to the stagnant job base and negligible new supply. For potential buyers, the current environment offers low entry costs, but the recent 35.3% price jump signals that the market is tightening rapidly. Renters face minimal financial burden, yet the rising price-to-rent ratio implies that long-term rental costs may not keep pace with the accelerating asset values, favoring those who can secure financing in the current 7.40% mortgage rate environment.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Chariton County is 8.4% below the Missouri state average and 29.3% below the U.S. national average. The median rent of $567/month is 27.5% below the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 23.6, renting is generally more cost-effective than buying in the short to medium term. The market favors buyers only for long-term holds.

Chariton County has an owner-occupancy rate of 83.5%, compared to 73.7% in Missouri and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more homeowner-heavy market than the U.S. average.
