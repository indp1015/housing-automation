📝 Local Market Analysis

Emmet County, Iowa, functions as a distinct buyer’s market, characterized by extreme affordability and strong price momentum. The price-to-income ratio stands at 1.79, significantly below the national average of 3.20, while the rent burden is only 13.8% of income compared to the national norm of 17.2%. These metrics indicate that housing costs are exceptionally low relative to local earning power, with a median household income of $63,730 supporting a median home value of just $114,100. The price-to-rent ratio of 13.0 further underscores the economic advantage of ownership over renting, as it is well below the national benchmark of 18.7. This structural affordability drives the high owner occupancy rate of 79.4%, suggesting a stable resident base less susceptible to rental market volatility. The market exhibits a notable divergence between price appreciation and rental growth, signaling a shift in asset value dynamics. Home values surged 12.9% in 2022 and continued to rise 7.4% in 2024, whereas the median gross rent remains modest at $733. Recent FHFA data confirms this upward trajectory, showing a 4.42% year-over-year increase in 2025, which outpaces the Iowa state average of 2.25% by 2.17 percentage points. This outperformance highlights specific local demand drivers rather than broad state-wide trends. The rapid price escalation relative to the slower rent growth suggests that capital appreciation is currently the primary value driver, potentially widening the gap between entry-level purchase prices and rental costs over time. Economic fundamentals provide a mixed backdrop for this momentum. While net migration of 223 filers and an average weekly wage of $966 support demand, the local economy is small, with only 2,626 total jobs and 300 establishments. Housing supply is extremely constrained, with just 14 new single-family units authorized in 2025 and zero multi-family units, creating an inventory shortage that likely exacerbates price increases. The current 30-year fixed mortgage rate of 7.40% increases monthly payment burdens, yet the low purchase price mitigates this impact. Consequently, the market’s strength is driven by affordability and scarcity rather than robust economic vitality, as evidenced by the moderate vitality component of the composite assessment. Buyers benefit from low entry costs, while renters face limited leverage due to the low rent burden and high owner occupancy.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Emmet County is 33.3% below the Iowa state average and 49.9% below the U.S. national average. The median rent of $733/month is 11.9% below the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 13.0, buying a home in Emmet County is generally more favorable than renting for those planning to stay long-term. A ratio below 15 typically signals that purchasing is financially advantageous over a multi-year horizon.

Emmet County has an owner-occupancy rate of 79.4%, compared to 75.8% in Iowa and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more homeowner-heavy market than the U.S. average.
