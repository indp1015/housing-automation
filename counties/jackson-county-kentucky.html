📝 Local Market Analysis

Jackson County, Kentucky, functions as a distinctively affordable buyer’s market, characterized by a price-to-income ratio of 2.79, which sits below the national average of 3.20. The median home value of $111,500 is 51.0% lower than the national mean and 30.7% below the state average, creating significant entry-level accessibility. This affordability is reinforced by a price-to-rent ratio of 14.0, well under the national benchmark of 18.7, suggesting that ownership costs are relatively low compared to rental equivalents. However, the rent burden stands at 20.0% of median household income, slightly exceeding the national average of 17.2%, indicating that while buying is accessible, renting imposes a moderate financial strain on the $40,000 median household. The market exhibits strong recent momentum, with home values rising faster than rents and a year-over-year increase of 4.2% in 2024. This follows a volatile period where prices dipped in 2020 and 2021 before surging 26.7% in 2022. The FHFA House Price Index recorded a 12.26% year-over-year change in 2021, outperforming the state by 8.00 percentage points, signaling a localized rebound that has since moderated. This trajectory contrasts with the broader economic constraints, as the local economy supports only 1,073 jobs with an average weekly wage of $846. Despite these limited employment metrics, net migration added 147 filers between 2021 and 2022, with inflows averaging $47,428 in income, slightly higher than outflows. This demographic shift suggests that external demand, rather than local wage growth, is driving the recent appreciation in property values. The overall market assessment reflects a tension between high momentum and low vitality. While the 80.8 momentum score highlights the rapid post-2021 price recovery, the 10.1 vitality score underscores the thin local economic base and limited establishment count of 178. Stability remains moderate at 45.3, influenced by the earlier price corrections and the current 7.32% mortgage rate, which increases monthly carrying costs for new buyers. For potential purchasers, the primary advantage is the substantial discount relative to national and state averages, offering equity potential in a market where prices are stabilizing after a sharp correction. Renters, meanwhile, face a market where ownership is increasingly attractive due to the low price-to-rent ratio, though the limited local job market may restrict long-term income growth for residents relying solely on county-based employment.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Jackson County is 30.7% below the Kentucky state average and 51.0% below the U.S. national average. The median rent of $665/month is 16.1% below the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 14.0, buying a home in Jackson County is generally more favorable than renting for those planning to stay long-term. A ratio below 15 typically signals that purchasing is financially advantageous over a multi-year horizon.

Jackson County has an owner-occupancy rate of 77.4%, compared to 73.4% in Kentucky and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more homeowner-heavy market than the U.S. average.
