📝 Local Market Analysis

Greenwood County, Kansas, functions as a distinctively affordable market, characterized by a price-to-income ratio of 1.48, significantly below the national average of 3.20. This extreme affordability is reinforced by a rent burden of 14.3%, which is lower than the national benchmark of 17.2%. The price-to-rent ratio of 10.3 further indicates that purchasing is comparatively cheaper than renting relative to national standards, suggesting a buyer’s advantage in terms of entry cost. However, the market’s small scale, with a population of 5,919 and only 998 total jobs, limits liquidity and economic depth. The average weekly wage of $872 supports local purchasing power but remains modest in absolute terms, aligning with the median household income of $60,042, which is 11.7% below the national average. Recent data reveals a sharp acceleration in home values, with median prices surging 27.3% in 2023 before moderating to 7.4% in 2024. This trend diverges from the relatively stable rental market, where the median gross rent stands at $715. The significant jump in home values, rising from $64,900 in 2022 to $88,700 in 2024, suggests a supply-constrained environment. Indeed, only one new housing unit was authorized in 2025, indicating negligible new supply to meet demand. This scarcity, combined with net positive migration of 177 filers between 2021 and 2022, likely contributed to the recent price appreciation. The high owner-occupancy rate of 80.5% reflects a stable, established community rather than a transient rental market. In regional context, Greenwood County’s median home value is 36.1% below the Kansas state average and 61.0% below the national average, positioning it as a low-cost outlier. The local economy, with 160 establishments, offers limited employment diversity, which may constrain long-term income growth. The current 30-year fixed mortgage rate of 7.40% increases the monthly cost of borrowing, potentially tempering further price increases despite the low asset values. For potential buyers, the primary consideration is the limited inventory and the recent rapid price appreciation, which may erode some of the affordability advantage if current trends persist. Renters face lower financial burdens compared to national averages, but the low rental stock may limit options. The market’s trajectory is heavily influenced by its small size and lack of new construction, creating a fragile equilibrium where minor demand shifts can cause significant price volatility.
