📝 Local Market Analysis

Stanton County, Kansas, operates as a distinctively affordable market characterized by extreme value relative to national benchmarks. The median home value of $67,800 sits 70.2% below the national average, while the price-to-income ratio of 0.95 is drastically lower than the national norm of 3.20. This disparity indicates a buyer’s market where local incomes, averaging $71,700, significantly exceed the cost of entry into homeownership. Similarly, the rent burden is only 11.8% of household income compared to the 17.2% national average, suggesting that rental costs are minimal relative to local purchasing power. The price-to-rent ratio of 8.0, nearly half the national average of 18.7, further underscores that buying is economically advantageous over renting, as the cost of ownership is low enough to outpace rental expenses rapidly. The market exhibits high volatility and unstable pricing trends rather than consistent appreciation. Home values peaked at $92,600 in 2019 before plummeting 31.7% in 2020 to $63,200, a correction that disrupted the previous upward trajectory. Subsequent years show erratic movement, with a 13.2% drop in 2023 followed by a 5.6% recovery in 2024. This instability contrasts with the noted signal that rents are rising faster than home values, creating a divergence that may pressure long-term affordability despite current low prices. The local economy supports this limited market depth, with only 387 total jobs and 72 establishments, resulting in an average weekly wage of $967. Migration data reveals zero net change, with 656 filers leaving and 656 arriving, indicating a static population base that limits demand expansion. Practical implications for stakeholders center on the intersection of low absolute costs and high mortgage rates. With the 30-year fixed mortgage rate at 7.40%, the low median home price keeps monthly debt service manageable, but the small population of 2,038 residents restricts liquidity and resale potential. The owner-occupancy rate of 76.5% suggests a stable housing stock, yet the economic base is fragile due to the lack of significant employment growth or net migration. Buyers should recognize that while the price-to-income ratio offers exceptional affordability, the historical volatility in home values, including the sharp 2020 decline, reflects a market sensitive to external shocks rather than local fundamental strength. Rents at $705 per month remain low in absolute terms, but the rising trend relative to home values suggests that the rental sector is becoming slightly more competitive, potentially narrowing the gap between renting and buying costs over time.

The median home value in Stanton County is 51.2% below the Kansas state average and 70.2% below the U.S. national average. The median rent of $705/month is 12.1% below the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 8.0, buying a home in Stanton County is generally more favorable than renting for those planning to stay long-term. A ratio below 15 typically signals that purchasing is financially advantageous over a multi-year horizon.
