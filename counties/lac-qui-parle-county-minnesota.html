📝 Local Market Analysis

Lac qui Parle County presents a balanced market structure, characterized by strong affordability metrics that offset limited economic vitality. The price-to-income ratio stands at 2.12, significantly below the national average of 3.20, while the rent burden is only 11.7% of income compared to the national 17.2%. These figures indicate that housing costs remain manageable relative to local earning power, with a median household income of $74,432 supporting a median home value of $157,600. The owner-occupancy rate of 82.5% further suggests a stable residential base, though the low vitality score of 32.0 reflects the county’s small scale, evidenced by just 1,384 total jobs and 212 establishments. Recent trends reveal a period of accelerated appreciation, with home values rising from $81,100 in 2017 to $157,600 in 2024. This represents a cumulative increase of nearly 94% over seven years, peaking at 19.0% year-over-year growth in 2022 before moderating to 9.9% in 2024. The FHFA HPI data for 2025 indicates a sharp 23.13% year-over-year increase, outperforming the Minnesota state average of 2.42% by 20.71 percentage points. This divergence highlights a localized surge in demand, likely driven by net migration of 117 filers between 2021 and 2022, despite the outflowing population having a slightly higher average income of $80,201 compared to the $78,753 average for inflows. The primary constraint in this market is supply, with only six new housing units authorized in 2025, all single-family. This scarcity, combined with high mortgage rates of 7.40%, creates tension between rising prices and buyer purchasing power. While the price-to-rent ratio of 18.0 aligns closely with the national average of 18.7, the rapid appreciation of home values relative to rents suggests that ownership costs are escalating faster than rental alternatives. For potential buyers, the affordability metrics remain favorable, but the momentum driven by limited inventory and strong price growth requires careful consideration of long-term holding costs. The market’s stability is underpinned by its low debt-to-income ratios, yet the small economic base limits broader growth potential, making the current price surge heavily dependent on external demand rather than local job creation.
