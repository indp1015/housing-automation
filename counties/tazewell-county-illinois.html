📝 Local Market Analysis

Tazewell County, Illinois, functions as a balanced market with distinct affordability advantages, characterized by a price-to-income ratio of 2.15 compared to the national average of 3.20. The median home value of $167,500 sits 26.4% below the national mean, while the median gross rent of $909 results in a rent burden of only 14.0%, well below the national benchmark of 17.2%. This dual affordability profile suggests that both purchasing and renting remain financially accessible relative to broader U.S. standards. The market’s stability is further evidenced by a price-to-rent ratio of 15.4, indicating that ownership costs are not disproportionately high compared to rental expenses, a metric that typically signals a healthy equilibrium between the two housing sectors. Recent trend data reveals a period of accelerated appreciation that has since normalized. Home values increased by 9.3% in 2022, a peak year of growth, before moderating to 3.4% in 2023 and 1.8% in 2024. However, current FHFA HPI data indicates a recent momentum shift, with a year-over-year county change of 6.74%, outperforming the state average of 5.54% by 1.20 percentage points. This divergence suggests that Tazewell County is currently experiencing stronger price appreciation than the surrounding Illinois market. The local economic foundation supports this activity, with a median household income of $77,982, which is 14.7% above the national average. Furthermore, positive net migration of 2,167 filers between 2021 and 2022 demonstrates sustained demographic interest, driven by an average inflow income of $83,835. Housing supply remains constrained relative to demand, with only 118 new units authorized in 2025, comprising 84 single-family and 34 multi-family homes. This limited new construction, combined with a high owner-occupancy rate of 78.4%, suggests that existing inventory is the primary driver of market dynamics. The national 30-year fixed mortgage rate of 7.40% presents a significant financing hurdle, yet the county’s low price-to-income ratio mitigates this impact compared to higher-priced markets. For prospective buyers, the combination of above-average incomes and below-average home values creates a favorable entry point, despite the current high interest rate environment. Renters benefit from low cost burdens, though the recent 6.74% price appreciation may gradually trickle down into rental markets as landlords adjust to higher asset values. The overall picture is one of a resilient, affordable market that is currently outpacing state-level growth metrics.
