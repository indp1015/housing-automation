📝 Editor's Insight

Yavapai County exhibits a distinct renter’s market, evidenced by a price-to-rent ratio of 26.0, significantly exceeding the national average of 18.0. This metric indicates that purchasing equity is comparatively more expensive than leasing relative to local income levels. The five-year trend reveals aggressive appreciation, with home values compounding at 11.7% annually, outpacing the 7.4% rent growth rate. This divergence suggests that asset values are expanding faster than the cost of living for tenants, potentially widening the wealth gap between owners and renters. Affordability metrics confirm severe strain; the price-to-income ratio stands at 5.94, nearly double the national benchmark of 3.09. Furthermore, the rent burden consumes 22.8% of median household income, surpassing the 17.2% national average. A critical insight is that while home values are 61.4% above the state average, median income remains virtually identical to the national mean. This disparity implies that local purchasing power has not kept pace with asset inflation, making entry-level homeownership exceptionally difficult despite the region’s high owner-occupancy rate of 73.5%.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Yavapai County is 61.4% above the Arizona state average and 85.7% above the U.S. national average. The median rent of $1,258/month is 23.8% above the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 26.0, Yavapai County is a strongly rent-favored market. Home prices are high relative to rents, meaning renting is typically far cheaper than owning in the near term.

Yavapai County has an owner-occupancy rate of 73.5%, compared to 70.3% in Arizona and 73.0% nationally. This indicates a more homeowner-heavy market than the U.S. average.
