📝 Editor's Insight

Lawrence County presents a distinct buyer’s advantage, evidenced by a price-to-rent ratio of 10.6, significantly below the national average of 18.0. This metric indicates that purchasing is substantially more cost-effective than renting. Over the past five years, however, rental costs have escalated at a 6.8% CAGR, outpacing the 4.4% growth in home values. This divergence suggests increasing pressure on the rental sector relative to homeownership. Affordability remains strong for buyers, with a price-to-income ratio of 2.06, well under the 3.09 national benchmark. Conversely, renters face a 19.4% income burden, exceeding the 17.2% national average. A critical non-obvious insight is the disparity between state and national metrics. While home values are 57.1% below the national mean, they are only 31.0% below the Arkansas average. This implies Lawrence County is relatively expensive compared to other parts of the state, despite its low absolute price point. Potential residents should recognize that local affordability is not uniform; the county commands a premium relative to its broader state context, which may limit cross-county purchasing power for those relying on regional income levels.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Lawrence County is 31.0% below the Arkansas state average and 56.9% below the U.S. national average. The median rent of $715/month is 5.8% below the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 10.6, buying a home in Lawrence County is generally more favorable than renting for those planning to stay long-term. A ratio below 15 typically signals that purchasing is financially advantageous over a multi-year horizon.

Lawrence County has an owner-occupancy rate of 68.2%, compared to 70.5% in Arkansas and 73.0% nationally. This indicates a more renter-heavy market than the U.S. average.
