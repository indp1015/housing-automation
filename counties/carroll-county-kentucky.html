📝 Local Market Analysis

Carroll County, Kentucky, exhibits characteristics of a buyer’s market, defined by a price-to-income ratio of 2.50, significantly below the national average of 3.20. The median home value of $140,900 sits 38.1% below the national mean, while the price-to-rent ratio of 12.9 is well under the national benchmark of 18.7, suggesting that purchasing remains more financially efficient than renting for qualified buyers. However, the market’s stability is compromised by extreme volatility in recent years. Home values surged 17.6% in 2022 before moderating to 8.6% in 2024, indicating a correction from a sharp spike rather than steady organic growth. This volatility contrasts with the broader state trend, as the county outperformed Kentucky’s 4.26% year-over-year home price increase by 11.43 percentage points, reaching a 15.69% gain in the latest FHFA HPI period. Affordability metrics present a mixed picture. While home prices are accessible relative to income, the rent burden stands at 19.3% of median household income, exceeding the national average of 17.2%. This divergence is highlighted by the trend signal indicating rents are rising faster than home values, a pattern that erodes the financial advantage of homeownership for lower-income households. The local economy provides limited support for sustained price appreciation, with only 7,097 total jobs and an average weekly wage of $1,535. Housing supply is critically constrained, evidenced by a single new unit authorized in 2025, which likely contributes to the upward pressure on both rents and home prices despite the small population of 10,954. Migration patterns offer a modest positive indicator, with a net inflow of 250 filers between 2021 and 2022, though the average income of incoming residents ($60,082) only slightly exceeds that of those leaving ($60,391). The national 30-year fixed mortgage rate of 7.40% further complicates entry for buyers, as high interest rates offset the low absolute price points. Consequently, the market’s vitality is driven primarily by strong momentum indicators rather than robust economic fundamentals or abundant supply. The low stability component of the overall assessment reflects the recent price swings and the tight inventory, while affordability remains a relative strength compared to national standards. Potential buyers should note that while entry costs are low, the lack of new construction and rising rental costs suggest that value retention may depend heavily on continued population growth rather than local economic expansion.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Carroll County is 12.4% below the Kentucky state average and 38.1% below the U.S. national average. The median rent of $910/month is 14.8% above the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 12.9, buying a home in Carroll County is generally more favorable than renting for those planning to stay long-term. A ratio below 15 typically signals that purchasing is financially advantageous over a multi-year horizon.

Carroll County has an owner-occupancy rate of 65.7%, compared to 73.4% in Kentucky and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more renter-heavy market than the U.S. average.
