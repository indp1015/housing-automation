📝 Local Market Analysis

Bradley County, Arkansas, functions as a distinctively affordable buyer’s market, characterized by a price-to-income ratio of 2.29, significantly below the national average of 3.20. The median home value of $94,800 stands 58.4% lower than the national benchmark and 33.0% below the Arkansas state average, reflecting deep economic divergence. While the price-to-rent ratio of 11.5 is well below the national norm of 18.7, suggesting that purchasing is financially superior to renting in the long term, the rent burden of 19.9% slightly exceeds the national average of 17.2%, indicating that rental costs remain a moderate strain on local incomes. This dynamic classifies the area as a buyer’s market, driven by low entry costs rather than high demand pressure. The multi-year trend reveals significant volatility rather than steady growth. Home values surged 15.4% in 2019 before contracting 10.3% in 2021, followed by a recovery to $97,000 in 2023. The most recent 2.3% decline to $94,800 in 2024 signals a stabilization phase after a period of erratic fluctuations. This instability is reflected in the low stability metric of 14.9, which, alongside a vitality score of 16.1, highlights the market’s fragility. The primary drivers of the overall assessment are the weak economic fundamentals and limited market depth, despite reasonable momentum scores of 52.7. The recent negative price adjustment suggests that the initial post-pandemic spike was unsustainable, leading to a correction that aligns prices more closely with local income levels. Economic context further constrains market vitality. With only 2,606 total jobs and an average weekly wage of $900, the local labor market offers limited purchasing power. Housing supply is nearly nonexistent, with just one new unit authorized in 2025, forcing the market to rely entirely on existing inventory. Although net migration is positive at +127 filers, the average income of incoming residents ($50,071) is only marginally higher than those leaving ($50,725), indicating that migration does not significantly boost local demand or price appreciation. Consequently, the market remains highly sensitive to external shocks, such as the current 7.32% mortgage rate, which further dampens buyer interest in an already low-income environment. Potential buyers face a market with high affordability but low liquidity and economic resilience, while renters encounter costs that are proportionally higher relative to local wages than the national average.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Bradley County is 33.0% below the Arkansas state average and 58.4% below the U.S. national average. The median rent of $689/month is 11.0% below the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 11.5, buying a home in Bradley County is generally more favorable than renting for those planning to stay long-term. A ratio below 15 typically signals that purchasing is financially advantageous over a multi-year horizon.

Bradley County has an owner-occupancy rate of 66.0%, compared to 71.1% in Arkansas and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more renter-heavy market than the U.S. average.
