📝 Editor's Insight

Cross County operates as a distinct buyer’s market, evidenced by a price-to-rent ratio of 10.9, significantly below the national average of 18.0. This metric indicates that purchasing is substantially more cost-effective than renting for long-term residents. Over the past five years, home values have appreciated at a 4.8% CAGR, outpacing the 2.9% CAGR for rents, suggesting that equity accumulation is currently favored over rental cost stability. Affordability remains strong relative to national benchmarks, with a price-to-income ratio of 2.21 compared to the national 3.09. However, the rent burden stands at 20.3%, exceeding the national average of 17.2%, which signals that rental costs are becoming a heavier financial strain despite low absolute dollar amounts. A critical, non-obvious insight for potential residents is the disparity between income and state averages. While home values are 21.2% below the Arkansas average, household income is 28.8% below the national mean. This divergence implies that while entry-level prices are low, local purchasing power is constrained, making the 66.1% owner occupancy rate a reflection of limited rental alternatives rather than purely high affordability.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Cross County is 16.9% below the Arkansas state average and 48.4% below the U.S. national average. The median rent of $764/month is 1.3% below the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 12.8, buying a home in Cross County is generally more favorable than renting for those planning to stay long-term. A ratio below 15 typically signals that purchasing is financially advantageous over a multi-year horizon.

Cross County has an owner-occupancy rate of 67.5%, compared to 71.1% in Arkansas and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more renter-heavy market than the U.S. average.
