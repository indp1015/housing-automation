📝 Editor's Insight

Mineral County exhibits characteristics of a renter’s market, evidenced by a price-to-rent ratio of 38.1, nearly double the national average of 18.0. This disparity suggests that purchasing is financially less attractive than renting relative to local income levels. The five-year trend reveals accelerating asset appreciation, with home values growing at a 7.6% CAGR compared to a 5.8% CAGR for rents. This divergence indicates that equity gains are outpacing rental cost increases, potentially narrowing the affordability gap for buyers over time. However, current affordability remains strained. The price-to-income ratio stands at 7.87, significantly exceeding the national benchmark of 3.09, while the rent burden of 20.7% also surpasses the national average of 17.2%. A critical insight for potential residents is that despite home values being 94.3% above the national average, local incomes are 20.5% below it. This income-value mismatch explains the extreme price-to-income ratio and highlights that local purchasing power is insufficient to support current median home prices without substantial external income sources.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Mineral County is 0.7% above the Colorado state average and 89.1% above the U.S. national average. The median rent of $928/month is 28.7% below the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 38.7, Mineral County is a strongly rent-favored market. Home prices are high relative to rents, meaning renting is typically far cheaper than owning in the near term.

Mineral County has an owner-occupancy rate of 69.7%, compared to 71.9% in Colorado and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more renter-heavy market than the U.S. average.
