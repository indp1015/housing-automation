📝 Local Market Analysis

Mineral County, Colorado, functions as a distinctively constrained market where extreme affordability challenges coexist with strong price momentum. The area is neither a buyer’s nor a renter’s market, but rather a high-barrier environment defined by a price-to-income ratio of 7.65, which is more than double the national average of 3.20. This disparity is compounded by a price-to-rent ratio of 38.7, nearly twice the national benchmark of 18.7, indicating that ownership costs are disproportionately high relative to rental alternatives. While the median rent burden sits at 19.8%, slightly above the national 17.2%, the primary barrier to entry remains the substantial gap between local incomes and asset values. From 2017 to 2024, median home values increased from $279,000 to $430,500, representing a cumulative gain of approximately 54%. This long-term appreciation has recently accelerated, with the FHFA House Price Index showing a 17.51% year-over-year increase, significantly outperforming the Colorado state average of -0.61%. This 18.12 percentage point outperformance suggests localized demand drivers that are decoupled from broader state trends. However, this momentum is structurally limited by a tiny housing supply, with only nine new single-family units authorized in 2025 and zero multi-family construction. The local economy supports this scarcity with just 497 total jobs and an average weekly wage of $948, which fails to keep pace with asset inflation. The market’s vitality is further dampened by zero net migration, where 365 incoming filers were exactly offset by 365 departures, indicating a static population base rather than growth-driven expansion. With a median household income of $56,250, which is 17.3% below the national average, the disconnect between earnings and home values creates a severe affordability crisis. The current 7.40% mortgage rate further exacerbates this strain, making debt service on a median-priced home unmanageable for the typical local resident. Consequently, the market exhibits high stability in terms of price retention but low vitality due to limited economic expansion and supply. The composite assessment reflects this tension: while momentum is strong due to rapid price appreciation, the overwhelming weight of poor affordability and stagnant economic fundamentals results in a stressed market classification. Potential participants must recognize that entry barriers are exceptionally high, and the lack of new supply ensures that existing inventory will remain in high demand regardless of broader economic shifts.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Mineral County is 0.7% above the Colorado state average and 89.1% above the U.S. national average. The median rent of $928/month is 28.7% below the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 38.7, Mineral County is a strongly rent-favored market. Home prices are high relative to rents, meaning renting is typically far cheaper than owning in the near term.

Mineral County has an owner-occupancy rate of 69.7%, compared to 71.9% in Colorado and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more renter-heavy market than the U.S. average.
