📝 Local Market Analysis

St. Francois County presents a balanced market leaning toward buyer advantage, characterized by a price-to-income ratio of 2.95, which sits below the national average of 3.20. The rent burden stands at 16.6% of income, also under the national benchmark of 17.2%, indicating that both ownership and rental costs remain relatively manageable for local households. The price-to-rent ratio of 17.8 is slightly lower than the national average of 18.7, further suggesting that buying is competitively priced against renting. These metrics collectively define a market where affordability is a primary strength, despite the broader economic constraints typical of rural Missouri. The county has experienced rapid appreciation, with median home values rising 53.6% from $110,200 in 2017 to $169,300 in 2024. This surge follows a volatile period, including a 2.5% dip in 2020, but has accelerated significantly since 2021. Recent FHFA data confirms this momentum, showing a 3.69% year-over-year increase in 2025, which tracks almost perfectly with the state average of 3.71%. However, a notable divergence exists between asset classes: home values are rising faster than rents, as indicated by the tightening price-to-rent ratio. This suggests that ownership costs are escalating more rapidly than rental alternatives, potentially shifting pressure on lower-income households who rely on the rental sector. Regional context reveals that while St. Francois County home values are 25.6% below the national average, they are only 3.6% below the state average. This proximity to state-level pricing, combined with a median household income of $57,477—15.5% below the national mean—highlights a stability challenge. The local economy supports this trajectory with 17,371 jobs and an average weekly wage of $865, though the high 30-year mortgage rate of 7.40% constrains purchasing power. Housing supply remains modest, with 144 new units authorized in 2025, split between 88 single-family and 56 multi-family units. Positive net migration of 929 filers in 2022 indicates sustained demand, yet the low vitality and stability scores reflect the tension between strong momentum and limited economic breadth. For potential buyers, the current environment offers entry points below national averages, but renters may face increasing competitive pressure as ownership costs climb.
