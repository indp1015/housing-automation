📝 Local Market Analysis

Jefferson County, Nebraska, operates as a distinctively affordable buyer’s market, characterized by a price-to-income ratio of 2.19, significantly below the national average of 3.20. The median home value of $129,500 sits 43.1% below the national mean and 21.3% below the state average, reflecting a low-cost entry point for homeowners. This affordability is reinforced by a rent burden of 13.5%, well under the 17.2% national benchmark, indicating that housing costs consume a relatively small portion of the median household income of $59,044. The market is further defined by a high owner-occupancy rate of 74.9%, suggesting a stable residential base despite the small population of 7,161 residents. Trend analysis reveals a complex trajectory where long-term appreciation has recently stalled. While the median home value increased from $75,500 in 2017 to $129,500 in 2024, the most recent FHFA HPI data indicates a 0.29% year-over-year decline, underperforming the state’s 1.96% gain by 2.25 percentage points. This divergence between the five-year Census trend and current quarterly mortgage data highlights a recent cooling effect, likely exacerbated by the 7.40% national mortgage rate. The housing supply remains extremely constrained, with only eight new single-family units authorized in 2025 and zero multi-family permits, which limits new inventory but also restricts rapid price escalation. Economic fundamentals show a modest labor base of 2,730 jobs with an average weekly wage of $971, supporting local demand but limiting broader economic vitality. The overall market assessment reflects strong affordability metrics offset by weak momentum and stability indicators. The price-to-rent ratio of 16.3 remains favorable compared to the national 18.7, supporting the argument for purchasing over renting. However, the recent negative price momentum and the lack of new construction activity contribute to a challenging stability profile. Net migration remains positive at 145 filers, with inflow incomes averaging $67,515, slightly below the outflow average of $68,379, suggesting a stable but not rapidly growing demographic. For potential buyers, the current environment offers low entry costs and favorable affordability ratios, though the recent price stagnation and high interest rates present a cautious landscape. Renters benefit from low cost burdens, but the scarcity of new multi-family units may limit rental options. The data suggests a market where affordability is the primary driver of value, while economic vitality and price momentum remain secondary concerns due to the small, rural nature of the county.

The median home value in Jefferson County is 21.3% below the Nebraska state average and 43.1% below the U.S. national average. The median rent of $662/month is 16.8% below the state average.
