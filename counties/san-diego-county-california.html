📝 Editor's Insight

San Diego County operates as a distinctively renter-favorable market, evidenced by a price-to-rent ratio of 30.6, significantly exceeding the national average of 18.1. This disparity indicates that purchasing equity is substantially more expensive relative to rental costs than in typical U.S. metros. The five-year trend reveals aggressive asset appreciation, with home values compounding at 8.9% annually versus a 6.8% rent CAGR. This divergence suggests capital gains are outpacing income growth, further straining entry-level buyers. Affordability metrics confirm severe stress; the price-to-income ratio stands at 7.74, more than double the national benchmark of 3.10. Additionally, the rent burden consumes 25.3% of median household income, compared to the 17.2% national average. A critical insight emerges from the 54.5% owner-occupancy rate. Despite high incomes, nearly half of households remain renters, indicating that high earnings do not guarantee home accessibility. Prospective residents must recognize that median income levels are insufficient for median-priced homeownership, forcing a reliance on higher-income segments or extended rental periods to accumulate necessary down payments.

Market Analysis

The median home value in San Diego County is 40.3% above the California state average and 275.4% above the U.S. national average. The median rent of $2,246/month is 32.9% above the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 31.7, San Diego County is a strongly rent-favored market. Home prices are high relative to rents, meaning renting is typically far cheaper than owning in the near term.

San Diego County has an owner-occupancy rate of 54.6%, compared to 63.4% in California and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more renter-heavy market than the U.S. average.
