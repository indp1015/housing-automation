📝 Local Market Analysis

San Diego County operates as a distinctly seller’s market, characterized by extreme price decoupling from local income levels. The median home value of $854,700 stands at 8.04 times the median household income of $106,268, a ratio more than double the national average of 3.20. This disparity is further highlighted by a price-to-rent ratio of 31.7, significantly exceeding the national benchmark of 18.7, indicating that purchasing equity is currently less cost-effective than renting relative to national norms. The market classification is reinforced by the owner occupancy rate of 54.6%, which is lower than typical suburban averages, suggesting a persistent barrier to entry for potential homeowners. The multi-year trend reveals a period of aggressive appreciation followed by recent stabilization. Home values surged 15.6% year-over-year in 2022, moderating to 8.0% in 2024, while the FHFA HPI shows a recent 0.66% annual increase, outperforming the California state average of -0.29%. This divergence suggests that while statewide prices are flat or declining, San Diego maintains upward momentum, likely driven by strong local fundamentals. The county’s home values are 40.3% above the state average and 275.4% above the national mean, reflecting a premium market status. However, this strength is tempered by a 7.40% national mortgage rate, which severely limits purchasing power despite the area’s robust economy. Economic vitality supports the market’s resilience, with 1,288,378 total jobs and an average weekly wage of $1,611. Housing supply remains constrained, with only 11,707 new units authorized in 2025, a figure insufficient to offset the net positive migration of 5,637 high-income filers. The affordability crisis is the primary driver of the market’s stress, with a rent burden of 25.4% compared to the national 17.2%. While the momentum and vitality metrics remain moderate due to steady employment and migration inflows, the near-zero affordability score dominates the overall assessment. Potential buyers face a high barrier to entry, whereas renters, despite elevated costs, benefit from a market where price growth has recently outpaced rent increases, potentially stabilizing long-term holding costs relative to asset appreciation.

Market Analysis

The median home value in San Diego County is 40.3% above the California state average and 275.4% above the U.S. national average. The median rent of $2,246/month is 32.9% above the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 31.7, San Diego County is a strongly rent-favored market. Home prices are high relative to rents, meaning renting is typically far cheaper than owning in the near term.

San Diego County has an owner-occupancy rate of 54.6%, compared to 63.4% in California and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more renter-heavy market than the U.S. average.
