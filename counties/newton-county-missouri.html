📝 Local Market Analysis

Newton County, Missouri, presents a balanced market leaning slightly toward buyers, characterized by strong price appreciation that has outpaced local income growth. The county’s median home value of $190,700 is 8.5% above the state average but remains 16.3% below the national mean, indicating a regional premium within Missouri while retaining broader national affordability. With a price-to-income ratio of 2.88, the county is more affordable than the national average of 3.20, and the rent burden of 14.8% is significantly lower than the national 17.2%, suggesting that housing costs remain manageable relative to the median household income of $66,301. However, the price-to-rent ratio of 19.5 exceeds the national average of 18.7, signaling that buying is becoming relatively more attractive than renting due to the divergence in growth rates. The market’s trajectory reveals a period of rapid acceleration followed by stabilization. Home values surged 18.3% in 2022, moderating to 8.8% in 2024, while the FHFA House Price Index shows a recent year-over-year increase of 3.80%. This recent 3.80% gain slightly outpaces the state’s 3.71% growth, indicating Newton County is maintaining momentum relative to its peers. Notably, home values are rising faster than rents, a pattern that typically favors homeownership over time. The local economy supports this stability with 20,367 jobs and an average weekly wage of $1,081. Despite positive net migration of 1,491 filers, new housing supply remains constrained, with only 56 units authorized in 2025, comprising 36 single-family and 20 multi-family homes. This limited supply, combined with a 7.40% national mortgage rate, creates a challenging environment for entry-level buyers despite the county’s relative affordability. Affordability remains the primary strength, scoring 63.2 on a 0-100 scale, driven by the favorable price-to-income ratio and low rent burden. Stability, however, lags at 54.0, reflecting the volatility of the 2022 price spike and the current high-interest-rate environment. Momentum scores 62.5, supported by consistent year-over-year gains and positive migration trends. Vitality is the weakest axis at 53.1, constrained by the limited new construction pipeline and the small total employment base. For prospective buyers, the data suggests a market where entry is still attainable compared to national standards, but the rapid recent appreciation and tight supply require careful consideration of long-term holding costs. Renters may find the low rent burden appealing, but the rising price-to-rent ratio implies that the financial advantage of renting over buying is narrowing. The convergence of high mortgage rates and limited new supply suggests that inventory levels will remain a critical factor in transaction dynamics.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Newton County is 8.5% above the Missouri state average and 16.3% below the U.S. national average. The median rent of $817/month is 4.5% above the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 19.5, the buy-vs-rent decision in Newton County is balanced. Whether buying or renting makes more sense depends on your time horizon, down payment capacity, and local market conditions.

Newton County has an owner-occupancy rate of 76.6%, compared to 73.7% in Missouri and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more homeowner-heavy market than the U.S. average.
