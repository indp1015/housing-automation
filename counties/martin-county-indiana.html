📝 Local Market Analysis

Martin County, Indiana, functions as a distinctly affordable market, characterized by a price-to-income ratio of 2.32, significantly below the national average of 3.20. The rent burden stands at 12.2% of income, well under the national benchmark of 17.2%, indicating strong residential cost efficiency. With a median home value of $159,400 and a median household income of $68,594, the county offers substantial value relative to earnings. This affordability is further underscored by a price-to-rent ratio of 19.1, which is only marginally above the national average of 18.7, suggesting that ownership remains a financially competitive alternative to renting. The owner-occupancy rate of 78.5% reflects a stable, rooted population, while the recent net migration of +180 filers indicates modest but consistent demographic growth, supported by an average inflow income of $66,183. The market exhibits a clear divergence between long-term appreciation and recent momentum. While home values have risen from $97,900 in 2017 to $159,400 in 2024, representing a cumulative increase of approximately 63%, the most recent FHFA HPI data shows a year-over-year decline of 1.60%. This recent softness contrasts sharply with the state’s year-over-year growth of 3.45%, placing the county 5.05 percentage points below the state average in recent performance. Historically, growth peaked at 13.5% in 2018 before moderating to 6.0% in 2024, suggesting a maturing market cycle. The local economic foundation is modest, with 2,976 total jobs and an average weekly wage of $1,184, which aligns with the county’s below-average home values but above-average income levels relative to the nation. Housing supply dynamics further shape the current landscape, with 64 new units authorized in 2025, predominantly multi-family (50 units) compared to single-family (14 units). This supply mix may address rental demand rather than expanding the ownership base. The high affordability metrics drive the overall market assessment, while the recent negative price momentum tempers the vitality score. The 7.32% national mortgage rate adds external pressure, yet the county’s low entry price point mitigates this impact. For potential residents, the data indicates a stable, low-cost environment where housing expenses consume a smaller portion of income than in most U.S. counties. The disparity between the strong long-term appreciation trend and the recent quarterly decline suggests a period of adjustment rather than structural decline, particularly given the county’s income levels are 0.9% above the national average. This combination of high affordability and moderate recent stability positions Martin County as a resilient, value-oriented market within the broader Indiana housing landscape.
