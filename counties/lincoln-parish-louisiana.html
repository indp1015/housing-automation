📝 Local Market Analysis

Lincoln Parish, Louisiana, functions as a distinctively stressed market where affordability metrics severely lag behind national benchmarks, despite recent price acceleration. The price-to-income ratio stands at 5.36, significantly higher than the national average of 3.20, while the rent burden consumes 25.3% of median household income compared to the national 17.2%. These figures indicate a market where housing costs outpace local earning power, creating a challenging environment for both buyers and renters. Although the median home value of $210,100 is 7.7% below the national average, it sits 25.5% above the state average, suggesting that local housing costs are inflated relative to the broader Louisiana economy. This divergence highlights a specific regional premium that is not supported by the county’s median household income of $39,172, which is 42.4% below the national mean. The market exhibits strong momentum, with home values rising 7.5% year-over-year in 2024 and 3.27% in recent FHFA data, outperforming the state’s 1.59% growth. This upward trajectory follows a peak of 15.5% growth in 2022, indicating a sustained, albeit moderating, appreciation trend. Notably, home values are rising faster than rents, a pattern that may signal shifting dynamics in the rental sector or an initial phase of owner-occupancy conversion. The local economic context supports this activity through positive net migration of 722 filers and a housing supply constrained by only 193 new units authorized in 2025, predominantly single-family. However, the high 30-year fixed mortgage rate of 7.40% exacerbates the affordability crisis, limiting purchasing power for the majority of residents given the low average weekly wage of $892. The overall market stress is driven primarily by poor affordability and stability scores, which are undermined by the significant gap between income and housing costs. While momentum remains healthy due to consistent price appreciation and outperformance relative to the state, vitality is constrained by limited employment growth and high entry barriers. For potential participants, the data suggests that while the asset value is appreciating, the immediate cost of entry remains prohibitive for the median household. The disparity between the county’s high relative home values and its low income base creates a fragile equilibrium, where continued price growth relies heavily on external migration and limited supply rather than robust local economic expansion. This structural imbalance necessitates careful consideration for any new entrants, as the current pricing structure does not align with typical local earning potential.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Lincoln Parish is 25.5% above the Louisiana state average and 7.7% below the U.S. national average. The median rent of $825/month is 9.7% below the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 21.2, renting is generally more cost-effective than buying in the short to medium term. The market favors buyers only for long-term holds.

Lincoln Parish has an owner-occupancy rate of 53.8%, compared to 72.2% in Louisiana and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more renter-heavy market than the U.S. average.
