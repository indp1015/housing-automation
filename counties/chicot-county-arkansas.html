📝 Editor's Insight

Chicot County functions as a distinct buyer’s market, evidenced by a price-to-rent ratio of 11.4, significantly below the national average of 18.0. This metric indicates that purchasing property is substantially more cost-effective than long-term renting. Over the past five years, median home values increased at a compound annual growth rate of 6.8%, rising from $69,700 to $90,500, while rents grew only 2.4%. This divergence suggests that equity accumulation through ownership is currently outpacing rental cost escalation. Affordability remains strong relative to national standards; the price-to-income ratio stands at 2.28, well below the 3.09 national benchmark. Although the rent burden is slightly elevated at 20.0% compared to the 17.2% national average, the low entry price point mitigates this pressure for purchasers. A critical insight for potential residents is that the 67.4% owner-occupancy rate, combined with the county’s 31.4% discount to state average home values, creates a stable, low-volatility environment. Unlike high-growth metros, Chicot County offers exceptional value per dollar spent, making it an attractive option for those prioritizing asset stability over rapid capital appreciation.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Chicot County is 37.0% below the Arkansas state average and 60.9% below the U.S. national average. The median rent of $574/month is 25.8% below the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 12.9, buying a home in Chicot County is generally more favorable than renting for those planning to stay long-term. A ratio below 15 typically signals that purchasing is financially advantageous over a multi-year horizon.

Chicot County has an owner-occupancy rate of 69.2%, compared to 71.1% in Arkansas and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more renter-heavy market than the U.S. average.
