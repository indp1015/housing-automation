📝 Editor's Insight

San Miguel County functions as a distinct renter's market, evidenced by a price-to-rent ratio of 43.6, nearly double the national average of 18.0. This extreme disparity indicates that renting is significantly more cost-effective than purchasing for most residents. Over the past five years, home values appreciated at a 6.3% CAGR, rising from $479,300 to $613,100, while rents grew merely 0.7%. This divergence suggests that the rental sector remains relatively stable and insulated from the rapid appreciation seen in the ownership segment. Affordability for buyers is severely constrained, with a price-to-income ratio of 7.65, substantially higher than the national benchmark of 3.09. Conversely, the rent burden stands at 17.6%, closely aligning with the national average of 17.2%. A critical insight emerges from the combination of high home values and a low owner-occupancy rate of 65.2%. This implies that a significant portion of the housing stock is likely held by investors or second-home owners, potentially limiting the availability of affordable single-family rentals and concentrating rental supply in specific segments of the market.

Market Analysis

The median home value in San Miguel County is 54.0% above the Colorado state average and 188.5% above the U.S. national average. The median rent of $1,173/month is 6.4% below the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 43.6, San Miguel County is a strongly rent-favored market. Home prices are high relative to rents, meaning renting is typically far cheaper than owning in the near term.

San Miguel County has an owner-occupancy rate of 65.2%, compared to 72.1% in Colorado and 73.0% nationally. This indicates a more renter-heavy market than the U.S. average.
