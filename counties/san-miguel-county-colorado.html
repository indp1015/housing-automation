📝 Local Market Analysis

San Miguel County exhibits characteristics of a highly stressed buyer’s market, driven by extreme valuation disconnects from local income levels. The median home value of $720,700 stands at a price-to-income ratio of 9.12, nearly triple the national average of 3.20, rendering homeownership inaccessible for the typical household earning $79,024. This affordability crisis is compounded by a price-to-rent ratio of 52.3, significantly exceeding the national benchmark of 18.7, which indicates that purchasing a home is economically less favorable than renting when compared to broader market standards. Consequently, the market is heavily skewed toward renters, with an owner-occupancy rate of only 63.0% and a median gross rent of $1,149 consuming 17.4% of median income, a figure barely above the national average of 17.2%. Recent trends reveal a volatile but aggressive appreciation cycle. After a dip to $448,900 in 2020, home values surged 17.1% in 2022 and another 17.6% in 2024 to reach current levels. This rapid momentum contrasts sharply with the county’s limited economic base, which supports only 5,217 jobs with an average weekly wage of $1,133. The housing supply remains constrained, with just 51 new single-family units authorized in 2025 and zero multi-family permits, failing to meet the demand signaled by a net inflow of 471 high-income filers between 2021 and 2022. These incoming residents, averaging incomes of $251,001, are driving prices far above the local median household income, creating a bifurcated market where wealthier migrants inflate asset values while local residents face stagnating purchasing power. The county’s home values are 216.5% above the national average and 68.6% above the Colorado state average, yet local incomes lag behind the national mean by only 16.2%. This disparity highlights a structural imbalance where asset values have decoupled from local economic fundamentals. With a 30-year fixed mortgage rate at 7.32%, the cost of entry is prohibitive for the median earner. The market’s vitality is high due to strong migration and price momentum, but stability and affordability are critically low. Potential buyers should recognize that current valuations are sustained by external wealth rather than local employment growth, while renters may find relative stability in rental costs that have not kept pace with the 17.6% annual home price appreciation. The divergence between soaring asset prices and static local wages suggests a market driven by speculative or investment capital rather than organic residential demand.

Market Analysis

The median home value in San Miguel County is 68.6% above the Colorado state average and 216.5% above the U.S. national average. The median rent of $1,149/month is 11.8% below the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 52.3, San Miguel County is a strongly rent-favored market. Home prices are high relative to rents, meaning renting is typically far cheaper than owning in the near term.

San Miguel County has an owner-occupancy rate of 63.0%, compared to 71.9% in Colorado and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more renter-heavy market than the U.S. average.
