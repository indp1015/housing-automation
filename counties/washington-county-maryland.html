📝 Local Market Analysis

Washington County, Maryland, operates as a moderately stressed market where strong momentum is offset by significant affordability constraints. The price-to-income ratio of 3.81 exceeds the national average of 3.20, while the price-to-rent ratio of 21.5 surpasses the national benchmark of 18.7, indicating that homeownership costs are disproportionately high relative to local earning power. Although the median household income of $77,747 is 14.3% above the national average, the median home value of $296,100 is 30.0% higher than the national median, creating a structural barrier for entry-level buyers. This disparity suggests that while the local economy supports higher wages, housing costs have outpaced income growth, resulting in a market that is less affordable than the broader U.S. average despite being 20.0% below the state median. Recent data reveals a distinct divergence between price acceleration and rental stability. Home values surged 14.2% year-over-year in 2022 and continued to rise 7.3% in 2024, significantly outpacing the modest rent burden of 17.7%, which aligns closely with the national average of 17.2%. This pattern indicates that capital appreciation is being driven by factors other than rental yield, potentially fueled by net migration of 3,081 filers between 2021 and 2022. The local labor market supports this demand, with 57,200 jobs and an average weekly wage of $1,112, yet the limited supply of 354 new units authorized in 2025 fails to fully absorb the influx of residents. Consequently, the market exhibits high vitality and momentum, as evidenced by the 69.7 momentum component, but suffers from low stability and affordability scores due to the widening gap between asset values and resident purchasing power. Current momentum remains positive but is moderating. The FHFA House Price Index shows a 3.28% year-over-year increase in 2025, outperforming the state’s 0.95% growth by 2.33 percentage points. However, with 30-year fixed mortgage rates holding at 7.40%, monthly carrying costs remain elevated, further straining the affordability metric. The composite assessment reflects a market with robust economic fundamentals and strong price appreciation, yet it is classified as stressed because the rapid rise in home values has outstripped the capacity of the median household to absorb these costs. For potential participants, the data suggests that while the market offers strong equity growth potential, the entry price point is currently misaligned with local income levels, requiring careful financial planning to navigate the high price-to-income ratio.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Washington County is 20.0% below the Maryland state average and 30.0% above the U.S. national average. The median rent of $1,147/month is 21.4% below the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 21.5, renting is generally more cost-effective than buying in the short to medium term. The market favors buyers only for long-term holds.

Washington County has an owner-occupancy rate of 66.8%, compared to 72.7% in Maryland and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more renter-heavy market than the U.S. average.
