📝 Local Market Analysis

Hart County, Kentucky, functions as a balanced market with distinct affordability advantages, evidenced by a price-to-income ratio of 2.79 compared to the national average of 3.20. The median home value of $146,000 sits 35.9% below the national mean, while the median household income of $52,285 is 23.1% lower than the national figure. This structural gap supports a rent burden of 15.9%, slightly below the national 17.2% benchmark, and a price-to-rent ratio of 17.6, indicating that ownership remains a financially viable alternative to renting for local residents. The market’s relative position is further defined by a 9.2% discount to the state average home value, suggesting that Hart County offers entry-level pricing within the broader Kentucky landscape. Recent price appreciation has been volatile, with median values surging 17.4% in 2022 and another 17.2% in 2024, following a modest 5.8% increase in 2021. This rapid escalation contrasts with the more subdued 3.83% year-over-year change indicated by the 2025 FHFA HPI, which tracks mortgage-based transactions and lags slightly behind state growth by 0.43 percentage points. The divergence between the sharp Census-based value increases and the more moderate HPI suggests a potential data lag or a shift in transaction mix, particularly given the recent supply of 272 multi-family units and zero single-family permits in 2025. This supply constraint on single-family housing may be driving up values for existing homes, while the influx of 297 net new filers with average incomes near $51,700 provides consistent demand pressure. Economic fundamentals present a mixed picture, with total employment at 4,555 jobs and an average weekly wage of $1,058. The local economy’s limited scale, reflected in only 378 establishments, constrains long-term growth potential, contributing to a lower stability assessment. However, momentum remains positive, driven by sustained price appreciation and population inflows. The affordability metrics remain strong relative to national standards, but the recent double-digit price jumps have compressed the margin of safety for buyers. For potential purchasers, the current 7.40% mortgage rate significantly increases monthly carrying costs, making the low median home value a critical offset. Renters benefit from a stable, below-average rent burden, but the shift toward multi-family construction may influence future rental availability. The market’s vitality is supported by demographic inflows, yet the lack of single-family supply and modest wage growth limit the ceiling for sustained price expansion.
