📝 Editor's Insight

Randolph County functions as a distinct buyer’s market, evidenced by a price-to-rent ratio of 14.7, significantly below the national average of 18.0. This metric indicates that purchasing equity is substantially more cost-effective than long-term renting. Over the past five years, home values appreciated at a compound annual growth rate of 7.9%, rising from $87,200 to $118,100, while rents increased merely 2.0%. This divergence suggests that local housing supply adjustments have favored ownership costs over rental premiums. Affordability remains robust, with a price-to-income ratio of 2.24, well under the national benchmark of 3.09. Additionally, the rent burden stands at 15.2% of median income, lower than the national 17.2% average. A critical, non-obvious insight is that despite home values rising nearly 35% since 2019, the county’s median income has not kept pace, creating a widening gap between asset value and local earning power. This dynamic implies that while entry barriers remain low relative to national standards, the affordability margin for local residents is gradually compressing as asset prices outstrip wage growth.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Randolph County is 10.5% below the Arkansas state average and 44.4% below the U.S. national average. The median rent of $671/month is 11.6% below the state average.
