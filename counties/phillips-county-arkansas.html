📝 Editor's Insight

Phillips County exhibits a distinct buyer’s market, evidenced by a price-to-rent ratio of 8.4, significantly below the national average of 18.0. This disparity suggests purchasing is comparatively more economical than long-term renting. Over the last five years, median gross rent surged at a 6.6% CAGR, rising from $586 to $756, while home values grew at a modest 1.9% CAGR. This divergence indicates rental costs are escalating faster than property equity accumulation. Affordability metrics reveal a nuanced picture. The price-to-income ratio stands at 1.96, well below the national benchmark of 3.09, indicating high relative purchasing power for homeowners. However, the rent burden reaches 23.3% of median household income, exceeding the national average of 17.2%. This implies renters face disproportionate financial strain compared to owners. A critical insight emerges from the 54.0% owner occupancy rate combined with the widening rent-value gap. Potential residents should recognize that while entry costs are low, the rapid rent inflation makes long-term rental stability financially precarious. Conversely, buyers benefit from low entry barriers, though the low population of 15,910 limits resale liquidity. The market favors those with capital to purchase rather than those relying on rental income flexibility.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Phillips County is 44.7% below the Arkansas state average and 65.7% below the U.S. national average. The median rent of $737/month is 4.8% below the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 8.8, buying a home in Phillips County is generally more favorable than renting for those planning to stay long-term. A ratio below 15 typically signals that purchasing is financially advantageous over a multi-year horizon.

Phillips County has an owner-occupancy rate of 51.2%, compared to 71.1% in Arkansas and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more renter-heavy market than the U.S. average.
