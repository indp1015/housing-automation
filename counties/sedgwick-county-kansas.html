📝 Local Market Analysis

Sedgwick County operates as a balanced market with distinct affordability advantages, characterized by a price-to-income ratio of 2.93, which sits below the national average of 3.20. The price-to-rent ratio of 17.0 further supports this classification, indicating that purchasing is relatively more attractive than renting compared to the national benchmark of 18.7. While the median home value of $203,300 is 10.7% below the national average, it commands a 46.4% premium over the Kansas state average, reflecting the county’s status as the region’s economic anchor. This premium is sustained by robust local fundamentals, including 233,272 total jobs and a median household income of $69,365, which is 2.0% above the national mean. The rent burden stands at 17.3% of income, virtually identical to the national average of 17.2%, suggesting no significant distortion in the rental sector relative to broader economic conditions. Recent momentum indicates a cooling but persistent upward trajectory. After peaking at 14.4% year-over-year growth in 2022, appreciation has moderated to 6.6% in 2024 and 3.25% in the latest FHFA HPI data, tracking the state’s 3.21% increase. This deceleration from the 2022 surge suggests the market is normalizing rather than correcting. A notable pattern emerges in the divergence between asset classes; home values are rising faster than rents, driven by a net migration inflow of 6,854 filers and strong employment stability. Housing supply remains robust, with 2,757 new units authorized in 2025, including 1,501 multi-family units, which helps mitigate supply constraints. However, the 7.40% national mortgage rate significantly impacts purchasing power, offsetting some of the county’s intrinsic affordability benefits for cash-strapped buyers. The composite assessment of this market reflects high vitality and momentum, driven by consistent population growth and wage stability, but is tempered by stability concerns due to the sharp post-2022 price correction. The affordability axis remains strong, with metrics consistently outperforming national benchmarks, while the stability component lags due to the volatility observed in the 2022-2023 period. For potential stakeholders, the data suggests that while entry prices are lower than national norms, the rapid appreciation of the past three years has compressed the gap between local and national valuations. The combination of positive net migration and a healthy job market supports continued demand, though the high interest rate environment continues to act as a headwind for new mortgage originations, keeping the market in a state of equilibrium rather than speculative excess.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Sedgwick County is 46.4% above the Kansas state average and 10.7% below the U.S. national average. The median rent of $998/month is 24.4% above the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 17.0, the buy-vs-rent decision in Sedgwick County is balanced. Whether buying or renting makes more sense depends on your time horizon, down payment capacity, and local market conditions.
