📝 Local Market Analysis

Crawford County, Kansas, operates as a distinctively affordable buyer’s market, characterized by a median home value of $132,600 that sits 41.8% below the national average. The price-to-income ratio of 2.51 is significantly lower than the national benchmark of 3.20, indicating strong relative affordability despite a median household income of $52,844. This affordability is reinforced by a price-to-rent ratio of 13.0, well below the national average of 18.7, suggesting that ownership remains a more financially accessible option than long-term renting for many residents. The market’s stability is further supported by a 60.8% owner-occupancy rate, which exceeds typical rural averages, reflecting a community rooted in long-term residential investment rather than transient rental demand. Recent price trends reveal a period of accelerated appreciation that has since begun to moderate. After a modest 1.9% increase in 2021, home values surged 15.6% in 2022 and continued to rise 8.3% in 2024. However, the most recent FHFA HPI data indicates a sharp deceleration, with only a 1.30% year-over-year increase in 2025, underperforming the Kansas state average of 3.21% by 1.91 percentage points. This divergence suggests that the initial post-pandemic price spikes have lost momentum, aligning the county’s growth rate more closely with a cooling national trend. Despite this slowdown, the underlying economic fundamentals remain consistent, with total employment at 12,709 jobs and an average weekly wage of $930, providing a stable base for housing demand. The limited new housing supply, with only 61 units authorized in 2025, primarily consisting of single-family homes, constrains new entry-level options but supports existing home values. Migration data shows a net inflow of 876 filers, with incoming residents earning slightly less than those leaving, indicating a demographic shift toward lower-income households that may pressure affordability metrics over time. The rent burden stands at 19.3% of income, slightly above the national average of 17.2%, suggesting that while buying is favorable, renting is becoming relatively more expensive. For potential buyers, the current environment offers entry points well below national norms, though the slowing price growth provides a more predictable purchasing landscape. The market’s vitality is driven by this combination of low entry costs and steady, albeit slowing, appreciation, making it a resilient, if modest, housing market within the broader Kansas context.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Crawford County is 4.5% below the Kansas state average and 41.8% below the U.S. national average. The median rent of $849/month is 5.9% above the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 13.0, buying a home in Crawford County is generally more favorable than renting for those planning to stay long-term. A ratio below 15 typically signals that purchasing is financially advantageous over a multi-year horizon.

Crawford County has an owner-occupancy rate of 60.8%, compared to 74.2% in Kansas and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more renter-heavy market than the U.S. average.
