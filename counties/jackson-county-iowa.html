📝 Local Market Analysis

Jackson County, Iowa, functions as a balanced market with distinct affordability advantages, characterized by a price-to-income ratio of 2.36, significantly below the national average of 3.20. The rent burden stands at 13.1% of income, also outperforming the national benchmark of 17.2%, while the price-to-rent ratio of 18.1 aligns closely with the national mean of 18.7. These metrics indicate that both purchasing and renting remain accessible relative to local earnings, which average $74,186 per household. The high owner-occupancy rate of 80.7% further underscores a stable residential base, though the limited new construction pipeline of only 32 authorized units in 2025 suggests constrained supply growth that could influence future inventory levels. Recent price momentum has been robust, with home values rising 11.68% year-over-year according to the FHFA HPI, substantially outpacing the state average of 2.25%. This surge follows a peak annual increase of 17.0% in 2022, which has since moderated to 2.9% in the 2024 Census estimates, indicating a transition from rapid appreciation to a more normalized growth phase. A notable divergence exists between the recent HPI acceleration and the slower Census-reported trend, suggesting that current transaction data reflects fresher price adjustments than the five-year averages. Meanwhile, net migration of +351 filers between 2021 and 2022, driven by inflows of 8,291 households, provides a demographic tailwind that supports demand despite a small total employment base of 4,665 jobs. The local economy presents a mixed vitality picture, with an average weekly wage of $909 and 606 establishments supporting the housing demand. Although home values are 23.1% below the national average, they sit 2.3% above the state mean, positioning the county as a premium market within Iowa. The strong affordability metrics and consistent price appreciation drive the favorable assessment of the market’s stability and momentum. However, the low vitality score reflects the limited economic scale and modest job creation. For potential buyers, the current 7.40% mortgage rate increases carrying costs, yet the low price-to-income ratio mitigates this pressure. Renters benefit from low cost burdens, while buyers face a market where prices are rising faster than rents, potentially shifting long-term value dynamics toward ownership. The combination of strong affordability and positive momentum creates a resilient environment, though the small population of 19,390 limits the depth of the buyer pool.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Jackson County is 2.3% above the Iowa state average and 23.1% below the U.S. national average. The median rent of $807/month is 3.0% below the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 18.1, the buy-vs-rent decision in Jackson County is balanced. Whether buying or renting makes more sense depends on your time horizon, down payment capacity, and local market conditions.

Jackson County has an owner-occupancy rate of 80.7%, compared to 75.8% in Iowa and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more homeowner-heavy market than the U.S. average.
