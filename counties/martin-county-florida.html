📝 Local Market Analysis

Martin County, Florida, operates as a distinctly seller’s market, characterized by significant affordability constraints despite robust economic vitality. The median home value of $432,200 stands 89.8% above the national average and 52.0% above the state average, while the median household income of $82,943 exceeds the national mean by only 22.0%. This disparity results in a price-to-income ratio of 5.21, nearly double the national benchmark of 3.20, and a price-to-rent ratio of 23.0, indicating that purchasing is significantly more expensive relative to rental costs than in the broader U.S. market. Consequently, the affordability metric scores a mere 7.0 out of 100, reflecting severe entry barriers for local buyers. The market’s trajectory reveals a period of intense appreciation followed by recent stabilization. Home values surged from $233,000 in 2017 to $432,200 in 2024, with a peak year-over-year increase of 17.1% in 2022. However, recent FHFA data indicates a shift, showing a 1.68% year-over-year decline in 2025, which underperforms the state’s 0.91% growth by 2.59 percentage points. This recent price correction contrasts with the long-term upward trend, suggesting a cooling phase rather than a collapse. Notably, home values are rising faster than rents, with a rent burden of 22.7% compared to the national average of 17.2%, signaling that rental costs have not kept pace with the surge in property values. Economic fundamentals remain strong, driving a vitality score of 83.5 and a stability score of 77.7. The local economy supports 72,055 jobs with an average weekly wage of $1,184, while net migration of 5,744 filers between 2021 and 2022, with an average inflow income of $195,959, demonstrates sustained demand from high-earning residents. Housing supply remains tight, with only 845 new units authorized in 2025, predominantly single-family homes. With 30-year mortgage rates at 7.40%, financing costs further exacerbate affordability challenges. The overall market assessment is driven by the tension between high economic vitality and low affordability, resulting in a composite rating of 45.5. Potential buyers face a high cost of entry, while renters may find relative relief as price growth moderates, though rent burdens remain above national norms. The market’s stability is underpinned by strong employment and migration, even as price momentum slows.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Martin County is 52.0% above the Florida state average and 89.8% above the U.S. national average. The median rent of $1,569/month is 20.4% above the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 23.0, renting is generally more cost-effective than buying in the short to medium term. The market favors buyers only for long-term holds.

Martin County has an owner-occupancy rate of 79.6%, compared to 74.6% in Florida and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more homeowner-heavy market than the U.S. average.
