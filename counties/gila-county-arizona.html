📝 Editor's Insight

Gila County presents a balanced market leaning toward buyers, evidenced by a 77.2% owner occupancy rate and a price-to-rent ratio of 20.6, which slightly exceeds the national average of 18.0. The five-year trend reveals aggressive appreciation, with home values compounding at 10.5% annually compared to a 5.2% rent increase. This divergence indicates that equity accumulation is outpacing rental cost inflation, favoring long-term ownership over leasing. However, affordability remains strained relative to income levels. The price-to-income ratio stands at 4.18, significantly higher than the national benchmark of 3.09, while the rent burden consumes 20.3% of household income versus the 17.2% national average. A critical insight emerges from the income disparity: despite home values being 1.5% above the state average, median household income is 10.4% below the national mean. This suggests that local purchasing power lags behind asset valuation, creating a structural gap where rising property costs are not matched by corresponding wage growth, making entry into the market increasingly difficult for average earners.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Gila County is 1.4% above the Arizona state average and 18.3% above the U.S. national average. The median rent of $1,030/month is 4.0% below the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 21.8, renting is generally more cost-effective than buying in the short to medium term. The market favors buyers only for long-term holds.

Gila County has an owner-occupancy rate of 77.5%, compared to 70.7% in Arizona and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more homeowner-heavy market than the U.S. average.
