📝 Local Market Notes

Clay County, Alabama, presents a complex market dynamic characterized by significant affordability advantages relative to national benchmarks, yet exhibits divergent signals regarding investment value. The price-to-income ratio stands at 2.84, well below the national average of 3.20, while the rent burden is 13.4% of income compared to the national 17.2%. These metrics suggest a buyer’s market in terms of entry-level accessibility, as median home values of $156,700 remain 31.2% below the national average. However, the price-to-rent ratio of 21.1 exceeds the national norm of 18.7, indicating that purchasing is currently less favorable than renting from a pure yield perspective. This divergence suggests that while absolute prices are low, the relative cost of ownership versus rental income is less attractive to investors than in the broader market. The multi-year trend reveals a period of rapid appreciation that has recently moderated but remains robust. Home values increased from $89,300 in 2017 to $156,700 in 2024, with a peak year-over-year growth of 17.7% in 2022. Recent FHFA data indicates a 26.55% year-over-year increase in 2025, significantly outperforming the Alabama state average of 2.79%. This sharp acceleration contrasts with the slower 8.3% growth recorded in the 2024 Census estimates, highlighting a potential lag in data reporting or a recent surge in transaction prices. The local economy supports this momentum through net positive migration of 200 filers and a stable employment base of 3,345 jobs, although the average weekly wage of $915 remains modest. Housing supply is extremely constrained, with only eight new single-family units authorized in 2025 and zero multi-family permits, creating a scarcity that likely underpins price increases despite high national mortgage rates of 7.40%. For potential participants, the market offers high affordability relative to income but carries elevated risk due to recent price volatility. The stark contrast between the 26.55% HPI growth and the 13.4% rent burden suggests that price appreciation has outpaced rental income growth, potentially squeezing rental yields. The low housing supply, combined with steady in-migration, indicates that inventory constraints will likely continue to support price levels. Prospective buyers should note that while the county is 1.9% below the state median home value, the rapid recent appreciation may limit further short-term gains compared to the state average. Conversely, renters benefit from a low rent burden, making the rental sector a more stable financial option given the current price-to-rent ratio. The data indicates a market where affordability is high in absolute terms but investment returns are compressed by rising acquisition costs relative to rental income.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Clay County is 1.9% below the Alabama state average and 31.2% below the U.S. national average. The median rent of $619/month is 24.5% below the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 21.1, renting is generally more cost-effective than buying in the short to medium term. The market favors buyers only for long-term holds.

Clay County has an owner-occupancy rate of 79.4%, compared to 73.2% in Alabama and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more homeowner-heavy market than the U.S. average.
