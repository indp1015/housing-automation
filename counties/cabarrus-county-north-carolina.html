📝 Local Market Analysis

Cabarrus County, North Carolina, currently functions as a balanced-to-seller’s market, characterized by strong demand outpacing new supply despite moderating price growth. The price-to-rent ratio of 20.9 exceeds the national average of 18.7, indicating that purchasing remains more expensive relative to renting than in most U.S. counties. However, the owner-occupancy rate of 71.2% suggests a stable housing base. Recent FHFA data shows a 2.09% year-over-year price increase, significantly outperforming the North Carolina state average of 0.38%. This relative strength highlights a market where local demand drivers are robust enough to sustain appreciation even as national mortgage rates sit at 7.40%, which typically suppresses buyer activity. Affordability remains the primary constraint for the county. The price-to-income ratio stands at 3.99, well above the national benchmark of 3.20, while the median household income of $89,005 lags behind the 30.9% premium in home values compared to national averages. Renters face a burden of 19.1% of income, also exceeding the national 17.2% average. These metrics explain the low affordability component of the overall market assessment. Conversely, the market exhibits high vitality, driven by a net migration gain of 5,870 filers and a population of 236,133. Local economic fundamentals support this demand, with 75,226 jobs and an average weekly wage of $1,056 providing a solid income base for prospective buyers. A notable pattern is the divergence between historical price acceleration and current stabilization. Home values surged 17.6% in 2022 before moderating to 11.4% in 2024, suggesting the market is digesting previous gains. Housing supply, with 1,991 new units authorized in 2025, is attempting to catch up with demand, though single-family permits (1,358) dominate over multi-family (633). The composite assessment reflects this tension: high vitality and decent stability are offset by poor affordability and slowing momentum. For potential buyers, the entry point is less favorable than the national average due to the elevated price-to-income ratio. Renters may find the current price-to-rent spread less attractive for long-term equity building compared to national norms, yet the county’s outperformance relative to the state suggests sustained local demand. The market is not in crisis, but it is priced for growth that may not materialize at the pace of the 2021-2022 period.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Cabarrus County is 50.8% above the North Carolina state average and 55.8% above the U.S. national average. The median rent of $1,414/month is 44.9% above the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 20.9, renting is generally more cost-effective than buying in the short to medium term. The market favors buyers only for long-term holds.

Cabarrus County has an owner-occupancy rate of 71.2%, compared to 71.8% in North Carolina and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more renter-heavy market than the U.S. average.
