📝 Local Market Analysis

Merrimack County, New Hampshire, operates as a distinctively renter-favorable market, characterized by a price-to-rent ratio of 22.5, significantly above the national average of 18.7. This metric indicates that purchasing is mathematically less advantageous than renting for many households, a dynamic reinforced by a median home value of $367,600 against a median gross rent of $1,360. The market exhibits strong momentum, with the FHFA House Price Index rising 4.72% year-over-year, outpacing the state average of 3.11% by 1.61 percentage points. This recent acceleration follows a period of extreme volatility, where home values surged 16.7% in 2022 and 11.2% in 2024, suggesting sustained demand pressure that has not yet fully normalized. Affordability remains the primary constraint, with a price-to-income ratio of 3.79, well above the national benchmark of 3.20. Consequently, the county’s affordability metrics lag significantly behind its momentum indicators. While the rent burden of 16.8% is slightly below the national average of 17.2%, the high cost of entry for homeownership creates a bifurcated market. Regional context further complicates the picture; home values are 5.2% above the state average, yet incomes are 42.7% above the national mean. This discrepancy suggests that local purchasing power is being eroded by rapid asset appreciation rather than stagnant wages. The local economy supports this demand through 60,473 jobs and an average weekly wage of $1,359, while net migration of 3,494 filers between 2021 and 2022 signals continued population growth. Notable data patterns reveal a divergence where home values are rising faster than rents, driven partly by limited new supply. Only 286 new units were authorized in 2025, with 248 being single-family, a volume insufficient to offset the inflow of higher-income migrants averaging $100,049. The current 30-year fixed mortgage rate of 7.40% exacerbates affordability challenges, reducing monthly purchasing power for prospective buyers. Overall, the market’s vitality is driven by robust economic fundamentals and positive migration, but its stability is undermined by affordability pressures. The composite assessment reflects this tension: high momentum and vitality scores are counterbalanced by low affordability and moderate stability, indicating a market that is dynamically active but increasingly difficult for middle-income households to enter.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Merrimack County is 5.2% above the New Hampshire state average and 61.4% above the U.S. national average. The median rent of $1,360/month is 3.2% above the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 22.5, renting is generally more cost-effective than buying in the short to medium term. The market favors buyers only for long-term holds.

Merrimack County has an owner-occupancy rate of 73.5%, compared to 74.3% in New Hampshire and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more homeowner-heavy market than the U.S. average.
