📝 Local Market Analysis

Marshall County, Kentucky, presents a balanced market leaning slightly toward buyers, characterized by strong momentum and relative affordability. The price-to-income ratio of 2.64 sits well below the national average of 3.20, while the rent burden stands at 14.5% compared to the national 17.2%. These metrics indicate that housing costs remain manageable for local income levels, which average $65,831 annually. The market’s strength is further evidenced by an owner-occupancy rate of 81.6%, suggesting a stable residential base. With a median home value of $173,800, the county trades at a 23.7% discount to the national average, yet commands an 8.1% premium over the Kentucky state average, highlighting its competitive position within the state. The recent trajectory reveals a significant acceleration in home values, culminating in an 18.5% year-over-year surge in 2022, followed by an 8.2% increase in 2023. Current FHFA data shows a robust 8.30% year-over-year gain, outperforming the state’s 4.26% growth by 4.04 percentage points. This outperformance is notable given the national 30-year fixed mortgage rate of 7.40%, which typically suppresses demand in higher-priced markets. The divergence between rapid price appreciation and slower rent growth, reflected in a price-to-rent ratio of 18.1, suggests that equity gains are outpacing rental yield improvements. This pattern is supported by positive net migration of 772 filers and a tight housing supply, with only 86 new units authorized in 2025, predominantly single-family homes. Local economic fundamentals provide a solid foundation for this growth. With 9,805 total jobs and an average weekly wage of $1,241, the labor market supports the current price levels. The high momentum score is driven by this consistent price appreciation and outperformance relative to state benchmarks. Conversely, the stability component is moderated by the volatility seen in the 2021 dip and the 2022 spike, indicating a market sensitive to broader economic shifts. The vitality score reflects the limited new construction pipeline, which constrains supply but supports existing home values. For potential buyers, the current environment offers entry points below national averages with strong recent equity growth, while renters face a market where ownership remains significantly more affordable than long-term rental commitments. The combination of low price-to-income ratios and strong price momentum defines this market as a high-value proposition within the regional context.
