📝 Local Market Analysis

Douglas County, Missouri, presents a balanced market structure characterized by strong homeowner equity but limited new supply. With an owner-occupancy rate of 80.6% and a price-to-rent ratio of 20.0, the area leans slightly toward ownership benefits, though the rent burden of 18.3% slightly exceeds the national average of 17.2%. The market is currently underperforming relative to the broader Missouri housing sector, with recent FHFA HPI data indicating a year-over-year increase of only 1.28%, compared to the state’s 3.71%. This divergence suggests that while the county maintains stability, it lacks the momentum driving statewide appreciation. The primary constraint on market vitality is affordability, evidenced by a price-to-income ratio of 3.66, which is 14.4% higher than the national benchmark of 3.20. Despite this, the county offers significant value relative to national averages, with median home values sitting 26.1% below the U.S. mean. A notable anomaly exists in the recent price trajectory; after a sharp 15.2% surge in 2022 and a 10.4% rise in 2023, growth has moderated to just 2.3% in 2024. This deceleration aligns with the current 7.40% mortgage rate environment, which suppresses demand velocity. Furthermore, home values are rising faster than rents, indicating that ownership costs are becoming more burdensome relative to leasing options, a trend that could pressure renters if wage growth does not keep pace. Economic fundamentals provide a mixed backdrop for long-term stability. Local employment is modest, with only 1,980 total jobs and an average weekly wage of $742, which is low relative to national standards. However, the county has experienced positive net migration of 384 filers, with inflows averaging $45,571 in income, slightly outpacing outflows. Housing supply remains tight, with only 24 new units authorized in 2025, including 16 multi-family units. This limited construction pipeline, combined with high existing equity, supports price stability despite the weak local job market. For potential buyers, the lower entry price point offers leverage, but the high price-to-income ratio and slowing price momentum suggest that appreciation may remain flat in the near term. Renters may find the current market less attractive as the gap between rental costs and ownership equity widens, driven by the disparity in growth rates between the two sectors.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Douglas County is 4.3% below the Missouri state average and 26.1% below the U.S. national average. The median rent of $700/month is 10.5% below the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 20.0, renting is generally more cost-effective than buying in the short to medium term. The market favors buyers only for long-term holds.

Douglas County has an owner-occupancy rate of 80.6%, compared to 73.7% in Missouri and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more homeowner-heavy market than the U.S. average.
