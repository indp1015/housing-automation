📝 Editor's Insight

El Dorado County exhibits a strong owner-occupancy preference, with a 76.4% rate significantly exceeding national norms, suggesting a buyer-oriented environment rather than a renter's market. The five-year trajectory reveals robust appreciation, with home values compounding at 8.6% annually compared to a 6.6% increase in rents. This divergence indicates that equity accumulation is outpacing rental cost inflation, reinforcing the financial advantage of homeownership over leasing in this specific jurisdiction. Affordability remains a critical constraint, however. The price-to-income ratio of 6.03 is nearly double the national average of 3.10, while the price-to-rent ratio of 32.8 far exceeds the 18.1 benchmark, signaling that purchase prices are disproportionately high relative to both local earnings and rental alternatives. Although the rent burden of 18.4% slightly exceeds the national 17.2% average, it remains manageable. A crucial, non-obvious insight is that while incomes are 60.1% above the national mean, they have not kept pace with the 39% surge in home values since 2019. Consequently, the widening gap between asset appreciation and wage growth suggests that entry-level buyers face increasing barriers despite the area’s relatively high median household income of $106,190.

Market Analysis

The median home value in El Dorado County is 11.6% above the California state average and 198.6% above the U.S. national average. The median rent of $1,715/month is 1.5% above the state average.
