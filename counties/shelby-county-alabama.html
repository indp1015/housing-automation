📝 Local Market Analysis

Shelby County, Alabama, operates as a balanced market where strong economic fundamentals offset moderate affordability constraints, resulting in a composite assessment of 60.9 out of 100. The market is characterized by high stability, scoring 74.4, driven by an 81.4% owner-occupancy rate and robust local employment totaling 75,336 jobs. However, affordability lags at 39.8, reflecting a price-to-income ratio of 3.33, which slightly exceeds the national average of 3.20. Despite this, the rent burden remains at 17.2%, perfectly aligned with the national benchmark, suggesting that while purchasing power is strained, rental costs remain proportionate to local earnings. The market outperforms the broader state, with home values sitting 104.2% above the Alabama average and 43.2% above the national mean, mirroring a household income level that is 44.1% higher than the U.S. norm. Price momentum has decelerated from a peak year-over-year increase of 17.4% in 2022 to 9.2% in 2024, indicating a cooling but still appreciating environment. Recent FHFA data confirms this moderation, showing a 4.01% year-over-year gain in 2025, which outpaces the state’s 2.79% growth by 1.22 percentage points. A notable divergence exists between asset classes: home values are rising faster than rents, pushing the price-to-rent ratio to 19.4 compared to the national average of 18.7. This suggests that the buying market is currently more expensive relative to renting than the national norm, potentially incentivizing continued rental demand despite the high owner-occupancy rate. New construction, with 1,308 units authorized in 2025, including 1,008 single-family homes, provides a moderate supply buffer against demand pressures. Economic vitality, scoring 69.9, is supported by positive net migration of 4,325 filers and an average weekly wage of $1,367. However, the current 30-year fixed mortgage rate of 7.40% significantly impacts purchasing power, contributing to the lower affordability score. The influx of migrants with average incomes near $117,000 sustains demand for higher-value properties. For potential buyers, the data indicates a market where entry costs are elevated relative to income, yet the underlying economic health and outperformance relative to state benchmarks provide structural support. Renters face a market where costs are stable relative to income, but the widening gap between price and rent ratios may limit long-term affordability for those transitioning to ownership. The overall trajectory reflects a mature, high-income market adjusting to higher interest rates without losing its competitive advantage within the state.

The median home value in Shelby County is 104.2% above the Alabama state average and 43.2% above the U.S. national average. The median rent of $1,401/month is 70.9% above the state average.
