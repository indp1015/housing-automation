Boone County functions as a balanced market, characterized by a price-to-rent ratio of 19.7, slightly above the national average of 18.0. This metric suggests homeownership is marginally more expensive relative to renting than the typical U.S. scenario, yet the 70.3% owner-occupancy rate indicates strong local attachment to property ownership rather than transient tenancy. The five-year trend reveals significant appreciation, with home values rising at an 8.0% CAGR compared to a modest 3.0% CAGR for rents. This divergence highlights that equity accumulation has outpaced rental cost inflation, benefiting current owners while potentially straining new entrants. Furthermore, the rent burden of 15.8% falls below the national average of 17.2%, indicating that rental costs consume a smaller portion of household income than in most other regions. This institutional anchor creates a localized demand spike that decouples local pricing from broader state trends, meaning buyers and renters face costs significantly higher than neighboring rural counties despite similar income levels.
