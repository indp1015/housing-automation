📝 Editor's Insight

Boone County functions as a balanced market, characterized by a price-to-rent ratio of 19.7, slightly above the national average of 18.0. This metric suggests homeownership is marginally more expensive relative to renting than the typical U.S. scenario, yet the 70.3% owner-occupancy rate indicates strong local attachment to property ownership rather than transient tenancy. The five-year trend reveals significant appreciation, with home values rising at an 8.0% CAGR compared to a modest 3.0% CAGR for rents. This divergence highlights that equity accumulation has outpaced rental cost inflation, benefiting current owners while potentially straining new entrants. Affordability remains robust, as the price-to-income ratio of 3.12 aligns closely with the national benchmark of 3.09. Furthermore, the rent burden of 15.8% falls below the national average of 17.2%, indicating that rental costs consume a smaller portion of household income than in most other regions. A critical insight for potential residents is the 28.3% premium over the Arkansas state average, driven by Boone County’s proximity to the University of Arkansas. This institutional anchor creates a localized demand spike that decouples local pricing from broader state trends, meaning buyers and renters face costs significantly higher than neighboring rural counties despite similar income levels.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Boone County is 33.5% above the Arkansas state average and 17.0% below the U.S. national average. The median rent of $733/month is 5.3% below the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 21.5, renting is generally more cost-effective than buying in the short to medium term. The market favors buyers only for long-term holds.

Boone County has an owner-occupancy rate of 70.8%, compared to 71.1% in Arkansas and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more renter-heavy market than the U.S. average.
