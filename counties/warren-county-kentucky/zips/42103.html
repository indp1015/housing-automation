This sustained growth reflects a robust local demand environment, with the index reaching its highest recorded level in the available 41-year dataset. The steady annual increases suggest that the sub-market has maintained momentum without significant volatility, positioning it as a stable asset class within the broader regional context. This deceleration suggests that the specific sub-market may be experiencing a cooling phase relative to its surrounding areas. While the county and state continue to see robust price appreciation, the slower pace in 42103 indicates a potential stabilization of values after the rapid gains observed in previous years. This divergence signals that local supply and demand dynamics are currently moderating, distinguishing the ZIP from the faster-moving broader Kentucky and Warren County markets.
