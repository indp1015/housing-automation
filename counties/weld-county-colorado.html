📝 Local Market Analysis

Weld County, Colorado, functions as a moderately stressed market where affordability constraints significantly outweigh strong economic vitality. The median home value of $471,700 stands at 107.2% above the national average, while the median household income of $97,097 is only 42.8% higher than the national norm. This disparity results in a price-to-income ratio of 4.86, substantially exceeding the national benchmark of 3.20, indicating that housing costs consume a disproportionate share of local earnings. Consequently, the market leans toward a renter’s disadvantage in terms of entry-level purchasing power, despite a high owner-occupancy rate of 76.0%. The price-to-rent ratio of 25.5, compared to the national average of 18.7, further suggests that buying is less attractive relative to renting than in most other U.S. regions, reinforcing the affordability strain. Trend data reveals a period of rapid appreciation that is now moderating. Home values rose from $245,000 in 2017 to $471,700 in 2024, with peak year-over-year growth of 17.1% in 2022 slowing to 6.1% by 2024. This deceleration aligns with the FHFA House Price Index, which shows a recent year-over-year change of +0.74%, outperforming the Colorado state average of -0.61% by 1.35 percentage points. This relative strength suggests Weld County retains momentum even as broader state markets cool. A notable pattern is the divergence between asset values and rental costs; while home prices have surged, the median gross rent of $1,539/month translates to a rent burden of 19.0% of income, slightly above the national average of 17.2%. This indicates that rental costs are not keeping pace with the sharp increase in property values, potentially offering a temporary hedge for renters against the high cost of ownership. The local economic context supports sustained demand despite affordability challenges. With 103,697 jobs and an average weekly wage of $1,350, the employment base is robust. Net migration of +10,611 filers between 2021 and 2022, driven by inflows averaging $88,725 in income, continues to bolster the housing market. Housing supply remains active, with 2,993 new units authorized in 2025, including 2,011 single-family homes. However, the 7.32% 30-year fixed mortgage rate significantly increases monthly payment burdens, exacerbating the affordability gap. The high vitality score of 87.6 reflects this strong demographic and economic growth, while the low affordability score of 16.9 highlights the tension between rising incomes and even faster rising home prices. Stability remains relatively high at 68.0, suggesting the market is adjusting rather than collapsing, but buyers face a challenging entry point compared to national standards.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Weld County is 10.3% above the Colorado state average and 107.2% above the U.S. national average. The median rent of $1,539/month is 18.2% above the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 25.5, Weld County is a strongly rent-favored market. Home prices are high relative to rents, meaning renting is typically far cheaper than owning in the near term.

Weld County has an owner-occupancy rate of 76.0%, compared to 71.9% in Colorado and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more homeowner-heavy market than the U.S. average.
