📝 Editor's Insight

Park County exhibits a distinct renter’s market dynamic, evidenced by a 21.0 price-to-rent ratio that exceeds the national average of 18.0. This metric indicates that purchasing is relatively more expensive compared to renting, discouraging immediate homeownership. The five-year trend reveals accelerating rental costs, with gross rent compounding at 14.6% annually, significantly outpacing the 10.6% growth in median home values. This divergence suggests a structural shift toward rental demand or supply constraints affecting lease rates disproportionately. Affordability remains severely strained, as the 5.13 price-to-income ratio is nearly double the national benchmark of 3.09. Furthermore, the 24.4% rent burden far surpasses the 17.2% national average, meaning residents allocate a substantial portion of earnings to housing. A critical, non-obvious insight emerges from the 87.4% owner-occupancy rate; despite high entry costs, the market is deeply entrenched in homeownership. Consequently, new entrants face a dual barrier: high upfront capital requirements and limited rental inventory, which may sustain elevated lease prices due to persistent demand from those unable to purchase.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Park County is 24.5% above the Colorado state average and 133.7% above the U.S. national average. The median rent of $2,161/month is 66.0% above the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 20.5, renting is generally more cost-effective than buying in the short to medium term. The market favors buyers only for long-term holds.

Park County has an owner-occupancy rate of 86.2%, compared to 71.9% in Colorado and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more homeowner-heavy market than the U.S. average.
