📝 Local Market Analysis

Park County, Colorado, functions as a distinctively unbalanced market where extreme affordability constraints coexist with robust economic vitality. The market is neither a traditional buyer’s nor renter’s advantage zone; rather, it presents a high-barrier entry point for both. With a median home value of $532,100 against a median household income of $103,670, the price-to-income ratio stands at 5.13, significantly exceeding the national average of 3.20. This disparity is mirrored in the rental sector, where the median gross rent of $2,161 consumes 25.0% of typical income, well above the national benchmark of 17.2%. Consequently, the market is characterized by high stability, evidenced by an 86.2% owner-occupancy rate, but low momentum for new entrants due to prohibitive costs. Historical data reveals a period of aggressive appreciation, with home values rising from $262,300 in 2017 to $532,100 in 2024. While the peak year-over-year growth reached 15.9% in 2022, the recent FHFA HPI indicates a sharp deceleration to just 1.26% in 2025. This recent flattening contrasts with the state’s negative 0.61% change, suggesting Park County is outperforming its regional peers despite the broader cooling trend. A notable pattern emerges in the supply-demand dynamics: while 152 new single-family units were authorized in 2025, multi-family construction remains at zero, limiting rental supply flexibility. This structural rigidity, combined with net migration inflows of 954 filers between 2021 and 2022, sustains underlying demand even as price growth moderates. The economic context further complicates entry, with local employment averaging $1,084 weekly wages and a national mortgage rate of 7.32% increasing monthly debt service burdens. The composite assessment reflects this tension: high vitality and stability scores are driven by strong local incomes and low turnover, while affordability scores plummet due to the 5.13 price-to-income ratio. The price-to-rent ratio of 20.5, slightly above the national 18.7, suggests that renting remains marginally more efficient than buying for those who cannot secure favorable financing. Ultimately, the market is defined by its premium positioning, where home values exceed the state average by 24.5% and the national average by 133.7%, creating a niche for high-income buyers while excluding median-income households from both ownership and affordable rental options.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Park County is 24.5% above the Colorado state average and 133.7% above the U.S. national average. The median rent of $2,161/month is 66.0% above the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 20.5, renting is generally more cost-effective than buying in the short to medium term. The market favors buyers only for long-term holds.

Park County has an owner-occupancy rate of 86.2%, compared to 71.9% in Colorado and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more homeowner-heavy market than the U.S. average.
