📝 Local Market Analysis

Granville County, North Carolina, functions as a moderately balanced market with distinct affordability challenges, characterized by a 77.1% owner occupancy rate and a price-to-rent ratio of 20.5, which exceeds the national average of 18.7. The market classification leans slightly toward buyers due to sustained price appreciation, yet the high ratio indicates that purchasing remains costlier relative to renting than in most other U.S. counties. This dynamic is reinforced by a median home value of $247,700, which sits 8.8% above the national average and 5.3% above the state average, reflecting a premium positioning within the regional housing landscape. Trend analysis reveals a period of accelerated growth followed by stabilization. Home values increased from $146,100 in 2017 to $247,700 in 2024, with a peak year-over-year surge of 17.5% in 2022 before moderating to 5.1% in 2024. Recent FHFA data confirms this deceleration, showing a 2.11% year-over-year increase that outpaces the state’s 0.38% growth rate, indicating continued relative strength. However, affordability metrics remain strained; the price-to-income ratio stands at 3.48, higher than the national benchmark of 3.20. While the rent burden of 17.0% aligns closely with the national average of 17.2%, the divergence between rising home values and slower rent growth suggests that the entry barrier for homeownership is widening relative to rental costs. Economic fundamentals support this trajectory, with local employment reaching 14,377 jobs and an average weekly wage of $1,128. Net migration of +1,360 filers between 2021 and 2022, coupled with 257 new housing units authorized in 2025, signals robust demand that outpaces new supply. The low multi-family permit count of just four units highlights a market heavily skewed toward single-family construction, which may limit rental inventory growth. With mortgage rates at 7.40%, the cost of financing remains a significant headwind. Consequently, the market’s vitality is driven by strong population inflows and economic activity, while affordability constraints and elevated price-to-income ratios temper overall stability. Potential buyers face a competitive environment where price appreciation has historically outpaced income growth, necessitating careful assessment of long-term cost structures against current wage levels.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Granville County is 5.3% above the North Carolina state average and 8.8% above the U.S. national average. The median rent of $1,008/month is 3.3% above the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 20.5, renting is generally more cost-effective than buying in the short to medium term. The market favors buyers only for long-term holds.

Granville County has an owner-occupancy rate of 77.1%, compared to 71.8% in North Carolina and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more homeowner-heavy market than the U.S. average.
