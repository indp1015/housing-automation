📝 Editor's Insight

Denver County exhibits a strong buyer’s market, evidenced by a 49.1% owner-occupancy rate and a price-to-rent ratio of 27.6, significantly exceeding the national average of 18.1. This disparity suggests that purchasing is often more financially advantageous than long-term renting. Over the past five years, home values surged at a 10.7% CAGR to $586,700, outpacing the 7.8% CAGR for rents, which reached $1,770 monthly. This divergence indicates that asset appreciation has accelerated faster than rental cost inflation. Affordability remains strained, with a price-to-income ratio of 6.40, more than double the national benchmark of 3.10. Additionally, the 23.2% rent burden exceeds the national average of 17.2%, reflecting high housing costs relative to the $91,681 median household income. A critical insight for residents is that while incomes are 38.3% above the national average, home prices are 174.0% higher. This gap implies that local salary premiums do not sufficiently offset the extreme premium paid for Denver real estate, making entry-level housing particularly challenging despite robust regional earnings.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Denver County is 47.4% above the Colorado state average and 177.4% above the U.S. national average. The median rent of $1,770/month is 41.3% above the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 27.6, Denver County is a strongly rent-favored market. Home prices are high relative to rents, meaning renting is typically far cheaper than owning in the near term.

Denver County has an owner-occupancy rate of 49.1%, compared to 72.1% in Colorado and 73.0% nationally. This indicates a more renter-heavy market than the U.S. average.
