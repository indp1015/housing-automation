📝 Local Market Analysis

Denver County operates as a distinctively balanced market, characterized by high vitality but severe affordability constraints. The owner-occupancy rate of 48.8% suggests a near-even split between renting and owning, yet the economic dynamics favor neither group significantly. The composite assessment reflects this tension, with vitality scores of 63.6/100 contrasting sharply against affordability metrics of 3.9/100. This divergence highlights a market where robust demand coexists with prohibitive entry costs, driven by a median household income of $94,718 that lags far behind the $616,000 median home value. The multi-year trend reveals a period of rapid appreciation that is now stabilizing. Home values surged from $322,900 in 2017 to $616,000 in 2024, peaking at 17.7% annual growth in 2022 before moderating to 5.0%. However, recent FHFA HPI data indicates a slight contraction, with a -0.43% year-over-year change, signaling a potential plateau or correction. This recent momentum contrasts with the long-term upward trajectory, suggesting that the initial post-pandemic boom has cooled. A notable pattern emerges in the price-to-rent ratio of 28.0, which is nearly 50% higher than the national average of 18.7. This indicates that purchasing equity is currently less attractive relative to renting, as home prices have outpaced rent growth, which stands at a median of $1,831 per month. Affordability remains the primary stressor, with a price-to-income ratio of 6.50, double the national benchmark of 3.20. Renters face a burden of 23.2% of income, exceeding the national average of 17.2%. Regionally, Denver’s home values are 44.1% above the Colorado state average and 170.5% above the national mean, reflecting its premium status within the Mountain West. The local economy supports this premium through 486,058 jobs and an average weekly wage of $2,106, yet high mortgage rates of 7.40% further restrict buyer eligibility. Housing supply, with 4,727 new units authorized in 2025, includes a heavy skew toward multi-family (3,700 units), which may alleviate rental pressure but does little to address the shortage of affordable single-family homes. Net migration of +9,921 filers continues to inject demand, sustaining price levels despite the high cost of entry. Consequently, the market’s stability is undermined by extreme valuation multiples, making it a high-risk, high-reward environment for investors and a challenging landscape for first-time buyers.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Denver County is 44.1% above the Colorado state average and 170.5% above the U.S. national average. The median rent of $1,831/month is 40.6% above the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 28.0, Denver County is a strongly rent-favored market. Home prices are high relative to rents, meaning renting is typically far cheaper than owning in the near term.

Denver County has an owner-occupancy rate of 48.8%, compared to 71.9% in Colorado and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more renter-heavy market than the U.S. average.
