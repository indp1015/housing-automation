📝 Editor's Insight

Modoc County presents a balanced market leaning toward buyer advantage, evidenced by a 77.3% owner occupancy rate and a price-to-rent ratio of 21.6. Although this ratio exceeds the national average of 18.0, indicating relatively higher purchase costs compared to rental expenses, the high ownership rate suggests established residential stability rather than speculative volatility. The five-year trend reveals significant appreciation, with home values growing at a 10.8% CAGR to reach $212,000, substantially outpacing the 3.4% CAGR in median gross rent. This divergence highlights a narrowing gap between purchasing and renting costs over time. Affordability remains tight; the price-to-income ratio sits at 3.74, surpassing the national benchmark of 3.09, while the rent burden of 17.3% aligns precisely with national averages. A critical insight for potential residents is that despite home values being 62.9% below the state average, local income is 14.1% below the national mean. This income disparity means that relative purchasing power is lower than raw price comparisons suggest, requiring careful budgeting against the $56,648 median household income to sustain homeownership in this rapidly appreciating rural market.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Modoc County is 63.1% below the California state average and 1.3% below the U.S. national average. The median rent of $836/month is 50.5% below the state average.

Modoc County has an owner-occupancy rate of 77.4%, compared to 63.4% in California and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more homeowner-heavy market than the U.S. average.
