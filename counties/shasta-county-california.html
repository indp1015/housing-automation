📝 Local Market Analysis

Shasta County operates as a highly stressed market characterized by severe affordability constraints, evidenced by a price-to-income ratio of 5.04, significantly exceeding the national average of 3.20. The median home value of $366,400 stands 60.9% above the national average, while the median household income of $72,636 is only 6.8% higher than the national norm. This disparity creates a rent burden of 21.8% of income, well above the 17.2% national benchmark, and a price-to-rent ratio of 23.2, indicating that owning remains disproportionately expensive relative to leasing. Consequently, the market leans heavily toward a seller’s dynamic for existing inventory, though the high cost of entry limits broad-based demand. The multi-year trend reveals a period of rapid appreciation followed by recent stabilization. Home values surged 16.3% year-over-year in 2022, moderating to 5.5% in 2024, while the FHFA HPI indicates a near-flat recent change of -0.03%. This divergence between the still-rising Census median values and the flat FHFA index suggests a potential cooling in transaction prices or a lag in data reporting. The local economy supports this mixed signal, with 58,359 total jobs and an average weekly wage of $1,111. However, housing supply is extremely tight, with only 254 new units authorized in 2025, predominantly single-family homes, which restricts new inventory from alleviating price pressure. Migration patterns further complicate the landscape, as the county absorbed a net gain of 3,024 filers between 2021 and 2022, with incoming households earning an average of $76,217. This inflow of income-eligible buyers likely contributed to the 2022 price spike. Currently, the 7.32% national mortgage rate exacerbates affordability issues, reducing purchasing power for local residents. The overall market assessment is driven primarily by the low affordability metric, which weighs heavily on stability and vitality scores. While momentum remains positive due to sustained price growth over the last seven years, the high cost relative to local wages and the scarcity of new supply create a fragile equilibrium. Potential buyers face a market where entry costs are high, yet rental alternatives also carry significant financial burdens, limiting flexibility for households navigating the transition from renting to owning.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Shasta County is 39.9% below the California state average and 60.9% above the U.S. national average. The median rent of $1,317/month is 22.1% below the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 23.2, renting is generally more cost-effective than buying in the short to medium term. The market favors buyers only for long-term holds.

Shasta County has an owner-occupancy rate of 65.6%, compared to 63.4% in California and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more renter-heavy market than the U.S. average.
