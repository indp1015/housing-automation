📝 Editor's Insight

Scott County functions as a distinct buyer’s market, evidenced by a price-to-rent ratio of 12.6, significantly below the national average of 18.0. This metric indicates that purchasing equity is substantially more cost-effective than long-term leasing. The five-year trend reveals robust capital appreciation, with home values compounding at 9.8% annually, outpacing the 6.9% growth in median gross rents. This divergence suggests that property values are rising faster than rental costs, favoring long-term ownership over renting. Regarding affordability, the price-to-income ratio stands at 2.41, well under the national benchmark of 3.09, confirming high relative purchasing power for local earners. However, the rent burden reaches 19.2% of household income, exceeding the national average of 17.2%, indicating that renting is becoming increasingly tight on household budgets. A critical insight for potential residents is that while absolute home values are 47.8% below the national mean, the rapid 9.8% annual appreciation rate implies that entry-level pricing is eroding quickly. Consequently, the current affordability gap between buying and renting is narrowing, making immediate purchase strategically superior for those with stable income, as the cost of waiting increases at a pace faster than rental inflation.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Scott County is 18.7% below the Arkansas state average and 49.5% below the U.S. national average. The median rent of $722/month is 6.7% below the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 13.3, buying a home in Scott County is generally more favorable than renting for those planning to stay long-term. A ratio below 15 typically signals that purchasing is financially advantageous over a multi-year horizon.

Scott County has an owner-occupancy rate of 78.1%, compared to 71.1% in Arkansas and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more homeowner-heavy market than the U.S. average.
