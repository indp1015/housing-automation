📝 Local Market Analysis

Cleburne County, Arkansas, presents a complex housing environment characterized by strong recent momentum but constrained affordability. The market leans slightly toward a renter’s advantage, evidenced by a price-to-rent ratio of 17.4, which is below the national average of 18.7. This metric suggests that renting is relatively more cost-effective than purchasing for local residents. However, the owner-occupancy rate stands at 80.6%, indicating a solid base of homeownership that stabilizes the local housing stock. The composite assessment of this market reflects high momentum, driven by significant recent price appreciation, yet it is tempered by poor affordability scores and limited economic vitality. From 2017 to 2024, median home values increased from $127,000 to $179,800, representing a cumulative gain of approximately 41.6%. This trajectory includes a volatile period where values dipped in 2019 and 2020 before surging 16.5% in 2022 and maintaining positive growth through 2024 at 3.0%. This pattern reveals a market that recovered from a brief stagnation to enter a phase of rapid expansion, now moderating. Notably, home values are rising faster than rents, creating a divergence that pressures potential buyers. The affordability challenge is acute; the price-to-income ratio is 3.22, nearly identical to the national average of 3.20, despite the county’s median household income of $55,798 being 17.9% below the national mean. Furthermore, the rent burden is 18.5% of income, exceeding the national benchmark of 17.2%. The regional context highlights a significant premium over the state average, with home values 27.1% higher than the Arkansas median, while remaining 21.0% below the national average. This disparity is driven by robust net migration of 841 filers between 2021 and 2022, where inflows slightly outpaced outflows. However, the local economy shows signs of strain, with only 5,654 total jobs and a mere five new housing units authorized in 2025. This severe supply constraint, combined with a 7.32% national mortgage rate, limits purchasing power. The high momentum score is driven by the recent price appreciation, while the low vitality score reflects the thin employment base and stagnant new construction. For stakeholders, the data indicates a market where entry is increasingly difficult due to rising prices outpacing income growth, despite the relative affordability of the asset compared to national peers.
