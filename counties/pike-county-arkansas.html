📝 Editor's Insight

Pike County functions as a distinct buyer’s market, evidenced by a price-to-rent ratio of 14.3, significantly below the national average of 18.0. This metric indicates that purchasing is substantially more cost-effective than renting relative to local income levels. The five-year trend reveals a divergence where home values expanded at an 8.0% CAGR, outpacing the 2.8% CAGR for rents. This suggests capital appreciation is driving value rather than rental demand, potentially signaling a shift in housing stock dynamics. Affordability remains robust, with a price-to-income ratio of 2.26, well under the national benchmark of 3.09. Additionally, the rent burden of 15.8% is lower than the national 17.2%, indicating low financial stress for tenants. A critical insight for potential residents is the 75.9% owner-occupancy rate. This high ownership concentration implies a limited rental inventory, meaning the low rent burden may not persist if the small rental pool tightens further. Buyers benefit from strong equity growth, while renters face a structurally constrained market with fewer alternatives.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Pike County is 12.6% below the Arkansas state average and 45.4% below the U.S. national average. The median rent of $671/month is 11.6% below the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 14.3, buying a home in Pike County is generally more favorable than renting for those planning to stay long-term. A ratio below 15 typically signals that purchasing is financially advantageous over a multi-year horizon.

Pike County has an owner-occupancy rate of 75.9%, compared to 70.5% in Arkansas and 73.0% nationally. This indicates a more homeowner-heavy market than the U.S. average.
