📝 Editor's Insight

Pima County functions as a renter’s market, evidenced by a price-to-rent ratio of 20.7, exceeding the national average of 18.0. This metric indicates that purchasing is less financially advantageous than leasing relative to local income levels. The five-year trend reveals aggressive appreciation, with home values growing at an 11.7% CAGR compared to a 6.2% CAGR for rents. This divergence suggests capital gains are outpacing rental yield improvements, further pressuring the purchase side of the equation. Affordability remains strained, as the price-to-income ratio stands at 4.22, significantly higher than the 3.09 national benchmark. Additionally, the rent burden consumes 20.4% of median household income, surpassing the 17.2% national norm. A critical, non-obvious insight is the disparity between income and asset growth. While median household income is only 3.0% above the national average, home values are 35.0% higher. This decoupling implies that local wage growth has failed to match real estate appreciation, creating a structural affordability gap that persists despite the region’s population of over one million residents.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Pima County is 20.3% above the Arizona state average and 40.4% above the U.S. national average. The median rent of $1,212/month is 13.0% above the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 22.0, renting is generally more cost-effective than buying in the short to medium term. The market favors buyers only for long-term holds.

Pima County has an owner-occupancy rate of 65.2%, compared to 70.7% in Arizona and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more renter-heavy market than the U.S. average.
