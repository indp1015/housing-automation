📝 Local Market Analysis

Escambia County, Florida, presents a complex market dynamic characterized by significant recent price appreciation that is now stabilizing. With a median home value of $257,200 and a price-to-rent ratio of 16.2, the market leans toward being favorable for buyers relative to the national average of 18.7, suggesting that purchasing offers a more cost-effective long-term strategy than renting. However, the recent FHFA House Price Index shows a year-over-year decline of 0.36%, indicating that the explosive growth phase has paused, with the county currently underperforming the state’s 0.91% gain by 1.27 percentage points. This stabilization follows a period of intense volatility, where home values surged 103.8% between 2017 and 2024, peaking at 20.1% growth in 2022 before moderating to 9.8% in 2024. Affordability remains a critical constraint for local residents. The price-to-income ratio stands at 3.81, significantly above the national benchmark of 3.20, driven by a median household income of $67,500 that lags behind rising asset values. Furthermore, the rent burden consumes 23.5% of household income, well above the national average of 17.2%, highlighting that both ownership and rental costs are straining local budgets. Despite these pressures, the county’s median home value is 9.5% below the state average, offering a relative discount within Florida’s broader landscape, though it remains 13.0% above the national mean. This divergence suggests that while Escambia is more expensive than the typical U.S. market, it is more accessible than other Florida counties. The local economic foundation supports sustained demand through robust migration and employment. Net migration added 8,854 filers between 2021 and 2022, with incoming households averaging $74,589 in income, slightly higher than the local median. Employment stands at 125,550 jobs with an average weekly wage of $1,208, providing a stable income base. Housing supply remains tight, with only 1,398 new units authorized in 2025, predominantly single-family homes, which limits the ability to absorb new demand. With 30-year mortgage rates at 7.40%, high borrowing costs continue to suppress transaction volume, yet the underlying demand from in-migrants and limited new construction suggests that price stability is likely to persist rather than reverse, maintaining a balanced but financially pressured environment for residents.
