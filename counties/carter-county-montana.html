📝 Local Market Analysis

Carter County, Montana, presents a complex micro-market characterized by extreme volatility and structural fragility. The market leans toward a renter’s environment, evidenced by a price-to-rent ratio of 20.1, which exceeds the national average of 18.7, suggesting that purchasing is currently less advantageous than renting relative to asset value. However, this classification is heavily distorted by the county’s minuscule population of 1,308 residents and a total employment base of only 196 jobs. The low owner-occupancy rate of 62.2% further indicates a housing stock that does not strongly support long-term residential stability. With a median home value of $199,200, the county sits 28.1% below the state average and 12.5% below the national mean, reflecting its remote, rural economic profile. The primary anomaly in this dataset is the explosive appreciation of home values relative to local income levels. Median home prices surged from $97,900 in 2017 to $199,200 in 2024, representing a cumulative increase of over 103%. This trajectory includes a peak year-over-year gain of 25.3% in 2023, which has since moderated to 12.8% in 2024. Despite this rapid price escalation, the median household income remains at $54,167, resulting in a price-to-income ratio of 3.68, significantly higher than the national benchmark of 3.20. This divergence suggests that price growth is decoupled from local earning power, likely driven by external demand or speculative activity rather than organic local economic expansion. The rent burden of 18.3% also slightly exceeds the national average of 17.2%, indicating that rental costs are tightening in tandem with rising property values. Economic fundamentals provide little support for sustained price growth. The local economy is constrained by a total of 38 establishments and an average weekly wage of $1,048, which translates to an annualized income well below the threshold needed to support the current median home price for the average resident. Housing supply is negligible, with only five new units authorized in 2025, three single-family and two multi-family, offering no meaningful buffer against demand shocks. Migration data shows perfect equilibrium, with 470 filers entering and 470 leaving, indicating no net demographic pressure. The current 30-year mortgage rate of 7.40% further exacerbates affordability challenges, making debt service costs prohibitive for the median household. Consequently, the market exhibits high volatility and low vitality, driven by external factors rather than internal economic strength, creating a high-risk environment for both buyers and investors.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Carter County is 28.1% below the Montana state average and 12.5% below the U.S. national average. The median rent of $824/month is 2.1% below the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 20.1, renting is generally more cost-effective than buying in the short to medium term. The market favors buyers only for long-term holds.

Carter County has an owner-occupancy rate of 62.2%, compared to 73.0% in Montana and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more renter-heavy market than the U.S. average.
