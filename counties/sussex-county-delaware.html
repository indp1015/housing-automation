📝 Local Market Analysis

Sussex County, Delaware, exhibits characteristics of a balanced market with a slight lean toward buyers, supported by robust economic fundamentals and moderate pricing relative to income. The price-to-income ratio stands at 4.69, which, while above the national average of 3.20, remains accessible given the county’s median household income of $81,497, a figure that exceeds the national average by 19.9%. The rent burden is 18.2%, only marginally higher than the national 17.2%, indicating that rental costs are not disproportionately straining household budgets. This affordability profile is reinforced by the high owner occupancy rate of 81.6%, suggesting a stable residential base. The market’s relative position is strong, with home values 9.2% above the state average and 68.0% above the national average, reflecting the county’s premium status within the regional landscape. The multi-year trend reveals a significant acceleration in home values, particularly between 2021 and 2022, when year-over-year growth peaked at 13.8%. Although this momentum has moderated to 8.3% in 2024, the FHFA House Price Index indicates continued strength in 2025, with a 5.34% year-over-year increase, outperforming the state’s 3.23% growth by 2.11 percentage points. A notable divergence exists between asset classes: home values are rising faster than rents, evidenced by a price-to-rent ratio of 25.8, significantly higher than the national average of 18.7. This suggests that the equity appreciation in homeownership is outpacing the cost of renting, potentially encouraging renters to transition to ownership if financing remains accessible. Economic drivers support this sustained demand. Net migration of 7,370 filers between 2021 and 2022, with inflowing residents earning an average of $94,749, indicates an influx of higher-income households that sustains purchasing power. Local employment, totaling 86,295 jobs with an average weekly wage of $1,092, provides a solid economic foundation. However, the 30-year fixed mortgage rate of 7.40% acts as a headwind, increasing monthly carrying costs for new buyers. Housing supply remains adequate, with 3,885 new units authorized in 2025, predominantly single-family homes, which helps mitigate supply constraints. For potential buyers, the market offers strong equity growth potential despite elevated interest rates, while renters face a stable but increasingly expensive rental environment relative to home price appreciation. The combination of high migration, strong wages, and outperforming price growth positions Sussex County as a resilient market within the broader Delaware context.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Sussex County is 9.2% above the Delaware state average and 68.0% above the U.S. national average. The median rent of $1,238/month is 8.0% below the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 25.8, Sussex County is a strongly rent-favored market. Home prices are high relative to rents, meaning renting is typically far cheaper than owning in the near term.

Sussex County has an owner-occupancy rate of 81.6%, compared to 74.4% in Delaware and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more homeowner-heavy market than the U.S. average.
