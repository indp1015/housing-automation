📝 Editor's Insight

Archuleta County functions as a distinctively balanced market, evidenced by a 26.7 price-to-rent ratio that slightly exceeds the national average of 18.1. This metric suggests homeownership costs are marginally higher than renting relative to local incomes, yet the 77.5% owner-occupancy rate indicates strong residential stability. Over the past five years, home values and rents have escalated at nearly identical rates, with CAGRs of 9.5% and 10.0% respectively. This parallel growth implies that the fundamental cost of shelter, whether owned or leased, has risen uniformly without one asset class significantly outpacing the other. Affordability remains a primary constraint, as the 5.90 price-to-income ratio is nearly double the national benchmark of 3.10. Additionally, the 22.1% rent burden exceeds the national average of 17.2%, signaling substantial financial pressure on tenants. A critical insight for potential residents is that while median household income is 15.4% above the national average, it has not kept pace with the 43.6% increase in median home values since 2019. This divergence highlights that local earnings growth has been insufficient to offset the rapid appreciation in housing costs, making entry into the market increasingly difficult for average earners despite the region’s relative wealth compared to the state average.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Archuleta County is 11.8% above the Colorado state average and 110.0% above the U.S. national average. The median rent of $1,432/month is 10.0% above the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 27.8, Archuleta County is a strongly rent-favored market. Home prices are high relative to rents, meaning renting is typically far cheaper than owning in the near term.

Archuleta County has an owner-occupancy rate of 80.1%, compared to 71.9% in Colorado and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more homeowner-heavy market than the U.S. average.
