📝 Local Market Analysis

Adams County, Colorado, operates as a distinctively stressed buyer’s market, characterized by extreme affordability constraints despite robust economic vitality. The median home value of $484,200 is 112.6% above the national average, while the median household income of $94,571 sits only 39.1% above the national mean. This disparity results in a price-to-income ratio of 5.12, significantly exceeding the national benchmark of 3.20, and a price-to-rent ratio of 22.7 compared to the national 18.7. Consequently, the affordability dimension of the market is critically low, with a rent burden of 22.6% of income versus the national 17.2%. However, the region’s vitality remains strong, supported by 201,588 total jobs and an average weekly wage of $1,366, which sustains demand even as purchasing power lags behind asset appreciation. The multi-year trend reveals a decelerating yet persistent upward trajectory in home values. After peaking at 17.9% year-over-year growth in 2022, appreciation moderated to 5.6% in 2024. Recent FHFA HPI data indicates a slight cooling, with a 1.05% year-over-year decline in 2025, tracking 0.44 percentage points below the state average. This recent negative momentum contrasts with the long-term appreciation, suggesting a stabilization phase rather than a crash. A notable anomaly exists in the divergence between price and rent growth; while home values have risen sharply, the relative strength of home values over rents signals that equity accumulation is outpacing rental yield potential, further discouraging rent-to-own transitions for high-income earners who remain priced out of ownership. Regional context highlights that Adams County home values are 13.3% above the Colorado state average, reflecting its position as a premium submarket within the state. The local supply pipeline, with 2,608 new units authorized in 2025 (1,884 single-family), attempts to address inventory shortages but remains insufficient to counteract the 10,538 net migration inflow observed in recent years. The current 7.32% national mortgage rate exacerbates the affordability gap, limiting buyer eligibility and reinforcing the stress indicators. The overall market assessment is driven primarily by the severe affordability deficit and slowing momentum, which overshadow the high vitality scores derived from employment and migration data. For potential buyers, the high entry cost and rising rates create significant barriers, while renters face a 22.6% income burden that leaves limited disposable income for down payment savings. The market remains competitive for sellers but highly restrictive for buyers, with stability compromised by the sharp divergence between asset values and local income levels.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Adams County is 13.3% above the Colorado state average and 112.6% above the U.S. national average. The median rent of $1,781/month is 36.8% above the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 22.7, renting is generally more cost-effective than buying in the short to medium term. The market favors buyers only for long-term holds.

Adams County has an owner-occupancy rate of 68.6%, compared to 71.9% in Colorado and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more renter-heavy market than the U.S. average.
