📝 Editor's Insight

Clarke County, Georgia, presents a complex housing landscape characterized by significant affordability strain despite robust price appreciation. The market leans toward a renter’s disadvantage, evidenced by a rent burden of 27.3% of median income, which substantially exceeds the national average of 17.2%. Simultaneously, the price-to-income ratio stands at 5.57, nearly double the national benchmark of 3.20, indicating that home ownership is increasingly out of reach for the average household earning $53,625. With an owner occupancy rate of only 41.4%, a majority of residents remain in the rental sector, facing costs that consume a disproportionate share of their earnings compared to broader national norms. The trajectory of home values reveals a period of accelerated growth that has recently moderated. Median home values surged from $154,600 in 2017 to $298,900 in 2024, representing a cumulative increase of approximately 93% over seven years. The most volatile period occurred in 2022, when prices jumped 21.7% year-over-year, before settling into a more stable 9.9% increase in 2024. Recent FHFA HPI data for 2025 indicates a continued but slowing momentum, with a 5.45% year-over-year increase. This performance outpaces the Georgia state average of 1.83% by 3.62 percentage points, suggesting that Clarke County remains a relative hotspot within the state despite the national cooling trend. A notable divergence exists between asset value and local economic fundamentals. Home values are 31.3% above the national average and 51.0% above the state average, yet median household income is 21.1% below the national mean. This disconnect suggests that price appreciation is driven by factors other than local wage growth, such as migration or investment demand. The price-to-rent ratio of 20.4, slightly above the national average of 18.7, further confirms that buying is not currently a financially superior alternative to renting for most households. For potential buyers, the high entry barrier relative to income levels poses a significant challenge, while renters face above-average cost burdens. The data indicates a market where equity is building rapidly for existing owners, but new entrants face steep hurdles due to the widening gap between asset prices and local earning power.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Clarke County is 51.0% above the Georgia state average and 31.3% above the U.S. national average. The median rent of $1,219/month is 26.3% above the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 20.4, renting is generally more cost-effective than buying in the short to medium term. The market favors buyers only for long-term holds.

Clarke County has an owner-occupancy rate of 41.4%, compared to 70.6% in Georgia and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more renter-heavy market than the U.S. average.
