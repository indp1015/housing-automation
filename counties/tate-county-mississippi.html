📝 Local Market Analysis

Tate County, Mississippi, functions as a balanced market with distinct affordability advantages, characterized by a median home value of $209,000 and a median household income of $69,704. The price-to-income ratio stands at 3.00, slightly below the national average of 3.20, while the rent burden is 16.6% of income, marginally lower than the 17.2% national benchmark. These metrics suggest that both purchasing and renting remain relatively accessible compared to broader U.S. standards, supporting a stable occupancy profile with a 76.0% owner-occupancy rate. The market is not skewed heavily toward buyers or renters, as the price-to-rent ratio of 18.1 aligns closely with the national average of 18.7, indicating equilibrium in housing costs relative to local earnings. The multi-year trend reveals significant volatility, with home values surging from $107,400 in 2017 to $209,000 in 2024, a cumulative increase of approximately 95%. The most recent year saw a 20.8% year-over-year jump, outpacing the state’s 0.60% growth and the national mortgage rate environment of 7.40%. This rapid appreciation contrasts with the more moderate rent increases, creating a divergence where asset values are rising faster than rental costs. The FHFA House Price Index for 2025 shows a 4.64% year-over-year change, outperforming the state by 4.04 percentage points, confirming sustained momentum despite high interest rates. This growth trajectory is supported by positive net migration of 512 filers and a local economy with 4,915 jobs and an average weekly wage of $882. However, the market faces structural constraints that temper its vitality. New housing supply is limited to 129 single-family units in 2025, with no multi-family construction authorized, which may contribute to upward price pressure. The composite assessment reflects this tension: while affordability remains strong at 50.4, stability is lower at 46.2 due to the volatility in price growth. The county’s home values are 53.9% above the state average, positioning it as a premium market within Mississippi, yet it remains 8.2% below the national average. For potential buyers, the current environment offers relative value compared to national peers, but the rapid recent appreciation suggests that entry costs are rising quickly. Renters benefit from a low burden, but the lack of new multi-family supply may limit long-term rental availability. The data indicates a market driven by local economic fundamentals and demographic inflows, rather than speculative bubbles, though the disparity between price growth and supply creation warrants close monitoring.
