📝 Local Market Analysis

Dukes County operates as a distinctively balanced market, characterized by high vitality but severe affordability constraints. The 82.1% owner-occupancy rate and robust 8.31% year-over-year home price appreciation, which outpaces the Massachusetts average of 3.63%, indicate strong demand stability. However, this momentum is counterbalanced by a price-to-income ratio of 9.27, nearly three times the national average of 3.20, signaling significant barriers for local buyers. The market’s classification is further nuanced by a price-to-rent ratio of 76.1, vastly exceeding the national norm of 18.7, suggesting that while owning is prohibitively expensive, renting remains relatively less burdensome compared to the cost of purchase. This divergence highlights a structural disconnect where asset values have decoupled from local earning power, creating a seller’s advantage in the purchase segment but a more neutral dynamic for renters. The multi-year trend reveals a sustained upward trajectory, with median home values climbing from $674,600 in 2017 to $1,165,800 in 2024. This represents a cumulative increase of approximately 72.8% over seven years, with peak growth of 16.4% occurring in 2022 before moderating to 5.6% in 2024. This deceleration aligns with the current 7.40% mortgage rate, which likely suppresses transaction volume despite persistent price increases. Notably, the rent burden stands at only 12.2% of income, well below the national average of 17.2%, indicating that households are not currently overextended in the rental sector. This anomaly suggests that while home prices are rising faster than rents, the absolute cost of renting remains manageable relative to the county’s high median household income of $125,786, which is 85% above the national mean. Regional context underscores the county’s premium status, with home values 83.8% higher than the state average and 412% above the national median. Local economic fundamentals support this premium, evidenced by an average weekly wage of $1,432 and positive net migration of 604 filers, driven by high-income inflows averaging $142,203. Housing supply remains tight, with only 183 new units authorized in 2025, insufficient to offset demand from affluent migrants. The composite assessment reflects high vitality and stability, driven by strong employment and consistent price appreciation, but is heavily penalized on the affordability axis. For prospective participants, the data indicates that while the rental market offers relative value compared to national peers, the purchase market remains exclusive, catering primarily to high-income households rather than local wage earners.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Dukes County is 83.8% above the Massachusetts state average and 412.0% above the U.S. national average. The median rent of $1,277/month is 20.9% below the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 76.1, Dukes County is a strongly rent-favored market. Home prices are high relative to rents, meaning renting is typically far cheaper than owning in the near term.

Dukes County has an owner-occupancy rate of 82.1%, compared to 67.2% in Massachusetts and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more homeowner-heavy market than the U.S. average.
