📝 Local Market Analysis

Dallas County, Arkansas, functions as a distinctively affordable buyer’s market, characterized by a price-to-income ratio of 2.29, significantly below the national average of 3.20. The median home value of $97,300 sits 57.3% below the national benchmark and 31.2% below the Arkansas state average, reflecting a deep discount that persists despite recent price appreciation. This affordability is reinforced by a price-to-rent ratio of 12.9, well under the national 18.7, indicating that purchasing offers a superior value proposition relative to renting. The local economy supports this dynamic through modest employment figures of 2,353 jobs and an average weekly wage of $923, which aligns with the median household income of $42,500. The market exhibits a clear trajectory of rapid appreciation followed by stabilization. Home values surged from $64,500 in 2017 to a peak of $98,200 in 2023, driven by a notable 20.7% year-over-year increase in 2022. However, momentum has cooled, with the 2024 median value declining 0.9% to $97,300. This recent deceleration contrasts with the broader trend of home values rising faster than rents, suggesting a potential correction or plateau in asset pricing. Migration data from 2021-2022 shows a net inflow of 148 filers, with incoming residents averaging an income of $59,764, slightly lower than those leaving at $61,127. This demographic shift indicates that the county is attracting lower-income households, which may constrain further upward pressure on home prices. For prospective buyers, the current environment presents a low entry barrier, with the median gross rent of $627 representing only 17.7% of median income, comparable to the national average of 17.2%. The 72.6% owner-occupancy rate suggests a stable resident base, yet the small population of 6,242 limits local demand elasticity. With the 30-year fixed mortgage rate at 7.40%, the high interest rate environment serves as a significant headwind for new entrants, potentially offsetting the low absolute price points. The divergence between the sharp 2022 price spike and the subsequent 2024 decline highlights the market’s volatility relative to its small size. Ultimately, the combination of low relative pricing, stabilizing values, and modest wage growth positions Dallas County as a low-cost housing option, though the limited job market of 190 establishments may restrict long-term income growth for residents.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Dallas County is 31.2% below the Arkansas state average and 57.3% below the U.S. national average. The median rent of $627/month is 19.0% below the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 12.9, buying a home in Dallas County is generally more favorable than renting for those planning to stay long-term. A ratio below 15 typically signals that purchasing is financially advantageous over a multi-year horizon.

Dallas County has an owner-occupancy rate of 72.6%, compared to 71.1% in Arkansas and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more renter-heavy market than the U.S. average.
