📝 Editor's Insight

Dallas County exhibits a distinct buyer’s market, evidenced by a price-to-rent ratio of 13.1, significantly below the national average of 18.0. This metric indicates that purchasing is substantially more cost-effective than renting relative to local income levels. Over the past five years, median home values surged at an 8.7% compound annual growth rate, rising from $70,400 to $98,200. Conversely, median gross rents declined by 1.8% annually, dropping to $626. This divergence suggests that capital appreciation has outpaced rental yields, likely driven by increased owner demand rather than rental pressure. Affordability remains robust, with a price-to-income ratio of 2.20, well below the 3.09 national benchmark. Additionally, the rent burden sits at 16.8%, marginally lower than the national average of 17.2%. A critical insight for potential residents is that the 71.2% owner-occupancy rate, combined with the rapid value appreciation, implies limited inventory liquidity. Buyers face a tightening market where entry costs are rising quickly, while renters benefit from stable, low-cost housing that does not reflect the broader appreciation trend seen in owned properties.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Dallas County is 31.2% below the Arkansas state average and 57.3% below the U.S. national average. The median rent of $627/month is 19.0% below the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 12.9, buying a home in Dallas County is generally more favorable than renting for those planning to stay long-term. A ratio below 15 typically signals that purchasing is financially advantageous over a multi-year horizon.

Dallas County has an owner-occupancy rate of 72.6%, compared to 71.1% in Arkansas and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more renter-heavy market than the U.S. average.
