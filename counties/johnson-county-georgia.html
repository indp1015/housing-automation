📝 Local Market Notes

Johnson County, Georgia, exhibits characteristics of a balanced market with a slight tilt toward buyers, driven by significant affordability advantages. The price-to-income ratio stands at 1.86, well below the national average of 3.20, while the rent burden is 15.2%, lower than the 17.2% national benchmark. These metrics indicate that housing costs are manageable relative to local earnings, which average $51,028 per household. The price-to-rent ratio of 12.2 is also substantially lower than the national figure of 18.7, suggesting that purchasing offers a more favorable long-term value proposition than renting, although the current 7.40% mortgage rate introduces higher carrying costs for new entrants. The market has experienced robust appreciation, with median home values increasing from $64,200 in 2017 to $94,900 in 2024. This trajectory includes a peak year-over-year growth of 12.2% in 2023, which has since moderated to 4.1% in 2024. This deceleration suggests a stabilization phase following a period of rapid expansion. A notable divergence exists between asset appreciation and income growth, as home values have risen significantly faster than rents, which remain modest at a median of $647 monthly. This dynamic implies that while entry-level costs are low, the pace of equity accumulation has outstripped typical rental yield expectations, potentially attracting investors seeking capital gains rather than cash flow. Regional context highlights Johnson County’s extreme affordability relative to broader averages. Home values are 58.3% below the national mean and 52.1% below the Georgia state average, reflecting its rural character and limited employment base of only 875 jobs. Despite this economic constraint, net migration remains positive, with 148 more filers arriving than departing between 2021 and 2022, indicating sustained demographic interest despite lower average incomes of $46,373 among inflow residents. The 75.9% owner-occupancy rate further underscores the stability of the local housing stock. For potential buyers, the low entry price point combined with moderating growth rates presents a favorable environment for acquisition, provided that local employment limitations are considered. Renters benefit from low absolute costs, though the widening gap between price and rent growth may eventually pressure rental availability as more residents transition to ownership. The data collectively points to a stable, affordable market undergoing a normalization of growth rates after a recent surge.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Johnson County is 52.1% below the Georgia state average and 58.3% below the U.S. national average. The median rent of $647/month is 33.0% below the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 12.2, buying a home in Johnson County is generally more favorable than renting for those planning to stay long-term. A ratio below 15 typically signals that purchasing is financially advantageous over a multi-year horizon.

Johnson County has an owner-occupancy rate of 75.9%, compared to 70.6% in Georgia and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more homeowner-heavy market than the U.S. average.
