📝 Local Market Analysis

Johnson County, Georgia, functions primarily as a buyer’s market, characterized by significant affordability advantages relative to national benchmarks. The median home value of $94,900 sits 58.3% below the national average, while the price-to-income ratio stands at 1.86, nearly half the national mean of 3.20. This deep discount is reinforced by a rent burden of 15.2%, which is slightly below the national average of 17.2%, and a price-to-rent ratio of 12.2, well under the national standard of 18.7. These metrics indicate that housing costs impose minimal financial strain on local households, creating a strong foundation for owner-occupancy, which currently stands at 75.9%. The market exhibits a distinct trend of accelerating home value appreciation that has recently begun to moderate. Home values rose from $64,200 in 2017 to $94,900 in 2024, with the peak year-over-year increase reaching 12.2% in 2023 before slowing to 4.1% in 2024. This growth trajectory outpaces rent increases, suggesting a divergence where asset values are rising faster than immediate housing costs. However, the economic vitality supporting this growth appears limited. With only 875 total jobs and 124 establishments, the local labor market is small, and the average weekly wage of $837 translates to an annual income that aligns with the median household income of $51,028. Net migration of +148 filers indicates modest population growth, but the inflow average income of $46,373 is lower than the outflow average of $47,340, hinting at potential income constraints for new residents. The primary driver of the county’s favorable positioning is affordability, which scores highly despite low vitality metrics. The stability of the market is moderate, reflecting the recent deceleration in price growth from its 2023 peak. The current 30-year fixed mortgage rate of 7.32% adds a layer of complexity, as higher borrowing costs may temper the momentum seen in previous years. For prospective buyers, the low entry price offers a compelling value proposition, particularly given the substantial discount compared to state and national averages. Renters benefit from low cost-of-living pressures, though the widening gap between home price growth and rent growth may eventually impact long-term housing cost dynamics. The data suggests a market where affordability remains the dominant feature, offsetting the limitations of a small local economy and slowing price momentum.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Johnson County is 52.1% below the Georgia state average and 58.3% below the U.S. national average. The median rent of $647/month is 33.0% below the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 12.2, buying a home in Johnson County is generally more favorable than renting for those planning to stay long-term. A ratio below 15 typically signals that purchasing is financially advantageous over a multi-year horizon.

Johnson County has an owner-occupancy rate of 75.9%, compared to 70.6% in Georgia and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more homeowner-heavy market than the U.S. average.
