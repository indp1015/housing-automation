📝 Editor's Insight

Benton County exhibits a balanced market structure, characterized by a price-to-rent ratio of 19.9, slightly above the national average of 18.0. This metric suggests that while ownership costs are elevated, rental demand remains robust, preventing a significant shift toward either extreme. The five-year trend reveals aggressive appreciation, with home values compounding at 11.7% annually compared to a 7.2% rent increase. This divergence indicates that asset values are expanding faster than income-based rental costs, potentially widening the wealth gap between owners and tenants. Affordability metrics align closely with national benchmarks, as the price-to-income ratio stands at 3.17 versus the 3.09 national average, and the rent burden of 15.9% remains below the 17.2% norm. However, the most critical insight lies in the local context: home values are 116.0% higher than the Arkansas state average, while incomes are only 36.2% above the national mean. This disparity signals that Benton County’s premium pricing is driven by localized demand factors rather than broader economic strength, making it significantly less affordable relative to the surrounding state than raw national comparisons suggest.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Benton County is 128.7% above the Arkansas state average and 42.1% above the U.S. national average. The median rent of $1,277/month is 65.0% above the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 21.1, renting is generally more cost-effective than buying in the short to medium term. The market favors buyers only for long-term holds.

Benton County has an owner-occupancy rate of 67.0%, compared to 71.1% in Arkansas and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more renter-heavy market than the U.S. average.
