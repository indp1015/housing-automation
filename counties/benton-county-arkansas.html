📝 Local Market Analysis

Benton County, Arkansas, operates as a distinctively strong renter’s market, characterized by significant pricing power for landlords relative to local income levels. The price-to-rent ratio of 21.1 substantially exceeds the national average of 18.7, indicating that purchasing equity is comparatively less efficient than renting despite high absolute home values. While the median home value of $323,500 sits 42.1% above the national mean, the median household income of $93,506 is only 37.5% higher than the national benchmark, creating a structural affordability gap. This imbalance is further highlighted by a price-to-income ratio of 3.46, which outpaces the national average of 3.20, suggesting that entry-level buyers face greater financial hurdles than their counterparts in other regions. The multi-year trend reveals a period of aggressive appreciation that has recently moderated but remains robust. Home values surged from $163,000 in 2017 to $323,500 in 2024, with a peak year-over-year increase of 20.3% in 2022. Recent FHFA data indicates a continued 4.69% year-over-year gain, outperforming the Arkansas state average of 1.09% by 3.60 percentage points. This sustained momentum contrasts with a rent burden of 16.4%, which is slightly below the national average of 17.2%, suggesting that while asset values are inflating, immediate rental costs remain manageable relative to earnings. The divergence between rapid price appreciation and stable rent burdens points to a market where equity accumulation is accelerating faster than carrying costs, a pattern often seen in high-growth suburban corridors. Economic fundamentals support this vitality, driven by 139,715 total jobs and an average weekly wage of $1,804. Inflowing residents, averaging $169,436 in income, contribute to a net migration gain of 8,950 filers, injecting higher-income demand into the market. However, a 30-year fixed mortgage rate of 7.32% significantly impacts purchasing power, reinforcing the preference for renting among price-sensitive buyers. New housing supply, with 6,307 units authorized in 2025, including 4,023 single-family homes, attempts to meet this demand, yet the high vitality metrics suggest supply is not yet sufficient to cool the market. Consequently, the overall assessment reflects high economic vitality and strong momentum, offset by challenging affordability and lower stability due to rapid price shifts. Potential buyers must account for the premium paid relative to state averages, while renters benefit from a market where income growth is keeping pace with rental obligations.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Benton County is 128.7% above the Arkansas state average and 42.1% above the U.S. national average. The median rent of $1,277/month is 65.0% above the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 21.1, renting is generally more cost-effective than buying in the short to medium term. The market favors buyers only for long-term holds.

Benton County has an owner-occupancy rate of 67.0%, compared to 71.1% in Arkansas and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more renter-heavy market than the U.S. average.
