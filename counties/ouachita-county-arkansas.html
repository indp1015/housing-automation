📝 Editor's Insight

Ouachita County presents a balanced market, though slightly favoring buyers. The price-to-rent ratio of 11.6, significantly below the national average of 18.0, indicates that purchasing is comparatively more economical than renting. This structural advantage is reinforced by a high owner occupancy rate of 71.2%. Over the past five years, home values grew at a 7.2% CAGR, slightly lagging the 8.0% CAGR for rents. This suggests rental demand has been marginally stronger than ownership demand during this period, though both metrics show consistent appreciation. Affordability remains robust relative to national benchmarks. The price-to-income ratio stands at 2.00, well below the national average of 3.09, while the rent burden matches the national mean at 17.2%. However, the county’s median household income of $49,838 is 24.5% lower than the national average. Consequently, while housing costs are low, local wage growth has not kept pace with the broader economy. Prospective residents should note that the low price-to-income ratio masks the fact that local incomes are significantly below national norms, meaning purchasing power is constrained by earnings rather than housing prices.
