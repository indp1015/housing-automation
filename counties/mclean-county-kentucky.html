📝 Local Market Analysis

McLean County, Kentucky, operates as a distinctly buyer’s market, characterized by substantial affordability relative to national benchmarks. The price-to-income ratio stands at 2.26, significantly below the national average of 3.20, while the rent burden is only 11.7% of income compared to the 17.2% national norm. These metrics indicate that housing costs remain manageable for the median household earning $65,596. The owner-occupancy rate of 82.8% further underscores the stability of the local housing stock, suggesting a community rooted in long-term residency rather than transient rental activity. Price trends reveal a period of rapid appreciation followed by stabilization. Median home values surged from $96,800 in 2017 to $148,500 in 2024, with a peak year-over-year increase of 17.0% in 2022. However, growth has moderated to 2.0% in 2024. Recent FHFA data indicates a 9.26% year-over-year increase in 2025, outperforming the Kentucky state average of 4.26% by 5.00 percentage points. This divergence suggests localized demand strength despite the broader national 7.40% mortgage rate environment. Notably, home values are rising faster than rents, which have remained relatively static, creating a price-to-rent ratio of 19.3, slightly above the national average of 18.7. The economic context presents a mixed picture of vitality and supply constraints. With only 1,210 total jobs and an average weekly wage of $918, the local labor market is small but stable. Housing supply is extremely limited, with just two new units authorized in 2025, all multi-family, and zero single-family permits. This scarcity likely supports the recent price momentum. Migration data shows a net gain of 112 filers between 2021 and 2022, with inflow incomes averaging $63,335, slightly below outflow incomes of $64,123, indicating a modest shift in demographic composition. The composite assessment reflects high affordability and momentum scores, driven by low entry costs and strong recent price gains, while vitality remains low due to minimal new construction and a small employment base. For potential buyers, the current environment offers favorable entry points relative to income, though the lack of new supply may limit long-term inventory expansion. Renters face lower cost burdens, but the shifting price-to-rent dynamic suggests increasing pressure on the rental sector as ownership costs rise.

Market Analysis

The median home value in McLean County is 7.7% below the Kentucky state average and 34.8% below the U.S. national average. The median rent of $641/month is 19.2% below the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 19.3, the buy-vs-rent decision in McLean County is balanced. Whether buying or renting makes more sense depends on your time horizon, down payment capacity, and local market conditions.

McLean County has an owner-occupancy rate of 82.8%, compared to 73.4% in Kentucky and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more homeowner-heavy market than the U.S. average.
