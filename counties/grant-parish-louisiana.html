📝 Local Market Analysis

Grant Parish, Louisiana, operates as a distinctively affordable buyer’s market, characterized by a price-to-income ratio of 2.24, significantly below the national average of 3.20. The median home value of $143,300 sits 37.1% below the national mean and 14.4% below the state average, reinforcing its status as a low-cost housing jurisdiction. This affordability is further underscored by a rent burden of 16.9%, which is marginally lower than the national benchmark of 17.2%. However, the market’s structural vitality is constrained by limited economic drivers, with only 1,925 total jobs and an average weekly wage of $1,249. The recent FHFA HPI decline of 14.15% year-over-year, starkly contrasting with the state’s 1.59% growth, signals a sharp correction in recent mortgage-backed transactions. This divergence suggests that while long-term census data shows steady appreciation, current transactional momentum has stalled, creating a complex environment where historical price growth does not align with immediate market velocity. The multi-year trend reveals a volatile but ultimately upward trajectory in home values, rising from $93,600 in 2017 to $143,300 in 2024. This 53% cumulative increase was punctuated by an 18.6% surge in 2020, followed by a moderation to 5.4% growth in 2024. In contrast, the median gross rent of $904 remains relatively stable, resulting in a price-to-rent ratio of 13.2, well below the national average of 18.7. This gap indicates that homeownership remains a more financially accessible option than renting for the local demographic, which is consistent with the high 79.4% owner-occupancy rate. The local economy supports this dynamic through modest net migration of +335 filers, with inflow and outflow incomes nearly identical at approximately $60,000, suggesting that demographic shifts are not driven by significant income differentials. Housing supply remains tight, with only 32 new units authorized in 2025, predominantly single-family homes. This limited construction activity, coupled with a 7.40% national mortgage rate, likely contributes to the recent HPI dip, as higher borrowing costs suppress transaction volume in a market with already low economic vitality. The composite assessment of the market reflects high affordability but low momentum and vitality. While the low price-to-income ratio provides a strong entry point for buyers, the recent negative price momentum and limited job base suggest that value appreciation may be slower compared to broader state and national trends. Prospective stakeholders should note that while entry costs are low, the lack of robust economic growth and recent price corrections indicate a market that prioritizes accessibility over rapid capital appreciation.

The median home value in Grant Parish is 14.4% below the Louisiana state average and 37.1% below the U.S. national average. The median rent of $904/month is 1.1% below the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 13.2, buying a home in Grant Parish is generally more favorable than renting for those planning to stay long-term. A ratio below 15 typically signals that purchasing is financially advantageous over a multi-year horizon.

Grant Parish has an owner-occupancy rate of 79.4%, compared to 72.2% in Louisiana and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more homeowner-heavy market than the U.S. average.
