📝 Editor's Insight

Long County, Georgia, exhibits characteristics of a balanced market, though with distinct affordability advantages relative to national benchmarks. The price-to-income ratio stands at 3.17, marginally below the national average of 3.20, while the rent burden is 17.0%, slightly lower than the national 17.2%. These metrics indicate that both homeownership and renting remain financially accessible compared to broader U.S. standards. The price-to-rent ratio of 18.6 aligns closely with the national average of 18.7, suggesting that the cost of owning versus renting is proportionate, which typically signals a stable, balanced environment where neither side holds a decisive economic advantage. The multi-year trend reveals a significant acceleration in home values, particularly between 2022 and 2024. Median home values surged from $159,900 in 2022 to $218,300 in 2024, representing a cumulative increase of approximately 36.5% over two years. The year-over-year growth peaked at 22.9% in 2023 before moderating to 11.1% in 2024. This rapid appreciation contrasts with the more stable trajectory of median gross rent, which remains at $976 per month. The divergence between sharp price growth and relatively steady rents suggests that the housing stock is tightening, likely driven by limited inventory or increased demand for ownership, pushing asset values higher while rental costs have not yet adjusted proportionally. In regional context, Long County’s median home value of $218,300 sits 10.3% above the Georgia state average, indicating it is a premium market within the state, yet it remains 4.1% below the national average. This positioning reflects a local market that has outpaced state trends but has not yet reached the higher price points seen in major metropolitan hubs nationally. The owner-occupancy rate of 65.2% supports a market structure where a majority of residents own their homes, contributing to local stability. For potential buyers, the recent momentum, evidenced by the 11.1% year-over-year increase, implies that entry costs have risen significantly in the last three years. However, the continued affordability metrics relative to national averages suggest that the market remains viable for those with incomes near the local median of $68,808. Renters may find that while current costs are manageable, the rising home values could eventually influence rental rates as the owner-occupant base expands. The data indicates a market in transition, moving from a low-base period in 2017-2018 to a high-growth phase that is now stabilizing at higher price levels.
