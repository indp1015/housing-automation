📝 Local Market Notes

Sumter County, Alabama, operates as a distinctively affordable buyer’s market, characterized by a median home value of $82,600 and a price-to-income ratio of 2.48, which is significantly below the national average of 3.20. The price-to-rent ratio of 8.9, less than half the national benchmark of 18.7, further underscores the relative economic advantage of homeownership over renting in this jurisdiction. Despite these favorable metrics, the market exhibits volatility, with home values spiking 26.1% in 2022 to $88,000 before moderating to a 1.8% decline in 2024. This recent stabilization follows a period of sharp correction from the 2020 low of $67,300, suggesting a market that has absorbed significant external shocks. The divergence between home value stagnation and the noted trend of rents rising faster than home values indicates a shifting dynamic where rental demand may be outpacing owner-occupancy growth, potentially driven by the high rent burden of 27.9% compared to the national average of 17.2%. The local economic foundation is modest, with total employment limited to 1,877 jobs and an average weekly wage of $906, which constrains broader purchasing power despite the low absolute cost of housing. New housing supply is extremely limited, with only four single-family units authorized in 2025, creating a tight inventory environment that may support price stability even amid declining values. Migration data reveals a net inflow of 131 filers, with incoming households earning an average of $44,481, slightly higher than those leaving, suggesting a slight demographic and economic strengthening. The county’s median household income of $33,310 is 51.0% below the national average, reinforcing that affordability is driven more by low asset prices than robust local earnings. For prospective buyers, the current 7.40% mortgage rate poses a challenge to monthly payment affordability, yet the low entry price point mitigates this risk compared to higher-cost regions. The owner-occupancy rate of 66.8% reflects a community with strong residential ties, though the significant gap between home values and state averages—48.3% below the state median—positions Sumter County as a deep value market. The recent price correction from the 2022 peak suggests that the market has adjusted to higher interest rates, eliminating the speculative bubble that characterized the early 2020s. Consequently, the market presents a stable, low-cost entry point for buyers, while renters face a disproportionate cost burden relative to their incomes. The combination of minimal new supply and steady, albeit low, migration inflows suggests that while prices may not surge, they are unlikely to decline further, maintaining the status quo of extreme affordability relative to national benchmarks.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Sumter County is 48.3% below the Alabama state average and 63.7% below the U.S. national average. The median rent of $775/month is 5.5% below the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 8.9, buying a home in Sumter County is generally more favorable than renting for those planning to stay long-term. A ratio below 15 typically signals that purchasing is financially advantageous over a multi-year horizon.

Sumter County has an owner-occupancy rate of 66.8%, compared to 73.2% in Alabama and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more renter-heavy market than the U.S. average.
