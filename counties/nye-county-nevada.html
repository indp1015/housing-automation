📝 Local Market Analysis

Nye County exhibits characteristics of a stressed renter’s market, driven by severe affordability constraints despite high owner occupancy. The price-to-income ratio stands at 4.79, significantly exceeding the national average of 3.20, while the rent burden consumes 23.3% of median household income compared to the 17.2% national benchmark. Although the price-to-rent ratio of 20.5 is only marginally above the national mean of 18.7, the absolute cost of living relative to local earnings creates substantial financial pressure. This dynamic is compounded by a median household income of $60,714, which trails the national average by 10.7%, limiting purchasing power for both buyers and renters in a market where median home values have surged to $290,900. The multi-year trend reveals a period of rapid appreciation that has recently stalled. Home values increased from $125,100 in 2017 to $290,900 in 2024, with peak year-over-year growth of 19.1% in 2022. However, recent momentum has decelerated sharply, with the FHFA House Price Index showing a negligible 0.03% year-over-year change in 2025. This flat performance contrasts starkly with the state’s 2.67% growth, indicating Nye County is underperforming by 2.64 percentage points. This divergence suggests that the initial speculative or migration-driven price surge has exhausted its immediate upward trajectory, leaving the market in a state of stagnation rather than continued expansion. Regional context highlights a county that is 27.8% more expensive than the national average but 5.1% cheaper than the Nevada state average, reflecting a localized premium likely tied to specific industrial or resource sectors. The local economy supports this with 11,652 jobs and an average weekly wage of $1,438, yet the high mortgage rate of 7.40% further suppresses buyer demand. Migration data shows a net inflow of 2,471 filers, suggesting sustained population growth that may eventually support housing demand, but current affordability metrics remain the primary barrier. The low affordability score of 7.6 is the dominant driver of the overall market assessment, outweighing the relatively stable ownership rate of 77.8% and moderate vitality. For potential residents, the data indicates a market where entry costs are high relative to local wages, and recent price stabilization offers a pause in appreciation but does not resolve the underlying affordability gap.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Nye County is 5.1% below the Nevada state average and 27.8% above the U.S. national average. The median rent of $1,181/month is 1.0% above the state average.

Nye County has an owner-occupancy rate of 77.8%, compared to 73.0% in Nevada and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more homeowner-heavy market than the U.S. average.
