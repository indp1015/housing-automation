📝 Editor's Insight

Colusa County exhibits a distinct buyer’s market dynamic, evidenced by a 62.2% owner-occupancy rate and a price-to-rent ratio of 27.4, significantly exceeding the national average of 18.1. This disparity suggests that purchasing equity is comparatively more advantageous than long-term leasing. Over the past five years, home values surged at a 9.1% CAGR, outpacing the 6.4% rent increase, indicating accelerating asset appreciation relative to rental costs. While the 4.99 price-to-income ratio is elevated compared to the national 3.10, the 18.2% rent burden aligns closely with the 17.2% national benchmark, suggesting rental affordability remains stable despite rising costs. A critical non-obvious insight is the county’s income resilience; with median household income at $75,149, exceeding the national average by 13.3%, local purchasing power mitigates the high price-to-income ratio. Consequently, despite home values being 75.2% above the national mean, the robust income base supports sustained demand, making the market structurally resilient against broader regional affordability pressures.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Colusa County is 34.3% below the California state average and 77.3% above the U.S. national average. The median rent of $1,139/month is 29.6% below the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 27.4, Colusa County is a strongly rent-favored market. Home prices are high relative to rents, meaning renting is typically far cheaper than owning in the near term.

Colusa County has an owner-occupancy rate of 62.2%, compared to 63.6% in California and 73.0% nationally. This indicates a more renter-heavy market than the U.S. average.
