📝 Local Market Analysis

Amador County presents a distinctively unbalanced housing landscape, characterized by severe affordability constraints despite robust economic stability. The market leans heavily toward a renter’s disadvantage, evidenced by a price-to-rent ratio of 26.4, significantly exceeding the national average of 18.7. This disparity suggests that purchasing equity is comparatively more accessible than long-term renting, yet the absolute cost of entry remains prohibitive for many local households. The median home value of $449,800 stands at a 5.11x multiple of the median household income, far surpassing the national benchmark of 3.20. Consequently, the affordability metric is critically low, driving down the overall composite assessment despite high stability scores. Trend analysis reveals a period of accelerated appreciation that has recently moderated. Home values surged 17.2% in 2022 before settling into a more sustainable 6.4% growth rate in 2024. This trajectory contrasts with the state of California, where recent FHFA data indicates a slight decline of 0.29%, highlighting Amador’s relative outperformance of 2.25 percentage points. The divergence between rising home prices and relatively stable rents, with home values rising faster than rental costs, indicates a structural shift in asset valuation. This pattern is supported by positive net migration of 1,125 filers between 2021 and 2022, suggesting sustained demand from incoming residents whose average incomes align closely with local medians. Economic fundamentals provide a mixed backdrop for this appreciation. While the local economy supports 7,474 jobs with an average weekly wage of $1,068, the housing supply is extremely constrained, with only 67 new units authorized in 2025, all of which are single-family. This lack of multifamily development limits the rental inventory, potentially exacerbating rent burdens, which currently sit at 19.4% of income compared to the national 17.2%. The current 30-year mortgage rate of 7.32% further complicates entry for prospective buyers, increasing monthly debt service costs. Ultimately, the market’s vitality is driven by demographic inflows and limited supply, but the affordability gap remains the primary structural barrier. Investors and buyers must recognize that while price momentum is positive relative to the state, the cost of ownership remains significantly elevated compared to national norms, requiring careful financial planning to mitigate the high price-to-income ratio.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Amador County is 26.2% below the California state average and 97.5% above the U.S. national average. The median rent of $1,421/month is 15.9% below the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 26.4, Amador County is a strongly rent-favored market. Home prices are high relative to rents, meaning renting is typically far cheaper than owning in the near term.

Amador County has an owner-occupancy rate of 80.4%, compared to 63.4% in California and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more homeowner-heavy market than the U.S. average.
