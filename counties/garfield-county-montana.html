📝 Local Market Analysis

Garfield County, Montana, functions as a highly affordable buyer’s market, characterized by a price-to-income ratio of 2.44, significantly below the national average of 3.20. The rent burden stands at 12.5% of income, well under the national benchmark of 17.2%, while the price-to-rent ratio of 19.5 suggests that purchasing is marginally more expensive relative to renting than the national norm of 18.7. Despite these strong affordability indicators, the market exhibits low vitality, with a score of just 3.1, reflecting a population of only 1,094 and total employment of merely 170 jobs. This limited economic base constrains local demand, resulting in an overall composite assessment of 45.6/100, classified as stressed due to low stability and vitality despite high affordability. The multi-year trend reveals significant volatility, with home values surging 25.1% in 2020 to $155,300 before moderating. The 2024 median home value of $169,900 represents a 4.7% year-over-year decline, signaling a recent correction after the 2023 peak of $178,300. This downward momentum contrasts with the long-term appreciation from $116,300 in 2017, indicating that the market has not fully stabilized. The 72.2% owner-occupancy rate suggests a preference for homeownership, yet the small population size limits liquidity. Notably, the average weekly wage of $674 translates to an annual income of approximately $35,048, which is substantially lower than the median household income of $69,583, highlighting a disparity between typical local wages and broader household earnings, likely driven by dual-income households or external income sources. Regional context places Garfield County at a distinct disadvantage compared to state and national averages, with home values 38.6% below the Montana average and 25.4% below the national mean. However, median household income is 2.3% above the national average, enhancing relative affordability. Migration data shows zero net movement, with 419 filers entering and 419 exiting, indicating a static population with no demographic growth to drive housing demand. The current 30-year fixed mortgage rate of 7.40% further pressures affordability, although the low home prices mitigate this impact. For potential buyers, the market offers entry-level pricing but lacks the economic dynamism typical of growing regions. Renters benefit from low burdens, but the flat migration and declining price momentum suggest limited appreciation potential. The low stability score of 26.1 underscores the market’s sensitivity to external economic shocks, given the minimal local employment base and lack of demographic inflows.
