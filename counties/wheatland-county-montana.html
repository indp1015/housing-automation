📝 Local Market Analysis

Wheatland County presents a distinct buyer’s market, characterized by a price-to-rent ratio of 20.6, which exceeds the national average of 18.7, indicating that purchasing offers better long-term value than renting. This classification is reinforced by a 75.5% owner occupancy rate and a median home value of $180,800, which sits 20.6% below the national average. The market exhibits strong momentum, evidenced by a cumulative price appreciation of 102.5% from 2017 to 2024, with the peak annual growth of 31.1% occurring in 2022 before moderating to 4.7% in 2024. This recent deceleration suggests the market is stabilizing after a period of rapid expansion, a trend that aligns with the broader national shift toward higher mortgage rates, currently at 7.40%. Affordability remains a significant constraint, with a price-to-income ratio of 3.65, surpassing the national benchmark of 3.20. While the rent burden of 17.7% is marginally higher than the national average of 17.2%, it remains within manageable limits for the local demographic. The county’s economic foundation is fragile, supported by only 411 total jobs and an average weekly wage of $878, which translates to an annual income significantly lower than the national standard. This economic weakness is reflected in the low vitality metrics, where the limited local employment base struggles to support sustained housing demand. Despite this, the county has seen positive net migration of 28 filers between 2021 and 2022, with inflows averaging $49,385 in income, suggesting a steady, albeit modest, influx of residents who may be drawn by the lower cost of living compared to urban centers. The divergence between price and rent growth is a notable pattern, as home values have risen faster than rents, potentially widening the gap between ownership and tenancy costs. This dynamic, combined with the county’s position 34.7% below the state average in home values, indicates that Wheatland is significantly more affordable than its Montana peers. However, the low overall stability and momentum scores highlight the risks associated with the small population of 2,056 and limited economic diversity. For potential buyers, the current environment offers an entry point at prices below state and national averages, but the high mortgage rates and modest income levels necessitate careful financial planning. The market’s trajectory is less driven by robust local economic expansion and more by external factors, such as migration trends and national interest rate environments, which continue to shape the affordability landscape for both buyers and renters in this rural jurisdiction.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Wheatland County is 34.7% below the Montana state average and 20.6% below the U.S. national average. The median rent of $730/month is 13.3% below the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 20.6, renting is generally more cost-effective than buying in the short to medium term. The market favors buyers only for long-term holds.

Wheatland County has an owner-occupancy rate of 75.5%, compared to 73.0% in Montana and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more homeowner-heavy market than the U.S. average.
