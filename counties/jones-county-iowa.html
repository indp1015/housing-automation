📝 Local Market Analysis

Jones County, Iowa, presents a balanced market leaning toward favorable conditions for renters, characterized by strong affordability metrics and moderate price growth. The price-to-income ratio of 2.51 stands significantly below the national average of 3.20, indicating that housing costs are proportionally low relative to local earnings. Furthermore, the rent burden of 12.8% is well under the national benchmark of 17.2%, suggesting that rental housing remains highly accessible for households. While the price-to-rent ratio of 19.6 slightly exceeds the national average of 18.7, the overall cost structure remains competitive, particularly when compared to the county’s median home value of $184,800, which is 18.8% below the national mean. Recent market dynamics reveal a distinct shift in momentum after a period of rapid appreciation. Home values surged by 12.8% in 2022 and 11.0% in 2023, but this growth has decelerated sharply to just 0.8% in 2024. This stabilization is corroborated by the FHFA House Price Index, which shows a year-over-year increase of 3.11%, outperforming the Iowa state average of 2.25% by 0.86 percentage points. This divergence suggests that while the county is still gaining value, the pace has normalized from the previous two-year spike. The local economy supports this stability, with a median household income of $73,531, which is 8.1% above the national average, providing a solid foundation for purchasing power despite the 7.40% national mortgage rate. Supply constraints and demographic trends further shape the local landscape. With only 18 new single-family units authorized in 2025 and zero multi-family permits, new supply is minimal, limiting the potential for significant price declines. However, net migration of +277 filers between 2021 and 2022 indicates a modest population influx, with inflow and outflow incomes nearly identical at approximately $76,000. This suggests that demographic shifts are not driving disproportionate price inflation. For potential buyers, the combination of high owner occupancy at 80.6% and low price-to-income ratios offers a stable entry point, while renters benefit from low financial burdens. The market’s outperformance relative to the state average, coupled with average weekly wages of $1,006, indicates a resilient local economy that supports current housing valuations without exhibiting signs of overheating.
