📝 Editor's Insight

Duval County, Florida, exhibits characteristics of a balanced market with a slight lean toward renters, defined by a price-to-rent ratio of 17.1, which is below the national average of 18.7. This metric suggests that the cost of purchasing a home is relatively moderate compared to the cost of renting, providing a structural advantage for buyers over the long term. However, the owner-occupancy rate of 58.2% indicates a significant rental population, reflecting the high demand for leasehold housing in a metro area with a population exceeding one million. The market classification is further nuanced by the fact that while home values are rising faster than rents, the absolute affordability barriers remain substantial for the median household. The multi-year trend reveals a period of aggressive appreciation, with median home values increasing from $156,200 in 2017 to $303,500 in 2024. This represents a cumulative gain of approximately 94.3% over seven years. The pace of growth peaked at 19.0% year-over-year in 2022 before moderating to 10.4% in 2024, indicating a stabilization in the rate of price acceleration. Despite this moderation, the recent FHFA House Price Index data for 2025 shows a county year-over-year change of only +0.26%, which is a significant deceleration from previous years. This recent momentum places the county underperforming relative to the Florida state average of +0.91%, suggesting that local price growth has temporarily stalled or normalized compared to the broader state market. Affordability metrics highlight distinct pressures on both buyers and renters. The price-to-income ratio stands at 4.26, significantly higher than the national average of 3.20, indicating that the median home is less affordable relative to local incomes than the typical U.S. home. Similarly, the rent burden is 24.8% of income, exceeding the national benchmark of 17.2%. Although home values are 6.7% above the state average and 33.3% above the national average, median household income is only 4.8% above the national mean, creating a widening gap between asset prices and earning power. A notable pattern is the divergence between the long-term high growth rates and the near-zero recent HPI growth, signaling a potential cooling phase. For potential buyers, the lower price-to-rent ratio offers a favorable entry point relative to rental costs, but the high price-to-income ratio requires careful budgeting. Renters face a heavy financial burden, with nearly a quarter of income dedicated to housing, necessitating strict cost management.
