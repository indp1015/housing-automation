📝 Local Market Analysis

Duval County, Florida, presents a complex housing landscape characterized by significant affordability stress despite robust economic vitality. The market leans toward a renter’s advantage in relative terms, evidenced by a price-to-rent ratio of 17.1, which sits below the national average of 18.7. However, this metric masks severe cost pressures for homeowners. The price-to-income ratio stands at 4.26, substantially higher than the national benchmark of 3.20, indicating that median home values are disproportionately elevated relative to local earning power. This disparity is further highlighted by a rent burden of 24.8% of income, compared to the national average of 17.2%, suggesting that both buyers and renters face significant financial strain despite the county’s median household income of $71,277. The multi-year trend reveals a dramatic acceleration in home values, with year-over-year growth spiking to 19.0% in 2022 before moderating to 10.4% in 2024. This surge has outpaced rent growth, creating a divergence where ownership costs escalate faster than rental alternatives. Recent FHFA HPI data shows a stark contrast to this historical momentum, with a mere 0.26% year-over-year increase in 2025, underperforming the Florida state average of 0.91% by 0.65 percentage points. This recent stagnation suggests a cooling of the rapid appreciation seen in prior years. Economically, the county supports 508,794 jobs with an average weekly wage of $1,446, and net migration of 16,305 filers between 2021 and 2022 indicates sustained population inflow. New construction permits for 6,017 units in 2025, split between 3,732 single-family and 2,285 multi-family homes, aim to address supply constraints, though the impact remains to be seen against the backdrop of a 7.32% national mortgage rate. The overall market assessment reflects a tension between high vitality and low stability. While the county’s home values exceed the national average by 33.3% and the state average by 6.7%, the affordability score of 9.2/100 dominates the composite view, dragging down the overall rating to 31.9/100. The stability component, rated at 30.4/100, likely reflects the volatility of recent price swings and the current high interest rate environment. Conversely, the vitality score of 65.3/100 underscores the strength of the local labor market and demographic growth. For potential stakeholders, the data suggests that while the economic engine remains strong, the entry barrier for homeownership is significantly higher than in comparable national markets, necessitating careful financial planning for both buyers and long-term renters.
