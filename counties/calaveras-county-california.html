📝 Editor's Insight

Calaveras County exhibits a strong owner-occupancy dominance, with 84.4% of residents owning their homes, suggesting a stable, long-term market rather than a transient rental environment. The price-to-rent ratio of 22.8, significantly above the national average of 18.1, indicates that purchasing is relatively more attractive than renting for those who can afford entry costs. Over the past five years, home values expanded at an 8.4% CAGR, substantially outpacing the 4.8% CAGR in rents. This divergence signals that capital appreciation has driven the market more aggressively than rental demand. However, affordability remains constrained; the price-to-income ratio of 5.53 is nearly double the national benchmark of 3.10, and the rent burden of 24.3% exceeds the national average of 17.2%. A critical insight for potential residents is that while the county sits 22.6% below the state median home value, this discount is eroded by the high price-to-income multiple. Consequently, the lower absolute price does not equate to relative affordability when compared against local earning power, requiring buyers to secure incomes well above the median to maintain standard debt-to-income ratios.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Calaveras County is 22.6% below the California state average and 108.9% above the U.S. national average. The median rent of $1,615/month is about equal to the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 22.8, renting is generally more cost-effective than buying in the short to medium term. The market favors buyers only for long-term holds.

Calaveras County has an owner-occupancy rate of 84.4%, compared to 63.6% in California and 73.0% nationally. This indicates a more homeowner-heavy market than the U.S. average.
