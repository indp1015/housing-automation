📝 Local Market Analysis

Calaveras County functions as a renter’s market, characterized by an extreme affordability deficit despite high owner occupancy. The price-to-income ratio stands at 5.81, nearly double the national average of 3.20, while the rent burden consumes 24.8% of median income compared to the national benchmark of 17.2%. These metrics indicate that purchasing power is significantly constrained relative to asset values. The market classification is further supported by a price-to-rent ratio of 23.4, exceeding the national average of 18.7, suggesting that rental yields are low relative to property costs, which discourages investor entry and limits housing supply elasticity. Trend data reveals a period of rapid appreciation followed by stabilization. Home values surged 14.0% year-over-year in 2022 before moderating to 3.5% in 2024. Recent FHFA HPI data indicates a 0.96% year-over-year decline in 2025, underperforming the state by 0.67 percentage points. This recent softening contrasts with the long-term upward trajectory, where median home values rose from $273,400 in 2017 to $457,200 in 2024. A notable divergence exists between asset appreciation and rental income growth; while home values have increased substantially, median gross rent remains at $1,625, implying that equity gains are outpacing rental income potential. This pattern suggests that the primary value driver is speculative or equity-based rather than income-based. Regional context places Calaveras County 25.0% below the state average home value but 100.8% above the national average, reflecting its position as a more affordable alternative within California, yet still expensive nationally. The local economy supports 7,113 jobs with an average weekly wage of $1,045, which struggles to keep pace with the $457,200 median home price. Housing supply remains tight, with only 72 new single-family units authorized in 2025 and zero multi-family permits, limiting new inventory. Migration data shows a net inflow of 1,201 filers between 2021 and 2022, with incoming residents earning slightly higher incomes ($87,196) than those leaving ($86,172), potentially sustaining demand. With mortgage rates at 7.40%, the cost of financing exacerbates the affordability gap. Consequently, the market exhibits high stability due to low volatility in ownership rates, but low momentum and vitality driven by suppressed purchasing power and limited new construction. Potential buyers face significant entry barriers, while renters encounter high relative costs, creating a dual-pressure environment where neither group enjoys significant financial advantage.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Calaveras County is 25.0% below the California state average and 100.8% above the U.S. national average. The median rent of $1,625/month is 3.8% below the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 23.4, renting is generally more cost-effective than buying in the short to medium term. The market favors buyers only for long-term holds.

Calaveras County has an owner-occupancy rate of 84.0%, compared to 63.4% in California and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more homeowner-heavy market than the U.S. average.
