📝 Editor's Insight

Yuma County exhibits a balanced market structure, evidenced by a price-to-rent ratio of 16.6, which falls below the national average of 18.0. This metric suggests that renting remains a competitive alternative to buying, preventing extreme buyer dominance. The five-year trend reveals a significant divergence in asset growth, with home values compounding at 11.2% annually compared to a 4.0% increase in rents. This rapid appreciation has narrowed the affordability gap, driving the price-to-income ratio to 3.24, slightly above the national benchmark of 3.09. Consequently, the rent burden sits at 19.5%, exceeding the national average of 17.2%. A critical insight emerges from the owner occupancy rate of 70.0%, which is notably high for a region where home values are 19.6% below the state average. This indicates strong local equity accumulation despite lower absolute prices. For potential residents, the data suggests that while entry costs are lower than statewide averages, the velocity of appreciation has made the transition from renting to owning increasingly urgent to capture existing equity, as the cost of entry has risen sharply relative to static income levels.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Yuma County is 18.0% below the Arizona state average and 4.4% below the U.S. national average. The median rent of $993/month is 7.5% below the state average.
