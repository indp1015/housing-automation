📝 Local Market Analysis

Johnson County, Arkansas, functions as a distinctly buyer’s market, characterized by structural affordability advantages that diverge from national norms. The price-to-income ratio stands at 2.94, significantly below the national average of 3.20, while the price-to-rent ratio of 15.5 remains well under the 18.7 national benchmark. These metrics indicate that purchasing equity is comparatively inexpensive relative to local earnings and rental costs. However, the rent burden reaches 19.0% of median household income, slightly exceeding the 17.2% national average, suggesting that while entry-level homeownership is accessible, rental housing imposes a slightly higher relative financial strain on tenants. Trend data reveals a period of accelerated appreciation rather than stagnation. Median home values increased from $95,400 in 2017 to $135,300 in 2024, representing a cumulative gain of approximately 41.8%. This growth was punctuated by a sharp 10.6% surge in 2022 and an 8.4% rise in 2024, outpacing the modest 0.7% increase seen in 2021. Despite this upward trajectory, the FHFA HPI indicates a recent cooling, with a mere 0.30% year-over-year change in 2025, underperforming the Arkansas state average of 1.09%. This divergence suggests that the rapid appreciation cycle is stabilizing, with price growth decelerating as the market finds a new equilibrium. The regional context highlights a market that is undervalued relative to both state and national averages, sitting 4.4% below the state median and 40.6% below the national median. Economic fundamentals provide modest support, with a median household income of $45,995 and average weekly wages of $834. Housing supply remains extremely constrained, evidenced by only 13 new building permits authorized in 2025, all for single-family units. This scarcity, combined with positive net migration of 566 filers, sustains demand pressure. The low vitality score reflects the limited economic base, with only 6,669 total jobs and 486 establishments. Consequently, while the market offers strong entry-level affordability and stable ownership rates at 74.1%, the limited job market and low construction activity cap long-term growth potential. Potential buyers benefit from current price-to-income advantages, whereas renters face slightly elevated cost burdens relative to their incomes.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Johnson County is 4.4% below the Arkansas state average and 40.6% below the U.S. national average. The median rent of $728/month is 5.9% below the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 15.5, the buy-vs-rent decision in Johnson County is balanced. Whether buying or renting makes more sense depends on your time horizon, down payment capacity, and local market conditions.

Johnson County has an owner-occupancy rate of 74.1%, compared to 71.1% in Arkansas and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more homeowner-heavy market than the U.S. average.
