📝 Editor's Insight

Johnson County operates as a distinct buyer’s market, evidenced by a price-to-rent ratio of 14.6, significantly below the national average of 18.0. This metric indicates that purchasing is substantially more cost-effective than renting for long-term residents. Over the past five years, home values appreciated at a 4.8% CAGR, outpacing the 3.6% CAGR for gross rents. This divergence suggests that equity accumulation is accelerating relative to rental costs, further favoring ownership. Affordability remains robust compared to national benchmarks; the price-to-income ratio stands at 2.79, well below the 3.09 national average. However, the rent burden is slightly elevated at 19.1%, exceeding the 17.2% national norm. A critical, non-obvious insight is that while the county is 41.3% cheaper than the national average, it is only 5.4% below the Arkansas state average. This proximity to the state mean implies that Johnson County offers near-statewide pricing without the typical rural isolation, positioning it as a value-oriented entry point for those seeking affordability within the broader Arkansas economic landscape.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Johnson County is 4.4% below the Arkansas state average and 40.6% below the U.S. national average. The median rent of $728/month is 5.9% below the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 15.5, the buy-vs-rent decision in Johnson County is balanced. Whether buying or renting makes more sense depends on your time horizon, down payment capacity, and local market conditions.

Johnson County has an owner-occupancy rate of 74.1%, compared to 71.1% in Arkansas and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more homeowner-heavy market than the U.S. average.
