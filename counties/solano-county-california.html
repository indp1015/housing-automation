📝 Editor's Insight

Solano County operates as a distinct renter’s market, evidenced by a price-to-rent ratio of 23.5, significantly exceeding the national average of 18.1. This metric indicates that purchasing is less economically efficient than renting relative to local income levels. The five-year trend reveals aggressive capital appreciation, with home values compounding at 9.7% annually compared to a 7.0% rent increase. This divergence suggests supply constraints are driving asset price inflation faster than rental demand adjustments. Affordability remains strained; the price-to-income ratio of 5.90 is nearly double the national benchmark of 3.10, while the 25.1% rent burden exceeds the 17.2% national average. Although median household income of $99,994 is 50.8% above the national mean, it fails to offset the 175.3% premium on home values. A critical insight for potential residents is that despite higher-than-average incomes, the disparity between asset growth and rental costs implies that long-term equity accumulation through ownership faces significant entry barriers, making immediate purchasing less accessible than the broader state average suggests.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Solano County is 3.3% above the California state average and 178.7% above the U.S. national average. The median rent of $2,088/month is 29.0% above the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 23.5, renting is generally more cost-effective than buying in the short to medium term. The market favors buyers only for long-term holds.

Solano County has an owner-occupancy rate of 63.2%, compared to 63.6% in California and 73.0% nationally. This indicates a more renter-heavy market than the U.S. average.
