📝 Local Market Analysis

Kalkaska County presents a balanced market leaning toward affordability, characterized by a price-to-income ratio of 2.75, which is significantly lower than the national average of 3.20. The rent burden stands at 14.9% of income, also below the national benchmark of 17.2%, indicating that both ownership and rental costs remain manageable relative to local earnings. With an owner-occupancy rate of 88.2% and a median home value of $188,900, the market is firmly entrenched in the owner-occupied segment. The price-to-rent ratio of 18.5 aligns closely with the national average of 18.7, suggesting no significant arbitrage opportunity exists between buying and renting. This equilibrium is further supported by the fact that home values are rising faster than rents, a trend that typically favors prospective buyers over long-term renters who face increasing opportunity costs. The multi-year trend reveals a period of sustained appreciation, with home values increasing from $105,300 in 2017 to $188,900 in 2024. This represents a cumulative gain of approximately 79.4% over seven years, with peak annual growth of 14.1% in 2023 moderating to 8.4% in 2024. Recent FHFA data indicates a year-over-year increase of 12.74%, outperforming the Michigan state average of 3.68% by 9.06 percentage points. This divergence highlights a localized momentum that exceeds broader state trends. However, this rapid appreciation contrasts with the limited housing supply, as only 38 new single-family units were authorized in 2025, with zero multi-family permits. This constrained supply, combined with a net migration inflow of 572 filers, likely contributes to the sustained upward pressure on prices despite the small population of 18,239. Economic fundamentals provide a mixed backdrop for this growth. While the average weekly wage of $1,275 supports the median household income of $68,578, the total employment base of 3,702 jobs across 378 establishments suggests a limited labor market. The high stability of the market is reflected in the consistent, albeit slowing, price appreciation and the low volatility of the rent burden. The affordability advantage remains the primary driver of the market’s strength, as the county’s home values are 17.0% below the national average and 4.3% below the state average. For potential buyers, the current environment offers entry points below national norms, though the 7.40% mortgage rate impacts monthly carrying costs. The data suggests that while momentum remains positive, the pace of appreciation is normalizing, and the lack of new multi-family construction may limit rental options for those preferring to lease.
