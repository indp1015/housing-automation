📝 Local Market Analysis

Stone County, Arkansas, functions as a moderately balanced market with distinct affordability pressures relative to local incomes. The price-to-income ratio stands at 4.19, significantly exceeding the national average of 3.20, indicating that home purchase costs are disproportionately high for residents. Conversely, the rent burden is 18.3% of income, only marginally above the national benchmark of 17.2%. This divergence suggests that while renting remains relatively accessible, homeownership is strained by elevated property values compared to earning potential. The market is not strictly a buyer’s or renter’s market but rather one where entry-level purchasing power is constrained by income levels rather than absolute price points. Recent trends reveal volatile price appreciation that has outpaced rental growth. Home values surged 26.4% in 2022 and continued rising 14.9% in 2024, reaching a median of $171,600. This trajectory contrasts sharply with the modest 18.3% rent burden, implying that equity accumulation is outstripping rental cost increases. The price-to-rent ratio of 22.8, well above the national average of 18.7, further highlights that owning is becoming more expensive relative to renting on a cash-flow basis. This pattern suggests a market adjusting to higher valuations without a corresponding surge in rental costs, potentially driven by limited local supply or external demand factors rather than broad-based local income growth. Regional context underscores Stone County’s unique position within Arkansas. Median home values are 21.3% above the state average, yet median household income is 39.8% below the national mean. This disparity creates a specific affordability gap not seen in more economically robust areas. Economic fundamentals show limited local job growth, with only 2,119 total jobs and an average weekly wage of $725. However, net migration of +476 filers between 2021 and 2022, with inflowing residents earning slightly more ($49,536) than outflowing residents ($48,978), indicates a modest influx of higher-income households potentially driving price appreciation. With 30-year mortgage rates at 7.40%, the high price-to-income ratio amplifies the cost of debt service. Potential buyers must account for the significant gap between local wages and home prices, while renters face stable but slightly above-average cost burdens. The market’s momentum is driven by migration and price appreciation rather than robust local employment expansion.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Stone County is 21.3% above the Arkansas state average and 24.6% below the U.S. national average. The median rent of $626/month is 19.1% below the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 22.8, renting is generally more cost-effective than buying in the short to medium term. The market favors buyers only for long-term holds.

Stone County has an owner-occupancy rate of 75.9%, compared to 71.1% in Arkansas and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more homeowner-heavy market than the U.S. average.
