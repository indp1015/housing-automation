📝 Local Market Analysis

Sheridan County, Kansas, operates as a distinctively affordable market relative to national benchmarks, characterized by a price-to-income ratio of 2.27, which is significantly lower than the national average of 3.20. Despite this affordability, the local market exhibits strong upward momentum, with median home values rising from $101,000 in 2017 to $167,200 in 2024. This trajectory represents a cumulative increase of approximately 65.5% over seven years, accelerating notably in the last three years with year-over-year gains of 11.4% in 2022, 12.7% in 2023, and 10.0% in 2024. The rapid appreciation contrasts with the relatively stable median gross rent of $689, resulting in a price-to-rent ratio of 20.2, slightly above the national average of 18.7. This divergence suggests that ownership costs are rising faster than rental alternatives, potentially narrowing the traditional cost advantage of renting in this small rural economy. The county’s economic fundamentals support this housing demand, evidenced by a median household income of $73,750, which exceeds the national average by 8.5%. However, the local labor market is constrained, with only 605 total jobs and 101 establishments, indicating a limited local employment base. Migration data reveals a net inflow of 39 filers between 2021 and 2022, with incoming residents averaging an income of $77,795, suggesting that new entrants possess financial capacity comparable to or slightly higher than the existing population. The owner occupancy rate stands at 76.8%, indicating a stable residential base. While the current 30-year fixed mortgage rate of 7.40% increases monthly payment burdens, the low entry price point mitigates this impact compared to higher-cost regions. Regionally, Sheridan County presents a complex positioning. Home values are 26.6% below the national average but 20.4% above the Kansas state average, reflecting a localized premium likely driven by specific community amenities or lifestyle factors rather than broad economic superiority. The rent burden is exceptionally low at 11.2% of income, far below the national 17.2% benchmark, highlighting the ease of housing access for renters. For potential buyers, the primary consideration is the recent acceleration in price growth relative to income stability. The combination of high owner occupancy and rising values suggests a tight supply environment. Investors and buyers must weigh the long-term affordability against the short-term velocity of price appreciation, particularly given the limited local job growth that may cap future income-driven demand. The market remains fundamentally sound due to low debt-to-income ratios, but the rapid recent gains warrant careful valuation analysis to avoid overpaying relative to local income fundamentals.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Sheridan County is 20.4% above the Kansas state average and 26.6% below the U.S. national average. The median rent of $689/month is 14.1% below the state average.
