📝 Local Market Analysis

Delta County, Colorado, functions as a distinctly buyer’s market, characterized by a price-to-rent ratio of 25.5, which significantly exceeds the national average of 18.7. This metric suggests that purchasing offers a more favorable long-term value proposition compared to renting, despite the high entry cost. The affordability profile is severely constrained, with a price-to-income ratio of 5.94, nearly double the national benchmark of 3.20. Consequently, the median household income of $57,774 supports only a limited portion of the median home value of $343,200, creating a structural barrier for local wage earners. The multi-year trend reveals sustained appreciation, with home values increasing from $206,200 in 2017 to $343,200 in 2024, representing a cumulative gain of approximately 66%. However, recent momentum has stalled; the FHFA HPI indicates a year-over-year decline of 0.61% in 2025, mirroring the state’s performance. This recent cooling contrasts with the robust 7.9% growth observed in 2024, suggesting a potential correction or stabilization phase. Notably, home values are rising faster than rents, a divergence that may indicate an imbalance in the rental sector or a shift in investor behavior. The local economy supports this dynamic through modest employment growth, with 6,122 jobs and an average weekly wage of $888, while net migration of +1,214 filers between 2021 and 2022 signals continued demographic inflow. Regional context further highlights the market’s unique position. While Delta County’s home values are 19.7% below the Colorado state average, they remain 50.7% above the national mean, reflecting the premium associated with the state’s housing market. The limited housing supply, evidenced by only 58 new units authorized in 2025, primarily single-family structures, likely contributes to persistent price pressure despite the recent HPI decline. The composite assessment underscores a stability score of 79.9, driven by consistent historical appreciation, but this is offset by a critically low affordability score of 4.6. For prospective buyers, the current environment presents a complex trade-off: while recent price corrections may offer slightly better entry points, the high price-to-income ratio and 7.32% mortgage rates maintain significant financial hurdles. Renter’s burden stands at 23.3% of income, well above the national average of 17.2%, indicating that the rental market remains expensive relative to local earnings. The data suggests that while the market is stabilizing from its rapid 2023-2024 growth, fundamental affordability challenges persist, limiting accessibility for lower-income households.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Delta County is 19.7% below the Colorado state average and 50.7% above the U.S. national average. The median rent of $1,121/month is 13.9% below the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 25.5, Delta County is a strongly rent-favored market. Home prices are high relative to rents, meaning renting is typically far cheaper than owning in the near term.

Delta County has an owner-occupancy rate of 77.9%, compared to 71.9% in Colorado and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more homeowner-heavy market than the U.S. average.
