📝 Local Market Analysis

Mahaska County, Iowa, functions as a distinct buyer’s market, characterized by significant affordability advantages relative to national benchmarks. The price-to-income ratio stands at 2.22, substantially below the national average of 3.20, while the rent burden is only 13.4% of median household income compared to the national 17.2%. This affordability is reinforced by a price-to-rent ratio of 16.6, which is lower than the national 18.7, indicating that purchasing is economically more favorable than renting. With a median home value of $157,800, the market sits 30.7% below the national average and 7.7% below the Iowa state average, despite median household income of $70,940 exceeding the national mean by 4.3%. Recent market dynamics reveal a pronounced acceleration in home values, with year-over-year growth jumping from 4.3% in 2023 to 12.0% in 2024. This surge follows a period of moderate stability and a brief dip in 2020, suggesting a shift from equilibrium to rapid appreciation. However, the FHFA House Price Index indicates a recent deceleration, with the county’s 2025 year-over-year change at +2.23%, tracking almost identically to the state average of +2.25%. This divergence between the sharp 2024 Census-based increase and the slower FHFA momentum suggests that the most recent quarterly transaction data reflects a normalization of price growth after a period of intense demand. The trend signal that home values are rising faster than rents further supports the structural shift toward ownership. The local economic environment provides modest support for this housing activity. Total employment stands at 6,531 jobs with an average weekly wage of $1,107, while net migration added 356 filers between 2021 and 2022, indicating a slight population influx. Housing supply remains constrained, with only 66 new units authorized in 2025, including just 12 single-family homes, which may contribute to sustained price pressure. The national 30-year fixed mortgage rate of 7.40% imposes a higher cost of entry, yet the low price-to-income ratio mitigates this burden for local buyers. Consequently, the market presents a strong value proposition for buyers, while renters face relatively low financial strain, though the recent acceleration in home prices may limit long-term affordability for lower-income households if the trend persists.
