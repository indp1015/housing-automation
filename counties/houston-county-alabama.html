📝 Local Market Analysis

Houston County, Alabama, operates as a relatively balanced market with distinct affordability advantages for owners compared to the national average. The price-to-income ratio of 3.13 sits slightly below the national benchmark of 3.20, indicating that median household incomes of $58,626 align closely with the median home value of $183,300. However, the rent burden of 19.3% exceeds the national average of 17.2%, suggesting that renting is proportionally more expensive relative to local earnings than buying. This dynamic is reinforced by a price-to-rent ratio of 16.2, which is significantly lower than the national average of 18.7, further favoring homeownership over long-term tenancy for those with stable income. The market has experienced a pronounced acceleration in home values, peaking at 12.7% year-over-year growth in 2022 before moderating to 3.0% in 2024. Recent FHFA data shows the index at 197.21, with a current year-over-year change of 2.82%, tracking almost perfectly with the state average of 2.79%. This stabilization follows a period where home values rose faster than rents, creating a divergence that has widened the gap between ownership and rental costs. While the county’s median home value is 19.5% below the national average, it commands a 14.8% premium over the state average, reflecting stronger local demand relative to broader Alabama trends. Economic fundamentals support this sustained interest, driven by a net migration inflow of 2,622 filers between 2021 and 2022. The local labor market, comprising 42,426 jobs with an average weekly wage of $1,054, provides a stable income base that supports the 63.8% owner occupancy rate. Housing supply remains robust, with 470 new units authorized in 2025, including 334 single-family homes, which helps mitigate potential price spikes. However, the current 30-year fixed mortgage rate of 7.40% introduces significant monthly payment pressure, requiring buyers to carefully assess debt-to-income ratios. For potential entrants, the data suggests that while entry costs are lower than national norms, the high rent burden and elevated mortgage rates necessitate a focus on long-term equity accumulation rather than short-term cost savings. The market’s trajectory indicates a cooling but still appreciating environment, where the premium on local housing values relative to the state average continues to solidify.
