📝 Local Market Analysis

Howell County, Missouri, exhibits characteristics of a balanced market leaning slightly toward buyers, driven by a price-to-rent ratio of 19.7, which is only marginally above the national average of 18.7. The median home value of $173,000 sits 24.0% below the national mean, reflecting a rural economic profile where affordability remains a primary driver. With an owner-occupancy rate of 70.3%, the local housing stock supports stable residential tenure, though the moderate affordability score suggests that rising prices are beginning to outpace local income growth. The market’s momentum is evident in the FHFA Home Price Index, which recorded a 5.28% year-over-year increase, outperforming the state average of 3.71% by 1.57 percentage points. This relative strength indicates that Howell County is experiencing stronger demand dynamics than the broader Missouri housing landscape, likely fueled by consistent net migration of 1,177 filers between 2021 and 2022. The multi-year trend reveals a significant acceleration in home values, with year-over-year growth peaking at 17.2% in 2022 before moderating to 7.5% in 2024. This trajectory contrasts with the rent burden, which stands at 17.2% of median income, precisely matching the national average. While home prices have surged from $102,200 in 2017 to $173,000 in 2024, the price-to-income ratio of 3.39 remains only slightly elevated compared to the national benchmark of 3.20. This suggests that while asset values are appreciating rapidly, the fundamental income base, with a median household income of $50,993, has not kept pace with the 17.2% peak growth seen in earlier years. The local economy, supported by 13,125 jobs and an average weekly wage of $886, provides a stable foundation, yet the low housing supply of 67 new units authorized in 2025 limits the ability to meet surging demand. The current market dynamic is shaped by a divergence between strong price momentum and constrained affordability. The 7.40% national mortgage rate adds friction for prospective buyers, yet the county’s outperformance relative to state trends suggests robust local demand. The editorial assessment of the market reflects this tension: high momentum scores are offset by lower stability and vitality metrics, as the rapid appreciation outstrips the slower growth in local wages. For stakeholders, the data indicates a market where equity gains are significant for existing owners, but entry barriers for new buyers are rising due to the combination of high interest rates and accelerating home values. The consistent net inflow of residents with average incomes near $56,600 further supports sustained demand, ensuring that the housing market remains active despite the broader economic headwinds.
