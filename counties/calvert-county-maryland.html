📝 Local Market Analysis

Calvert County operates as a balanced market, characterized by strong price momentum tempered by moderate affordability constraints. The composite assessment reflects this equilibrium, where high stability and robust momentum are offset by lower vitality and affordability metrics. With a price-to-income ratio of 3.44, the county exceeds the national average of 3.20, indicating that median home values are slightly more expensive relative to local earnings than the broader U.S. market. However, the rent burden stands at 15.8% of income, which is below the national average of 17.2%, suggesting that rental housing remains comparatively accessible despite rising property values. This divergence highlights a market where ownership costs are tightening while rental costs remain manageable, creating a bifurcated pressure on household budgets. The multi-year trend reveals a significant acceleration in home values, which surged 11.9% in 2022 before moderating to 4.5% in 2024. This trajectory contrasts sharply with the state’s recent 0.95% year-over-year growth, as the county’s FHFA HPI indicates a 4.00% increase, outperforming the state by 3.05 percentage points. This sustained outperformance is driven by consistent net migration of 2,241 filers and an average weekly wage of $1,150, which supports demand despite a limited housing supply of only 157 new units authorized in 2025. The price-to-rent ratio of 21.8, well above the national average of 18.7, further underscores that home values are appreciating faster than rental rates, a pattern often associated with strong underlying demand and constrained inventory. Regional context places Calvert County at a premium, with median home values 24.4% above the state average and 102.1% above the national mean. This premium is supported by a median household income of $133,922, which is 96.9% higher than the national average, ensuring that local purchasing power aligns with local price levels. The current 7.40% mortgage rate adds friction to entry-level purchases, yet the high owner-occupancy rate of 87.2% suggests entrenched equity and low turnover. For stakeholders, the data indicates a resilient market where investment value is protected by consistent appreciation, though entry barriers are rising. The combination of high stability and positive momentum suggests that while immediate affordability is challenging, the long-term trajectory remains supported by demographic inflows and economic fundamentals, distinguishing it from volatile or declining markets.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Calvert County is 24.4% above the Maryland state average and 102.1% above the U.S. national average. The median rent of $1,759/month is 20.6% above the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 21.8, renting is generally more cost-effective than buying in the short to medium term. The market favors buyers only for long-term holds.

Calvert County has an owner-occupancy rate of 87.2%, compared to 72.7% in Maryland and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more homeowner-heavy market than the U.S. average.
