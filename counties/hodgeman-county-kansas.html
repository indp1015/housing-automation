📝 Local Market Analysis

Hodgeman County, Kansas, functions as a distinctively affordable buyer’s market, characterized by a price-to-income ratio of 1.69, which is less than half the national average of 3.20. The median home value of $101,600 sits 55.4% below the national mean and 26.8% below the Kansas state average. This affordability is reinforced by a price-to-rent ratio of 9.0, significantly lower than the national benchmark of 18.7, indicating that purchasing remains substantially cheaper than renting over time. However, the market exhibits unusual volatility, with median home values swinging from a low of $76,800 in 2021 to the current $101,600, reflecting a 32.3% increase over two years. This recent upward momentum contrasts with the broader trend signal that rents are rising faster than home values, suggesting a shifting dynamic in local housing demand. The local economic foundation is fragile, with only 342 total jobs and an average weekly wage of $874, which supports the low absolute price points but limits income growth potential. Housing supply is virtually non-existent, with only one new unit authorized in 2025, creating a static inventory environment that likely contributes to the recent price appreciation despite the tiny population of 1,652. Migration data reveals a net gain of 29 filers, with inflows and outflows averaging similar incomes around $71,000, indicating that demographic shifts are not driven by high-earning professionals but rather by modest household movements. The 79.1% owner-occupancy rate suggests a stable, long-term resident base, yet the 18.8% rent burden, slightly above the national average of 17.2%, indicates that rental costs are becoming a heavier financial strain relative to local earnings. For potential buyers, the primary implication is that entry costs are minimal, but the lack of new construction means competition for existing inventory may intensify if the recent price trajectory continues. The 7.40% national mortgage rate applies to this market, but the low loan amounts required for a $101,600 home mitigate the impact of high interest rates on monthly payments compared to higher-cost regions. The divergence between the stagnant local economy, evidenced by low employment and wages, and the rising asset values presents a unique risk-reward profile. While the price-to-rent ratio favors ownership, the rapid appreciation from 2021 to 2024 suggests that the market is adjusting to scarcity rather than economic expansion. Investors and buyers must weigh the low absolute price against the limited liquidity of a market with only 56 establishments and negligible new supply, recognizing that value appreciation is likely driven by supply constraints rather than robust local income growth.
