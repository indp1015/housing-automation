📝 Local Market Analysis

Gunnison County, Colorado, exhibits a distinct renter’s advantage despite elevated home valuations, characterized by a price-to-rent ratio of 41.4 compared to the national average of 18.7. This disparity suggests that renting is significantly more cost-effective relative to ownership costs, a structural feature driven by the median home value of $621,800 against a median gross rent of $1,252. The market classification is further supported by the owner occupancy rate of 66.8%, indicating a substantial rental segment that benefits from this pricing divergence. While home prices have surged, the relative affordability of rental units remains a primary driver for household formation in the area. The multi-year trend reveals a period of intense appreciation followed by recent stabilization. Home values increased from $313,900 in 2017 to $621,800 in 2024, with peak year-over-year growth of 21.7% in 2022, moderating to 4.1% in 2024. However, recent momentum data indicates a resurgence, with the FHFA House Price Index showing an 8.61% year-over-year increase in 2025, outperforming the state average of -0.61% by 9.22 percentage points. This divergence highlights a specific local demand shock rather than a broad state-wide trend. Affordability remains the primary stressor, as the price-to-income ratio stands at 7.36, more than double the national benchmark of 3.20. Despite a median household income of $84,527, which is 24.3% above the national average, the high entry price for homeownership creates significant barriers for local earners. Regional context underscores the county’s premium positioning, with home values 45.4% above the state average and 173.1% above the national mean. This premium is sustained by strong vitality metrics, evidenced by a net migration inflow of 935 filers between 2021 and 2022, where incoming residents reported an average income of $112,765. The local economy supports this demand through an average weekly wage of $1,023 and total employment of 7,188 jobs. Housing supply remains constrained, with only 138 new units authorized in 2025, predominantly single-family homes. This limited supply, coupled with a 7.40% national mortgage rate, exacerbates affordability challenges. The composite assessment reflects this tension: high vitality and momentum scores are offset by critically low affordability and stability metrics, resulting in a stressed market profile where price growth outpaces income growth, creating a persistent gap between local earning power and asset values.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Gunnison County is 45.4% above the Colorado state average and 173.1% above the U.S. national average. The median rent of $1,252/month is 3.8% below the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 41.4, Gunnison County is a strongly rent-favored market. Home prices are high relative to rents, meaning renting is typically far cheaper than owning in the near term.

Gunnison County has an owner-occupancy rate of 66.8%, compared to 71.9% in Colorado and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more renter-heavy market than the U.S. average.
