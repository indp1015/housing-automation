📝 Local Market Analysis

DeKalb County, Georgia, operates as a distinctively stressed buyer’s market, characterized by significant affordability constraints despite moderate recent price appreciation. The county’s median home value of $357,800 exceeds the national average by 57.1% and the state average by 80.8%, while the median household income of $80,644 sits only 18.6% above the national mean. This disparity results in a price-to-income ratio of 4.44, substantially higher than the national benchmark of 3.20, indicating that housing costs consume a disproportionate share of local purchasing power. Consequently, the market exhibits low affordability, a primary driver of the overall stress classification. While the price-to-rent ratio of 17.6 is slightly below the national average of 18.7, suggesting relative value in purchasing versus renting, the rent burden stands at 25.2% of income compared to the national 17.2%, highlighting that even rental housing imposes a heavy financial load on residents. The multi-year trend reveals a period of rapid expansion that has recently decelerated. Home values surged from $176,000 in 2017 to $357,800 in 2024, with a peak year-over-year increase of 17.8% in 2022, before moderating to 8.0% in 2024. This cooling is further confirmed by the FHFA Home Price Index, which shows a recent year-over-year change of +0.69%, significantly underperforming the Georgia state average of +1.83%. This divergence suggests that DeKalb’s market is stabilizing or correcting relative to the broader state economy. Economically, the county supports 266,367 jobs with an average weekly wage of $1,546, while new housing supply remains limited, with only 1,321 units authorized in 2025. Although net migration of +6,169 filers indicates sustained demographic growth, the high 7.40% national mortgage rate continues to suppress demand, contributing to the current momentum profile. The market’s vitality is supported by steady employment and population inflows, yet stability is compromised by the sharp contrast between historical price spikes and current stagnation. The composite assessment reflects low scores in affordability and momentum, driven by the high cost of entry relative to local wages and the recent flattening of price growth. For potential stakeholders, the data indicates a market where entry barriers are high, and recent price appreciation has slowed considerably compared to the state. The combination of elevated price-to-income ratios, high rent burdens, and decelerating price momentum defines a market that is currently constrained by affordability rather than supply shortages, as evidenced by the continued, albeit modest, authorization of new residential units.

Market Analysis

The median home value in DeKalb County is 80.8% above the Georgia state average and 57.1% above the U.S. national average. The median rent of $1,692/month is 75.3% above the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 17.6, the buy-vs-rent decision in DeKalb County is balanced. Whether buying or renting makes more sense depends on your time horizon, down payment capacity, and local market conditions.

DeKalb County has an owner-occupancy rate of 58.8%, compared to 70.6% in Georgia and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more renter-heavy market than the U.S. average.
