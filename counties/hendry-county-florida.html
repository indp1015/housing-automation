📝 Local Market Analysis

Hendry County, Florida, presents a complex housing landscape characterized by rapid appreciation against a backdrop of constrained local income. The market leans toward a renter’s advantage in relative terms, evidenced by a Price-to-Rent Ratio of 18.2, which sits slightly below the national average of 18.7. However, this metric masks significant affordability stress for potential homeowners. The Price-to-Income Ratio stands at 3.89, exceeding the national benchmark of 3.20, indicating that median home values are disproportionately high relative to the median household income of $56,393. This disparity is further highlighted by a rent burden of 21.3%, well above the national average of 17.2%, suggesting that housing costs consume a larger share of local wages than in most other U.S. regions. The primary driver of this tension is a sharp divergence in growth rates. While median home values surged from $79,700 in 2017 to $219,200 in 2024, representing a cumulative increase of nearly 175%, median gross rent has remained relatively static at $1,003. This decoupling suggests that the recent price spike is not fully supported by corresponding increases in rental yields or local purchasing power. Recent momentum continues to outpace the broader state, with the FHFA HPI showing a +5.06% year-over-year change compared to Florida’s +0.91%. This outperformance occurs despite a low vitality score, driven by a modest employment base of 10,317 jobs and an average weekly wage of $1,111. The economic context is further shaped by 470 new housing units authorized in 2025, predominantly single-family, which may not be sufficient to cool the market given the net inflow of 1,212 filers in the 2021-2022 period. Affordability remains the critical constraint, reflected in the low affordability component of the overall market assessment. Stability is also compromised by the extreme volatility seen in 2022, when prices jumped 31.1% year-over-year. Although growth has moderated to 15.6% in 2024, the cumulative effect leaves the market significantly less affordable than its 2017 baseline. For prospective buyers, the 7.32% national mortgage rate exacerbates the cost burden, making entry into the market difficult without substantial income above the local median. Renter dynamics are similarly pressured by the high rent burden, limiting disposable income. The data indicates a market where asset values are rising faster than local economic fundamentals can sustain, creating a fragile equilibrium that relies heavily on external demand rather than internal economic strength.

The median home value in Hendry County is 22.9% below the Florida state average and 3.7% below the U.S. national average. The median rent of $1,003/month is 23.0% below the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 18.2, the buy-vs-rent decision in Hendry County is balanced. Whether buying or renting makes more sense depends on your time horizon, down payment capacity, and local market conditions.
