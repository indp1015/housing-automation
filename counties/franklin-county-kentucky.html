📝 Local Market Analysis

Franklin County, Kentucky, presents a complex housing landscape characterized by significant affordability constraints despite modest nominal growth. The market leans toward a balanced structure with a slight tilt toward renters, evidenced by a price-to-rent ratio of 18.7, which aligns precisely with the national average. However, the owner occupancy rate of 64.5% and a median home value of $216,600 suggest a traditional suburban profile. The primary stressor is affordability, where the price-to-income ratio stands at 3.32, exceeding the national benchmark of 3.20. Although the rent burden is marginally higher than the national average at 17.8% versus 17.2%, the purchasing power required to enter the owner-occupied segment is increasingly strained relative to the median household income of $65,298. Trend analysis reveals a period of rapid appreciation followed by stabilization. Home values surged from $139,300 in 2017 to $216,600 in 2024, with a peak year-over-year increase of 18.7% in 2022. Recent momentum has moderated, with the FHFA HPI showing a 1.59% year-over-year change in 2025, significantly underperforming the state average of 4.26%. This divergence indicates that Franklin County’s recent price growth has decelerated relative to the broader Kentucky market. While home values are rising faster than rents, the absolute gap remains narrow, keeping the price-to-rent metric neutral. The local economy supports this trajectory through steady employment, with 21,575 jobs and an average weekly wage of $1,156, though net migration of +1,116 filers suggests moderate demographic pressure. Supply constraints are evident in the 2025 building permit data, which authorized only 96 new units, predominantly single-family (92). This limited new construction, combined with a 7.40% national mortgage rate, restricts buyer entry and sustains demand for existing inventory. The market’s vitality is driven by consistent economic fundamentals, yet stability metrics remain low due to the historical volatility of 2020-2022 price spikes. Consequently, the overall assessment reflects a stressed environment where affordability is the dominant negative factor, offsetting positive momentum in employment and population growth. Potential buyers face a challenging entry point, as income levels have not kept pace with the 55% cumulative increase in home values since 2017, while renters experience costs that are statistically average nationally but rising locally.

The median home value in Franklin County is 34.7% above the Kentucky state average and 4.9% below the U.S. national average. The median rent of $967/month is 21.9% above the state average.
