📝 Local Market Analysis

Washington County, Iowa, presents a balanced market structure, leaning slightly toward buyers due to strong affordability metrics despite recent price acceleration. The price-to-income ratio stands at 2.70, significantly below the national average of 3.20, while the rent burden is 13.9% of income, well under the 17.2% national benchmark. These figures indicate that housing costs remain manageable relative to local earning power, with a median household income of $75,647 supporting a median home value of $203,900. Although the price-to-rent ratio of 19.3 slightly exceeds the national average of 18.7, suggesting a marginal premium on ownership, the overall cost structure remains favorable for both renters and buyers compared to broader U.S. standards. The multi-year trend reveals a distinct shift in market dynamics. After modest growth between 2017 and 2021, home values surged by 14.6% in 2022 and 9.4% in 2023, before moderating to 2.2% in 2024. This deceleration aligns with the FHFA HPI’s recent 1.41% year-over-year increase, which lags behind the state’s 2.25% growth, indicating that Washington County is currently underperforming its regional peers. A notable anomaly exists in the divergence between asset classes; while home prices have risen sharply, the median gross rent of $879 suggests that rental costs have not kept pace with the equity appreciation, reinforcing the relative value of ownership. This pattern is supported by positive net migration of 427 filers, though the local economy shows limited vitality, evidenced by a low vitality component in the composite assessment. Economic context further shapes this trajectory. With only 25 new housing units authorized in 2025, supply constraints are minimal, potentially limiting future price volatility. However, the local employment base of 7,171 jobs and an average weekly wage of $1,008 provide a stable but modest foundation. The current 7.40% mortgage rate imposes higher carrying costs, yet the county’s home values remain 10.5% below the national average and 19.2% above the state average, positioning it as a premium option within Iowa. The market’s stability is tempered by slower momentum relative to the state, driven primarily by affordability strengths that offset lower economic vitality and moderate growth rates.
