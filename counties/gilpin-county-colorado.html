📝 Local Market Analysis

Gilpin County, Colorado, functions as a highly constrained, owner-dominated market where affordability is severely compromised by rapid asset appreciation. With an owner-occupancy rate of 87.5% and a price-to-income ratio of 6.02—nearly double the national average of 3.20—the market is structurally unfavorable for first-time buyers. The median home value of $573,900 sits 34.2% above the state average and 152.0% above the national mean, reflecting a premium driven by limited supply rather than broad-based income growth. This scarcity is evident in the 2025 building permit data, which shows only 22 new units authorized, all single-family, with zero multi-family construction to alleviate rental pressure. Consequently, the market classification leans heavily toward a seller’s advantage for existing homeowners, while remaining challenging for new entrants due to the high barrier to entry. The multi-year trend reveals a consistent pattern of outpacing inflation and state averages, with home values increasing from $301,700 in 2017 to $573,900 in 2024. This 90% cumulative gain contrasts sharply with the recent FHFA HPI data, which indicates a modest 0.76% year-over-year increase in 2025, outperforming the state’s -0.61% decline. This divergence suggests that while long-term appreciation has been robust, recent momentum has stabilized relative to the broader regional slowdown. The price-to-rent ratio of 28.8, significantly higher than the national 18.7, further indicates that purchasing is less economically rational than renting for many households, despite the rent burden of 20.9% exceeding the national 17.2% average. Economic fundamentals provide limited support for these price levels, with total employment at 4,296 jobs and an average weekly wage of $1,305. Although median household income is $95,361, 40.2% above the national average, it does not scale proportionally with home values. Migration data from 2021-2022 shows a net inflow of 283 filers, with incoming residents earning slightly less than those departing, suggesting the market is attracting non-wealthy migrants who may face higher cost-of-living pressures. The 7.32% national mortgage rate further dampens purchasing power, reinforcing the current challenging environment. Stability remains high due to the low volatility of the small population base, but vitality is constrained by the lack of new housing stock. Ultimately, the data indicates a market where long-term equity gains are strong, but immediate entry costs are prohibitive for the average household, driven by supply constraints and premium pricing relative to local income levels.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Gilpin County is 34.2% above the Colorado state average and 152.0% above the U.S. national average. The median rent of $1,658/month is 27.3% above the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 28.8, Gilpin County is a strongly rent-favored market. Home prices are high relative to rents, meaning renting is typically far cheaper than owning in the near term.

Gilpin County has an owner-occupancy rate of 87.5%, compared to 71.9% in Colorado and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more homeowner-heavy market than the U.S. average.
