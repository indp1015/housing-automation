📝 Editor's Insight

Gilpin County operates as a distinct owner-occupier market, evidenced by a 79.6% ownership rate and a 31.9 price-to-rent ratio, significantly exceeding the 18.0 national average. This disparity indicates that purchasing is structurally more advantageous than renting for long-term stability. Over the past five years, median home values surged at a 9.7% CAGR to $512,600, outpacing the 3.7% CAGR in median gross rents. This divergence suggests capital appreciation is concentrating in the ownership sector rather than rental yields. Affordability remains strained relative to national benchmarks; the 5.78 price-to-income ratio is nearly double the 3.09 national average, despite household incomes running 34.4% above the national mean. However, the 18.1% rent burden is comparable to the 17.2% national standard, providing some relief for non-owners. A critical insight is that while home prices are 28.8% above the Colorado state average, the modest population of 5,877 limits inventory liquidity. Consequently, buyers face a thin market where few listings exist, potentially extending time-to-close despite high nominal values. Renters should note that while current burdens are manageable, the widening gap between asset appreciation and rental growth may erode long-term affordability if ownership remains inaccessible.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Gilpin County is 34.2% above the Colorado state average and 152.0% above the U.S. national average. The median rent of $1,658/month is 27.3% above the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 28.8, Gilpin County is a strongly rent-favored market. Home prices are high relative to rents, meaning renting is typically far cheaper than owning in the near term.
