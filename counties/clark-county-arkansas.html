📝 Editor's Insight

Clark County, Arkansas, functions as a balanced market leaning toward buyer advantage. The price-to-rent ratio of 16.7 sits below the national average of 18.0, indicating that purchasing is relatively more cost-effective than renting compared to broader U.S. trends. This structural dynamic is reinforced by the owner-occupancy rate of 61.5%, suggesting a stable local demand base for homeownership rather than transient rental activity. Over the past five years, home values expanded at a compound annual growth rate of 7.2%, significantly outpacing the 4.9% increase in median gross rent. This divergence implies that equity accumulation in Clark County has accelerated faster than rental costs, rewarding long-term holders. Affordability metrics remain favorable; the price-to-income ratio of 2.89 is slightly below the national benchmark of 3.09, while the rent burden of 17.3% aligns almost perfectly with the national average of 17.2%. A critical insight emerges from the county's position relative to state averages. Although home values are 30.8% below the national mean, they are 11.5% above the Arkansas average. This premium suggests Clark County offers distinct local amenities or economic stability that justify higher prices than the surrounding state, a factor not immediately apparent when comparing only to national figures.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Clark County is 9.6% above the Arkansas state average and 31.9% below the U.S. national average. The median rent of $793/month is 2.5% above the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 16.3, the buy-vs-rent decision in Clark County is balanced. Whether buying or renting makes more sense depends on your time horizon, down payment capacity, and local market conditions.
