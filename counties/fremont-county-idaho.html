📝 Local Market Analysis

Fremont County, Idaho, presents a complex housing landscape characterized by significant valuation premiums relative to local income, classifying it as a challenging environment for both buyers and renters. The market exhibits a distinct divergence between asset appreciation and rental costs, with home values rising 38.0% above the national average while the median gross rent remains modest at $864 per month. This disparity results in a price-to-rent ratio of 30.3, substantially higher than the national benchmark of 18.7, indicating that purchasing equity is currently less financially efficient than renting for many households. The affordability metric further underscores this strain, as the price-to-income ratio stands at 4.32 compared to the national average of 3.20, suggesting that local wages do not fully support the current cost of homeownership despite a median household income of $72,683. The trend trajectory reveals a period of rapid expansion followed by moderating growth. Home values surged from $157,700 in 2017 to $314,200 in 2024, with peak annual growth reaching 23.6% in 2021 before decelerating to 11.5% in the most recent year. Recent momentum data from the FHFA HPI shows a year-over-year increase of 2.47%, which lags behind the state average of 3.48%, signaling a relative underperformance in the immediate term. This slowdown contrasts with the county’s strong vitality, evidenced by a net migration inflow of 434 filers and new construction permits for 156 units, including 126 single-family homes. However, the local economic base remains limited, with only 2,753 total jobs and an average weekly wage of $875, which constrains the purchasing power necessary to sustain high price points. Regional context highlights that while Fremont County’s home values are 11.6% below the state average, its income levels are 6.9% above the national mean, creating a unique position where high-income residents face high housing costs. The 82.6% owner occupancy rate suggests a stable, established community, yet the current 7.32% national mortgage rate exacerbates affordability challenges. The composite assessment reflects this tension, with high vitality scores driven by migration and construction activity, but lower momentum and affordability scores due to the widening gap between asset prices and local earnings. For potential participants, the data indicates that while the market is active and growing, the premium paid for housing relative to local wages remains a significant barrier, particularly for first-time buyers relying on median incomes.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Fremont County is 11.6% below the Idaho state average and 38.0% above the U.S. national average. The median rent of $864/month is 10.7% below the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 30.3, Fremont County is a strongly rent-favored market. Home prices are high relative to rents, meaning renting is typically far cheaper than owning in the near term.

Fremont County has an owner-occupancy rate of 82.6%, compared to 75.2% in Idaho and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more homeowner-heavy market than the U.S. average.
