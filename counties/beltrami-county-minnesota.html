📝 Local Market Analysis

Beltrami County presents a balanced market structure, characterized by a price-to-rent ratio of 18.8, which aligns almost precisely with the national average of 18.7. This parity suggests that neither buyers nor renters hold a distinct structural advantage, though the high owner-occupancy rate of 70.1% indicates a strong preference for homeownership. The multi-year trend reveals a significant acceleration in asset values, with median home prices surging from $153,100 in 2017 to $237,500 in 2024. This represents a cumulative increase of approximately 55%, driven by a peak year-over-year gain of 15.5% in 2022. Although recent growth has moderated to 6.8%, the FHFA House Price Index confirms continued momentum, showing a 5.00% year-over-year increase in 2025, outperforming the Minnesota state average of 2.42% by 2.58 percentage points. Affordability metrics indicate emerging stress relative to national benchmarks. The price-to-income ratio stands at 3.44, exceeding the national average of 3.20, while the rent burden consumes 18.3% of median household income compared to the 17.2% national norm. These figures suggest that housing costs are rising faster than local earnings, which remain at a median of $68,975. Regionally, the county’s home values are nearly identical to the state average (-0.4%) but sit 4.3% above the national mean. A notable pattern is the divergence between the sharp historical price appreciation and the current economic base, where total employment stands at only 15,288 jobs with an average weekly wage of $1,034. This limited labor market capacity may constrain future price sustainability despite recent inflows of 973 net migrants. The composite assessment reflects high momentum and vitality, driven by the 5.00% HPI growth and positive net migration, but is tempered by low stability and affordability scores. Housing supply remains modest, with 168 new units authorized in 2025, insufficient to rapidly offset demand. For potential participants, the 7.40% national mortgage rate significantly impacts borrowing costs, making the elevated price-to-income ratio a critical consideration. While the market outperforms the state in price appreciation, the alignment of price and rent growth suggests a stable, albeit increasingly expensive, environment where entry barriers are rising. The data indicates a market transitioning from rapid expansion to a more constrained equilibrium, where affordability pressures are becoming the primary limiting factor for both buyers and renters.
