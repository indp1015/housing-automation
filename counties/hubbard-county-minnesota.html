📝 Local Market Analysis

Hubbard County, Minnesota, functions as a balanced market leaning toward buyer advantage, characterized by strong stability but moderate affordability. The 83.1% owner-occupancy rate and a price-to-rent ratio of 26.0, significantly above the national average of 18.7, suggest that ownership remains the dominant and often more financially viable housing strategy compared to renting. However, the price-to-income ratio of 3.93 exceeds the national benchmark of 3.20, indicating that median home values have outpaced local income growth. This dynamic is reinforced by the fact that home values are rising faster than rents, creating a widening gap between asset appreciation and rental costs. The market exhibits robust momentum, with the FHFA House Price Index showing a 3.96% year-over-year increase in 2025, outperforming the Minnesota state average of 2.42% by 1.54 percentage points. This recent acceleration follows a volatile but upward trajectory, including an 11.9% surge in 2022 and a 10.4% jump in 2024. Such consistent appreciation is supported by positive net migration of 583 filers between 2021 and 2022, though the local economy remains modest, with only 5,071 total jobs and an average weekly wage of $919. Housing supply is tightly constrained, with just 25 new units authorized in 2025, primarily single-family homes, which likely contributes to sustained price pressure despite the high national mortgage rate of 7.40%. Affordability metrics reveal a mixed picture for different resident groups. While the rent burden stands at 15.1%, below the national average of 17.2%, making renting relatively manageable, the premium on home ownership is evident. The county’s median home value of $282,600 is 18.6% higher than the state average, reflecting its relative desirability or scarcity. The high stability score is driven by the consistent, albeit accelerating, price trends and low volatility in occupancy rates. Conversely, the lower vitality and affordability scores stem from the limited employment base and the significant gap between asset values and local incomes. For potential buyers, the current environment offers strong equity growth potential, but the high entry cost relative to local wages requires careful financial planning. Renters benefit from a lower cost-of-living burden, yet face a market where long-term housing costs are increasingly driven by asset appreciation rather than rental inflation. The divergence between strong price momentum and moderate economic fundamentals suggests a market shaped more by supply constraints and external demand than by local income growth.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Hubbard County is 18.6% above the Minnesota state average and 24.1% above the U.S. national average. The median rent of $905/month is 3.5% below the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 26.0, Hubbard County is a strongly rent-favored market. Home prices are high relative to rents, meaning renting is typically far cheaper than owning in the near term.

Hubbard County has an owner-occupancy rate of 83.1%, compared to 77.9% in Minnesota and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more homeowner-heavy market than the U.S. average.
