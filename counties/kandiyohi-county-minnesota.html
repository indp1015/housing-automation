📝 Local Market Analysis

Kandiyohi County, Minnesota, functions as a balanced market with distinct affordability advantages, evidenced by a price-to-income ratio of 3.18, which aligns closely with the national average of 3.20. The rent burden stands at 14.2% of income, significantly lower than the national benchmark of 17.2%, suggesting that rental housing remains accessible relative to local earnings. However, the price-to-rent ratio of 22.3 exceeds the national average of 18.7, indicating that purchasing is comparatively more expensive than renting when viewed through a long-term cost lens. This dynamic creates a neutral environment where neither buyers nor renters hold a decisive structural advantage, though the lower rent burden provides immediate cash flow relief for tenants. The market exhibits a clear trajectory of accelerating appreciation followed by recent stabilization. Home values rose from $167,000 in 2017 to $243,200 in 2024, with peak year-over-year growth reaching 13.8% in 2022 before moderating to 5.5% in 2024. This pattern reveals a post-pandemic correction phase where initial rapid gains are normalizing. A notable divergence exists between asset classes: while home values have appreciated substantially, median gross rent has remained relatively stable at $909, contributing to the widening price-to-rent gap. This suggests that supply constraints or demand shifts are affecting owner-occupied housing more intensely than the rental sector. Regional context places the county slightly above state and national home value averages, at +2.0% and +6.8% respectively, while household income exceeds the national mean by 12.6%. This income premium supports the observed price levels. However, recent momentum signals a cooling trend; the FHFA House Price Index declined by 0.89% year-over-year, underperforming the Minnesota state average of +2.42% by 3.31 percentage points. This relative weakness is likely influenced by the high 7.40% national mortgage rate, which dampens buyer demand. Economically, the county supports 20,087 jobs with an average weekly wage of $1,030, and net migration of +623 filers indicates sustained inbound interest. Housing supply remains tight, with only 73 new units authorized in 2025, limiting new inventory. The overall market assessment reflects moderate vitality and stability, driven by strong income levels but tempered by slowing price momentum and high borrowing costs. Potential buyers should note the current price correction, while renters benefit from low burden rates, though the gap between buying and renting costs continues to widen.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Kandiyohi County is 2.0% above the Minnesota state average and 6.8% above the U.S. national average. The median rent of $909/month is 3.1% below the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 22.3, renting is generally more cost-effective than buying in the short to medium term. The market favors buyers only for long-term holds.

Kandiyohi County has an owner-occupancy rate of 72.5%, compared to 77.9% in Minnesota and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more renter-heavy market than the U.S. average.
