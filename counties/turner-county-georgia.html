📝 Editor's Insight

Turner County, Georgia, functions as a distinct buyer’s market, characterized by significant affordability advantages relative to national benchmarks. The price-to-income ratio stands at 2.64, well below the national average of 3.20, indicating that median household incomes of $36,799 support home ownership more easily than in most other U.S. regions. Furthermore, the price-to-rent ratio of 11.9 is substantially lower than the national average of 18.7, suggesting that purchasing a home is financially more advantageous than renting for long-term stability. With an owner-occupancy rate of 63.8%, the market maintains a healthy baseline of equity holders, yet the low median home value of $97,300 remains the primary driver of this classification. The multi-year trend reveals a period of accelerated appreciation following a temporary dip in 2020. Home values rose from $72,700 in 2017 to $97,300 in 2024, with peak year-over-year growth reaching 11.6% in 2022 before moderating to 2.2% in 2024. This recent slowdown contrasts sharply with the FHFA Home Price Index data, which reports a 20.76% year-over-year increase for the county, significantly outperforming the state average of 1.83%. This divergence suggests that while long-term ACS estimates show stabilization, recent mortgage transaction data indicates a sharp, recent spike in transaction prices, possibly driven by limited inventory or specific high-value sales skewing the index. Meanwhile, median gross rent remains low at $679, resulting in a rent burden of 22.1%, which is higher than the national average of 17.2%, implying that rental costs are becoming a larger share of local incomes despite low absolute dollar amounts. Regionally, Turner County’s median home value is 50.8% below the Georgia state average and 57.3% below the national average, reflecting its rural economic profile. The county’s median income is 45.9% below the national mean, yet the housing market has not adjusted downward proportionally, leading to the observed affordability metrics. The notable pattern here is the disparity between the moderate 2.2% growth in the five-year ACS estimate and the 20.76% growth in the FHFA index, highlighting a potential data lag or a recent surge in high-end transactions. For potential buyers, the low entry price and favorable price-to-income ratio present a strong value proposition, while renters should note that although absolute rent is low, the relative burden is above the national norm. The market currently favors those seeking equity accumulation over those prioritizing immediate liquidity, given the significant discount to state and national price levels.
