📝 Local Market Analysis

Washington County, Illinois, functions as a distinctly affordable market, characterized by a price-to-income ratio of 1.99, significantly below the national average of 3.20. This affordability is reinforced by a rent burden of 12.5%, well under the national benchmark of 17.2%. The market leans toward buyer-favorable conditions, evidenced by a price-to-rent ratio of 15.9, which is lower than the national average of 18.7, suggesting that ownership remains a more cost-effective long-term strategy than renting. With an owner-occupancy rate of 84.1%, the housing stock is heavily dominated by homeowners, limiting the depth of the rental inventory and further supporting the economic logic of purchasing. Recent market dynamics reveal a period of accelerated appreciation followed by stabilization. Median home values surged 19.1% in 2022, rising from $123,500 to $147,100, before moderating to a 3.9% increase in 2024 to reach $155,700. This trajectory contrasts with the FHFA House Price Index, which indicates a more robust 8.59% year-over-year growth in 2025, outperforming the Illinois state average of 5.54% by 3.05 percentage points. This divergence suggests that while census-based estimates show a cooling trend, recent mortgage transaction data reflects sustained upward pressure. The county’s median household income of $78,224 is 15.0% above the national average, providing a strong income base that supports these price levels despite the county’s small population of 13,627. Economic fundamentals indicate a constrained supply environment that supports price resilience. Only 15 new single-family units were authorized in 2025, with zero multi-family construction, signaling minimal new supply to offset demand. Local employment stands at 5,434 jobs with an average weekly wage of $1,240, while net migration added 174 filers between 2021 and 2022, with inflowing residents earning an average of $83,135. Although the 30-year fixed mortgage rate stands at 7.40%, the county’s home values remain 31.6% below the national average, offering significant entry-point value. For potential buyers, the combination of low price-to-income ratios and strong local income levels creates a favorable environment, whereas renters face a limited inventory that may restrict negotiation leverage. The market’s relative strength compared to state averages suggests that Washington County is capturing a disproportionate share of regional housing demand.
