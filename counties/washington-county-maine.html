📝 Local Market Analysis

Washington County, Maine, functions as a balanced market leaning slightly toward renters, characterized by a price-to-rent ratio of 17.2, which remains below the national average of 18.7. This metric indicates that purchasing offers a relatively favorable entry point compared to long-term leasing, while the 79.7% owner-occupancy rate suggests a stable, established residential base. The market’s affordability is reinforced by a price-to-income ratio of 2.89, significantly lower than the national benchmark of 3.20, and a rent burden of 16.8% of household income, which is marginally below the national average of 17.2%. These figures position the county as an accessible option for households earning the median $56,024, despite the broader economic headwinds present in the region. The primary driver of recent market activity is a sharp acceleration in home values, which have risen from $110,000 in 2017 to $161,800 in 2024, representing a compound annual growth rate of approximately 5.5%. This surge is particularly evident in the 14.5% year-over-year increase recorded in 2022, followed by continued double-digit gains in 2023 and 2024. Current momentum remains strong, with the FHFA House Price Index showing a 6.47% year-over-year increase in 2025, substantially outperforming the state average of 0.98%. This divergence highlights a specific local dynamic where home prices are rising much faster than rents, creating a widening gap between asset values and rental costs. While the median gross rent has stabilized at $783, the rapid appreciation of home equity suggests that the market is responding to localized demand rather than broad-based inflationary pressures affecting the entire state. Regional context further illuminates this trajectory, as Washington County’s median home value sits 37.9% below the state average and 28.9% below the national mean. This discount reflects the county’s smaller economic footprint, with only 7,491 total jobs and an average weekly wage of $1,020. However, positive net migration of 971 filers between 2021 and 2022 indicates sustained demographic interest, likely driven by the relative affordability compared to neighboring areas. Housing supply remains constrained, with only 107 new units authorized in 2025, predominantly single-family homes, which limits the ability to absorb increased demand. Consequently, the combination of low inventory, positive migration, and strong price momentum suggests that while the market remains affordable relative to national standards, the rapid pace of appreciation may challenge long-term affordability for local earners unless wage growth accelerates to match housing costs. The high momentum score is thus driven by these accelerating price indices, whereas stability is supported by the low price-to-income ratio and consistent ownership rates.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Washington County is 37.9% below the Maine state average and 28.9% below the U.S. national average. The median rent of $783/month is 23.2% below the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 17.2, the buy-vs-rent decision in Washington County is balanced. Whether buying or renting makes more sense depends on your time horizon, down payment capacity, and local market conditions.
