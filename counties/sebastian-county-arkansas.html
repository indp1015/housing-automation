📝 Local Market Analysis

Sebastian County, Arkansas, functions as a buyer’s market characterized by strong price momentum but constrained affordability. The price-to-rent ratio of 16.6 sits well below the national average of 18.7, indicating that purchasing is financially more efficient than renting relative to national norms. However, the affordability landscape remains challenging, with a price-to-income ratio of 3.05 and a rent burden of 18.4%, both slightly exceeding the national benchmarks of 3.20 and 17.2% respectively. This dynamic is driven by a divergence in asset growth; home values have surged 50.8% since 2017, rising from $116,800 to $176,200, while median gross rent has increased at a slower pace. The recent FHFA HPI data confirms this trajectory, showing a year-over-year price increase of 5.91%, which significantly outpaces the state average of 1.09%. This robust momentum contrasts sharply with the county’s stability metrics, which are weighed down by the rapid appreciation rate and the high cost of entry for local residents. The regional context reveals a county that is outperforming its state peers in value appreciation, sitting 24.6% above the Arkansas average, yet remains 22.6% below the national median home value. This gap is partially explained by the local income level of $57,790, which is 15.0% lower than the national average. The economic foundation supports this growth through steady employment, with 60,539 jobs and an average weekly wage of $1,074. Migration data further underscores the market’s vitality, recording a net inflow of 2,634 filers between 2021 and 2022, where incoming residents earned an average of $71,009. Housing supply is responding to this demand, with 525 new units authorized in 2025, predominantly multi-family (382 units), suggesting a shift toward rental density to accommodate the growing population. The primary drivers of the market’s current standing are the high momentum and vitality, which are offset by low affordability and stability scores. The 7.32% national mortgage rate exacerbates the affordability challenge, making the $176,200 median home value a significant hurdle for households earning the local median income. The notable pattern of home values rising faster than rents suggests that the equity growth is outpacing the rental market’s adjustment, potentially widening the wealth gap between owners and renters. For potential buyers, the data indicates a market where entry costs are rising rapidly, necessitating careful budgeting against the local income baseline. For renters, the 18.4% rent burden indicates a tightening market, though the focus on multi-family construction may provide some relief in future supply. The overall assessment reflects a market with strong upward pressure and demographic growth, but one where the cost of living is increasingly decoupled from local wage growth.
