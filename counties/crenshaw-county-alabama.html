📝 Local Market Analysis

Crenshaw County, Alabama, exhibits a distinct buyer’s market profile, driven by a price-to-rent ratio of 14.4, significantly below the national average of 18.7. This metric indicates that purchasing is substantially more cost-effective than renting, a trend reinforced by a rent burden of only 16.3% of income, which is marginally lower than the national benchmark of 17.2%. The affordability profile is further supported by a price-to-income ratio of 2.35, well below the national average of 3.20, suggesting that median household incomes of $48,560 can comfortably sustain median home values of $114,000. Despite these favorable affordability indicators, the market displays a notable divergence in growth rates. While home values have risen from $76,800 in 2017 to $114,000 in 2024, the recent FHFA HPI data reports a county year-over-year change of +47.26%, a figure that vastly outpaces the state’s +2.79% increase. This extreme outlier in momentum likely reflects the small sample size of mortgage transactions in a rural county with only 2,794 total jobs, creating volatility that contrasts with the steady, single-digit annual appreciation observed in the Census data, which moderated to +5.2% in 2024. The economic context reveals a constrained supply environment that supports price resilience. With only four new housing units authorized in 2025, all single-family, the lack of new inventory limits entry-level options. Although net migration was positive at +212 filers, the average income of inflowing residents ($53,696) slightly exceeds that of outflowing residents ($53,490), indicating a modest improvement in the local income base. However, the local economy remains fragile, with an average weekly wage of $979 and a low establishment count of 264. The current 30-year fixed mortgage rate of 7.40% poses a significant hurdle for affordability, as high interest costs can erode the low price-to-income advantage. Consequently, while the market offers strong relative value compared to the national average, where Crenshaw County home values are 49.9% lower, potential buyers face a landscape defined by high momentum but low economic vitality. The stability of this market is compromised by the disparity between the explosive HPI growth and the modest underlying income levels, suggesting that while entry prices remain accessible, the rapid appreciation rate may outpace the ability of local households to keep up with rising housing costs.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Crenshaw County is 28.6% below the Alabama state average and 49.9% below the U.S. national average. The median rent of $661/month is 19.4% below the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 14.4, buying a home in Crenshaw County is generally more favorable than renting for those planning to stay long-term. A ratio below 15 typically signals that purchasing is financially advantageous over a multi-year horizon.

Crenshaw County has an owner-occupancy rate of 73.2%, compared to 73.2% in Alabama and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more renter-heavy market than the U.S. average.
