📝 Local Market Analysis

Howard County, Maryland, operates as a distinctively balanced market leaning toward buyer advantage, driven by robust economic fundamentals despite elevated entry costs. The median home value stands at $597,900, significantly outpacing the national average by 162.6% and the state average by 61.6%. This premium is supported by a median household income of $149,763, which exceeds the national mean by 120.2%. Consequently, while the price-to-income ratio of 3.99 is higher than the national benchmark of 3.20, the rent burden of 16.8% remains slightly below the national average of 17.2%, indicating that rental costs are not the primary driver of financial stress. The price-to-rent ratio of 23.7, however, suggests that buying is economically more viable than renting for long-term residents, as home values are rising faster than rents. Recent momentum indicates a stabilizing market after a period of rapid appreciation. Home values increased by 14.1% in 2022 before moderating to 3.7% in 2024. The FHFA House Price Index reflects this deceleration, showing a year-over-year change of +2.02% in 2025, which outperforms the state’s +0.95% growth. This positive divergence highlights the county’s relative strength. Economically, the market is underpinned by 154,406 jobs and an average weekly wage of $1,747. Housing supply remains constrained, with only 728 new units authorized in 2025, split between 329 single-family and 399 multi-family homes. Net migration of +3,541 filers further tightens demand, with incoming households earning an average of $151,838. The current 30-year fixed mortgage rate of 7.40% adds significant carrying costs, limiting price growth potential despite strong local wealth. The overall market assessment is characterized by high momentum but low affordability. The stability metric is bolstered by consistent income levels and moderate price appreciation, whereas the affordability axis is dragged down by the high price-to-income ratio. Vitality is constrained by the limited supply of new construction relative to population growth. For potential buyers, the market offers strong equity growth potential, evidenced by the outperformance of state averages, but requires substantial income to mitigate the impact of high mortgage rates. Renters may find the current environment less favorable for long-term cost savings compared to buyers, given the widening gap between home value appreciation and rent increases. The data suggests a market where economic resilience supports price levels, but affordability remains the primary barrier to broader participation.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Howard County is 61.6% above the Maryland state average and 162.6% above the U.S. national average. The median rent of $2,099/month is 43.9% above the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 23.7, renting is generally more cost-effective than buying in the short to medium term. The market favors buyers only for long-term holds.

Howard County has an owner-occupancy rate of 71.5%, compared to 72.7% in Maryland and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more renter-heavy market than the U.S. average.
