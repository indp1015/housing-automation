📝 Editor's Insight

Calhoun County, Florida, functions as a distinct buyer’s market, characterized by a price-to-rent ratio of 15.5, which sits well below the national average of 18.7. This metric indicates that purchasing is significantly more cost-effective than renting relative to the asset’s value. The market’s affordability is further reinforced by a price-to-income ratio of 2.88, which is lower than the national benchmark of 3.20. Consequently, the median household income of $50,517 supports a median home value of $145,300 with less financial strain than seen in most U.S. markets. The rent burden stands at 18.5% of income, slightly exceeding the national average of 17.2%, suggesting that while ownership is accessible, rental costs are becoming a marginal pressure point for tenants. The multi-year trend reveals a period of aggressive appreciation followed by a recent deceleration. Home values surged from $80,000 in 2017 to $145,300 in 2024, representing an 81.6% cumulative increase. The peak year-over-year growth of 14.5% in 2022 has moderated to 8.4% in 2024, signaling a cooling of the rapid expansion phase. A notable divergence exists between asset classes, as home values are rising faster than rents, which remain stagnant at a median of $779 per month. This disparity suggests that the rental market is not keeping pace with the speculative or demand-driven growth seen in the ownership sector, potentially widening the wealth gap between owners and renters over time. Regionally, Calhoun County remains a low-cost outlier. The median home value is 36.2% below the national average and 48.9% below the Florida state average. Similarly, the median household income is 25.7% below the national mean. This significant discount relative to both the state and national benchmarks positions the county as an entry-level market. For potential buyers, the current environment offers high value relative to income, provided they can secure financing at prevailing rates. Renters, however, face a market where the long-term cost of renting is increasingly disadvantageous compared to the purchase price, given the low price-to-rent ratio. The 78.8% owner-occupancy rate further solidifies the community’s stability, indicating that a large majority of residents have already capitalized on the lower entry costs, leaving a smaller pool of available rental inventory that may drive future rent adjustments upward if demand persists.
