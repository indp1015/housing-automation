📝 Editor's Insight

Tehama County exhibits a distinct renter’s market dynamic, evidenced by a price-to-rent ratio of 22.7, which exceeds the national average of 18.0. This metric indicates that purchasing offers superior long-term value compared to renting, despite high entry barriers. The five-year trend reveals robust appreciation, with home values growing at a 10.0% CAGR versus a 7.4% CAGR for rents. This divergence suggests that asset values are expanding faster than rental yields, potentially signaling an overextension of equity relative to cash flow fundamentals. Affordability remains constrained; the price-to-income ratio stands at 5.10, significantly above the national benchmark of 3.09. Additionally, the rent burden consumes 22.5% of median income, surpassing the 17.2% national average. A critical insight emerges from the county’s 67.6% owner-occupancy rate. This high tenure stability implies limited rental inventory, which likely suppresses rent growth relative to home values. Consequently, prospective buyers face a competitive acquisition landscape, while renters encounter a tight supply environment that elevates housing costs despite the county’s lower median income of $61,834.

Market Analysis

The median home value in Tehama County is 44.3% below the California state average and 49.1% above the U.S. national average. The median rent of $1,184/month is 29.9% below the state average.

With a price-to-rent ratio of 23.9, renting is generally more cost-effective than buying in the short to medium term. The market favors buyers only for long-term holds.

Tehama County has an owner-occupancy rate of 67.1%, compared to 63.4% in California and 73.3% nationally. This indicates a more renter-heavy market than the U.S. average.
