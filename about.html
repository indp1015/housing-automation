About US Housing Data

Last updated: 2026-10-08

US Housing Data is an independent data resource that makes U.S. housing market information accessible to everyone — home buyers, renters, investors, journalists, researchers, and policymakers.

Our Mission

The U.S. housing market is complex and fragmented. Reliable information about home values, rent prices, and homeownership rates is often locked behind paywalls or buried in government databases. We collect, organize, and present this data in a clear, comparable format so anyone can make informed decisions.

Data Sources

All data on this site is sourced from the U.S. Census Bureau's American Community Survey (ACS), specifically the 2023 5-Year Estimates, the most recent comprehensive dataset available at the county level.

We use the following ACS variables:

  • B25077 — Median Home Value
  • B25064 — Median Gross Rent
  • B25001 — Total Housing Units
  • B25003 — Tenure (Owner vs. Renter Occupied)

The Price-to-Rent Ratio on our pages is calculated as Median Home Value ÷ (Median Monthly Rent × 12), a common rule-of-thumb metric for comparing the cost of buying vs. renting.

Methodology

Our data pipeline fetches raw ACS data directly from the U.S. Census Bureau API, normalizes it, and calculates derived metrics. Pages are regenerated automatically on a daily schedule to reflect any data corrections or updates.

We do not modify the underlying values. We exclude counties where the Census Bureau reports missing or zero values for the key variables.

Editorial Standards

US Housing Data is an automated data project. Content is generated from public government data using open-source software. No sponsored content, affiliate relationships, or paid placements influence the data we publish.
